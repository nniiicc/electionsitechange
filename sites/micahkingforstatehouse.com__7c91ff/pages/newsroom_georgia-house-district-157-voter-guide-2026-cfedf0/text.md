The Complete 2026 Voter Guide to Georgia House District 157: Tattnall, Jeff Davis, Appling & Evans Counties
Every two years, a small number of voters across four rural Southeast Georgia counties decide who represents their communities in the Georgia House of Representatives.
That representative votes on the state budget, on Medicaid policy, on Public Service Commission oversight, on property tax law, and on dozens of other decisions that determine whether rural Georgia gets a fair shake in Atlanta.
This guide is designed to give voters across District 157 everything they need in one place: what the district actually is, who is on the 2026 ballot, exactly how and when to vote, the issues driving this race, and where to find trustworthy, nonpartisan information along the way.
What Is District 157?
Georgia House District 157 is a rural, agricultural district in Southeast Georgia made up of Tattnall, Jeff Davis, and Evans Counties in their entirety, along with a portion of Appling County.
It is represented in the Georgia General Assembly’s lower chamber, one of 180 House districts statewide, and its current representative, Bill Werkheiser, has held the seat since he was first elected in 2014.
Geography and Shared Identity
The four counties that make up this district sit in the Coastal Plain and wiregrass region of the state, roughly an hour or two inland from Savannah.
Tattnall County, the district’s largest by population, is the historic parent county of the region, having given up territory over time to help form neighboring Evans County in 1914.
Jeff Davis County, centered on the county seat of Hazlehurst, was itself carved out of Appling and Coffee Counties in 1905.
Appling County, whose county seat of Baxley sends part of its territory into District 157, was established in 1818 and once served as the parent county for a dozen other Georgia counties, including Jeff Davis.
In other words, these four communities are not simply adjacent to one another on a map.
Their political and civic identities are literally descended from one another, a shared lineage that shows up today in overlapping school systems, shared hospital service areas, and a regional economy built on the same core industries: Vidalia onions, timber, poultry, and row crops.
Demographics That Define the District
The numbers tell a consistent story across all four counties.
Median household income in Jeff Davis County sits at roughly $40,283, in Appling County at roughly $46,651, and in Tattnall County at roughly $51,868, all well below Georgia’s statewide median of approximately $77,353.
Poverty rates in Jeff Davis and Appling Counties run above 21 percent, nearly double the national average, while Tattnall and Evans Counties see rates in the 13 to 15 percent range.
This is a working-class, rural district, and its representation in Atlanta should reflect that reality rather than treat it as an afterthought.

How to Vote in the 2026 Election
Georgia’s election calendar has several key dates that every District 157 voter should mark now.
Key 2026 Dates
- Voter Registration Deadline: Monday, October 5, 2026, which is 29 days before the general election, as required under Georgia law.
- Early (Advance) Voting: State law requires early voting to begin roughly three weeks before Election Day, with the 2026 general election early voting period expected to run from approximately October 13 through October 30, 2026.
Georgia law also requires Saturday early voting availability, though specific hours and additional Sunday voting dates vary by county, so confirm your local schedule with your County Board of Elections.
- Election Day: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.
Polls are open from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., and if you are in line by 7 p.m., you are legally entitled to vote.
How to Register
Georgia residents can register to vote online, by mail, or in person through their County Board of Registrars.
Online registration and status checks are available through the Secretary of State’s My Voter Page at mvp.sos.ga.gov.
To register, you must be a United States citizen, a legal resident of Georgia and the county in which you are registering, at least 17 and a half years old (though you must be 18 by Election Day to actually vote), and not currently serving a felony sentence or found to be mentally incompetent by a court.
What to Bring on Election Day
Under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 21-2-417), all in-person voters must show a valid form of photo identification.
Acceptable forms include a Georgia driver’s license, a Georgia voter ID card issued free of charge by your County Board of Registrars, a U.S. passport, a military ID, or a tribal photo ID.
If poll workers are unable to verify your identity or eligibility, you have the right to request a provisional ballot, and your vote will count once you resolve the issue with your County Board of Registrars within three days of the election.
Absentee and Advance Voting Options
Any registered Georgia voter may request an absentee ballot, which can be applied for online, by mail, email, fax, or in person at your county registrar’s office.
Completed absentee ballots can be returned by mail or dropped off at a designated drop box or your County Board of Registrar’s Office up until polls close at 7 p.m. on Election Day, but only if you already requested and received that ballot in advance.
Key Issues in the District
Every issue on this ballot ultimately traces back to a handful of core challenges facing Tattnall, Jeff Davis, Appling, and Evans Counties.
Cost of Living
Median incomes across this district run well below the statewide average, while electricity bills, grocery costs, and property values have all climbed in recent years.
Georgia’s move to a flat state income tax delivers its largest dollar benefit to the state’s highest earners rather than to working families here, and rising Georgia Power bills, tied in part to statewide data center growth, land hardest on rural households that already use more electricity relative to income than their urban counterparts.

Rural Healthcare
Evans Memorial Hospital in Claxton is currently planning for a multimillion-dollar budget shortfall and weighing whether to close its Intensive Care Unit.
Appling, Jeff Davis, and Evans Counties are formally classified as maternity care deserts, and Georgia remains one of only ten states that has not adopted full Medicaid expansion.
These are not abstract policy debates.
They determine whether a family in this district can get emergency care, prenatal care, or specialist treatment without driving an hour or more.

Protecting Agriculture
Tattnall and neighboring Toombs County together grow more than 90 percent of the nation’s Vidalia onion crop, and agriculture and agribusiness generate an estimated $91.4 billion in economic impact statewide each year.
Family farms across this district face mounting pressure from rising utility costs tied to data center growth, property tax complications from heirs’ property, and increasingly volatile weather.

County Election Directory
Voter registration and elections in Georgia are administered at the county level.
Contact information and hours can change, so it is always worth confirming current details directly with your county before Election Day.
Tattnall County Board of Elections and Registration
Located in Reidsville, the Tattnall County Board of Elections and Registration is responsible for voter registration, early voting, and Election Day administration for the county, operating under the Georgia Election Code and State Election Board rules.
The board consists of three members appointed by the Tattnall County Board of Commissioners.
Contact the Tattnall County Courthouse in Reidsville or visit tattnallcountyga.com for current hours and polling locations.
Jeff Davis County Board of Elections
Based in Hazlehurst, the Jeff Davis County Board of Elections manages voter registration and conducts all elections held within the county under the Georgia Elections Code, State Election Board rules, and applicable federal regulations.
The county’s general office line is 912-375-6611.
Visit jeffdaviscountyga.gov for elections-specific hours and updates.
Appling County Board of Elections
Located in Baxley, the Appling County Board of Elections handles voter registration and election administration for county residents, including the portion of Appling County that falls within District 157.
Contact the Appling County Courthouse in Baxley or visit the county’s official website to confirm current polling locations and early voting hours.
Evans County Board of Elections and Registration
Based in Claxton, the Evans County Board of Elections and Registration is responsible for registering voters and conducting fair, impartial elections in accordance with the Georgia Election Code and State Election Board rules, and it issues absentee ballots and oversees both early voting and Election Day operations.
Visit evanscounty.org for current office hours and polling information.
