Absentee Voting Rules Under SB 202 • The Bottom Line With the November 3, 2026 General Election rapidly approaching, voters across Southeast Georgia are focused on the kitchen-table issues that determine our future: spiraling electric bills, rural hospital access, and keeping our local tax dollars accountable.
However, none of those issues can be addressed at the ballot box unless your voter registration is completely in order.
Under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 21-2-224), the strict cutoff to register to vote or update your address for the General Election is the fifth Monday before Election Day.
For the 2026 election, that date is Monday, October 5, 2026 .
That leaves just six days to ensure your voice is heard.
Because House District 157 spans four rural counties (Appling, Evans, Jeff Davis, and Tattnall), unique local addressing factors like rural route mail delivery, P.O.
Boxes, and recent county boundary changes frequently cause eligible citizens to be flagged as “Inactive” without their knowledge.
Here is everything you need to audit your registration status in three minutes, understand the law, and find your local election office.
1.
The 3-Minute Registration & Status Audit Do not assume your registration is current simply because you voted a couple of years ago or have a valid driver’s license.
Routine state voter list maintenance occurs continuously.
Take three minutes right now to complete this checklist: Visit the Georgia My Voter Page (MVP): Navigate to mvp.sos.ga.gov .
Log In: Enter your first initial, last name, county of residence, and date of birth.
Verify Three Key Fields: Registration Status: Confirm whether you are marked “Active” or “Inactive.” Residential Address: Ensure the physical address listed is where you actually reside (not a P.O.
Box).
Precinct & Polling Place: Confirm your assigned Election Day voting location.
Download Your Sample Ballot: Click the link labeled “Click Here for Sample Ballots” to view the exact composite ballot you will see on the voting machine in November, including federal, legislative, and local county referendums.
If you need to register for the first time or update your address, you can submit the change digitally before 11:59 PM on Monday, October 5 via the Georgia Online Voter Registration Portal using your Georgia Driver’s License or State ID number.
2.
Deciphering the “Inactive” Voter Myth One of the most widespread points of confusion across rural Georgia is the difference between an “Inactive” voter and a “Purged” voter.
If you log into the My Voter Page and see your status flagged as “Inactive,” do not panic: you have NOT been removed from the voter rolls, and you CAN still cast a regular ballot.
How List Maintenance Works (O.C.G.A. § 21-2-234 & § 21-2-235) Under Georgia statute, voters who have had no contact with the electoral system for five consecutive years (no ballots cast, no absentee requests, and no address updates at the DDS) are mailed a forwardable confirmation notice by the Secretary of State.
If that notice is not returned within 30 days, or if official election mail was returned by the post office as undeliverable, the voter’s status changes to “Inactive.” What to Do If You Are Inactive You Can Vote Normally: An inactive voter retains the exact same legal right to vote as an active voter.
You do not have to cast a provisional ballot.
The Electronic Poll Pad Fix: When you arrive at your early voting hub or Election Day precinct, the check-in poll pad will prompt you to confirm that you still reside at your registered address.
Automatic Restoration: Once you sign the affirmation screen and cast your ballot, your voter record is automatically and instantly restored to “Active” status.
When Cancellation Happens: A voter is only fully removed from the rolls if they remain inactive and fail to vote in two consecutive federal general election cycles (a window spanning roughly nine years).
The Rural Address Trap: P.O.
Boxes vs.
911 Physical Addresses In rural agricultural areas across Appling, Evans, Jeff Davis, and Tattnall counties, many families receive mail at a P.O.
Box in town rather than their physical road address.
Because Georgia law assigns voting precincts based strictly on physical residence, county election mail sent to a physical address without a delivery receptacle gets returned to the registrar as “undeliverable.” That returned letter automatically triggers an inactive notice.
Checking mvp.sos.ga.gov now allows you to verify that both your physical residence and your mailing address are recorded accurately.
3.
Moving Within the County vs.
Crossing County Lines If you have moved recently, Georgia law treats your registration differently depending on whether your move crossed a county line: Moving Within the Same County: If you moved from one home to another within Appling, Evans, Jeff Davis, or Tattnall County, you remain eligible to vote in your county.
If you did not update your address by October 5, you can still appear at your designated precinct on Election Day, complete a change-of-address affirmation, and vote a regular ballot.
Moving Across County Lines (O.C.G.A. § 21-2-218): If you moved across county lines, such as moving from Reidsville in Tattnall County to Claxton in Evans County, you must submit an updated registration by Monday, October 5 .
If you fail to update your registration in your new county by the deadline, state law requires you to return to your previous county’s polling precinct to vote in this election.

6.
Photo ID Requirements & Free Voter ID Cards Under O.C.G.A. § 21-2-417, voters in Georgia must present one of six approved forms of photo identification when voting in person: Georgia Driver’s License (acceptable even if expired) State or Federal Government Employee Photo ID (including city, county, or state agency IDs) Valid U.S.
Passport Valid U.S.
Military Photo ID Valid Tribal Photo ID Free Georgia Voter Identification Card How to Get a Free Georgia Voter ID Card If you do not have a driver’s license or passport, state law guarantees you the right to a free Georgia Voter Identification Card .
You can obtain this card in person at any of our four county registrar offices (Baxley, Claxton, Hazlehurst, or Reidsville) or at any Department of Driver Services (DDS) center.
To receive a free voter ID, you must execute Form DDS-579 and present: A document showing your full legal name and date of birth (such as a birth certificate).
Proof that you are actively registered to vote in Georgia.
Proof of your residential address (a current utility bill, bank statement, or paystub).
7.
Absentee Voting Rules Under SB 202 If you plan to vote by mail, be aware of two critical statutory rules established under the Election Integrity Act of 2021: Strict 11-Day Application Deadline: Applications must be physically received by your county elections office by Friday, October 23, 2026 .
Because rural mail delivery can take several days, apply as early as possible.
Identification Numbers Instead of Signatures: You must write your Georgia Driver’s License number or State ID number on both your absentee application and the inner ballot oath envelope.
If you do not have either, write the last four digits of your Social Security Number.
Drop Box Rules (O.C.G.A. § 21-2-382): Ballot drop boxes are located strictly indoors inside the early voting facilities listed above and are accessible only during early voting hours .
Once early voting ends on October 30, drop boxes are sealed, and ballots must be hand-delivered to the registrar’s desk or mailed.
All absentee ballots must be received by 7:00 PM on Election Day, November 3 .
