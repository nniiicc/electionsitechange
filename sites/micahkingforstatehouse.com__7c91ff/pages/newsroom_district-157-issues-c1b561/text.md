The Issues That Matter Most in Georgia House District 157
Georgia House District 157 is full of hardworking families, farmers, small business owners, veterans, and faith communities.
They deserve a representative who fights for them, not for party leadership, lobbyists, or the status quo in Atlanta.
I am running for State Representative because the people of Tattnall County, Glennville, Reidsville, and the surrounding communities have been patient long enough.
The issues facing our district are real, they are urgent, and they deserve more than campaign slogans.
They deserve honest conversation and genuine commitment.
Here is where I stand.
The Cost of Living Is Squeezing Our Families
Grocery runs.
Insurance renewals.
Utility bills.
Families in District 157 feel the pressure in every one of them.
Property taxes have climbed alongside home values, and insurance premiums have increased sharply in recent years without proportional improvement in service or coverage.
The State of Georgia has made some progress.
Recent income tax reductions have helped, and the exemption on most grocery purchases from the state sales tax matters for working families.
But more needs to be done.
I believe we must continue reviewing homestead exemptions, increase transparency when local governments raise property taxes, and carefully evaluate any regulation that adds cost without adding value to Georgia families.
The issue isn’t whether government should do something.
It’s whether government is doing the right things in the most efficient way possible.
Our district deserves a representative who understands the difference.
You can read more about my full strategy in my cost of living and economy plan.
Our Emergency Services Are Being Stretched Too Thin
This is one of the most urgent and underreported issues in our district.
Tattnall County EMS responds to hundreds of emergency calls a year at state-operated correctional facilities.
Those are calls that pull ambulances away from our own residents.
When an ambulance is transporting an inmate from Smith State Prison to a regional hospital, that ambulance is unavailable for the grandmother having a stroke, the child injured in a car accident, or the farmer who took a fall in the field.
The State of Georgia operates Smith State Prison.
It should also ensure that counties providing emergency services to those facilities are fairly compensated and adequately resourced.
Our local taxpayers should not be subsidizing the cost of state operations without relief.
I will advocate in the General Assembly for state reimbursement frameworks that protect rural counties who carry this burden without recognition or support.
For a detailed breakdown of how much this is costing our county, read my full report on who is paying for Smith State Prison EMS calls.
Agriculture Is the Backbone of This District
District 157 is agricultural land.
Tattnall County is one of Georgia’s premier sweet onion-producing counties, and farming (row crops, timber, livestock) defines the economic and cultural identity of this community.
Agricultural families face unique pressures: rising input costs, labor shortages, unpredictable weather, and regulations that were often designed for large corporate operations, not family farms.
I will fight to protect agricultural tax exemptions, expand workforce training for farm labor and agricultural trades, and oppose any state-level policy that unfairly burdens small and mid-sized farm operations.
Georgia’s farmers need a seat at the table instead of just a mention in a campaign speech.
You can view my complete agriculture and farm policy platform to see how we can fight back against rising input costs and corporate monopolies.
Rural Healthcare Access Is a Crisis in Slow Motion
Too many families in District 157 drive 45 minutes or more for routine medical care.
Too many rural hospitals are operating on thin margins, and too many communities are one facility closure away from a genuine healthcare desert.
Georgia’s Certificate of Need (CON) laws require state approval before healthcare facilities can expand or add services, and they deserve honest review.
I believe we can maintain protections for rural hospital viability while also expanding access through telehealth, mobile clinics, and healthcare workforce development programs.
Healthcare is not a partisan issue.
It is a family issue.
And in rural Georgia, the status quo is not working.
Read my comprehensive rural healthcare policy page to understand the full picture for Evans, Jeff Davis, Appling, and Tattnall counties.
Education and Workforce Development
The strongest economic development tool any community has is an educated, skilled workforce.
Our local schools deserve the resources, facilities, and teacher compensation that attract and retain great educators.
At the same time, not every path to a good career runs through a four-year university.
I strongly support expanding access to vocational and technical training in trades like construction, welding, electrical, HVAC, and agricultural technology.
These are careers that pay well, that cannot be outsourced, and that our community desperately needs.
I will advocate for curriculum partnerships between our local school systems and technical colleges that give students a real pathway to economic independence, starting here in Tattnall County.
Responsive, Accountable Government
One of the most common things I hear when I knock on doors is simple: “Nobody listens to us.”
People feel ignored by their representatives.
They feel like decisions are made in Atlanta with no regard for how they land here in Reidsville or Glennville.
They are right to feel that way, and it has to change.
If I am elected to represent District 157, I will hold regular town halls.
I will answer my phone.
I will respond to my constituents every year, not just during election years.
Representing this district isn’t a part-time obligation.
It’s full-time.
