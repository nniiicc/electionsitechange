This seat has seen significant political transition this year.
After ten years of service, incumbent Republican Representative Bill Werkheiser was defeated in the May 19, 2026 Republican primary by challenger Bradley Anderson.
Voters now face a clear choice between two local residents with distinct backgrounds, experiences, and legislative visions: Micah King and Bradley B.
Anderson .
To help voters make an informed decision at the ballot box, this guide provides an objective, side-by-side comparison of the candidates, their professional backgrounds, and their approaches to the most urgent issues facing rural Southeast Georgia.

Where the Candidates Stand on Key Issues District 157 faces challenges that transcend national partisan talking points.
From hospital solvency and utility rates to timber preservation and local school funding, here is how the race frames the central questions facing Southeast Georgia.
1.
Rural Healthcare & Hospital Solvency Southeast Georgia has experienced significant strain on its healthcare infrastructure.
Facilities like Evans Memorial Hospital in Claxton and Appling Healthcare in Baxley provide essential emergency care, local jobs, and maternal health services, but operate under tight margins.
Micah King’s Approach: King views rural healthcare preservation as an urgent economic and public safety priority.
He advocates for targeted state stabilization funds for rural independent hospitals, higher Medicaid reimbursement rates for rural healthcare providers, and incentives to recruit and retain doctors and nurses in Appling, Evans, Jeff Davis, and Tattnall Counties.
Bradley Anderson’s Approach: Drawing from his business background, Anderson emphasizes reducing bureaucratic burdens, lowering state healthcare mandates, and supporting free-market options to expand access while maintaining fiscal discipline in state budget appropriations.
2.
Utility Bills, Power Rates & Data Center Growth Georgia power bills have climbed sharply due to multi-billion-dollar infrastructure buildouts and an unprecedented surge in commercial electricity demand from high-load data centers across the state.
Micah King’s Approach: King has called for aggressive consumer ratepayer protection.
He supports legislation to ensure large industrial power consumers (such as massive AI data centers) pay their fair share of new power generation costs, rather than having utility monopolies shift grid expansion costs onto rural homeowners, small businesses, and church electricity bills.
Bradley Anderson’s Approach: Anderson aligns with pro-growth economic development, emphasizing that Georgia’s competitive energy sector and business-friendly regulatory climate attract capital investment, create commercial tax revenue, and support statewide job creation.
3.
Agriculture, Timber & Family Farm Taxes Agriculture and forestry are the economic lifeblood of District 157.
Local onion growers in Glennville, timber tract owners across Appling and Jeff Davis, and poultry producers face rising input costs, unpredictable weather events, and property reassessments.
Micah King’s Approach: King strongly supports Georgia Constitutional Amendment 1 on the 2026 ballot to increase the Conservation Use Value Assessment (CUVA) acreage cap from 2,000 to 4,000 acres, ensuring family farms and multi-generational timber growers are not taxed out of their land.
He supports targeted tax exemptions for agricultural equipment and fertilizer inputs.
Bradley Anderson’s Approach: Anderson supports protecting agricultural property rights, reducing state regulations on farm operations, and maintaining low statewide income and business tax rates to keep Georgia agribusiness globally competitive.
4.
Constituent Access & Town Halls Representation in the Georgia General Assembly requires staying connected to the people back home throughout the legislative session.
Micah King’s Approach: King has pledged a strict open-door policy, promising to hold regular, quarterly town halls across Appling, Evans, Jeff Davis, and Tattnall Counties.
The campaign formally invited Bradley Anderson to a joint candidate debate at the Glennville Town Hall on Saturday, October 17, 2026, keeping a reserved podium open for him to engage directly with constituents.
Bradley Anderson’s Approach: Anderson has conducted traditional grassroots campaigning, meeting with constituents at community events, civic club meetings, and local small businesses throughout the district, emphasizing his established reputation and deep personal ties as a trusted local businessman.
