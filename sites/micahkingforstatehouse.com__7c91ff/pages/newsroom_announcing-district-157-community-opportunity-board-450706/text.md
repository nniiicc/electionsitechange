Introducing the District 157 Opportunity Board: Keeping Good Jobs and Local Talent Right Here at Home
One of the most persistent heartbreaks across Southeast Georgia is watching our brightest young people pack their bags and leave home because they believe there are no good careers here.
For years, politicians have stood on debate stages offering vague slogans about “economic growth” while doing very little to connect our people with actual opportunities on the ground.
Meanwhile, the sons and daughters of Appling, Evans, Jeff Davis, and Tattnall counties graduate from high school or technical college and assume they have to move to Savannah or Atlanta just to earn a decent living.
I reject that notion completely.
You should not have to leave the community that raised you just to build a secure, prosperous life.
Today, our team is taking direct action.
We are officially launching the District 157 Community Opportunity Board: a free, hyper-local employment and workforce hub designed specifically for the families, small businesses, and working people of our four counties.
You can explore the live board right now at votega157.com/opportunities.
1.
The Rural “Digital Desert” and the $750 Indeed Tollbooth
Why did we build this ourselves?
Because corporate hiring platforms were never designed to serve rural Georgia.
If you talk to any small business owner, contractor, or family farmer in Baxley, Claxton, Hazlehurst, or Reidsville, you hear the exact same frustration: “We need good, dependable help, but we cannot find local folks who want to work.”
At the exact same time, you can talk to young workers, apprentice tradesmen, and recent graduates who say: “I want to stay here close to my family, but nobody is hiring.”
How can both of those statements be true at the same time?
The answer lies in what labor economists call the rural digital hiring desert.
Historically, local shops relied on classified sections in our community newspapers - like The Baxley News-Banner, The Claxton Enterprise, or The Tattnall Journal - to hire workers for a flat, predictable fee.
Over the past decade, national Silicon Valley platforms took over that market.
But in recent years, their monetization models turned predatory:
- The $750 Monthly Floor: In July 2025, Indeed enforced a strict $25-per-day minimum budget floor for sponsored job postings.
For a family-owned repair shop in Tattnall County, a lumber yard in Jeff Davis, or a retail store in Claxton, sponsoring a single job opening now costs a minimum of $750 per month.
- Pay-Per-Click Gambling: Employers are charged every time someone clicks a link, whether that person is qualified or clicked by accident.
The true cost to acquire a single completed application routinely reaches $15 to $50, and climbs over $300 for specialized technical roles.
- Resume Search Paywalls: Simply viewing local candidate resumes on Indeed Smart Sourcing costs between $120 and $300 a month, while ZipRecruiter and LinkedIn Recruiter charge anywhere from $299 to $999 monthly.
Small businesses operating on thin margins cannot afford to gamble thousands of dollars on national algorithms.
When local employers cannot pay the fee, their “free” posts get pushed to the bottom of search results.
Worse, because corporate recruiters in Atlanta and Savannah bid aggressive advertising budgets, a young person in Baxley searching on their phone for “maintenance technician” gets bombarded with listings in Atlanta, Savannah, or Macon.
The algorithm actively siphons our local talent out of the district.
The District 157 Opportunity Board completely removes that corporate tollbooth.
It provides a direct digital bridge between hometown employers and hometown workers, 100% free of charge.

Real Purchasing Power: Why $55,000 in District 157 Beats $72,000 in Atlanta
When our young people compare career options, they often fall into a simple trap: seeing a $72,000 corporate salary in Atlanta and assuming it beats a $55,000 skilled trade job in Southeast Georgia.
As an Accredited Financial Counselor, I’ve run these calculations with local families before.
When you factor in the spatial friction of metropolitan living - crushing rents, high auto insurance premiums, toll roads, and urban sales taxes - the so-called “metropolitan wage premium” disappears completely.
Take a look at the real math comparing a skilled worker in District 157 to a corporate worker in Metro Atlanta:

|---|---|---|
| Gross Monthly Salary | $4,583 | $6,000 |
| Estimated Taxes (Federal, State & FICA) | -$825 | -$1,260 |
| Net Monthly Take-Home Pay | $3,758 | $4,740 |
| Median Rent / Mortgage (2-Bedroom) | -$850 | -$1,950 |

The Bottom Line: Despite earning $17,000 less on paper, the skilled worker living in Baxley, Claxton, Hazlehurst, or Reidsville walks away with $588 more in net cash every single month - over $7,000 more per year - than the worker in Atlanta.
In District 157, that $55,000 salary allows you to buy a home, save for retirement, pay for your children’s extracurriculars, and build real generational wealth without taking on $60,000 in student loan debt.

High-Wage Technical Careers Anchoring Our Region
Our district is home to premier technical, industrial, and energy employers offering exceptional earnings.
According to recent Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) and Georgia Department of Labor (GDOL) records, average weekly wages in energy-rich Appling County reached $1,083 to $1,201 (an annualized average of $56,000 to $62,000), competing directly with metropolitan counties.
The Opportunity Board features verified career openings across our four counties right now:
- Nuclear Energy & Radiation Protection (Plant Hatch - Baxley): Starting wages for nuclear technicians in training begin at approximately $28.30 per hour, with specialized Radiation Protection (RP) Technicians earning upwards of $46.15 per hour ($96,000/yr) plus overtime and union protections.
- Industrial Automation & Millwrights (Interfor - Baxley): Maintaining high-tech computerized timber processing lines pays $28.36 to $39.55 per hour ($59,000 to $82,000/yr).
- Electrical Utility Linemen (Canoochee EMC - Reidsville): Entry-level apprentice linemen start between $53,500 and $76,500 annually, scaling past $85,000 with storm-restoration experience.
- Healthcare & Clinical Care (Evans Memorial & Appling Healthcare): Critical care registered nurses, physical therapy assistants, and licensed practical nurses (LPNs) earn strong local salaries while qualifying for rural healthcare loan-forgiveness programs.
- Agribusiness, Processing & Logistics (Claxton Poultry & Dot Foods): Quality assurance technicians, transportation management, and regional CDL-A drivers earning $50,000 to $80,000+ keeping our food and logistics supply moving.
4.
The Debt-Free Pipeline: HOPE Career Grant & Local Technical Colleges
You do not need a four-year university diploma to enter these fields.
Our region is served by two outstanding institutions in the Technical College System of Georgia (TCSG): Coastal Pines Technical College (with its campus in Baxley) and Southeastern Technical College (serving Swainsboro, Vidalia, and our surrounding communities).
Their results outpace major universities:
- Coastal Pines Technical College maintains an astonishing 99% overall graduate job placement rate.
- Practical Nursing graduates at Coastal Pines achieved a 100% first-time pass rate on the NCLEX-PN national licensing exam across its campuses.
- Under the State of Georgia’s HOPE Career Grant, tuition is 100% free for eligible Georgia residents enrolling in high-demand fields like Commercial Truck Driving (CDL), Practical Nursing, Welding, Industrial Systems Technology, and Electrical Linework.
A 19-year-old in Appling, Evans, Jeff Davis, or Tattnall County can complete an apprenticeship or technical certificate with zero debt and step straight into a $50,000 to $70,000 job.
The breakdown has always been communication: getting those talented, debt-free graduates connected with local employers before national recruiters lure them away.
Our board serves as that missing bridge.

6.
Keeping Our Tax Base Strong Beyond November
Census Bureau commuter outflow data shows that over 31% of workers living in Appling County commute outside the county for work, with similar numbers across Evans, Jeff Davis, and Tattnall.
When a worker commutes an hour away to Savannah or Macon, two bad things happen to our hometowns:
- Their productive labor builds another county’s economy.
- Their daily spending - gas, lunch, hardware, groceries - happens in that other county, draining local sales tax (SPLOST) revenue away from our rural schools, roads, and emergency services.
When the local tax base shrinks, county commissioners are forced to raise property tax millage rates on our family farmers and homeowners just to keep the sheriff’s department and ambulances running.
Connecting local workers with local jobs keeps dollars in our local banks, keeps sales taxes in our hometowns, and relieves the tax burden on working property owners.
This platform is not a campaign gimmick that disappears on November 4.

It is designed to keep our small businesses thriving, keep our families together, and prove that Southeast Georgia has a bright, prosperous future.
