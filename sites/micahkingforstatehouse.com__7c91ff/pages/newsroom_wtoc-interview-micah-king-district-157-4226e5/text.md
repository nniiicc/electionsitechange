Staffing and Resource Relief for Smith State Prison • An Independent Approach Rooted in Rural Values • Glennville Town Hall: Saturday, October 17 at 6:00 PM On Friday, October 16, Savannah CBS affiliate WTOC 11 aired an in-depth profile on the race for Georgia House District 157, featuring a wide-ranging interview with Democratic nominee Micah King conducted by reporter Madalyn Bierster.
The feature examined King’s background as a father of six, a former four-year member of the Glennville City Council, and his current work as a military career counselor for the Transition Assistance Program, where he helps soldiers leaving Fort Stewart transition into civilian careers.
The complete report and broadcast video can be viewed on WTOC.com .
View this post on Instagram A post shared by WTOC (@wtoc11) Stepping Up to Give District 157 Voters a Choice King explained to WTOC that his decision to run crystallized over the winter before candidate qualifying, driven by a conviction that rural Southeast Georgia communities deserve an active, competitive election: “If no one else was going to step up and do it, somebody needed to,” King told WTOC.
“Democracy works best when you have options… my goal is to give people a voice, whether I win or not.” King emphasized that his perspective is rooted in daily working-class life in Tattnall County rather than political detachment: “I’m a part of the working class, so any decision I make that influences how much things cost or how we can buy certain things, that’s going to impact me,” King said.
“I’m not separated from the issues.
I’m directly related.
I’m in the issues, and I plan to lead from that position.” King added that legislative decisions impacting public school funding directly touch his own family, as his children attend local public schools.
Key Priorities Discussed in the WTOC Feature 1.
Expanding Healthcare and Clinic Access for Veterans With thousands of soldiers transitioning out of nearby Fort Stewart and choosing to build lives in Hinesville, Glennville, Reidsville, Claxton, and Savannah, King highlighted the lack of localized veteran care in Southeast Georgia.
King advocated for establishing local veteran centers across the four counties so former service members do not have to make two-to-three-hour drives just to reach a clinical facility or transition office, while ensuring veterans receive practical support translating their military training into high-demand civilian jobs.
2.
Protecting Rural Community Hospitals & Maternal Care King underscored that healthcare access is an existential issue across District 157, pointing to severe operational pressures facing community hospitals in Evans, Jeff Davis, and Appling counties.
He noted that long travel times for medical appointments take parents away from work and families.
Sharing his family’s personal experience, King noted that driving an hour and a half to Savannah for medical appointments can consume an entire day.
Furthermore, with significant maternal health deserts across rural Georgia, his family has had to travel to Statesboro during maternal health emergencies involving his daughter.
King called for targeted state support to keep local emergency rooms open, recruit rural doctors, and expand pediatric dental and preventive care.
3.
Strengthening Family Agriculture Describing agriculture as “king” in Southeast Georgia, King outlined an economic vision that connects local Vidalia onion growers, row-crop farmers, and timber operations directly with local distributors, regional grocery chains, and public school lunch programs - allowing Georgia-grown food to feed Georgia students.
4.
Property Tax Relief for Fixed-Income Homeowners Addressing rising home assessments, King shared conversations with homeowners in Baxley and throughout the district who have lived in their homes for decades, have no intention of selling, but face climbing county tax bills driven by external market pressures.
King proposed collaborating with local tax assessors and real estate professionals to develop relief criteria based on length of residency or age so longtime residents and seniors are not penalized for staying in their homes.
5.
Staffing and Resource Relief for Smith State Prison King noted the significant local impact of Smith State Prison in Tattnall County.
He stressed that adequate correctional staffing is urgently needed so that county emergency services, such as local ambulances and first responders, are not chronically overburdened by emergency calls from the state facility.
He also expressed support for structured rehabilitation and community reintegration programs.
An Independent Approach Rooted in Rural Values Asked by WTOC how he approaches political identity in a traditionally conservative district, King emphasized practical problem-solving over partisan conformity: “I’m Micah King before I’m a Democrat,” he said.
King described himself as an advocate of Second Amendment rights, pointing to rural Georgia’s deep-rooted hunting and sporting traditions, and stressed that lawmakers should stay focused on economic, educational, and healthcare needs rather than divisive cultural debates.
