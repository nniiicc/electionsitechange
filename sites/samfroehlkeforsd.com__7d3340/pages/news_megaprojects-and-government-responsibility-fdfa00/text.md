Megaprojects and government responsibility
As someone seeking legislative office, I should clearly state my view of what state policy should be regarding data centers:
There needs to be no special treatment to entice them to come here.
These are massive companies, and can sink or swim on their own merits, neither helped nor hindered by the state government.
I am not as entirely against the data center project as I am sure some would like me to be.
An individual, a private business, or even a publicly traded corporation, should be entitled to the right of building what they wish on their own property.
Guardrails on government power are there for a reason.
But that is not the real issue in this controversy.
Whether we like it or not, I understand why a corporation wants to do what they do, and I don't really hold it against them to act in their own self-interest.
The worse problem is that those in public office, the only voice of our residents, seem to represent the corporations more than the constituents they're sworn to.
Why do we need to be talked into doing what Applied Digital wants?
Why aren't they told to adapt to what we want?
Speaking as a private citizen, and local to Toronto; I don't want it, and I do not think these projects are a good idea.
Of course I don't want this in my backyard, nobody does.
We all understand that everything has to be built near at least someone, and that not everyone can be happy about it.
But does that mean that the wishes of those people in affected areas should be ignored?
Certainly not!
Whether the project in question is a carbon pipeline, a factory dairy, wind farm, or data center, the responsibility of our elected officials is to speak for their voiceless constituents.
When these issues come to a vote in Pierre, the affected people have the entire rest of the state to vote against them; should they have no one to vote their wishes?
In cases of eminent domain, the wishes of the people should be taken with special gravity.
Eminent domain should never be used simply because it can be.
If there is no need the project fills among the people of South Dakota, then it should never even be considered.
Why build a transmission line from Big Stone to Brookings, when we are already a net exporter of electricity?
Years from now, when the data centers, windmills, power plants, and dairies have all come in, and the land has been taken for transmission lines to service it all, maybe there really was nothing the residents could have done to stop them.
But until that day comes, they, like all of us, are entitled to a voice.
And that voice is clear.
They do not want this.
And maybe they're wrong; maybe this has been the opportunity of a lifetime, one we're all foolish and shortsighted to oppose.
Maybe none of us know what we're talking about, and we shouldn't be speaking against what we're told is progress.
But that's democracy.
And that's a hell of a lot more valuable than a data center.
