Meet Sam Platform Yard Signs and Highway Signs News and Articles News and Articles / Hypothetical voting record 2026 (Part 1) 19 Apr Sunday, 11:29 PM · 2026 Hypothetical voting record 2026 (Part 1) I’ve heard a lot of purity testing going on this year.
Votes on particular bills seem to be used more for a political talking point than a serious attempt to legislate.
There were some bills brought which were just flat out bad laws.
Not that I disagree with them from the point of intent, or even policy, but just weren’t handling the problem well.
However, there are a lot of politicians who will go too far the other way, and never tell you how they would actually vote until they do.
It should be possible to have the best of both worlds.
If I want the job, I should be able to give an unequivocated yea or nay to a bill.
Maybe isn’t an option on that board.
I should also be able to explain why I went one way or another.
After all, if my reasoning finds a different conclusion than yours, we can disagree, have a discussion, and keep the lines of communication open.
But there should be a reason for every vote.
Not just liking the political bent the bill’s title seems to imply.
So I decided to go through all the bills that reached the house floor this last session.
Reading the bills, listening to the committee testimony, listening to the floor debate, and saying how I believe I would have voted.
Some of these are far too technical for me to fully understand, and others aren’t nearly technical enough, but I will try my best to make an informed choice on every one.
Even the bills that went through on the consent calendar.
HR7001: A RESOLUTION recognizing the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence.
Yea - This should have been straightforward and uncontroversial.
But the original resolution had some really concerning language in it, taking it away from the intended subject, and focusing on social issues.
I appreciate the changes that were made by the amendment, and would fully support the amended resolution.
HCR6002: A CONCURRENT RESOLUTION Honoring the life and achievements of the late Charlie Kirk.
Yea – The resolution says it all.
I never really listened to Kirk, but he seemed like a good man, and hopefully his legacy will be respect and open communication in our increasingly divided political climate.
HB1027: An Act to update certain citations to federal regulations regarding pipeline safety inspections.
Yea – Consent calendar.
Updating South Dakota’s code to match federal regulations.
HB1033: An Act to update the reference to the Internal Revenue Code to reflect current federal law for the administration of higher education savings plans.
Yea – Another consent calendar item.
There are a few of these.
A lot of them are just updating the current year in code.
Like this one.
HB1055: An Act to update the official code of laws.
Yea – Consent.
Updating more dates.
HB1009: An Act to revise the speed limit restriction applicable to the operation of a golf cart on certain highways.
Yea – I’m in favor of allowing golf carts in 35 mph zones.
If it turns out that it’s a horrible mistake, well, the law can always change again.
There are areas on the edge of a lot of towns where the speed goes to 35 before it drops all the way to 25, and that gives some more people this use.
HB1052: An Act to authorize the transfer of certain surplus real property by the Department of Game, Fish and Parks to the Spearfish Canyon Foundation for a public purpose.
Yea – It was stated that this building was in immediate need of repairs.
GFP owned it, and since the museum / historical exhibit business is not up their alley, this relieves the state of a liability while giving the property to someone who can repair and renovate it at no expense to the taxpayer.
HB1028: An Act to establish a criminal background check requirement for multistate licensure under the social work licensure compact.
Yea – Consent.
As I understand the committee testimony, the state had already entered into this compact, and the codification was necessary to work with federal regulation.
HB1049: An Act to protect electric infrastructure in this state.
Yea – It’s good that we’re trying to be careful who owns our infrastructure.
Maybe we should be as careful when it comes to proscribed foreign powers owning other things in the state.
HB1016: An Act to modify provisions related to the controlled substances schedule and to declare an emergency.
Yea – Another clean-up bill.
There are always some new drugs to schedule.
And more clarification regarding hemp.
HCR6003: A CONCURRENT RESOLUTION recognizing the importance of conservation and restoration practices in addressing water shortages and forest fire mitigation in the Black Hills, and commending the organizations engaged in this work.
Yea – Conservatism and conservationism should go hand in hand.
We are supposed to be stewards of this planet.
Not worshiping nature, but not destroying it either.
HCR6006: A CONCURRENT RESOLUTION urging this state's congressional delegation to end foreign aid programs pending control of the United States debt situation.
Yea – It was disappointing to me, although not surprising, that this failed.
I’m not against spending, I’m just against spending more than you have.
And South Dakota, a state which publicly prides itself on a balanced budget (leaning heavily on federal money notwithstanding), cannot even manage to bring a non-binding resolution to our own congressional delegation urging slightly less spending.
There are good reasons for some foreign aid.
But as a nation, we have to lay out what we can spend, and then stay within that.
There are an infinite number of possible good things to spend money on, and a finite amount of money.
HB1012: An Act to revise and repeal provisions allowing probation for contempt of a custody or visitation decree.
Yea – Consent.
I can’t say I strongly agree or disagree with this change, but it does reduce government power, and should lighten law enforcement workload.
Time will tell if this was a good idea.
HB1013: An Act to clarify the purposes permitted for certain offenders to operate a motor vehicle.
Yea – Consent.
This one, I agree with more.
It makes it easier for people who want to rehabilitate to stay within the law.\ HB1014: An Act to modify the surcharge for a responding party in a forcible entry and detainer action.
Yea – Consent.
Removing fees in some cases.
Not all fees are bad, but committee testimony did a good job of explaining the counterproductive nature of this charge in eviction cases, which is what this bill is covering.
HB1022: An Act to revise and repeal provisions related to the crime victims' compensation program.
Yea – Consent.
Although I don’t really like everything going paperless, and the decreasing amount of physical records; emailing these copies can save money, and the communication is faster.
HB1023: An Act to establish requirements and liability protection for registered nurses and licensed practical nurses serving on ambulance crews.
Yea – Consent.
The more people whom we can get to work EMS the better.
Especially when they are already medical professionals.
HB1024: An Act to modify requirements for ambulance operators.
Yea – Consent.
See above.
This lays the requirements out more in statute, and should give more freedom to local ambulance organizations, not going through the state government.
HB1054: An Act to repeal the creation and budgeting for the Digital Dakota Network.
Yea – Consent.
I get to end on an easy one.
Since this program no longer exists, HB1054 just cleans the references to it out of statute.
This is, unless I missed something, the first seven legislative days of 2026.
There is more coming, but it takes a while to go through it all.
I hate AI, so no summaries or cheating.
Just listening to a lot of testimony, reading a lot of code, and trying to put my position in words.
After all, if I can’t give a good reason why I would vote yea or nay, maybe I shouldn’t be applying for the job.
In case you’re wondering, after going through the session, I will write up some of my thoughts on the more controversial bills that didn’t make it to the floor.
