Starting up again with the eighth and ninth legislative days, there are a few more controversial bills in here.
HB1030: An Act to amend provisions pertaining to the timing of municipal elections.
Yea – Cleaning up some language related to local elections, and making provisions for municipalities with elections on odd-numbered years, or with odd-numbered terms.
HB1060: An Act to remove the five percent calculation requirement from the county budgetary process.
Yea – Gets rid of an irrelevant oddity in county budget calculations.
This doesn’t actually change the amount the county needs, or taxes, just makes the law and budget more clear.
HB1088: An Act to remove the requirement that counties remit to municipalities an amount equal to the road levy for calendar years 1984, 1985, and 1986.
Yea – If a municipality needs money for roads, there has to be a better way than this transfer from the counties.
After all, the municipality can charge sales tax, where (at least for now) the counties cannot.
HB1034: An Act to increase fees for decals and license plates mailed to motor vehicle owners.
Yea – There was a fight over amending this, and I believe I would have to oppose the amendment.
There is a hidden cost in administration to determine postage, and a flat rate causes less overhead difficulty.
For higher expenses, like express mailing, the bill does require the specific cost charged.
As far as the bill itself, I’m usually in favor of self contained fee systems.
If you use a service, you pay a fee that covers the cost of that service.
If the fee is higher than the cost, that’s still a better alternative to the cost being higher than the fee, and everyone else having to subsidize the service.
HB1078: An Act to direct the amendment of the Administrative Rules of South Dakota regarding the coursework required for high school graduation.
Yea – It’s frequently argued that the legislature should stay out of education.
I agree that there should be local control where it applies to the local schools.
It does not apply where it is currently controlled by the state.
The DOE is a state organization, this is not a local control issue.
The appointed bureaucracy does not supersede the elected body.
HCR6004: A CONCURRENT RESOLUTION encouraging the use of physical cash in transactions.
Yea – Not only in an emergency situation, but also to apply a bit of pressure against credit card monopolization.
The more locations only take cards, the more leverage credit card companies and electronic payment providers have to raise their fee percentages.
HB1035: An Act to modify provisions related to the licensure of public accountants.
Yea – Less schooling mandated, more experience based learning is always good.
HB1011: An Act to modify fees charged by the clerk of courts for certain copies of court records.
Yea – Removes the fee for some services, and increases the fee for others, which take more time.
Ultimately, when government services are utilized by the public, the cost of operation will come from a fee on the service or your taxes.
The more fee based services are, the less those operations are subsidized by taxpayers who are not receiving the services.
HB1015: An Act to create a pilot program in the Unified Judicial System to develop a pretrial release program.
Nay – This bill was not about cashless bail.
Mostly.
It does seem like a good idea to take the people who were going to be released on bail anyway, and have them supervised by court service officers.
Because of this supervision, it’s possible that cashless bail may have become more likely if it passed.
And that is not a good thing.
The main problem I had with this is that it’s something that could be done anyway, without being an act of the legislature.
Minnehaha county did it.
But the some of the costs of this program would have been taken up through a grant from the MacArthur Foundation.
And even besides the strings that would have come with that, passing it as a law means that the taxpayers would be legally mandated, in one way or another, to cover the cost if the foundation money dried up.
HB1008: An Act to direct the amendment of the Administrative Rules of South Dakota regarding the awarding of high school credit for participation in extracurricular athletics.
Yea – Again, the requirements for graduation are in state administrative rules.
The state law is the domain of the state legislature, and the rules are all but law.
The legislative branch needs to claw back this power from the executive branch.
HB1029: An Act to revise provisions related to the practice of addiction counseling and prevention services.
Nay – It does seem like this bill patches some potholes in the current system, but I’m still against the expansion of rule making and over-regulation.
Some regulation is needed.
But not all regulation.
HB1002: An Act to revise the daily meal allowance for wildland fire employees and fire suppression forces in certain circumstances.
Yea – Inflation has gone up, food prices have gone up with them.
HB1020: An Act to increase the maximum amount allowable for the livestock ownership inspection fee and to declare an emergency.
Yea – Like in HB1002, prices go up.
If we’re operating at a loss, at some point, the fees have to go up to cover expenses.
HB1044: An Act to make an appropriation to implement the rural health transformation program, and to declare an emergency.
Nay – I’m not against spending money if we have it, but we need to be honest; the only reason we’re exited about this is because it’ll be taxpayers in other states paying for us.
And I know if we don’t take it someone else will.
But we still have to do the right thing.
If we want to be a state which spends money in this way, we need an income tax.
Since we don’t appear to want an income tax, we have to accept that we cannot spend this much.
HB1080: An Act to allow a business owner who is a veteran to display military specialty plates on a noncommercial vehicle registered to the veteran's business and to limit personal liability to the business owner and veteran.
Yea – There really isn’t anything in the full text of the bill that didn’t make it to the title.
Sure.
Good idea.
HB1062: An Act to revise a provision related to aggravated assault.
Nay – If we’re including simulated weapons in this statute, next time around, are we going to think that the penalty for a real weapon is too low, and raise it?
They simply are different things.
If anything, we’re encouraging people to use real weapons in the commission of a crime, because there’s no real incentive not to under this bill.
