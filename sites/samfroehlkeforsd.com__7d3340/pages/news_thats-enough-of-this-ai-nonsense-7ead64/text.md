Meet Sam Platform Yard Signs and Highway Signs News and Articles News and Articles / That's enough of this AI nonsense 9 Sep Wednesday, 6:09 PM · 2026 That's enough of this AI nonsense There are many reasons to oppose AI.
For morality; I believe it devalues truth.
For our humanity; it destroys, degrades, steals, and insults human art, endeavor, and accomplishment.
For our minds; generative AI is behavioral lead poisoning.
It cripples critical thought and imagination.
For our safety; we don't know what risks unchecked and unregulated development will bring.
But here, I will try to stick to why I believe this AI hysteria is an economic disaster.
So, data centers.
To start with, I want to clear up a couple misconceptions.
First, data centers in space.
No.
You can’t cool them, you can’t run fiber to them, you can’t maintain them, you can’t get nearly the power you need, and it’s wildly cost prohibitive to even construct a fraction of what’s on earth.
Just no.
Secondly, it’s been repeatedly stated that “If you use the internet, you are using data centers”, “All of your saved pictures are in data centers”, or “If you use Google or Facebook, you’re using AI”.
I’m going to be blunt about this; any politician who tells you that is either a fool or a liar.
The internet has existed for a long time before hyperscale AI data centers, and seems to be existing perfectly well right now without all of the data centers currently being planned.
Of course, if you define “data center” to mean any room with a computer or server in it, then yes, the internet does run through data centers.
But that is so clearly not what this discussion is about, that it brings me firmly back to the beginning of this paragraph.
And as far as Google and Facebook shoehorning in AI functions to their services, yes they do, but why would we want them to have done that?
No one asked for it, and just because it’s attached to the service, it doesn’t mean there’s a demand for it.
This is where the difference between use and demand becomes relevant.
When you google something, an AI summary comes up.
On any platform that can host video, there is a plague of AI content fighting for views.
There is no real demand for these things, they just steal other people’s work, then rearrange it and take credit.
There is certainly use of AI.
But use doesn’t keep the lights on at these insanely expensive data centers, demand does.
And as soon as these debt laden companies need to start making payments, they will need to raise prices; and use, without demand, will dry up.
This craze is built on sand.
There is simply not enough demand to justify this much supply.
Don’t get me wrong, there is certainly a market for AI, but that market is being overserved to the point of insanity.
There are simply not enough customers, and not enough money in the world, to pay the bill for all this.
When is it no longer private businesses’ private business?
We have largely accepted that a truly free market is less desirable than a fair market.
That’s why we break up monopolies, restrict stock manipulation and insider trading, limit interest, and require reporting to the SEC.
Even if all of these rules are more honored in the breach than the observance, they’re still good limits to have.
When private practice becomes a threat to public order, it becomes the responsibility of a regulating body to restrain unfair or irrational corporate behavior.
Usually, if a company wants to have a bad idea, like selling mittens for snakes, they just fail on their own and no one else really gets hurt.
But if Snake Mittens Inc. becomes a massive part of the US economy, starts buying politicians, builds factories across the country that no one wants, and finances it all on junk bonds, it makes itself everyone else’s problem too.
About a year ago, I wrote that I thought not helping them was enough.
I stated that we shouldn’t give them tax breaks, and certainly not use eminent domain for their power and fiber, just let them fail on their own.
But times have changed, and this lunacy has become an increasing threat to the order of the public market.
The government of South Dakota needs to step up and regulate.
At least a one year moratorium on data centers.
Ban any use of AI in grade school, as well as in government and by public employees.
It’s been pretty popular this year in the state legislature to quote Billy Preston with “Nothing from nothing leaves nothing”, when trying to sell us on AI centered economic development.
But I say instead of rushing headlong into this nonsense because everyone else is, let’s take some advice from George Harrison.
Think for yourself.
