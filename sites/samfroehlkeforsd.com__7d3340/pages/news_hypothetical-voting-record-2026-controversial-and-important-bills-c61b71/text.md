Let’s start with the bills particularly controversial to District 4.
I will preface these two bills with my opinion on JR 7-7.
The intent behind “smoking out” a bill is to force a floor vote on a bill which failed in committee.
There is, as far as I can tell, only one occasion on which a smoke out would be reasonable.
If the bill died in a particularly unfavorable committee, but it was clear that there was broad support for it, I think a smoke out would be at least understandable, even if I did not support the bill.
If the bill was smoked out successfully, but the committee delivers it to the floor with a “Do Not Pass”, or “No Recommendation”, I would at least vote to calendar it.
I would not vote to smoke out when it is clearly not supported, but someone just can’t accept not getting what he wants.
HB1005 - An Act to provide a sales and use tax exemption for goods and services related to data center operations.
Nay – There was a claim that this bill was sent to an unfavorable committee intentionally to kill it.
That is sadly true, and while I have to confess being glad of the outcome, I can’t ignore that.
I do think this probably should have actually gone to Commerce And Energy instead of either State Affairs or Taxation.
And the reason for that is pretty simple.
A yea or nay on this bill was a yea or nay on the data center in Toronto.
The text of the bill may be about taxation, but the subtext has far, far greater implications.
Whether or not to run blindly into the data center business is not just a question for the House Taxation committee.
There is a lot I could say about why these data centers are a terrible idea, but I’ll leave this to the tax implications.
It is repeated frequently that we have a laundry list of tax exemptions in the state, and while that is true, maybe it shouldn’t be.
The biggest problem is that it’s created a precedent, which we can see here, of companies threatening to leave if they don’t get preferential treatment.
To paraphrase committee testimony; if a company this big needs a tax exemption to be profitable, or is willing to grease the legislature to get it, it’s just not the kind of business we want here anyway.
As far as the speculation about this bill having a chance at a smoke out; it was railroaded into a dead end, but it died in committee on its own merits.
I would not have supported a smoke out.
HB1212 - An Act to revise provisions in order to strengthen protections for unborn children.
Yea – I feel like I’m in a bit of a no man’s land on this one.
I’ve talked a little bit with people in the pro-life camp on either side of the bill, and neither group seems able to admit that their opponent might have had good intentions.
I agree with Fred Deutsch’s appraisal that this would drive popular opinion away from the pro-life movement, and objectively make legal abortion in South Dakota more likely.
But I can’t help but keep coming back to the simple question: Is this a person?
And knowing the answer, I cannot in good conscience vote against the bill.
The bill itself is not the only controversial subject here, though.
How the process was handled brought disrepute to both sides.
Many of the Nays turned on some of the most loyal pro-lifers, not by opposing the bill, but by attacking the people who supported it.
The Yeas fired back, and the situation got even messier when there was an attempt to smoke out the bill.
Then everybody who didn’t vote to smoke out the bill, or support the roll call motion, was suddenly called pro-abortion.
The Nays were looking at the fact that HB1212 would realistically increase the risk of something like Amendment G passing in the future.
The Yeas were looking at the fact that murder is murder, and as all men are created equal, there cannot be a special exception for the murder of some people.
Both of these are true.
And both sides need to stop attacking each other as fringe lunatics, or as pro-abortion.
This topic needs to be kept out of political grandstanding.
I would have voted yea in committee, or on the floor; but would not have supported the smoke out.
SB3 - An Act to limit the types of residential improvements for which counties, municipalities, and townships may require a permit.
Yea – This bill had a long road to travel.
There were several amendments watering it down, and I think I would have supported it more in the original strongest wording.
But I would still vote for it.
There is a very real role for consumer protections in state government, but we must seek balance.
Over-regulation is stifling to productivity, and requiring permits for everything only encourages people to either not build, or ignore laws.
I can only dream of a state where we care half as much about protecting our citizens from predatory lending, as we do protecting them from gutters not being up to code.
SCR604 - A CONCURRENT RESOLUTION urging the people of the state of South Dakota to return to the Lord Most High, the Almighty and Uncreate, and plead for His mercy upon the state.
Nay – I appreciate the intent, but the separation of church and state is as much about protecting the church from the state as vice versa.
It’s a dangerous slope to get the state involved in taking a stance on doctrine.
We do need a Christian and moral people for the success of our state and society.
But the state cannot make them so.
It is only the response of an individual’s heart toward Christ.
I can see four outcomes from this.
First, the people who truly are passionate about it, and will follow the prescriptions of the resolution, regardless of its passage.
Second, those who kind of meant it, but forget or decide not to follow through.
Third, there will be a group who oppose the resolution, but feel like it’ll look bad if they don’t vote for it.
And fourth, those who simply are, were, and will be, against the resolution.
The first and fourth groups, at least, are honest.
I have no real issue with them.
But the second and third need to examine themselves, and take far more seriously what they’re doing.
Is it a light thing to make a resolution for the state and not follow it themselves?
They are putting themselves in the place of religious leaders, but not feeling the gravity thereof.
James 3:1 Do not become teachers in large numbers, my brothers, since you know that we who are teachers will incur a stricter judgment.
And it encourages dissimulation, as people who do not mean it in the slightest engage in theatrics of piety.
As much as I disagree with Kent Roe on a great deal of policy, he raised a good point by referring to the gospel of Matthew here.
Matthew 6:1-6 “Be careful not to perform your righteous acts before men to be seen by them.
If you do, you will have no reward from your Father in heaven.
So when you give to the needy, do not sound a trumpet before you, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and on the streets, to be honored by men.
Truly I tell you, they already have their full reward.
But when you give to the needy, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your giving may be in secret.
And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.
And when you pray, do not be like the hypocrites.
For they love to pray standing in the synagogues and on the street corners to be seen by men.
Truly I tell you, they already have their full reward.
But when you pray, go into your inner room, shut your door, and pray to your Father, who is unseen.
And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.
In short, I don’t feel that those who truly meant it it, or honestly opposed it, e.g.
Mykala Voita and Kent Roe, are wrong.
I will criticize those who didn’t mean it, or didn’t care.
I would still vote against it, not against the contents of the resolution, but against the legislature taking the role of religious leadership.
HB1044 - An Act to make an appropriation to implement the rural health transformation program, and to declare an emergency.
Nay – Free money is not free.
That is the core of it.
We have always been opposed to the idea of a state income tax, but when it comes to federal dollars, we have no problem spending other’s tax money.
If we really need to do this, we should raise our own money with which to do so.
Everyone knows that found money spends faster than earned money.
It should hurt to spend, and the only way to feel that hurt is to be the ones paying the bills.
That’s not to say I’m against rural health care.
A read through of my platform should show ideas to expand it.
But doing so in a self contained manner, which doesn’t just funnel money to the big three health cartels.
Because let’s be honest, is there anyone who truly believes that real South Dakotans on the ground will see a dime’s worth of difference in the cost or availability of medical care after these hundreds of millions are spent?
If we’re serious when we talk about the national debt, we cannot keep spending like this.
And if we still want the money, it’ll mean a state income tax.
South Dakota doesn’t want a state income tax.
I don’t want a state income tax.
But I’d still take that over robbing it from all of our grandchildren.
HB1077 - An Act to consider a cultivated-protein food product to be adulterated food.
Yea – I don’t think that this is necessarily the best form for this, but I can understand the reasoning behind it.
I would rather have seen this as a separate section deeming lab grown meat unfit for human consumption, rather than including it in the definition of adulterated foods.
That being said, the case for lab grown meat being an adulteration of the original cells is still reasonable enough that I would vote for the bill.
HB1056 - An Act to require that the Department of Social Services submit a federal waiver request to exclude soft drinks from the supplemental nutrition assistance program.
Yea – My biggest issue here is not the bill itself; I agree on the intention.
But how on earth does it cost us a quarter million dollars to do it?
The fiscal note indicates an estimated cost of ~$250,000 in FY2028, as well as over $80,000 for another FTE, and unspecified other expenses to be absorbed into the existing budget.
I understand the need for enforcement requires an employee to verify compliance, but the fiscal note only describes the larger sum as such: “Other states, like Nebraska and Iowa, have used contracted vendors to meet reporting obligations.
A contract to meet reporting obligations could cost $250,000 per year based on similar existing contracts made by other states.”
I would very much like more explanation of what those reporting obligations are, and why they cost this much.
But the intended consequences of the bill are still important, and I would have to vote to pass it.
Our health and obesity crisis is bad enough without SNAP paying people to eat unhealthily.
SB85 - An Act to require an election for an excess tax levy of a school district.
Yea – I absolutely agree with this.
Specifically SB85B.
Allegedly the school board represents the people of the district, but I think a quick check of reality will tell us that hardly anyone in the public is involved in monitoring the school boards’ activity.
We have no good way of knowing how anyone running for the board will vote, and they can effectively run it however they want.
If your bank could just take money out of your account, and you had twenty days to get hundreds or thousands of signatures together to stop them, you could hardly blame the account holder for not being civically minded enough to stop them.
The opt-out may be a necessity in rare cases, but it should always go to a vote of the people.
The education lobby always likes to claim that the legislature is taking away local control by changing law.
I’ve noticed that that never seems to apply when the DOE wants to bring a bill to flex their control with the force of state law behind them.
I would say that if it is in state law, it is completely appropriate for the state legislature to change it.
If they don’t want the legislature involved in opt-outs, I would be more than happy to oblige by fighting to remove 10-12-43 and 10-12-43.1 entirely, ending all opt-outs.
HCR5001 - A JOINT RESOLUTION proposing and submitting to the voters at the next general election an amendment to the Constitution of the State of South Dakota, prohibiting the transfer of private property to a non-governmental entity for the sole purpose of economic development or increased tax revenue.
Nay – I should make very clear, this is a nay on HJR5001C.
In the form of its final vote in the House, I could not support it.
I would love to support HJR5001A/B, or concur in amendment 5001E.
But John Hughes summed this up pretty well in his opposition to 5001C; adding the word “solely” is a poison pill, which would codify abuse of eminent domain in the constitution, provided the use wasn’t 100% for economic development.
Even if I knew something like amendment E was going to be brought in the Senate, I could not vote to pass a resolution to modify the constitution in a way I felt to be flawed.
Eminent domain, if there’s even an argument for its existence at all, should only be considered when the public uses the result of the project.
Roads, rails, water, and power are used by the public.
And even then I have a hard time justifying eminent domain in a country where private property rights are supposed to be paramount.
Carbon pipelines, fiber connections to data centers, and high power lines going out of state could all make a case for why they have a right to eminent domain under HJR5001C.
As long as it isn’t solely for economic development, or tax revenue, they could argue that they’ve met all the requirements set before them.
I’d like it to be as difficult as possible for them instead.
HB1209 - An Act to require employment verification eligibility through the e-verify program and to provide a penalty therefor.
Yea – This is a yea on HB1209 or 1209A, the way it left the house.
Just like the last one, there was an unfriendly amendment tacked on, torpedoing the bill, this time in the Senate.
I could agree with an amendment which only added the provision for the e-verify program being temporarily unavailable, but 1209B also limits the scope to only employers with over 50 employees, and requires written confirmation from the DHS or DOJ that the employer knowingly employed an unauthorized person in violation of immigration law.
This would make it almost impossible to pursue the intended execution of this law.
While we do need to enforce our immigration laws, that should absolutely include the law being enforced against those employers exploiting immigrant workers.
I would have preferred the original HB1209 over 1209A, since there were more penalties, but I would have voted for either.
HB1275 - An Act to create requirements for age verification and parental consent for application stores and to provide a penalty therefor.
Yea – My vote here would be less about content, and more about contract.
There is a plague of terms and conditions documents which no one reads, and fewer understand.
But worse than that, many of these agreements are being entered into by minors, who cannot legally sign a contract.
We need to start caring about enforcing laws when it comes to the internet.
When someone writes an app which sells your personal information, and a minor downloads it, agreeing to the T&C, it strains the law regarding legal minority, a law which exists for good reasons.
There were some very valid concerns about these tech companies getting even more of our data.
That is a good point, but it just tells me that there should be other bills being brought to cut back on all of the other current abuses of data scraping and selling.
For now, I think this is a good bill, and I would vote for it.
SJR505 - A JOINT RESOLUTION proposing and submitting to the voters at the next general election an amendment to the Constitution of the State of South Dakota, limiting the assessed value of real property and limiting real property taxes.
Yea – Can I say yea twice?
This may have been the single best item brought in the last session.
I still support this, even after the amendment limited it to owner occupied single family.
If elected, I will work to bring this exact resolution back as many times as it takes to pass.
Not much more to say than that.
HB1245 - An Act to authorize municipalities to establish a local funding mechanism for capital improvement projects.
Yea – I like the stipulation that this must receive the support of at least sixty percent of the voters, but I think I would like it to be somewhat more specific.
At least sixty percent of registered voters in the municipality would be more clear, removing the potential confusion on if it’s sixty percent of the electorate, or sixty percent of those who voted on it.
There are a few projects that come to mind which would benefit from this.
The main advantage is that fewer towns would have to borrow to construct public projects.
That means less taxpayer money going to the bank in interest.
This can, of course, be misused.
Anything can be misused.
But this bill lines up squarely with my tax philosophy that spending is acceptable, provided it’s our own money, and the people get a say.
SB96 - An Act to authorize the imposition of a county option gross receipts tax to reduce owner-occupied property taxes.
Yea – Eh.
This is fine.
It does kick the can down the road a ways, but with the failure of actual solutions like SJR505, you have to kick that can somewhere.
Does it raise taxes?
It shifts taxes from property owners to sales tax payers.
But that is a far, far, cry from robbing single mothers and shoveling money to wealthy landowners, as certain people would have you believe.
The property owners are already paying a great deal of other people’s bills, and it is not giving them money to tax them less.
I would like this bill better with both amendments 96C and 96E; much like HB1245, an actual public vote and a sixty percent threshold would have made this pill a bit easier to swallow.
I’d vote for it, but it’s by no means a spectacular bill.
SB245 - An Act to create the homeowner property tax reduction fund, and to transfer moneys to the homeowner property tax reduction fund.
Yea – For all the hay that was made from this politically, all it really does is the same thing as state aid to education.
I don’t love the way state aid to education works, but it does exist, and this is basically just expanding it.
Sure.
Better spent keeping property taxes down than going into the general fund.
SB218 - An Act to provide for the establishment of charter schools.
Yea – The educational system is a multi-billion dollar industry, the most powerful lobby in the state, and almost an entire government in and of itself.
A little competition is a healthy thing, and our school system is a deeply unhealthy creature.
We have a social contract between taxpayers and government, whereby taxpayers accept that they will give away money and get nothing back, because it is being spent where it is needed.
In this way, our sales taxes go to fund the operation of state government which we may never use.
Our property taxes go to school districts, which, if we don’t have children, we also don’t use.
The taxpayers understand this is the price of us all living in a society with each other, and we just get on with our lives.
However, when the spending starts to feel unreasonable, and the taxes become burdensome, there is pushback from taxpayers.
And that pushback has not been met with thanks for all that has been given already, but with entitled, ungrateful greed for more.
Property tax payers who have nothing left to give, and need relief, are attacked as if they are the ones that are taking other’s money.
I think it is entirely reasonable for those taxpayers to take that money they have been giving, and go somewhere else.
If the education lobby doesn’t like it, they should not have burned all of these bridges.
When bills like SJR505 and SB85 get killed, it leaves the taxpayers no choices.
I support charter schools, and I would vote for this bill.
HB1326 - An Act to appropriate money for the ordinary expenses of the legislative, judicial, and executive departments of the state, the current expenses of state institutions, interest on the public debt, and common schools.
Yea – Ending on the budget, of course.
In almost any other year, this would not be on the list of controversial bills.
But it became a huge political football in the last days of session.
Everyone who voted yea was attacked as a secret big government liberal, and everyone who voted nay was attacked as a fringe anarchist loon.
It is an election year, after all.
But the amount by which the budget increased year over year was less than inflation.
This was almost as much of a non-issue as theoretically possible, and it still turned into a campaign event for everyone.
Yes, the government today is like a modern pickup.
Huge, less functional than it used to be, frequently breaking, and insanely overpriced.
But the process to convert it into the 45 year old Datsun it needs to be is grueling, complicated, and takes a long, long time.
Just hacking off a wheel, because that way it’s smaller, accomplishes nothing and loses the time and political capital that could have been better spent elsewhere.
