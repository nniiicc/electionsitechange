Fiscal Policy There is a likelihood that this next legislature will face a significant loss of revenue, especially from the federal government.
We will need to be creative in addressing this possible shortfall.
It is imperative that we look for options that make sure everyone pays their fair share.
One possibility is to replace incentives for larger corporations to those that encourage better pay for their employees.
Instead of giving them the "up front" break on property taxes, we should give them breaks based on their payroll.
If people are making enough, they will rely less on public assistance and be able to contribute more to the tax base.
Under no circumstance will I ever support a general sales tax.
This is a decidedly unfair tax that places more of the burden on lower income citizens.

Biography Patrick grew up as a Navy dependent, moving around extensively.
He enlisted in the Navy himself, serving for over six years.
Shortly after he was discharged, he moved to Montana, where he became involved in local issues, including Montana legislation.
Using the G.I.
Bill, he attended the University of Montana, where he received his BA in History/Political Science and his Master of Public Administration degrees.
He then worked at Dawson Community College for a few years before being awarded a fellowship from the University of Southern California, where he completed his Doctor of Public Administration degree.
After serving as a faculty member at UM for a while, he struck out on his own as a consultant, helping with organizational issues and community development, especially focusing on voter rights issues.
Feeling a calling, he attended seminary and completed his M.Div degree from the Franciscan School of Theology, though it was decided that the priesthood was not to be.
He then worked for 10 years building fences for Grizzly Fence in Missoula.
Later, he was hired as a professor at Southern Arkansas University, running the MPA program there.
When an opportunity arose for him to return to Montana, he seized it and became the Director of the State Professional Development Center in Helena for ten years, until his retirement.
During this time, he also followed the Pat Sullivan campaign closely, which focused on important state matters.
Patrick is the father of two children, grandfather of seven, and great-grandfather of two.
His family is the joy of his life.
He enjoys outdoor activities of all kinds, including camping, fishing, and golf.
