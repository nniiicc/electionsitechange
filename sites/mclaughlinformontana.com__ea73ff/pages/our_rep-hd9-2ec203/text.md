House District 9 How Did Our Representative Vote Last Session?
HB 15- Voted No I mplement K-12 Inflations Our representative voted against inflationary adjustments to K-12 public education.
This bill requires the state to adjust for inflation to school funding formulas, supporting operational costs, teacher salaries and class room sources.
It was passed in to law, even though our representative voted against it.
His quote about teachers: teachers “get back to teaching and not be distracted with promoting social issues or enabling students to behave contrary to parents’ wishes.” HB 388-Voted Yes Red Flag Gun Orders Our representative voted in favor of putting lives in danger by voting that city and county government do not have to adopt or enforce red flag gun orders.
Red flag gun orders are put in place by a judge when an individual gun owner poses imminent danger to others or themselves.
It was passed in to law, 55-44.
HB 912- Sponsored Bill Generally revise behavioral health laws to create secure forensic facility Our representative sponsored this bill.
He wanted to create a facility to house 70 people who are found guilty of a crime or are either mentally ill or waiting determination of mental illness.
This would have come at the cost of $30,000,000 tax payer dollars.
It died in the Senate.
HB 273- Voted No Limit collection action for Medical Deb t This bill would have required a wait time and notice before your debt for health care services went into collections.
It also limited a collection action when an insurance denial is being appealled.
Our representative was on the Health and Human Services Committee.
If this bill passed the committee vote it would have moved on to the House for a vote.
He voted against it, this bill died in the committee.
HB 782- Sponsored Bill Revise County Planning Boards Our representative voted in favor of changing our law ( MCA 76-1-211) so that counties could remove the requirement that a county must have conservation district representation on the county planning board.
Other sponsors include four other Flathead County state representatives including Courtney Sprunger, Lukas Schubert and Braxton Mitchell and Ed Byrne.

EDUCATION My sons have all attended Flathead High School.
I believe it is very important to continue education to be a place that builds skills ( kids can learn construction, various art mediums, music, welding and other skills).
Students are encouraged to excel and push themselves towards higher education ( IB Program, partnership with Running Start FVCC).
I attended public school, both my in-laws were teachers and I have friends and family who are currently teaching.
Our education system should support the teachers that work there, ensure buildings that are safe and have adequate space.
Legislation can create a school funding formula to allocate money to provide what our Montana Constitution states: “ Equality of educational opportunity is guaranteed to each person of the state”.
Your zip code should not reflect the value of education your children receive.
ENVIRONMENT The majority of HD9 is public lands (81%) which totals about 54,938 acres of land which is managed by various agencies including: County government, MT Fish, Wildlife and Parks, MT State Trust Lands, US Fish and Wildlife Service and US Forest Service.
This land includes seven recreation sites: Smith Lake, Somers Fishing Access, Lone Pine State Park, Somers Beach State Park, Herron Park and Foys Lake Park.
We are very fortunate to have this land available and belong to all of us.
These areas give us places to swim, fish, hike, hunt, boat, sled and ice skate, among other things.
The Montana Constitution guarantees our right to a clean and healthful environment.
I support a moratorium on data centers in this state until we can study from other states the effects of the water usage, electricity increases to residents, noise and air pollution.
I also support alternative energy sources to reduce our use on a finite source of energy.
I also oppose corporations from other countries coming in to mine our Montana resources.
HEALTHCARE #% of Flathead County is enrolled in Medicaid.
The medicaid expansion program funded through federal dollars continues behavioral health services, and substance use disorder treatment, it also allows seniors to receive long term care.
Continuing Medicaid expansion also keeps Logan Health ( now merged with Billings Clinic) to provide care for our community.
In a Flathead Beacon article they reported " The failure to reauthorize Medicaid expansion would impact 7,500 statewide jobs ... and $775 million in economic activity that it generates.
Montana medicaid has grown to include home births, which helps in keeping costs down, as the average hospital birth is $11,000.
Montana also faces many areas without close access to maternal care and many rural area clinics are in danger of closing.
I support home births and home care for elders in their own homes, but we face a serious lack of healthcare workers in these areas which needs to be addressed.
