Kassner claims she is lowering health care costs and closing gaps in care that put the district’s families at risk.
Her efforts to describe these successes barely makes grammatical sense, and that appears to be the point.
“Strengthens.” “Bans.” “Expands.” The sentences often omit the person responsible, allowing readers to assume Kassner produced the result.
She did not.
The Alzheimer’s, CMV, and EpiPen bills passed the House by overwhelming or unanimous margins and remain unfinished.
The biomarker bill is still in committee.
The primary-care bill was another unanimous House vote.
Even where a measure became law, her documented role was generally one vote on legislation led by others.
Her page identifies no sustained advocacy, negotiation, or legislative leadership behind these measures.
Voting yes is support.
It is not authorship, advocacy, or personal delivery.
