I'm grateful to serve as Senate Vice Chair of the critical Natural Resources and Energy Committee.
I support dependable generation and careful oversight of utility costs.
I have supported changes to the Public Service Commission and legislation that gives it a tool to reduce volatility in fuel-cost adjustments.
My focus is reliability, affordability and understanding the long-term costs of energy decisions.

View my energy record Career and technical education: skills that open doors A good education should give people the skills to build a life and earn a living without taking on debt.
Kentucky needs to give career and technical education the attention and support it deserves.
It is a top priority for me.
I want students to have meaningful choices, including skilled trades, technical training and college.
Programs that prepare people for careers in electrical work, plumbing, advanced manufacturing, healthcare and technology can offer a practical route into the workforce without requiring years in a four-year degree program.
Affordability means considering tuition, debt and how long it takes to start earning.
I support making career and technical education a greater funding priority.
Our education dollars should support training that prepares people for available jobs and gives them skills they can build on throughout their careers.
I have backed that commitment with action.
I cosponsored legislation expanding KEES scholarship options for qualifying workforce training programs.
I voted to improve career and technical education funding and allow qualifying hands-on training to count toward certain trade licenses.
The 2026–28 budget I supported also provides more than $127 million annually for career and technical education, alongside funding for Work Ready Kentucky and Dual Credit Scholarships.
I will continue working to connect education with opportunity, support practical training and make sure students can pursue the path that fits their abilities and goals.
