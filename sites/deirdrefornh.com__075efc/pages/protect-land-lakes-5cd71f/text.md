Protecting these resources is not only an environmental issue.
It is also an economic, public-health, and quality-of-life issue.
Lake Winnipesaukee supports tourism, recreation, property values, local businesses, and the identity of our communities.
But clean water cannot be taken for granted.
Polluted runoff, shoreline erosion, aging septic systems, road salt, overdevelopment, and cyanobacteria blooms all pose growing risks to our lakes and ponds.
Through my work on the Development Committee for the Wentworth Watershed Association, I have seen firsthand how closely the health of our waters is connected to the health of our communities.
Protecting our lakes and the waters that flow into them means protecting the places where our children swim, families gather, businesses thrive, and residents and visitors experience the natural beauty that makes this area so special.
We can support responsible growth while safeguarding clean water, forests, open spaces, and rural character.
I believe the best solutions are practical, informed by science and local knowledge, and developed with the people most affected by them.
That means respecting property rights, listening to residents and municipalities, and keeping local voices central to important land-use decisions.
Wolfeboro and Tuftonboro are defined by their connection to the land and water around us.
We have a responsibility to be good stewards of these resources—not only for ourselves, but for the generations that will follow us.
As your State Representative, I will work to: Protect our lakes, drinking-water sources, and the surrounding watershed.
Support water-quality monitoring, cyanobacteria prevention and response, and efforts to reduce polluted runoff into lakes, rivers, and streams.
Help communities address shoreline erosion, aging septic systems, road salt, stormwater, and other threats to water quality.
Encourage responsible growth that respects property rights, local decision-making, environmental limits, and the rural character of Wolfeboro and Tuftonboro.
Support voluntary conservation partnerships that protect forests, open spaces, working lands, and wildlife habitat.
Help towns access state and federal resources for clean water, wastewater, stormwater, and infrastructure improvements.
Work across the aisle with municipalities, property owners, businesses, conservation organizations, and residents to develop practical solutions that can earn broad local support.
The weathered pilings along our shoreline remind us of what makes our communities strong.
Each stands on its own, but bound together, they are strong enough to weather the storms and steady enough to meet changing waters.
That same strength comes from neighbors working together—listening to one another, finding common ground, and remaining firmly anchored in the values we share.
