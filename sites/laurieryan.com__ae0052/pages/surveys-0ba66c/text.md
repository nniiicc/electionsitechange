New York State Taxes and High-Income Earners New York’s highest-income taxpayers contribute a significant share of the state’s personal income-tax revenue.
In 2024, taxpayers earning $1 million or more—about 0.9% of New York taxpayers—paid approximately 44.6% of all New York State personal income taxes .
The top 1% of earners paid roughly 46% .
The top 200 taxpayers alone accounted for approximately 7.7% of the state’s personal income-tax revenue.
These figures refer to the share of total state income-tax revenue paid , not the percentage of an individual’s income that is taxed.

As of September 2026, New York has not eliminated Medicaid or enacted a blanket reduction in Medicaid benefits.
The state’s 2026 budget continues to provide substantial funding for Medicaid.
However, federal legislation enacted in 2025 will result in significant changes to Medicaid eligibility and coverage in New York.

Medicaid enrollment in New York has also declined from a pandemic-era peak of more than 8 million people in June 2023 to approximately 6.8 million in December 2025, largely following the end of pandemic-era continuous-coverage rules.
