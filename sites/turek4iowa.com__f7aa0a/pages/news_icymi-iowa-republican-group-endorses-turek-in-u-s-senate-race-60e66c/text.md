Council Bluffs Daily Nonpareil: Josh Turek emphasizes bipartisan record in Council Bluffs campaign stop
- U.S.
Senate candidate Josh Turek returned to his hometown on Monday, using a campaign appearance to highlight his record of working with Republicans.
- “I first ran to represent my hometown of Council Bluffs and Carter Lake because of what we were seeing with the privatization of Medicaid,” Turek said.
“We were seeing a 1,000% increase in the denial rate for literally the most vulnerable in society, and all Iowans deserve to have basic healthcare.”
- Turek said his campaign for the Iowa House began in a district where Republicans had a significant advantage, but he believed he could win by speaking directly with voters regardless of their political affiliation.
- He said he knocked on the doors of Republican and independent voters as well as those of Democrats, including carrying his wheelchair upstairs to reach some voters — a visual he replicated the day before the Democratic primary canvassing in Council Bluffs.
- “I’m very proud to be one of the most effective and one of the most bipartisan members because that’s what people deserve — representatives who care and who are going to go to work in a bipartisan fashion to provide solutions to people’s problems,” Turek said.
“I’ve worked across the aisle more than 700 times, voted with Republicans to cut taxes, to lower costs, to keep our community safe, and that’s exactly what I will do in the United States Senate.”
- “I’m running to take on the corruption that we’re seeing of both parties,” Turek said.
“Iowans across the state and across the political spectrum are paying the cost of that corruption.”
- He said his experience as a Paralympic athlete shaped his approach to public service, arguing that elected officials should represent everyone rather than only members of their own political party.
- “Regardless if this was a Republican idea or a Democratic idea, let’s do what’s best for the people,” Turek told The Nonpareil.
“I think that’s what’s missing: people that are willing to work across the aisle, willing to work with anyone.”
- In a news release Monday afternoon, Turek’s campaign touted that he voted with Republicans more than 700 times in the Iowa Legislature, where Republicans control both chambers.
- “I want to be a senator for all Iowans, regardless of where you sit on the political spectrum,” Turek said.
“Republicans, independents, Democrats — I want to represent anyone because we should be voting for the person, not for the party.”
