Iowa Capital Dispatch: Turek highlights cancer advocacy, pledges support if elected to U.S.
Senate Iowa Rep.
Josh Turek said Wednesday his work across the aisle to support pediatric cancer research and funding in the state Legislature shows he would be the best candidate to advocate for these priorities in the U.S.
Senate Turek, the Democratic nominee for Iowa’s open Senate seat, spoke to cancer patients, advocates and volunteers at a “coffee cancer chat” in Cedar Rapids, hosted by the American Cancer Society Cancer Action Network (ACS CAN).
Cancer has become a top issue on the Iowa campaign trail this election season because of the state’s position as having the fastest-rising cancer rate and the second-highest number of new incidences of cancer in the nation.
Turek said Wednesday his work to address cancer in Iowa began well before his 2026 campaign for U.S.
Senate, as he worked on legislation related to cancer as a member of the Iowa House.
Turek said the first year he was in the Iowa Legislature, 2023, he advocated for a bill to dedicate $1 for every Iowan toward pediatric cancer research at the University of Iowa — a similar proposal to the measure supported by Iowa House Republicans this session.
Ultimately, Gov.
Kim Reynolds signed a law providing $3 million in funding for pediatric cancer research through a 5-cent tax on vapes and alternative nicotine products, which Turek said represented “a form” of his proposal.
“As you’ve heard me say, even here today, with some of the examples that I gave you, sometimes you don’t end up getting credit for the work that you end up doing on these bills, but what I’ve learned in the Legislature is, it’s amazing what we can get done with people who don’t care who takes credit,” Turek said.
“And when we’re willing to work to do the hard work behind the scenes, to work in a bipartisan manner, to get results.
And that’s what I’d like to do.
I’m not going up there to play political games, I’m going up there to make sure that people have healthcare, and people are taken care of.” The Gazette: Josh Turek: Funding needed to address Iowa cancer crisis Turek heard testimonies and fielded policy questions from multiple Iowans — including cancer survivors, caregivers, awareness advocates and researchers — at the American Cancer Society Cancer Action Network’s “Cancer Coffee Chat” at the Shuttleworth & Ingersoll law office.
Turek began his remarks by highlighting his personal experience with cancer.
His grandfather died of pancreatic cancer, which Turek described as “one of the most horrific experiences that I’ve ever seen someone go through.” His father was exposed to Agent Orange while in Vietnam, which led to throat and skin cancers, Turek said.
Most recently, Turek’s sister was diagnosed with breast cancer around the time he launched his campaign for the U.S.
Senate.
Turek said her private insurance denied her claim for a PET scan to see if the cancer had spread because her cancer was at Stage 2, not Stage 3 or Stage 4.
“I would love to see guardrails on insurance companies because I believe that doctors should be deciding care, not insurance companies,” Turek said.

Senate Candidate Josh Turek Says Expanding Health Care And Investing More In Cancer Research Would Be Priorities For Him If Elected Democratic U.S.
Senate candidate Josh Turek says expanding health care and investing more in cancer research would be priorities for him if elected.
Turek met with cancer survivors and advocates in Cedar Rapids today to discuss how he says the federal government can better support patients and those at risk of developing cancer.
Turek says the issue is personal – pointing to his own experience navigating the healthcare system with spina bifida and his own sister’s cancer diagnosis.
He says he wants to put more limits on insurance companies while expanding access to coverage, cancer research and clinical trials.
Turek pledged to be an advocate for cancer patients in the Senate, saying the cost of getting care should not force families into medical debt or bankruptcy.
