Whether that means fighting for our most vulnerable friends and neighbors, promoting safe and clean communities, working for good jobs and a strong economy, or protecting our environment, we need advocates who can work across the aisle to get results.
Fighting For Our Most Vulnerable The 21st District is one of the most beautiful places to live, but there are too many of our friends and neighbors struggling to make ends meet.
Federal budget cuts to essential services and an economy that is not working for everyone is only making things harder.
As chair of the Housing Committee, it’s my job to help pass legislation to help people stay in their homes and access the critical services they need so they can keep food on the table.
Promote Strong, Safe Communities I am proud to report that we continue to pass significant, common sense gun legislation.
Over the past few years we passed legislation banning the sale of assault weapons and high capacity magazines, prohibited ghost guns, and restricted firearms at public meetings and election-related locations, as well as a permit to purchase which will significantly reduce gun deaths by suicide.
We have seen gun violence right here in the 21st District and we don’t want to experience it again.
Of course the opioid epidemic is still destroying too many families.
From the Secure Medicine Take-Back Act to record investments in treatment, I will continue to lead on fighting substance use disorder, investing in more behavioral and mental health services right here in Snohomish County.
Fuel Our Local Economy and Create Jobs One of the best ways to create a strong economy and create generational stability is to reduce the cost of housing.
Over the past few years we have focused efforts on the three S’s of housing– stabilization, supply, and support .
For the nearly 50% of Washingtonians who are renters, we passed rent stabilization, capping rental increases at 7% plus CPI (not to exceed 10%).
To help fill the need for more affordable home ownership (supply), we passed the middle housing and other legislation making it easier to build small scale condos, duplexes, smaller homes, and accessory dwelling units.
We also passed the Covenant Homeownership Act creating homeownership opportunities for families left out of the housing market due to racist red-lining policies of the past.
And on the support side, we have invested nearly a billion dollars into the Housing Trust Fund, partnering with non-profit developers to build housing for seniors, veterans, our neighbors with developmental disabilities, and families struggling to make ends meet.
Not only do these policies and investments help keep a roof over someone’s head, they also create great family wage jobs to help boost our middle class.
Fixing Our Upside-Down Tax Code This year I was proud to cosponsor and vote for the Millionaire’s Tax to fix our outdated tax code that has relied on working families and low income households to fund critical services like K-12 education, healthcare, and higher education as well as fund the working families tax credit and paying for free breakfast and lunch for every kid during the school day.
This is a foundational change in our tax code so those fortunate enough to take full advantage of Washington’s economy will pay a tax rate of 9.9% on any income over $1 million.
That’s right, if you don’t make a million dollars in income a year, you won’t pay this tax.
