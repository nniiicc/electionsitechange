Putting Patients Before Paperwork
Carrie hears it from families all across the district: our healthcare system feels like it's being run by insurance companies instead of the doctors and nurses who actually know what their patients need.
Decisions about care are too often made by someone reviewing a claim, not someone in the exam room.
That's why a recent conversation with the team at One Cross Medical stuck with Carrie.
Their holistic approach, treating the whole person instead of just a list of symptoms, is a reminder of what healthcare can look like when it's built around patients instead of paperwork.
It's the kind of model more of our communities deserve access to.
Carrie will work to strengthen insurance regulations so coverage decisions reflect what's actually best for patients.
She'll push for pricing transparency, so families know what they're paying for before a bill shows up in the mail.
And she'll fight to expand access to community health centers and rural clinics, because primary care is always better than emergency care, for families and for the budget.
Getting people to a doctor before a small problem becomes a crisis isn't just good medicine.
It's good policy.
Carrie will work to build a healthcare system that puts patients, and prevention, first.
