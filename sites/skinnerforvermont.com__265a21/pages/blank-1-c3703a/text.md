Q's & A's MILTON INDEPENDENT - October 1, 2026 ​ Please introduce yourself and explain why you are running to represent the Grand Isle-Chittenden District. ​ I’m Dave Skinner, and I am running for the Vermont House to represent Grand Isle and West Milton.
I am running because Vermonters deserve better than the party-line voting that has come to define Montpelier.
I will represent every voice in my community by listening and, above all, serving.
As our state faces new challenges, we must work across the aisle to find lasting solutions that improve life for all Vermonters.
After the COVID-19 pandemic, I joined Grand Isle Rescue as a first responder.
The people I have met and the experiences I have had inspired me to take the next step and help ensure that Vermonters have access to the basic necessities they need.
Affordability remains a major concern for Vermont residents.
What specific action should the Legislature take to reduce pressure on household budgets, including property taxes, housing and energy costs? ​ Successful, responsible state government requires independent thinking, strong relationships, and a commitment to keeping party politics from getting in the way of good governance.
We need to focus on what is causing the problem, not who is to blame.
That means examining government spending and reviewing passed legislation to determine whether it is working and being implemented effectively.
If it is not, we must work together to fix it. ​ The district includes both the Champlain Islands and part of Milton.
How would you ensure that communities with different geographic, economic and infrastructure needs receive equitable representation?
The key to ensuring equitable representation is simple.
Listening to my constituents, and not just every two years, but throughout my time in office.
As your rep in the Vermont House, I want to know what concerns the voters have and take that back to the state house.
More than an occasional social media post, I would have set times to meet with each of the towns in Grand Isle and West Milton.
My focus would be 100% on my entire district, and not just my hometown, my party, or my donors.
Housing availability, transportation, water quality and access to essential services are significant concerns across the district.
Which of these issues requires the most immediate state action, and what would you propose? ​ Ensuring access to essential services must be a top priority for Vermont.
Rural healthcare providers are struggling to make ends meet, while limited public transportation places an added burden on local communities.
As a first responder I see local EMS and fire departments struggle to recruit volunteers, and food banks and assistance programs face steadily rising demand, it is time to put communities first.
We must adequately fund essential services such as 3SquaresVT, and Crop Cash.
These programs not only support recipients but also strengthen our local farms.
We must also ensure that constituents know about existing heating and healthcare assistance programs.
Moving toward universal primary care, supported by accessible community health centers, is an important first step in providing essential care for new families and older Vermonters, who are among those most at risk. ​ What is one bill or policy you would make a priority during the next legislative biennium, and what measurable difference would it make for residents of this district? ​ One policy I would prioritize in January is lowering healthcare costs by increasing transparency in healthcare and prescription drug pricing and examining the impact of private equity ownership in our healthcare institutions.
For too many Vermonters, a single accident or diagnosis can wipe out their savings and plunge them into debt.
My priority is to put patient care ahead of profits and Vermont’s hospital workforce ahead of boards and investors. ​ ​ THE ISLANDER NEWSPAPER October 8, 2026 ​ Vermont is restructuring its public education system under Acts 73 and 170, with changes involving school governance, funding and regional cooperation.
What should the Legislature prioritize as this work moves forward?
The next legislative session must prioritize listening to the communities and avoid any quick fix solutions.
Rural schools are the hub of our Island communities and we owe it to our students to do the hard work now to build better institutions for their future.
I support strong community input as well as shared services programs that allow neighboring schools to share costs and services such as purchasing, special ed, and administrative duties.
Many Vermonters are concerned about the rising cost of living.
What do you see as the biggest affordability challenge facing the people you would represent, and what should the Legislature do about it?
Housing prices are out of control.
Although tourism provides valuable tax revenue, many homes remain vacant for part of the year.
All-season second homes should be taxed at the same rate as year-round residences; in some towns, they are not.
To attract families to the Islands, communities should offer tax incentives for affordable housing construction and invest responsibly in town-center infrastructure, making it easier for residents to open and sustain small businesses and strengthen economies.
Health care costs continue to rise while access to care remains a concern in many Vermont communities.
What changes should the Legislature pursue to make health care more affordable and accessible?
Rural healthcare providers are struggling to make ends meet.
It is time to put communities first.
We need to start the path toward universal primary care, supported by accessible community health centers, which is an important first step in providing essential care for new families and older Vermonters, who are among those most at risk.
The quality of your healthcare should not be determined by the drive time to the nearest hospital.
Lake Champlain is central to the district's environment, economy and quality of life.
What specific action would you support to improve water quality and help communities address flooding, erosion or other threats to the lake?
We must protect wetlands and floodplains, regulate phosphorus runoff, and continue to fund, and learn from, successful programs.
Lake Champlain is vital to the islands’ drinking water, irrigation, and tourism.
We should fund organizations that study and protect the lake, listen to experts, and coordinate with New York and Quebec through groups such as the Lake Champlain Basin Program and Land Trust, which work with communities along every shore. © # Skinner For Vermont.
