About Media PRESS RELEASES NEWS ARTICLES MEMOS Reconnecting California Cleaner Communities Donate Opportunity Through Education No matter who you are or where you come from, who your parents are, or how much education those around you have, a good education can make a huge impact in one’s life and is the catalyst in providing better opportunities for the future.
I’m evidence that the challenges and barriers are real, but as are the opportunities that can come from education.
I was the first one in my family to attend college, and I believe that college should be accessible for everybody.
Unfortunately, financial burdens and other barriers often prevent high school students from applying to the college of their choice, hindering their opportunities for success.
In addition to financial barriers, children and youth in District 80 face other challenges, such as being English language learners, having parents with little or no higher education, and a lack of awareness of available services.
I understand these challenges firsthand, as my own parents were not English speakers and struggled to navigate educational opportunities for their children.
As a result, my siblings and I missed out on many opportunities.
As a passionate advocate for education, I am determined to make a lasting impact by ensuring that educational opportunities are expanded and accessible to all, but there is still much work ahead in Sacramento to achieve this goal. so far we have worked on : Providing an historic investment in TK-12 education to close the achievement gap and expand before/after-school options Ensuring high-quality education for students in nearby communities like Logan Heights, Barrio Logan, Golden Hill, and North Park Secured land for South Bay’s first university Providing an opportunity for low-income students near the California-Mexico border to attend local community colleges for job growth MILESTONES OF OUR FIGHT The Chula Vista Bachelor’s Degree Act of 2026 (AB 664) Access to a four-year degree should not require leaving your community.
AB 664 would authorize Southwestern College to offer four workforce-aligned bachelor’s degree programs right here in South County San Diego.
This will help lay the foundation for our future university campus in South Bay!
The Chula Vista University Act of 2025 (AB 662) Bringing a four-year public university to Chula Vista has been a vision for decades.
AB 662 establishes the South County Higher Education Task Force, bringing together the University of California, California State University, California Community Colleges, the City of Chula Vista, Sweetwater Union High School District, and local residents to produce a report on the governance, funding, and legal pathways needed to make that vision a reality.
Their deadline is July 1, 2027!
The Phone-Free School Act of 2024 (AB 3216) In a world where smartphones are everywhere, keeping students focused in the classroom has introduced new challenges.
AB 3216 requires all school districts to adopt a policy limiting student smartphone use, while giving each district the flexibility to determine what works best for their community.
This means students across California will benefit from fewer distractions, less bullying, and improved mental health and academic performance.
The First Generation Opportunity Act of 2024 (AB 2953) Being the first one in your family to go to college can be daunting, but it starts with knowing what opportunities exist.
AB 2953 requires public higher education systems to develop outreach plans for first-generation college students.
This will help our communities educate the next generation of teachers, doctors, engineers, and civic leaders.
The Brilliance Without Borders Act / Global Joint Degrees Act (AB 2633) Providing a study abroad experience in higher education allows a student to gain a firsthand understanding of other countries and cultures.
AB 2633 gives California State University campuses the green light to award joint degrees with recognized international institutions.
This means a student enrolled at San Diego State will have the opportunity to travel across the world to discover new perspectives, prepare to compete in a global economy, and represent California on the world stage.
Title IX Oversight and Compliance / Stop Gender Discrimination Act (AB 2326) A college campus should be a place where every student feels safe, respected, and free to learn.
AB 2326 requires University of California, California State University and the Community College system to report annually what they are doing to enforce Title IX protections against sex discrimination, harassment, and assault.
This improves transparency, strengthens accountability, and ensures that campuses are taking real action to protect students and promote gender equity in education.
Protecting Youth from Fentanyl (AB 2429) AB 2429, the Fentanyl Education Act, equips California’s middle and high school students with life-saving information about the dangers of fentanyl, a synthetic opioid driving a rise in youth overdose deaths.
This law ensures that students and educators have the tools to raise awareness, reduce stigma, and prevent tragedy.
It's part of a broader public health strategy to save lives through education.
Supporting Undocumented Students (AB 278) AB 278, the Dream Resource Center Grant Program, establishes grant funding for Dream Resource Centers across California colleges, ensuring undocumented students have access to essential resources like legal aid, academic advising, and mental health care.
These services create a safer, more supportive environment where all students—regardless of immigration status—can thrive and complete their education.
Opening Doors for Binational Students (AB 91) David introduced the Binational Tuition Exemption Pilot Program (AB 91), which became law in the fall of 2023.
This legislation removes a major financial barrier by waiving out-of-state tuition at California community colleges for low-income students living within 45 miles of the California-Mexico border.
This helps many binational and immigrant families who live in Baja California but have deep ties to California gain affordable access to higher education.
It’s a step toward equity and inclusivity in a dynamic border region where we need to educate more students to fill the jobs required for growth.
According to the San Diego Regional Economic Development Corporation, San Diego must double the amount of people with post-secondary education by 2030 to meet the demands of the local economy.
That equates to approximately 20,000 new skilled workers each year.
Secured Land for South Bay’s First University (AB 837) In 2023, David broke ground on the creation of UniverCity, a new library in Chula Vista, California.
This visionary project embodies a forward-thinking approach to education and community development, closely aligned with the goals of The Chula Vista University bill, AB 837, which would secure 383 acres for the City of Chula Vista.
The bill would give the city an exemption to the Surplus Lands Act for the land that has been acquired for the University-Innovation District.
As Assemblymember, David will continue to work to secure additional funding to expand this new facility and to bring more higher education opportunities to the South Bay.
Major Local Investment: Chula Vista University-Innovation District David secured $30 million in state funding to help launch South San Diego County’s long-awaited public university in Chula Vista, marking a historic investment in the region’s educational future.
In 2023, the project broke ground on a 168,000 square foot education center that will serve as a cornerstone of the 380-acre UniverCity Innovation District.
This transformative initiative is designed to expand access to higher education for local students, particularly those from underserved communities, while also driving long-term economic development by attracting research, business partnerships, and workforce opportunities to the South Bay.
Historic Investment in TK-12th Grade Education David voted for a State Budget that includes a historic investment in TK-12th grade education.
Our kids are still behind after two years of pandemic learning loss.
The achievement gap has grown and a majority of kids are currently reading and writing below their grade level.
This funding will allow all school districts to expand their before and after school options and makes California the first state in the nation with a Universal Meal Program for students.
Saving San Diego High School San Diego High School is the oldest high school in San Diego county and the only one that serves many nearby communities like Logan Heights, Barrio Logan, Golden Hill, and North Park.
As a San Diego High alumnus, David championed the successful passage of San Diego City Proposition I in 2016, which enabled the school to renew its lease and remain in Balboa Park, preserving access to high quality education to many families in the area.
