Like a lot of families in that community, mine registered Democrat and voted Democrat because… well, that’s what everybody else did.

My family relied on government programs - Medicare, WIC, food stamps, unemployment benefits, financial aid, the Obama phone - and that support built a kind of loyalty to the party that provided it.

Being a Democrat felt empowering… right up until I noticed the empowerment was always about being a victim of something: the system, the culture, somebody else’s success.

The Other Side of the Story I’m not going to pretend the pharmaceutical industry has clean hands in the opioid crisis.
Some companies did, indeed, market these drugs recklessly.

The same pharmacy chains he called out by name – Walgreens, CVS, and Walmart - fill prescriptions for diabetes, heart disease, and cancer, and increasingly provide the vaccines that keep our kids healthy.
The same manufacturers making generic opioids also make the affordable generic versions of medications that families in this district depend on to stay alive and stay well.

Making the Adoption Option Real So instead of relitigating a settled law, here’s what I think Carson City should actually focus on: 20 | P a g e Making it genuinely easier for a woman facing an unplanned pregnancy to choose life - if that’s the choice she wants to make - and easier still to choose adoption, if that’s the path that’s right for her.
I believe many women facing an unplanned pregnancy would choose to have the baby, and place that baby with a loving family, if the system actually made that possible instead of exhausting.
That means real financial support during the pregnancy itself, so money isn’t the reason a woman feels she has no choice.
And it means streamlining the adoption process on both ends - for the birth mother and for the family hoping to adopt - so it isn’t a maze of paperwork and delay at the exact moment everyone involved needs clarity and compassion.
I know from experience that the system on the receiving end can be its own kind of agonizing.
Families who want to say yes to a child get buried in paperwork, shuffled between caseworkers, and put through hearings that treat a two-year-old like a file number instead of a little girl who already has a family that loves her.
If Carson City wants more women to choose adoption, the answer isn’t another speech about the sanctity of life.
It’s fixing the system on both ends, so a birth mother is genuinely supported through her decision, and a family that says yes to a child doesn’t have to fight a courtroom for two years to keep her - the way I did.
