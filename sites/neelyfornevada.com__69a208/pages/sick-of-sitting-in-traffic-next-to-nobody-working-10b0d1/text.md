There’s one color in Las Vegas that unites Republicans (red), Democrats (blue), and Independents (purple)… ORANGE Orange cones.
Orange barrels.
Orange lane closures.
And somehow the roads are still blocked – even when nobody’s working!
Las Vegas drivers are fed up and it’s time somebody did something about it.
Who the $*%& is Getting Paid to Block Empty Roads?
Welcome to Cone City, USA!
Last spring, KTNV political reporter Steve Sebelius wrote something that stopped thousands of Nevada drivers cold – because every single one of them knew exactly what he was talking about: “End phantom construction zones by making it illegal to close any lane of traffic unless work is actually being performed.
Violations are civil fines starting at $5,000 per incident per day.
Budget problems solved!” Honestly?
Most drivers would vote for that tomorrow!
Because if you live in Las Vegas, you’ve lived this: Orange cones everywhere.
Lanes closed for miles.
Traffic backed up for blocks.
And not a single worker in sight!
Here’s what makes it worse: this isn’t even a new problem.
Back in 2011 … over fifteen years ago … then-State Assemblyman Tick Segerblom introduced AB275 to crack down on these so-called “phantom construction zones.” The bill would’ve required contractors to reopen lanes when no work was actually being done.
Makes sense, right?
Not to the politicians in Carson City.
They killed it.
So here we are, a decade and a half later, still sitting in traffic staring at orange cones and empty construction zones.
How Many Orange Cones Does It Take to Screw Up a City?
I’m Erica Neely .
I’m running to represent you in the Nevada Assembly.
Not to give speeches.
Not to play political games.
Not to make friends in Carson City.
But to walk in on Day One and start fighting for the people who are actually living here.
The working families.
The small business owners.
The parents just trying to get their kids to school.
All without losing thirty minutes to a construction zone nobody’s using !
Look, no one expects road work to disappear completely.
Las Vegas is still growing.
Fast.
And roads need repairs.
We all get that.
But there’s a difference between an honest work zone and a cone sitting in the road because nobody bothered to move it.
One is an inconvenience.
The other is government telling you your time doesn’t matter.
You leave early for work.
You budget your morning carefully.
You’re trying to get your kids to school on time.
Then suddenly: two lanes closed, traffic crawling, nobody working – and you’re late anyway.
And it’s not just an annoyance.
It’s a safety issue.
Frustrated drivers weave through lanes.
Others slam their brakes trying to merge at the last second.
Accidents happen.
Blood pressure spikes.
All because somebody decided it was easier to leave cones sitting there than do their job and reopen the road.
That’s not how government is supposed to work.
Government works for the people.
Not the other way around.
And respecting taxpayers means respecting their time.
Politicians call this a small issue.
You and I know better.
These frustrations add up.
Day after day, week after week.
And when government ignores them year after year, people stop trusting the people running the show – and for good reason.
If elected, I’ll push for updated legislation to end phantom construction zones once and for all.
Simple rule: If your crew isn’t on the road , your cones can’t be either.
And if you leave them up anyway?
Real penalties.
With teeth.
Not suggestions.
Not guidelines.
Fines.
This isn’t a Republican issue or a Democrat issue.
It’s a Nevada issue.
And it’s been ignored long enough – including by my opponent.
