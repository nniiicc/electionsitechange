Devin Heath was seventeen.
He wanted to be a firefighter.
Like his dad.
But on March 18, 2024, a driver speeding at 73 miles an hour in a 30-mph zone near a Las Vegas park – where kids play and families walk – hit Devin as he crossed the street.
He died at the hospital a few days later.
Devin’s father sat through the entire trial with his son’s photo hidden under his shirt.
A jury took about an hour to convict the driver.
Prosecutors asked for up to six years in prison.
However… The judge gave him probation, a curfew, and a driving class!
That’s it.
No prison whatsoever.
Devin’s dad called the sentence “trash.” He’s right.
It was outrageous.
But Devin Heath’s case isn’t the only one.
In another case, a 17-year-old was robbed on a North Las Vegas street.
But instead of calling police, he went home and got a gun.
He then tracked down the kid who robbed him and shot him at least seven times while, according to witnesses, the victim begged for his life.
A jury convicted him of voluntary manslaughter with a deadly weapon.
Prosecutors asked that he be taken into custody immediately, calling it a revenge killing.
But the judge disagreed.
He called it two boys on “a collision course” and gave the killer a suspended sentence and probation.
No prison.
Just like Devin’s case.
And here’s one more… A 36-year-old convicted felon with at least 35 prior arrests – including prison time for drugs and involuntary manslaughter – was arrested again earlier this year in Las Vegas.
A judge ordered him released with an ankle monitor despite Sheriff Kevin McMahill warning that “I cannot effectively supervise these people when they ask me to put them out on electronic monitoring.” Well, guess what happened?
Yep.
Shortly thereafter he was arrested AGAIN on 27 new felony charges.
Too many judges today care more about the criminal than the victim – or the public.
