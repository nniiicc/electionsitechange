IT’S NOT WHAT THE MEDIA WANTS YOU TO HEAR But it’s exactly why I’m running to represent you.
The political “professionals” told me not to waste my time writing this letter.
They said most non-partisan voters don’t really care about midterm elections and likely wouldn’t be voting this year.
I’m wrote it anyway.
My name is Erica Neely .
I’m running to represent you in Assembly District 9.
And I know I’m supposed to be mailing you those slick , full-color , oversized postcards that every other candidate is flooding your mailbox with.
You know – the ones screaming that their opponent is responsible for every piece of human misery on the planet – right down to the missing sock in the dryer.
In fact, you’ve probably already gotten a few from my opponent.
But I’m a different kind of candidate and I’m running a different kind of campaign.
I think you deserve to know more about the people you vote for than just a name on the ballot and a bunch of slick mailers written by a DC political consultant.
So instead, I’m going to send you a series of letters over the next couple of weeks telling you in detail: EXACTLY who I am… EXACTLY where I stand, and… EXACTLY why I would best represent you in Carson City.
Ready?
From El Salvador and Mexico . . . to South Central L.A.
My mom is from El Salvador.
My dad, Mexico.
My mom was brought to this country illegally to flee the civil war .
My dad was brought to the country illegally after his own father was killed by the drug cartels .
They weren’t chasing handouts.
They were running for their lives.
After Ronald Reagan granted amnesty in 1986, they got in line.
Followed the rules.
Did the paperwork.
No shortcuts.
No excuses.
Here legally .
They later met in southern California.
Fell in love.
Got married.
And not long after, I was born.
I guess some critics would say I’m an original “anchor baby.” But I think of myself as a proud, first-generation AMERICAN .
Growing up, Spanish was the only language spoken in our house.
So when I went to school, I was completely lost.
I didn’t learn to read and write English until I was in high school.
I had to teach myself English, with the help of some classmates.
And yet, the system kept passing me through , grade after grade, despite not understanding what was being taught.
Danger, real danger , was also part of where I grew up.
Police helicopters were normal.
Police sirens were background noise.
And yes, on more than one occasion we had to dodge gang-bangers , drug dealers , and bullets walking home from school.
So shortly after graduating, I fled L.A. and moved to Las Vegas – as a “refugee,” not a “missionary.” Believe me, the LAST thing I want is for our neighborhoods – where we’re raising our own kids and grandkids – to turn into the hell-on-earth I left behind.
A Fight That Changed Everything My husband and I had already fostered several kids when we got a call from Child Haven letting me know they had seven babies who needed a home.
One little girl caught my eye.
Ten days old.
Born in a motel bathroom toilet .
To a mother in the grip of addiction.
Who was so strung out she didn’t even know she was in labor.
By the time paramedics got there, the baby had already turned blue.
They saved her life .
I signed the paperwork and carried her out.
We named her Sophia that night.
What came next was a two-year legal fight with the government to adopt and keep her.
A judge once looked me in the eye and told me, “Sophia is not yours.
She is property of the state.” That turned my blood cold.
Eventually, we won.
She’s ours.
She’s safe.
And she’s loved.
Last year I wrote the full story in a book titled Fighting for Sophia .
And if you’d like a free copy mailed to you, or dropped off at your door, just request one by going to: fightingforsophia.com Taking the Fight to the Next Level That fight with the government over adopting Sophia changed something in me.
I realized the same government bureaucracy touches every family in this district in a hundred other ways.
Your kids’ schools, your taxes, your streets, your parks, your health insurance, your auto insurance, your water bill – and the very safety of your neighborhood.
So in 2024, I ran for this same seat against the powerful sitting Speaker of the Assembly, Steve Yeager.
He outraised me by nearly twenty to one.
But I still came within two points of beating him – which I guess is why he decided to quit rather than run against me again this year.
But quitting has never been an option for me.
In anything.
So after that race, I didn’t disappear .
I founded the Hispanic Latin Alliance and started writing about issues affecting our district, our state and our country as a regular columnist for Nevada News & Views .
I’ve covered everything from water rights to school choice; from immigration to the phantom construction zones jamming up our streets.
You can read every archived column yourself to get to know me better by going to: nevadanewsandviews.com/author/ericaneely And I’m still writing for them today, even while I run again to represent you.
