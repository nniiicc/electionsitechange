Raised in Bremerton, she has faced some of the biggest barriers to success and opportunity — from poverty, to opioid use disorder and incarceration.
It was with the help of this community — friends, family, colleagues and neighbors — that she was able to overcome these circumstances and become a tireless advocate for change.
Tarra doesn’t simply talk about challenges; she has faced them herself.
After her incarceration, she experienced firsthand the challenges of re-entry — economic insecurity, housing affordability, and lack of access to healthcare, education, and job skills training.
These are big challenges that many people from all walks of life in our community face on a daily basis.
A CHAMPION FOR PROGRESS Tarra has played a key role in advocacy efforts on behalf of her community, focusing on education, healthcare, and income equality.
Prior to serving as State Representative, her activism and leadership helped pass the New Hope Act, a bipartisan piece of legislation that streamlines the process for reintegration after incarceration.
Since elected, she has passed over twenty impactful bills increasing healthcare, housing and opportunity for others while advocating for millions of dollars in investments across the district.
Tarra is also a co-founder and consultant for several nonprofits that serve formerly incarcerated people with direct reentry supports, develop leadership capacity and build political power.
Furthermore, she is a successful small business owner and successful lawyer that continues to invest in the most marginalized communities throughout her district as a fierce advocate for justice.

REPRESENTING THE 23RD LEGISLATIVE DISTRICT Lowering Costs She reduced financial burdens for small businesses hardest hit during the pandemic, invested in school support staff and helped reduce the price of life-saving prescription drugs, including insulin.
Keeping Kitsap Moving She supported a $17 billion transportation package that includes funding to build hybrid-electric ferries, improve current ferry operations, address staffing shortages, and provide free fares for anyone 18 and under.
SAFER TRANSPORTATION This transportation package also funded improvements to bike and pedestrian safety, toll relief, and expansions and upgrades to Highway 3.

Does Tarra have a criminal record?
Tarra’s criminal record was vacated by a Kitsap County Superior Court judge in September 2023 under Washington’s New Hope Act, which expanded eligibility to vacate certain convictions.
Her 2013 convictions were set aside and the underlying cases dismissed under Washington law.
Was Tarra's criminal record vacated?
Tarra met Washington’s legal requirements for vacation under the New Hope Act, which expanded eligibility for certain individuals who have demonstrated rehabilitation and remained crime-free for the period of time since their convictions.
Why is Tarra's past important to her work?
Tarra has experienced many of the systems she now works to change.
Her connection to the experiences in the real lives of constituents empowers her work from healthcare and addiction treatment to incarceration, reentry, housing, and employment barriers.
Her legislative work includes reforms addressing legal financial obligations, addiction treatment, behavioral healthcare, and reentry that deliver for families across Kitsap County and beyond.
