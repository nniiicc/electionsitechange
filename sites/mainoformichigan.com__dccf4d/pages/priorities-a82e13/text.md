Lowering the Cost of Living
Michigan families and seniors are working hard to make ends meet, but rising prices, from groceries and health care to prescription drugs and housing, are making it harder to stay financially secure, even when people are doing everything right.
Jan believes everyone should be able to afford the basics and live with dignity in the community they call home, whether they are raising a family, building a career, or enjoying retirement after a lifetime of hard work.
In Lansing, she’ll focus on practical solutions that lower everyday costs, expand access to affordable housing, protect access to affordable prescriptions and quality health care, and ease pressure on household budgets.
Working families and seniors alike deserve the security to stay in their homes, get the care they need, and plan for the future with confidence.
Investing in Public Schools
Jan believes our children should always come first — and that every child deserves a strong public school and a fair shot at success—no matter where they live.
As president of the East Jackson School Board, she has fought to strengthen public schools and stand up for teachers, knowing that when educators are supported and schools have what they need, students have a better chance to succeed.
In Lansing, Jan will continue standing up for public education so our kids are prepared for the future.
Fighting for Good Paying Jobs
Jan believes in the dignity of work and an economy that rewards it.
She’ll fight for good-paying jobs with strong benefits, stand up for union workers, support small businesses, and expand access to technical and job training so people can gain in-demand skills and build lasting careers close to home.
She’ll back investments that create good jobs people can raise a family on—because when working families do well, the entire community is stronger.
Increasing Access to Mental Health Care
Jan has seen firsthand what happens when families reach out for help and are met with long waitlists, high costs, or nowhere to turn.
She believes mental health care is health care, and in Lansing she’ll work to expand access to counseling and crisis services, strengthen the mental health workforce, and ensure insurance coverage actually meets people’s needs.
Because when individuals and families can get support early, communities are healthier, stronger, and better equipped to thrive.
