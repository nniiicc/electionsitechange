The effort was first reported by WIRED on Tuesday.
Billboard design for the new “Mike Rogers Is Watching You” campaign.
During nearly 15 years in Congress, Rogers helped author and pass the PATRIOT Act and chaired the House Intelligence Committee, with oversight authority over the NSA and other U.S. intelligence agencies.
After Congress, he entered Washington’s revolving door, cashing in by advising companies in the same intelligence and cybersecurity industries he once regulated.
This new effort puts that record front and center at mikeiswatching.com , tracing Rogers’ history on surveillance from his time in Washington through his post-congressional career.
The site also features a new digital ad launching today across Abdul for Senate’s social channels, with billboards promoting the website going up across Michigan this week.
Key elements of Mike Rogers’ record highlighted on mikeiswatching.com include: Rogers’ Surveillance Votes: 2001 : Rogers “helped author” and voted for the PATRIOT Act.
2008 : Rogers voted for a spying law that let the government collect the calls and emails of people overseas without a warrant.
Americans’ messages got swept in too.
When people warned it would spy on Americans, he called that “fear mongering.” 2008 : Rogers voted to protect phone companies from being sued for helping the government spy on customers without warrants.
2011 : Rogers wrote a bill that would have let companies hand your emails, chats, and browsing history to the government, including the NSA.
Companies and groups that lobbied on that bill gave Rogers at least $103,000 .
2013 : The House voted on stopping the government from collecting millions of Americans’ phone records.
Mike Rogers helped lead the fight to keep collecting them.
2014 : Rogers voted against a bipartisan plan to stop the government from searching Americans’ emails, texts, and photos without a warrant.
Three Times : Rogers voted against making the government get a warrant before wiretapping Americans.
Rogers’ Revolving Door Record: Rogers joined the board of a cybersecurity company started by the former head of the NSA, Keith Alexander.
It gave him more than $360,000 in stocks and compensation in two years.
Rogers advised a phone company that admitted helping Nicolás Maduro’s government spy on Venezuelans.
It paid him more than $94,000 .
Rogers’ surveillance record is taking on renewed relevance as he seeks a return to Washington amid the rise of AI-powered surveillance and growing concern over Flock cameras, with recent polling finding two-thirds of Midwestern voters concerned about their use.
