Pennsylvania already has an estimated 101 active data centers, with dozens more proposed statewide — not just here.
They're driving up everyone's electric bill, and once one is built next to a neighborhood or a school, there's no undoing it.
Caren Lowrey is a lifelong South Whitehall resident, a Parkland graduate, a mother of three, and a drug prevention specialist running for State Representative to stop it, alongside lower costs, stronger schools, student safety, and accountability.

City & State PA Why this can't just be a local fight The bill that helps build them isn't local either.
House Bill 502 wouldn't just affect one township — it would create a state board with the power to approve data-center power facilities anywhere in Pennsylvania and override local zoning entirely.
Mike Schlossberg co-sponsored it.
At a May 2026 public forum on data centers, he said plainly: "I don't think a moratorium is appropriate from an economic or from an infrastructure development perspective." He didn't propose a pause — he said no to one, then backed the bill that clears the way for more of these, in every district, including yours.
Follow the Money Right here at home The Atlas data center: our neighborhood's version of it.
Every one of these fights has a local face.
Ours is the Atlas data center — a hyperscale proposal directly across Route 309 from Parkland High School, down from an original 5.1 million sq ft to roughly 1.5 million sq ft after community pushback, with a planning commission decision still pending.
Caren has fought it since before she was a candidate for anything.
