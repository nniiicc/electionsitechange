Donate About Me Affordability + Michiganders are struggling to afford to live in the communities they work in, with rising housing costs, inflation keeping prices of food and essentials high, unsustainable healthcare expenses, and utility rates among the highest in the nation.
Our schools lack the funding to meet the needs of every child.
On top of the challenges of everyday living to the working class, our basic freedoms, democratic rights and safety nets are also under attack.
Lack of affordable housing is a major issue in our district so I support investing in low income housing, raising revenue through short term rental taxes, and working on permitting reform to accommodate 20,000 accessory dwelling units in the region.
Affordability also means good jobs, which is why I support paid family leave and worker’s rights, including standing with our unions.
Money Out of Politics + Over the past year, I have been collecting signatures for ballot initiatives including Michiganders for Money Out of Politics, a legislative initiative to stop public utilities and large government contractors from donating in campaigns they have a conflict of interest.
By increasing accountability in our election funding, we can push back on utility rate hikes and rising health insurance premiums.
Once elected, I will take this further and enact policies that keep more money in the pockets of everyday people.
Healthcare + I’m also a supporter of MICare, a Michigan proposal that would provide basic healthcare for all while cutting out bureaucracy and expense.
I’m tired of seeing the people I love jumping through a million hoops to get the care they need, and I’m tired of people not being able to afford the care they need.
We also need to make sure that our healthcare workers are fairly compensated and can afford to live in the communities they are served.
Keep Michigan Clean + I love our beautiful shoreline, inland lakes, our forests, and the many agricultural areas that sustain us.
I want to make sure that these areas stay protected with common-sense policies that ensure they remain toxin and pollution-free for generations to come.
Civil Rights + I love this community and know the goodness in the hearts of Michiganders.
Many of us are heartbroken to see attacks on our immigrant neighbors, racial profiling of our tribal members, dehumanization of our LGBTQ+ loved ones, rhetoric that wants to set back women’s rights and take away medical choices, and rising hate and resentment towards the poor, those with disabilities, and anyone else deemed other or lessor.
This cannot stand.
We are a community that should come together around our kindness and building systems that work for all.
As we observe the 250th anniversary of our nation, it is imperative that we lean into the “Liberty and Justice for All” mentality over rising authoritarianism and oligarchy.
Fund our Schools + I also volunteered as an informational session leader and signature collector for Invest in MI Kids, an initiative to have fair-share taxes in Michigan that would put more money into the school aid fund.
The school aid fund helps fund community college and trade programs, ensuring people have the skills for good jobs and businesses can find amazing workers here.
Ranked Choice Voting + This past year, I worked on the Rank MI Vote initiative to bring ranked choice voting to Michigan.
This is a form of instant runoff voting that makes sure that the winning candidate is supported by a majority of voters rather than a plurality.
We need democracy to work better for us and call all people into the voting process.
Many people feel disillusioned with the whole system not representing their views and needs.
Ranked voting can help ensure that elected officials better represent the people over special interests.
Data Centers + I am supportive of local communities coming together to address zoning and planning concerns, the environment, privacy and AI concerns, utility rates, and noise generation around data centers.
We see the need for moratoriums as communities work to figure out next steps.
These decisions should be in the hands of the people who are affected, not politicians and big tech.
I also oppose the installation of Flock cameras in our communities, which are powered by these data centers.
We need to stand against this establishment of a surveillance state.
