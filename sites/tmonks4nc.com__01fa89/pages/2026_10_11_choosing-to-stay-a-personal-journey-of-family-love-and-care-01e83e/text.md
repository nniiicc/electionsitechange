The people who spent years caring for us, guiding us, and making sacrifices to give us a better life begin to need a little more care themselves.
And suddenly, we find ourselves standing at a crossroads, trying to figure out what it means to be there for the people who have always been there for us.
For me, that moment has come with my mother’s retirement and the reality of watching her grow older.
My decision to stay at home and help care for my mother was not made in a single moment.
It has been a gradual realization shaped by love, responsibility, family, and an understanding that life does not always follow the timeline we once imagined for ourselves.
It is a decision that has changed how I think about the future, what I value, and what it means to show up for someone you love.
The Woman Who Has Always Been There My mother has spent much of her life taking care of other people.
She put herself through nursing school, built a life for our family, and showed me what it means to keep going even when circumstances are difficult.
Growing up, I knew my mother as the person who figured things out.
She was the steady presence, the person who kept things moving, and the one who found a way forward when life presented challenges.
Like many children, I probably took some of that steadiness for granted.
When you grow up with someone who consistently shows up for you, it can be difficult to imagine a time when they might need you to show up for them in a different way.
As I’ve gotten older, I’ve come to understand the sacrifices that went into creating the life I had.
I understand more about the decisions she had to make, the responsibilities she carried, and the strength it took to keep our family going.
Now, as she enjoys retirement and begins navigating the realities of aging, I have the opportunity to return some of that care.
That does not mean I can fix everything.
It does not mean I can take away every worry or make the difficult parts of getting older disappear.
It means I can be present.
I can help with the practical things, share the responsibilities, and make sure she does not have to navigate this chapter of life alone.
For me, that matters.
Making the Decision to Stay There is a particular kind of uncertainty that comes with making a decision that changes the direction of your life.
We grow up hearing about independence, careers, accomplishments, and the importance of building a future of our own.
We make plans for where we want to go, what we want to achieve, and the person we hope to become.
Then life happens.
Sometimes, the most important decision you make is not about moving somewhere new or reaching the next milestone.
Sometimes, it is about recognizing what is happening right in front of you and deciding that being present is where you are needed most.
Choosing to stay home and care for my mother has required me to think differently about independence and success.
It has meant considering not only what I want for my future, but also what kind of support my mother needs as she enters a new stage of hers.
There are practical realities to consider, too.
Aging can bring changes in mobility, finances, daily routines, and the ability to manage responsibilities that once seemed effortless.
Retirement can be a wonderful opportunity to slow down and enjoy life, but it can also bring uncertainty, particularly when someone is living on a fixed income.
These are not abstract concerns when they involve someone you love.
They become part of your everyday life.
They influence the decisions you make, the responsibilities you take on, and the way you think about the years ahead.
My choice has been about doing what I can to help create stability, preserve her independence where possible, and make sure she knows she has someone beside her.
I know that staying is not the right decision for everyone.
Some people provide care from a distance.
Others share responsibilities among siblings, work with professional caregivers, or find other arrangements that meet their family’s needs.
There is no single way to love someone well.
For me, this is the choice that makes sense for my family right now.
Making Room for Both When I began seriously considering running for public office, I knew it was a decision that would affect more than just me.
Running for office requires time, energy, and a willingness to put yourself out into the world in a way that is different from most other experiences.
There are evenings away from home, community events, meetings, and responsibilities that do not always fit neatly into a predictable schedule.
It means opening up parts of your life to public scrutiny while taking on a commitment that extends beyond your own household.
I could not make a decision about stepping into public life without also thinking carefully about what that would mean for my life at home.
I had to ask myself some difficult questions.
How would I continue to be there for my mother?
What would happen when my schedule became unpredictable?
How could I pursue something I felt called to do without compromising the care, stability, and presence she deserves?
These were not questions I could simply set aside and hope would work themselves out later.
They were conversations I needed to have and plans I needed to make.
My mother and I have had to talk honestly about what this next chapter could look like.
We have had to think about our routines, the responsibilities that need to be handled, and how we would adjust when my commitments outside the home required more of my time.
We have had to recognize that life will not always go according to plan and that being prepared means being willing to adapt.
Those conversations matter because I do not see caring for my mother and pursuing my goals as two parts of my life that must compete with one another.
Both matter to me.
Both require thought, commitment, and support.
I also recognize that I cannot do everything alone.
Just as I believe families need to communicate openly and share responsibilities when circumstances change, I have had to think about the people and resources that can help us navigate this next chapter.
Making a plan does not mean pretending there will never be difficult days.
It means acknowledging those possibilities and deciding how we will respond when they come.
I know that entering public life bring new demands and challenges.
I also know that my responsibilities at home do not disappear simply because I have taken on responsibilities elsewhere.
I will have to be intentional about my time, honest about what I can manage, and willing to adjust when circumstances change.
That is part of the commitment I am making to myself, to my family, and to my constituents.
For a long time, I thought about the future in terms of the things I wanted to accomplish and the direction I wanted my life to take.
Now, I think just as much about how I want to live while pursuing those goals: the responsibilities I want to honor, the relationships I want to nurture, and the kind of person I want to be when life asks me to balance competing demands.
Running for office is one part of that future.
Being there for my mother is another.
Neither diminishes the importance of the other.
If anything, this experience has reminded me that a meaningful life is rarely built around just one responsibility or one ambition.
We are constantly navigating the commitments we make to ourselves and the commitments we make to the people we love.
The Parts of Caregiving People Don’t Always See When people talk about caring for an aging parent, they often focus on the big moments: the medical appointments, the major decisions, or the times when something goes wrong.
But caregiving is often made up of much smaller things.
It is thinking ahead about what needs to be done.
It is making sure bills and household responsibilities are managed.
It is adjusting your schedule around someone else’s needs.
It is paying attention to the little changes that might otherwise go unnoticed.
Sometimes, it is simply being in the next room.
There is no dramatic soundtrack accompanying these moments.
There is no clear finish line or checklist that tells you that you have done enough.
Much of the work is quiet, ordinary, and repetitive.
And that is precisely why it can be difficult.
You can love someone deeply and still feel overwhelmed.
You can be grateful for the time you have together and still worry about what the future holds.
You can know that you made the right decision for your family and still wonder how to balance your own goals, responsibilities, and needs with the commitment you have made.
Two things can be true at once.
I am learning that caregiving is not simply about giving something up.
It is also about learning to see the value in things that our culture does not always recognize as accomplishments.
Being dependable is an accomplishment.
Creating a stable home is an accomplishment.
Helping someone you love maintain their dignity and independence is an accomplishment.
Not every meaningful thing we do comes with a job title, a paycheck, a public acknowledgment, or a milestone we can point to later.
Some of the most important work in our lives happens behind closed doors, witnessed by very few people.
That does not make it any less valuable.
Choosing to Be Present I do not know exactly what the future will look like.
I cannot predict how my mother’s needs will change or what challenges we may face along the way.
What I do know is that this season of life has given me a different understanding of what matters.
I am learning to value presence over perfection, patience over certainty, and the quiet work of showing up over the idea that every meaningful decision must lead to some grand achievement.
I am learning that a life can change direction without losing its purpose.
And I am learning that choosing to care for someone is not a small thing.
It is a commitment that touches nearly every part of your life, often in ways you do not fully understand until you are living it.
My mother spent years helping me become the person I am today.
I cannot repay every sacrifice she made, nor do I believe love should be measured by a ledger of debts and obligations.
But I can choose to be present in this chapter of her life.
I can help where I am able, listen when she needs someone to listen, and make sure she knows that she does not have to face every change alone.
For now, that is what staying means to me.
It means recognizing that life is not always about moving forward by moving away.
Sometimes, moving forward means staying close to the people who helped you get here.
It means understanding that caring for someone is not a detour from life.
It is part of life itself.
And perhaps most importantly, it means remembering that none of us should have to navigate the hardest seasons of life entirely on our own.
We need each other at the beginning of our lives.
We need each other as we find our way through the middle.
And, if we are fortunate, we have the opportunity to be there for one another as the years go by.
I chose to stay because my mother matters to me.
Because our time together matters.
Because I believe that being present for the people we love is one of the most meaningful things we can do with the time we have.
I do not know every challenge this decision will bring.
But I know why I made it.
For now, I am right where I need to be.
