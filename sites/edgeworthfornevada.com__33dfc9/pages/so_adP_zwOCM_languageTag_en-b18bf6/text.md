ASSEMBLY DISTRICT 35 AUGUST NEWSLETTER I hope everyone had a wonderful Independence Day celebrating our nation's 250th birthday.
This milestone gave me an opportunity to reflect on what makes America so special.
One of the greatest gifts our founders gave us was the opportunity to have a voice in our government.
Whether it's voting, volunteering, attending a community meeting, or simply helping a neighbor, our country is strongest when people stay engaged.
Nevada has always embodied that pioneering spirit.
Our military families, first responders, healthcare professionals, educators, entrepreneurs, and small business owners make our communities stronger every day.
It is an incredible honor to serve as your Assemblywoman and be your voice in Carson City.
One of the best parts of this job is getting to spend time with the people I represent.
Every conversation helps me better understand the challenges, ideas, and priorities of our community.
This month I met with healthcare professionals, educators, veterans, students, business owners, and many of you at community events throughout the district.
Our monthly Coffee with Constituents at Aware Coffee continues to be one of my favorite traditions.
We've been gathering there for years, and I always leave with new ideas and a better understanding of what's important to you.
We also held our first Happy Hour at Spaghetty Western, and the turnout was incredible.
It was such a success that we're making it a monthly event.
I hope you'll join me at one of these gatherings.
I truly enjoy hearing directly from you.
The Nevada Constitution makes the Legislature a part-time citizen legislature, and I believe that's one of its greatest strengths.
The best legislators stay connected to the people they serve by continuing to live, work, and serve in their communities.
When people ask what I do for a living, I tell them I'm a physician and a professor.
Both roles are deeply rewarding.
This month I taught medical students about caring for older adults, a field that has always been close to my heart.
I also brought students into the community to care for underserved patients, because I believe future healthcare providers learn just as much by serving people where they are as they do in the classroom.
My work as a physician helps shape my work in Carson City, and my work in Carson City helps me fight for better healthcare for my patients.
This month Nevada released the latest Point in Time Homeless Census, which showed an increase in homelessness across our state.
Every week I volunteer to provide medical care to people experiencing homelessness, and even as temperatures climbed above 110 degrees, our team continued visiting places like The Courtyard and HELP of Southern Nevada to care for those who needed it most.
Expanding access to healthcare, strengthening programs that help people get back on their feet, and reducing unnecessary emergency room visits remain priorities for me both in the clinic and in the Legislature.
On July 8, I had the honor of participating in the Sons of the Revolution's reading of the Declaration of Independence, commemorating the 250th anniversary of its first public reading.
Communities across the country and around the world participated at the same time, and it was a privilege to read a portion of this historic document.
Standing among so many patriotic Nevadans, and yes, Benjamin Franklin too, is a memory I'll never forget.
I also had the opportunity to present a legislative update to the Nevada Osteopathic Medical Association.
Many people don't realize how much legislation affects the practice of medicine, from licensing and graduate medical education to regulations that directly impact patient care.
I enjoy helping physicians understand the legislative process and encouraging them to get involved, because healthcare policy is always better when the people caring for patients have a seat at the table.
This is also the time of year when legislators begin preparing Bill Draft Requests for the next legislative session.
Each incumbent Assembly member is allotted ten bill drafts, and I've been working with physicians, patients, healthcare organizations, and community members to develop ideas that will improve healthcare in Nevada.
While the details are still coming together, I can tell you that expanding access to quality healthcare remains my top priority.
Good ideas don't belong to one political party, and I'm always willing to work with anyone who wants to make Nevada healthier.
As we head into a busy fall, I'll continue adding opportunities to meet throughout the district.
I hope you'll stop by, introduce yourself, and share what's on your mind.
Your ideas, concerns, and experiences help make me a better legislator, and I always appreciate the chance to connect. -Rebecca Upcoming Events • Saturday, August 15 | 10:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m.
