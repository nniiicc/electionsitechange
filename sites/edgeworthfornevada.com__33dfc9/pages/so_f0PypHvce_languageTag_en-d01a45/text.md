ASSEMBLY DISTRICT 35 JULY NEWSLETTER June was a very busy month!
While I didn't have a primary election, there wasn't much time to slow down.
Now that the primaries are behind us, we know what's ahead in the general election, and the real work begins.
Outside of the Legislature, I stayed busy in my work as a physician and professor, teaching the next generation of healthcare providers.
Summer is one of the toughest times of the year for those experiencing homelessness, and our team continued providing care to men, women, and children across the valley.
By bringing healthcare directly to people in need, we can often prevent unnecessary emergency room visits.
I had the opportunity to visit The Courtyard, SHARE Village, the Catholic Worker, and several other community locations.
June 20 was also World Refugee Day, and it was a privilege to provide care to many members of our immigrant communities.
I always enjoy practicing my Spanish and connecting with patients from many different backgrounds.
June also marked the inaugural meeting of the Nevada Therapeutic Association, bringing together professionals from speech pathology, occupational therapy, and physical therapy from across the state.
I was honored to serve on the legislative panel and appreciated the opportunity to discuss ways we can improve access to quality healthcare for all Nevadans.
We also kicked off the Nevada Forum, which brought together people from many different backgrounds to discuss the issues we all share.
I've always believed that lasting progress happens when people are willing to come together, listen, and work toward practical solutions.
This month I also had the chance to return to University Medical Center, where I completed my residency years ago.
It was incredible to see how much UMC has grown.
The hospital continues expanding training opportunities, including Nevada's first radiology residency program, which officially began on July 1.
UMC also hosted its first Academic Summit, bringing together educators from Roseman University, UNLV, Touro University, Nevada State University, the College of Southern Nevada, and many others.
It was encouraging to see so many institutions working together to strengthen Nevada's healthcare workforce.
Whenever someone offers me a tour, I almost always say yes.
Every visit gives me a better understanding of our community and how I can be a more effective legislator.
This month I toured the future Campus for Hope, which is still under construction but has tremendous potential to address homelessness in Southern Nevada.
I also had the opportunity to ride in a Zoox autonomous vehicle, and I have to admit it was one of the coolest experiences I've had.
The technology was impressive, and I was amazed by how comfortable and safe the ride felt.
Another highlight was spending time with students at the Power to Parent Civics Academy.
These young people proudly demonstrated their knowledge by reciting the verses of "The Star-Spangled Banner," the Preamble to the Constitution, and the Bill of Rights.
It was inspiring to see their enthusiasm for our nation's history, especially as we prepare to celebrate America's 250th anniversary.
Many people ask how the Legislature can accomplish everything in a 120-day session every other year.
The truth is, we can't.
That's why our interim committees continue meeting between sessions.
During June's Health and Human Services Committee meeting, we focused on several important issues, including pediatric healthcare, mental health, and workforce development.
While it's clear Nevada still faces significant challenges, I was encouraged to hear that so many organizations are making workforce expansion a priority.
I look forward to working on legislation next session that will help train and retain more healthcare professionals here in Nevada.
One of the best parts of being an Assemblywoman is connecting directly with the people I represent.
We held another Coffee with Constituents at Aware Coffee, and it was our largest turnout yet.
These monthly conversations have become one of my favorite traditions because they give me the opportunity to hear directly from you.
The topics vary, but education, public safety, our economy, and healthcare continue to be at the top of the list.
I also enjoyed participating in the Mountains Edge Red, White & Backyard celebration, where I had the chance to visit with hundreds of constituents and celebrate our community together.
July is already shaping up to be another busy month.
I hope you'll join me for Coffee with Constituents on Saturday, July 18, from 10:00 a.m. to noon at Aware Coffee.
Bring a friend or neighbor, enjoy a cup of coffee, and let's talk about what's on your mind.
You can also visit my website to see upcoming events.
And if you can't make it, please don't hesitate to reach out.
