ASSEMBLY DISTRICT 35 JUNE NEWSLETTER What an action-packed May it has been!
This month gave me many opportunities to connect with business and community leaders across Southern Nevada.
I attended the Henderson Chamber's Meet the Candidates event, several Vegas Chamber gatherings, and a Latin Chamber luncheon focused on the future of business and transportation in Nevada (planes, trains, and automobiles!) It was exciting to hear directly from leaders who are helping shape our state's future.
I'm especially proud to be endorsed by these organizations because it reflects a shared commitment to economic growth, job creation, and keeping Nevada a great place to live and do business.
One of the unique privileges of serving in the Legislature is the opportunity to issue legislative proclamations recognizing extraordinary achievements in our community.
This month, I was honored to present a proclamation at Sunrise Hospital on Trauma Survivors Day and help celebrate their new Level I Trauma Center designation.
Sunrise has served our valley for more than 60 years, and this achievement means Southern Nevadans will have access to the highest level of trauma care when they need it most.
One of my favorite parts of serving in the Assembly is meeting constituents and hearing directly about the issues that matter to them.
This month, we hosted a fantastic community event in Southern Highlands where hundreds of residents stopped by to say hello, and enjoy an afternoon together.
Between the tacos, face painting, and the Nevada Memorial fire truck, there was something for everyone.
Most importantly, it was a wonderful opportunity to connect with neighbors and strengthen our community.
I can't wait to do it again!
May is Mental Health Awareness Month, and much of my work this month focused on improving mental health care in Nevada.
I toured the Adelson Clinic, where they are doing remarkable work providing evidence-based treatment for people struggling with substance use disorders.
I also participated in a legislative panel discussing emerging treatments, including psychedelics for depression, PTSD, and opioid use disorder.
It's encouraging to see promising new therapies being explored for people who have often run out of other options.
The Health and Human Services Interim Committee devoted an entire meeting to mental health issues affecting children, seniors, veterans, first responders, and families throughout our state.
As a physician, I believe strongly that mental health IS health, and I'm encouraged by the progress we're making to expand access to care and reduce stigma.
May was also a time to celebrate some of our most important community members.
During National Police Week, we honored the men and women who put themselves in harm's way to keep our communities safe.
Their service and sacrifice deserve our gratitude every day.
We also celebrated Nurses Day.
Nurses are among the hardest-working professionals in healthcare, and Nevada desperately needs more of them.
Our state is currently short more than 2,500 nurses.
Recruiting, training, and retaining healthcare professionals must remain a priority if we're going to improve healthcare access for all Nevadans My work in the community continues year-round.
Many of you know that I regularly bring my healthcare students into the community to care for people with limited access to medical services.
This month we served individuals experiencing homelessness and other vulnerable populations at locations including The Courtyard, HELP of Southern Nevada, the East Las Vegas Community Center, and the Catholic Worker.
These experiences benefit everyone involved.
We provide care that can prevent unnecessary emergency room visits and hospitalizations, while helping future healthcare providers develop compassion, understanding, and a deeper connection to the communities they serve.
Workforce development remains one of the keys to improving healthcare in Nevada.
We simply need more doctors, nurses, and healthcare professionals.
One of the most meaningful events of the month was Memorial Day.
I attended the ceremony at the Veterans Memorial Cemetery in Boulder City alongside Governor Lombardo and many veteran leaders from across Nevada.
It was a powerful tribute to the men and women who gave everything in service to our country.
Closer to home, Southern Highlands held its 24th Annual Memorial Day celebration, where hundreds of neighbors came together to honor our fallen heroes through food, music, fellowship, and patriotism.
It reminded me once again how proud I am to represent this incredible community.
As we head into summer, I hope you'll stay connected and involved.
Our next Coffee with Constituents will be held on June 20 from 10:00 a.m. to noon at Aware Coffee on South Rainbow Boulevard.
I hope you'll stop by, grab a cup of coffee, and join the conversation about how we can continue working together to make Nevada even better.
