ASSEMBLY DISTRICT 35 APRIL NEWSLETTER March Recap: Exciting Times in Nevada Politics March is an exciting and pivotal month in Nevada politics.
It’s when all prospective candidates officially file to run for office.
Even though I went through this process two years ago, walking into the Clark County Government Center to file as a candidate again was just as inspiring.
It’s a moment that reminds me why I do this work: to serve and represent you.
Now that filing has closed, we know what the election landscape looks like, and I’m focused on running a strong, positive campaign for your continued trust and support.
Community Work: Continuing to Serve March was also busy on the community front.
I continued my outreach helping individuals experiencing homelessness and others who struggle to access essential care.
The work can be challenging, but it’s deeply rewarding to see real change in people’s lives.
Supporting our most vulnerable neighbors remains at the heart of everything I do as a community leader.
Legislative Highlights: Making Progress on Key Issues Two of my bills from the last legislative session have proven especially timely.
AB161 tackled the alarming growth of poorly run hospices in Clark County.
The goal was to educate patients and families on expectations and ensure quality care for patients at the most vulnerable times in their lives.
Recently, the federal government announced a major crackdown on hospice care fraud.
This move aligns closely with what we’ve been fighting for in Nevada.
To be clear, many hospices do incredible work.
Our task is to make sure all of them meet the high standards that families deserve.
AB315 targeted Medicaid provider fraud.
This is a serious issue costing Nevada millions of dollars that should instead be helping those most in need.
I’m continuing to collaborate with stakeholders and experts to protect taxpayer funds and ensure that every dollar goes toward genuine care and services.
As I’m sure you’ve heard in the news, there has been a light shined on Medicaid Fraud and I can only imagine Nevada will be improving exponentially on this.
