This November, the people of Long Neck, Oak Orchard, Angola, Lewes, Millsboro, The Peninsula, Peninsula Lakes, and every community in the Fourth District will choose who represents them in Dover.
My opponent and I both recognize what every family here sees every day: too much growth without enough roads, too much pressure on schools and medical care, rising household costs, and government decisions that too often leave working people paying the bill.
The difference is simple.
My opponent talks about “investment,” “smart growth,” “affordable housing,” and “protecting rights.” I believe voters deserve to know the exact plan, the cost, who pays, what a State Representative can actually do, and how we will measure results.
Here is what I will do as your State Representative.
Jump to an issue 01 Growth & Infrastructure 02 Lower Costs & Taxpayers 03 Reliable Energy 04 Public Safety 05 Schools & Parental Rights 06 Health Care 07 Farmland & Water 08 Homeowner Protections 09 Rights & Representation 10 Veterans 01 Growth Must Pay Its Own Way I will fight for an “Infrastructure Before Occupancy” standard for major developments in our fast-growing communities.
That means I will support legislation and budget conditions requiring large developments to identify, fund, and complete their proportional share of transportation, drainage, water, wastewater, emergency-service, and school capacity needs before residents move in, not years afterward, when taxpayers are already stuck in traffic and stuck with the bill.
I will work with DelDOT, DNREC, Sussex County, local fire companies, school districts, and community representatives to establish a District 4 infrastructure priority list that is public, updated annually, and tied to deadlines.
Developers should pay for the road, signal, drainage, utility, and public-safety impacts their projects create.
State transportation funding must be directed first to the congestion and safety problems residents already live with.
No developer should receive special treatment while existing residents wait years for a safe intersection, reliable drainage, or emergency-response capacity.
Sussex County makes land-use decisions, but Dover controls transportation dollars, environmental permitting, state infrastructure, and much of the regulatory framework.
I will use that authority to make growth accountable.
Growth is not the enemy.
Unplanned growth that shifts costs onto families, seniors, and small businesses is.
02 Lower Costs & Protect Taxpayers Delaware families do not need more fees, more hidden costs, or another excuse for government to spend beyond its means.
I will oppose increases in the gas tax, broad new taxes, and unnecessary state fees that make it harder to live, work, operate a small business, or retire in Delaware.
Before supporting any new spending, I will ask three questions: What does it cost?
Who pays?
What measurable result will the public receive?
A line-by-line review of state programs that fail to produce results, with savings redirected to priorities such as roads, public safety, classrooms, and health care access.
Opposition to utility policies that force families and small businesses to subsidize costly mandates or large corporate power users.
Support for transparent utility planning so ratepayers can see why bills are rising and who is responsible.
A requirement that large new electricity users, including data centers, pay their fair share of the generation, transmission, and grid upgrades they require instead of shifting those costs to ordinary Delawareans.
A small-business review of proposed state regulations and fees before they take effect, so Dover stops treating local employers as an endless source of revenue.
Affordable living begins with a government that respects the people who pay for it.
03 Reliable, Affordable Energy I support an all-of-the-above energy policy that keeps the lights on, protects reliability, and keeps electricity affordable.
Delaware cannot build its future on political mandates that raise bills and make the grid less dependable.
We need energy policy based on reliability, affordability, and real capacity, not slogans.
Oppose energy mandates that raise electric bills without proving they improve reliability or reduce costs.
Require transparent, independent reviews before energy-intensive projects are approved.
Fight to ensure large commercial users pay for the grid improvements they cause.
Support a balanced energy mix that protects reliable generation while allowing innovation that can stand on its merits.
Demand public accountability from state agencies and utilities when rate increases, reliability concerns, or infrastructure failures affect Delaware residents.
Families should not have to choose between paying an electric bill and buying groceries.
Energy policy must protect ratepayers first.
04 Safer Communities & Stronger First Responders Public safety is not optional.
It is the first responsibility of government.
As a Navy veteran and former police officer, I understand that laws only work when they are enforced, victims are respected, and the men and women who answer the call have the training, equipment, and support they need.
Oppose policies that weaken accountability for repeat violent offenders.
Support law enforcement with the staffing, training, equipment, and legal tools needed to protect law-abiding residents.
Work to increase state support for volunteer fire companies, EMTs, paramedics, and emergency medical services facing higher call volumes from rapid growth.
Push for state funding formulas that recognize the real population growth and service demands in District 4.
Support treatment and recovery programs that help people overcome addiction, while holding drug traffickers and repeat offenders accountable.
Public safety means protecting families, supporting first responders, and refusing to let Dover become disconnected from the consequences of its criminal justice policies.
05 Strong Schools & Parental Rights Every child deserves a safe, disciplined classroom, a strong academic foundation, and the opportunity to succeed.
Parents deserve a meaningful voice in their children's education.
I will support school funding reform that directs more resources into classrooms while demanding transparency and measurable results.
Reform cannot mean simply sending more money into a system without accountability.
Protect parents' right to know what their children are being taught and to be involved in important decisions affecting their education, health, and safety.
Prioritize reading, math, science, career and technical education, and workforce preparation.
Support teachers and classroom learning while reducing bureaucracy that pulls resources away from students.
Require greater transparency in school spending and clearer reporting on academic outcomes.
Support education options that give families access to the learning environment that best meets their child's needs.
Oppose policies that place ideology ahead of parents, student privacy, fairness, or academic success.
We should judge education policy by one standard: is it helping children learn, achieve, and prepare for a successful life?
06 Health Care People Can Afford District 4's growth has made it harder for many residents to find timely primary care, mental health care, specialists, and emergency services close to home.
I will pursue a patient-centered health care plan focused on access, affordability, and accountability.
Expanding incentives to recruit and retain doctors, nurses, mental health professionals, and emergency medical providers in Sussex County.
Supporting practical reforms that reduce administrative burdens on providers and make it easier for qualified professionals to practice in Delaware.
Requiring greater price transparency so patients can understand costs before care whenever possible.
Supporting local mental health and addiction treatment access, especially for veterans, first responders, seniors, and young people.
Protecting patients, families, and medical professionals from government policies that undermine conscience rights or informed consent.
Health care should be easier to access, easier to understand, and less expensive for Delaware families.
07 Preserve Farmland, Water & Rural Character The Fourth District is growing, but we must not lose the land, water, open space, farms, and local character that brought so many people here.
I support conservation that works with property owners, farmers, hunters, anglers, and local communities, not against them.
Expand voluntary farmland preservation incentives so more family farms can remain farms for the next generation.
Support right-to-repair protections so farmers can maintain and repair the equipment they depend on.
Protect private property rights while ensuring new development does not damage nearby wells, waterways, drainage systems, or established rural communities.
Demand stronger oversight of water quality and well-water concerns, with public reporting and coordination between DNREC, Sussex County, and affected communities.
Protect wetlands, woods, and open space through practical conservation tools and responsible development standards.
Oppose one-size-fits-all environmental mandates that punish farmers, homeowners, and small businesses without producing measurable environmental results.
We can protect our natural resources and preserve private property.
We do not have to choose one over the other.
08 Protect Homeowners From Abandoned Obligations Homeownership should provide security, not leave families trapped with bills for roads, drainage, sidewalks, stormwater systems, community amenities, or infrastructure a developer promised but never completed.
I will work hand in hand with homeowner advocates, including organizations such as SHARP, homeowners associations, manufactured-home residents, local community leaders, and state agencies to protect residents from being left with unfinished work and unexpected costs after a developer walks away or transfers control.
Require developers of major planned communities to post adequate, enforceable performance bonds before construction begins, sufficient to complete roads, stormwater systems, drainage, sidewalks, utilities, common areas, and other promised infrastructure if the developer defaults or leaves the project unfinished.
Require a public, itemized developer obligation checklist for each approved development, so homebuyers and HOA members can see exactly what was promised, what has been completed, what remains unfinished, who is responsible, and the deadline for completion.
Require an independent financial and infrastructure review before a developer turns control of an HOA over to homeowners, so residents don't inherit incomplete projects, unpaid bills, inadequate reserves, or a budget designed to fail.
Give homeowners and homeowner associations a clearer path to request state intervention when a developer abandons required obligations, violates approval conditions, or leaves infrastructure that threatens property values, safety, drainage, or access.
Support stronger enforcement when developers or community owners repeatedly fail to maintain legally required water, sewer, septic, gas, or electric service, and ensure Delaware's existing surety-bond protections are enforced promptly and expanded where gaps remain.
Require meaningful disclosure before a home is sold in a developer-controlled community, including pending HOA fee increases, unfinished infrastructure, reserve funding, developer-controlled contracts, expected future costs, and any unresolved violations or obligations.
Protect manufactured-home owners from unfair treatment and negligent infrastructure by advocating for adequate staffing and follow-through at Delaware's Office of the Manufactured Housing Ombudsperson, so residents can get answers without having to hire an attorney first.
Support homeowner associations and resident groups that seek to hold developers accountable, while preserving the right of homeowners to organize, speak out, and obtain records about their community.
Developers must be responsible for the promises they make.
They should not be allowed to collect the profits, leave the community, and force homeowners to pay for the unfinished work.
09 Rights, Responsibility & Representation I took an oath to support and defend the Constitution, and I take that oath seriously.
I will defend the Second Amendment rights of law-abiding Delawareans.
I will protect parental rights.
I will stand for the dignity of human life, oppose assisted suicide, and support compassionate care for vulnerable patients and families.
I will oppose policies that erode fairness, privacy, and common sense in the name of politics.
A representative's job is not to disappear into Dover, vote with a party, and return only at election time.
The job is to listen, explain, fight for the district, and be accountable for results.
As your elected State Representative, I will hold regular district office hours, publish a clear constituent service contact process, provide updates on legislation and budget decisions, and report publicly on the projects and priorities I am pursuing for District 4.
10 Veterans Deserve Care Close to Home Too many Sussex County veterans still face long travel, long waits, confusing systems, and gaps in access to specialty care, mental health care, housing assistance, and transportation.
The Sussex County VA Clinic in Georgetown provides outpatient services, but it is not a full-scale medical center, and many veterans still must travel to Wilmington for certain services.
As a Navy veteran, I will make veterans' access to care and benefits a District 4 priority.
Establish regular District 4 Veterans Service Days in partnership with the Delaware Office of Veterans Services, the Sussex County VA Clinic, veterans organizations, and trained service officers so veterans and their families can get direct help filing claims, appealing decisions, accessing health care, finding transportation, and connecting with housing or employment resources.
Fight for expanded specialty care access in Sussex County, including mental health care, PTSD treatment, traumatic brain injury support, women veterans' services, and tele-health options that reduce unnecessary travel to Wilmington.
Seek stronger transportation support for veterans who cannot reliably get to medical appointments, and make sure eligible Sussex County veterans know about, and can actually use, existing VA transportation and beneficiary travel assistance.
Support a statewide veteran care coordination system that connects the VA, the State Office of Veterans Services, local nonprofits, county resources, and first responders so no veteran falls through the cracks.
Make veteran homelessness prevention a priority by ensuring local veterans are connected quickly to emergency shelter, transitional housing, benefits assistance, addiction treatment, mental health care, and job support.
Require regular public reporting to the General Assembly on Sussex County veterans' access to appointments, specialty services, transportation, benefits assistance, and housing support, so Dover can no longer ignore unmet needs south of the canal.
Veterans fulfilled their obligation when they served this country.
Government must fulfill its obligation when they come home.

I am running to provide that leadership.
