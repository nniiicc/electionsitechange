Answer to the 8th District Not to Hartford.
Every vote I cast will be measured against one question: does this help our towns?
Know What Things Cost As a small business owner, I feel every new fee, tax, and mandate.
I will never forget what they cost you.
Freedom Over Mandates I trust families and small businesses to make their own decisions more than I trust Hartford to make them for us.
Priority Property Taxes Here's how the shell game works.
Hartford passes a mandate and takes credit for it.
Then it hands the cost to your town.
Your town has no choice: it becomes an unwilling tax collector for the state, and the bill lands on your property taxes.
Your first selectman didn't create that cost, but they take the blame at budget time — while the legislators who caused it are back in Hartford holding a press conference.
I'll fight for a simple standard: no unfunded mandates.
If Hartford requires it, Hartford funds it — on the record, with a vote, with names attached.
Priority Electricity Prices Connecticut families pay some of the highest electric rates in the country - and a big piece of your bill, the Public Benefits Charge, isn't about delivering electricity at all.
Hartford created programs it didn't want to fund through the state budget, where spending gets debated in the open.
So it buried the cost in your electric bill and made the utilities collect it.
Eversource and UI become tax collectors for the state — and the state never puts its name on the tax.
I'm not attacking the programs; some help people who need it.
I'm attacking the trick.
If a program is worth funding, it's worth funding honestly - in the budget, with a vote, with names attached - not hidden in the bill you pay to keep the lights on.
Priority The Housing Market A generation ago, a young person in eastern Connecticut could finish school, start a career, buy a house, and raise a family - right here.
Ask yourself why that stopped.
It's not that people don't want that life; it's that Hartford has priced them out of it.
Housing costs, energy costs, taxes on every step of getting started - it adds up to a state where young families do the math and leave.
Every one of us, or our parents, or our grandparents, came to Connecticut because it had a reputation: a place you could work, raise a family, and get ahead.
We can have that reputation again.
I'll fight for a Connecticut where staying is the smart choice - where housing gets built, costs come down, and the starting line of an adult life is back within reach.
Priority Parental Rights Parents are the first and most important teachers their children will ever have - and no bureaucrat in Hartford knows a child better than their own mother or father.
I believe decisions about a child's education, health, and upbringing belong in the home, not in a government office.
That means standing up for curriculum transparency, so parents can see exactly what is being taught in their child's classroom.
It means keeping families in the loop on what happens at school, every step of the way.
And it means defending the right of parents to raise their children according to their own values.
I'll fight for a Connecticut where parental rights are respected - not overridden.
Priority Law Enforcement The men and women who put on the uniform every day are the reason our towns are safe places to live and raise a family.
They run toward danger when the rest of us run away from it.
But in recent years, Hartford has made their job harder - stripping away the tools they need and treating them like the problem instead of the solution.
I'll stand with our law enforcement officers.
That means backing policies that let them do their jobs safely and effectively, fighting efforts to defund or demean them, and making sure they have the resources and respect they deserve.
A safe community is the foundation of everything else we want to build - and it starts with supporting the people who protect it.
Priority Small Business I'm not a career politician.
I'm a small business owner - and I've felt what it's like to sign the front of a paycheck, meet a payroll, and watch every new fee, tax, and mandate eat into the bottom line.
Small businesses are the backbone of our towns and the source of most of our jobs.
But Hartford treats them like a piggy bank, piling on costs and regulations until too many owners decide it's not worth it anymore.
I'll fight to cut red tape, lower the cost of doing business, and make Connecticut a place where entrepreneurs can start, grow, and thrive.
When our small businesses succeed, our whole district succeeds.
