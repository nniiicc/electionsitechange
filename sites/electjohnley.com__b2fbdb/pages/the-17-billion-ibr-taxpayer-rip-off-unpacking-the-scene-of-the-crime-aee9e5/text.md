Home News Latest News 60 Seconds With John Ley About Me Donate Issues Statement The $17 Billion IBR Taxpayer Rip-Off: Unpacking the Scene of the Crime October 12, 2026 by John Ley No value in current bridge proposal for taxpayers By Representative John Ley Standing outside the office of the I-5 Interstate Bridge Replacement (IBR) Program, you are at the scene of an ongoing financial crime: the systematic waste and theft of your hard-earned taxpayer dollars.
Regional transportation planners are currently proposing an astronomical $14 billion to $18 billion mega-project.
Yet, despite this eye-watering price tag, their plan replaces an over-congested three-lane bridge with another three-lane bridge.
That in and of itself ought to be a crime.
For over two decades, the number one priority requested by Southwest Washington residents has been simple: relieve traffic congestion and save commute time .
What planners are actually delivering will accomplish the exact opposite.
Doubling Your Commute Time by 2045 The IBR project’s own official disclosures reveal a shocking truth: after spending up to $18 billion of your money, your drive into Portland will take longer .
Today, driving from Salmon Creek to I-405 in North Portland takes approximately # minutes in average traffic.
Under the IBR’s proposed design, that same commute is projected to balloon to # minutes or more by 2045 .
To put this expenditure in perspective, consider Washington State’s two recent major transportation packages: Connecting Washington: $17 Billion Move Ahead Washington: $15 Billion Either one of those statewide packages bought and paid for hundreds of critical transportation projects across the entire state.
The IBR program wants to spend more than those entire statewide packages combined on a single project that leaves Clark County drivers trapped in worse traffic than today.
Guaranteed 32% Consultant Profit Margins The waste doesn’t stop at poor bridge design; it extends directly into how state contracts are being written.
The Washington State Department of Transportation (WSDOT) signed a contract with the primary general engineering consultant for the IBR program that guarantees them a 32% profit margin .
Very few private businesses earn a guaranteed 32% profit for consulting work—let alone work funded entirely by public tax dollars.
While consultants lock in guaranteed multi-million-dollar payouts, taxpayers are being set up for forced tolling schemes and light-rail additions that expand costs without adding general-purpose traffic capacity.
The Common-Sense Alternative: A $2 Billion Express Bridge We do not need an multi-billion-dollar boondoggle crammed with light-rail mandates to fix the Columbia River crossing.
By removing unneeded, bloated project components, the state could construct a practical, simple express bridge over the Columbia River for roughly $2 billion —a figure derived directly from the IBR program’s own baseline estimates.
A common-sense express bridge would deliver true congestion relief at a fraction of the cost, saving billions of taxpayer dollars while getting drivers home to their families faster.
Standing Up for Southwest Washington Taxpayers This isn’t just bad planning—it’s picking the pockets of Southwest Washington families who are already struggling with high gas taxes, inflation, and a rising cost of living.
Southwest Washington deserves transparent governance, fiscal responsibility, and infrastructure that actually solves our transportation needs.
