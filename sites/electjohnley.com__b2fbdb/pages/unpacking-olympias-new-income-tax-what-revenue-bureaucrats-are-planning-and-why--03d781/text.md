Home News Latest News 60 Seconds With John Ley About Me Donate Issues Unpacking Olympia’s New Income Tax: What Revenue Bureaucrats Are Planning and Why Voters Must Act October 11, 2026 by John Ley The democrat majority is coming for your wallet By Representative John Ley Standing outside the State Department of Revenue in Washington, it’s easy to miss the quiet bureaucrat-driven expansion taking place behind closed doors.
But make no mistake: while everyday residents are stretching their household budgets to cover rising inflation, housing costs, and basic living expenses, state administrators are busy laying the groundwork to implement Washington’s newly established income tax framework.
Olympia Democrats didn’t just pass a sweeping change to our state’s tax structure—they funded an army of bureaucrats to enforce it.
A closer examination of the ongoing rulemaking reveals troubling details about what this tax expansion will cost Washington families in both dollars and personal privacy.
300 New Bureaucrats and the Race to Draft Enforcing Rules When a major new tax becomes the law of the land, the real work of extraction falls on state regulatory agencies.
To operationalize the new state income tax, state leadership approved funding to hire 300 brand-new government bureaucrats specifically dedicated to creating the enforcement rules and administrative processes.
These bureaucrats are currently tasked with answering critical operational questions: What financial records will citizens be forced to disclose?
How will compliance be audited and tracked?
How far can state authority reach into private financial reporting?
Rulemaking turns vague legislative mandates into rigid state enforcement.
Behind the scenes, these hundreds of new hires are designing the exact mechanisms that will dig into the pockets of hard-working Washingtonians.
Deep Invasions of Personal Financial Privacy To levy and track an income tax, state government inevitably requires unprecedented access to your private financial life.
Among the administrative rules currently under consideration, the Department of Revenue is evaluating aggressive disclosure demands, including: Mandatory Submission of Federal Tax Returns: State administrators are actively exploring whether Washingtonians should be forced to turn over copies of their entire federal tax return (IRS Form 1040 along with all supporting schedules).
Detailed Household Financial Tracking: Because the legislation introduces a distinct marriage penalty, rulemakers are scrutinizing overall household income definitions—requiring multi-earner families to expose sensitive financial data simply to determine their filing obligations.
For decades, Washington residents have enjoyed a high standard of personal financial privacy from state-level tax authorities.
Under these proposed compliance rules, that privacy is rapidly eroding.
The Fine Print: Retirement Income, Pensions, and Social Security Exposed Supporters of the tax measure frequently attempt to downplay its scope, but a close reading of the underlying statutory changes reveals a far broader target.
In passing this legislation, lawmakers modified 11 separate sections of state law.
These deliberate statutory revisions systematically opened the door to tax revenue streams that hit retirees directly: Retirement Portfolios & Investments: Traditional retirement savings vehicles and investment gains that seniors rely on for fixed-income living.
Pensions: Earned public and private pension distributions earned through decades of hard work.
Social Security Benefits: Income streams intended to provide basic financial security for elderly residents.
By altering these 11 statutory sections, the state position is clear: no pool of capital—even the hard-earned retirement nest eggs of elderly residents—is off-limits.
You Have the Final Say This Fall The administrative machine in Olympia is moving rapidly, but the ultimate authority remains with the people of Washington State.
This fall, voters across our state have a direct opportunity to stop this overreach, halt the administrative intrusion into private tax filings, and eliminate the new tax altogether.
