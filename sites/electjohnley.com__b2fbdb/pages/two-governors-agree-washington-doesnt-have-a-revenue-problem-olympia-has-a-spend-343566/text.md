Home News Latest News 60 Seconds With John Ley About Me Donate Issues Two Governors Agree: Washington Doesn’t Have a Revenue Problem—Olympia Has a Spending Addiction October 9, 2026 by John Ley State spending grew at 3 times the rate of personal income When a state budget grows three times faster than average family paychecks, working households inevitably get squeezed.
Across Washington State, families are cutting back, balancing household budgets, and prioritizing essential needs over extra spending.
Unfortunately, state leaders in Olympia have taken the exact opposite approach.
Even former Washington governors have warned that the state’s fiscal trajectory is fundamentally unsustainable.
Bipartisan agreement — Washington does not have a revenue problem; Olympia has a spending addiction. $17 Billion in New Taxes: Squeezed at Every Turn Over the last two legislative sessions alone, Olympia Democrats increased taxes on Washington citizens and businesses by approximately $17 billion .
This massive influx of revenue hasn’t brought lower costs or simpler government.
Instead, it has driven up the cost of living statewide—making everyday essentials like gas, housing, energy, and groceries increasingly unaffordable for families across the 18th Legislative District.
Despite mounting pressure on working families, the current administration refused to veto these tax increases or cut state spending in any meaningful way to offer tax relief.
Fighting on the House Floor: 84 Amendments, 24.5 Hours Fiscal discipline isn’t built on rhetoric; it’s proven through action.
During the legislative session, Representative John Ley and House Republicans stood up for taxpayers in an exhaustive floor fight: 24.5 Hours of Debate: House Republicans spent over a full day and night on the House floor fighting against runaway state spending and new tax burdens.
80 Common-Sense Amendments Offered: Proposals were put forward to lower tax bills, reduce regressive taxes, and cut unnecessary administrative spending.
Protecting the No-Income-Tax Tradition: Representative Ley voted firmly against state income tax proposals, offering key amendments designed to prevent tax creep from harming working families and job creators.
Regrettably, the majority party in Olympia refused to accept these amendments, choosing instead to push forward with unchecked state budget growth.
The Path to Fiscal Sanity in Washington Restoring affordability to Washington requires structural budget discipline, accountability, and a firm commitment to stopping tax hikes: Cap Budget Growth: Mandate that state budget increases never outpace the median growth of family paychecks.
Stop Income Tax Expansion: Defend Washington’s traditional no-personal-income-tax status and block progressive tax schemes.
Prioritize Essential Services: Demand that Olympia spend existing revenue more wisely before asking taxpayers for another dime.
