John Ley Raises New Concerns Over $321 Million in IBR Spending September 4, 2026 by John Ley Rep.
John Ley joined Lars Larson on The Lars Larson Show on Sept.
2 to discuss new concerns over the Interstate Bridge Replacement Program’s spending, design progress and rising cost.
During the interview, Ley said the IBR had spent $321 million as of the end of June.
He noted that the project is only 30% designed after six and a half years.
Ley also pointed to the earlier Columbia River Crossing effort, saying the current spending comes on top of money already spent on the failed CRC.
He said that, taken together, more than half a billion dollars in public money has been spent while the region still does not have a new bridge.
The project’s rising price tag was another major focus of the interview.
Ley said the cost has tripled to more than $15 billion since the program began.
At the same time, he said the project only has about $4 billion to $5 billion available.
Ley argued that lawmakers in both Washington and Oregon need to take a much harder look at the project’s components and funding.
He said the IBR has a list of 30 components, but only four are funded.
Rather than continuing with an oversized project, Ley said legislators should cut unnecessary pieces and focus on a basic, affordable bridge that can be built within the money available.
Ley also repeated several concerns about the current proposal, including that it does not fix traffic congestion, includes a bridge he says is too low, and allocates too much space to transit, bikes and pedestrians.
The interview also covered oversight concerns.
Ley criticized the project’s consulting costs, saying the original contract called for delivery of the federal record of decision for $44 million, but the project has now spent $321 million.
He said the increase shows a lack of accountability and oversight from transportation officials in both states.
For Ley, the bottom line is that transportation dollars are limited, and Washington and Oregon have many other transportation needs.
He said lawmakers should reduce the IBR to the essentials and stop allowing the project to grow beyond what taxpayers can afford.
