The names have been changed to protect the innocent.
But the waste?
That part is entirely real, and it is coming straight out of your pocketbook.
Behind me stands the I-5 Interstate Bridge.
For decades, it has served as a vital artery connecting Southwest Washington and Oregon.
But the current proposal by the Interstate Bridge Replacement (IBR) program is steering us straight toward a fiscal disaster.
We are looking at a project projected to cost an astronomical $14 billion to $15 billion .
And what are taxpayers getting for that staggering price tag?
A plan to replace an over-congested three-lane bridge with… another three-lane bridge.
That is the definition of insanity.
A Lower Bridge and Million-Dollar “Buy-Offs” The problems start with the physical design itself.
The existing bridge provides 178 feet of clear headroom for maritime traffic.
The IBR’s proposed design drops that clearance down to 116 feet .
Because that lower clearance restricts large vessels, the project has baked in a deal to pay $140-plus million to four upriver firms just to buy them off—and essentially buy their approval for a bridge that is built too low.
Meanwhile, look at the track record of spending so far: they’ve burned through about $280 million of your tax dollars , and we still don’t have a final design.
That follows right on the heels of the $200 million wasted on the defunct Columbia River Crossing project.
Where Is the Money Actually Going?
Thanks to a public records request uncovered by a Portland economist at the City Observatory, we have a clear look at the laundry list of unneeded, bloated add-ons stuffed into this project that have nothing to do with a simple replacement bridge.
(These were Cortright data costs, as the IBR has moved money around even more). $320 million for a Ruby Junction maintenance facility for TriMet (roughly 20 miles away from the corridor). $356 million for light rail track systems. $296 million to remove the current two bridges, plus another $56 million removing the North Portland Harbor Bridge. $188 million for new light rail vehicles. $132 million for a Hayden Island guideway. $104 million for a waterfront park-and-ride facility. $96 million for a separate North Harbor Transit bridge connecting North Portland with Hayden Island. $87 million for an Evergreen park-and-ride at the end of the light rail line.
This is supposed to be about a five-mile bridge influence corridor.
Instead, it has morphed into a massive regional transit wish list funded by drivers who just want a functioning highway.
The Common-Sense Alternative: A $2 Billion Solution We don’t need a $15 billion boondoggle.
If we built a simple, streamlined replacement bridge, it could be done for roughly $2 billion , delivering massive value and keeping funds free for transportation projects across the rest of Washington state.
Even better, we could repurpose the existing two bridges : Restripe them down to two lanes each.
Slow local traffic down to 45 mph.
Create a dedicated local connection for Vancouver and Hayden Island residents to shop, commute, and enjoy the waterfront without clogging up the main freeway.
To put this in perspective, look at Kentucky and Cincinnati, Ohio.
They are spending about $4 billion to build a brand-new 10-lane bridge over the Ohio River—with no tolls and no heavy transit bloat .
Delivering Value, Stopping the Crime Taxpayers in Southwest Washington deserve fiscal sanity, transparency, and real solutions.
Accepting a crippled three-lane design for nearly fifteen billion dollars is a crime against taxpayers.
I’m Representative John Ley, and I’m fighting to deliver real value for your transportation dollars, stop the runaway spending, and shut down this boondoggle.
