From helping businesses launch and grow to supporting organizations that serve Delaware families, I have seen firsthand the innovation, resilience, and determination that exist throughout our state.
I have also witnessed the challenges that too many people face when trying to access opportunities that should be within reach.
Those experiences have shaped my belief that Delaware's future depends on building an economy that works for everyone.
An inclusive economy is not about choosing one community over another.
It is about recognizing that when more people have access to opportunity, our entire state benefits.
Strong economies are built when entrepreneurs can start businesses, workers can access quality jobs, families can achieve financial stability, and communities have the resources they need to thrive.
The question is not whether Delaware can achieve this vision.
The question is whether we are willing to make the investments and policy decisions necessary to get there.
Throughout my work supporting businesses across Delaware, I have met entrepreneurs with innovative ideas who struggled to access capital.
I have met small business owners eager to expand but overwhelmed by regulatory barriers.
I have spoken with workers seeking career advancement opportunities and employers searching for skilled talent.
I have met families doing everything right yet still struggling to keep up with the rising cost of living.
These are not isolated challenges.
They are interconnected issues that require thoughtful, practical policy solutions.
I believe Delaware must continue to be one of the best places in the nation to start, grow, and sustain a business.
Small businesses are the backbone of our economy.
They create jobs, support local communities, and drive innovation.
Yet too often, entrepreneurs face obstacles that make success more difficult than it should be.
That is why I support policies that expand access to capital, strengthen technical assistance programs, reduce unnecessary barriers to business growth, and create pathways for entrepreneurs from all backgrounds to participate in our economy.
Economic development should not be measured solely by the number of large projects we attract.
It should also be measured by the number of small businesses that succeed, the number of local jobs that are created, and the number of families that achieve economic stability.
We must also recognize that workforce development and economic growth go hand in hand.
Businesses cannot thrive without a skilled workforce, and workers cannot succeed without access to education, training, and career pathways.
Delaware must continue investing in workforce development programs that connect people to high-demand industries, support lifelong learning, and prepare the next generation for the jobs of the future.
At the same time, we must address the barriers that prevent too many Delawareans from fully participating in our economy.
Access to affordable housing, reliable transportation, quality childcare, healthcare, and broadband connectivity are not separate issues from economic development.
They are economic development issues.
When families cannot access these essentials, businesses struggle to attract talent, workers face obstacles to employment, and communities are left behind.
Building an inclusive economy means understanding that prosperity must be shared.
It means creating opportunities in every community, not just a select few.
It means ensuring that urban, suburban, and rural communities all have access to investment and growth.
It means supporting minority-owned businesses, women-owned businesses, veteran-owned businesses, and entrepreneurs who have historically faced barriers to success - not as a matter of charity, but because Delaware benefits when all talent has the opportunity to flourish.
The strongest economies are those that harness the full potential of their people.
This philosophy also applies to how we approach public policy.
Policy should be informed by the people who live with its consequences every day.
Business owners, educators, healthcare providers, nonprofit leaders, workers, and families all have valuable perspectives that should help shape decisions.
Effective leadership begins with listening and continues with collaboration.
The future of Delaware will not be determined by government alone.
It will be shaped by partnerships between the public, private, and nonprofit sectors.
It will be built by entrepreneurs willing to take risks, workers striving to build better futures, community organizations addressing local challenges, and leaders committed to finding solutions rather than assigning blame.
I remain optimistic because I have seen what Delaware is capable of achieving when we work together.
I have seen small businesses transform neighborhoods.
I have seen nonprofit organizations create life-changing opportunities.
I have seen communities come together to solve difficult problems.
And I have seen people from different backgrounds unite around a shared belief that Delaware's best days are ahead.
Our challenge now is to ensure that our policies match that vision.
We need policies that encourage entrepreneurship, support small business growth, strengthen workforce development, invest in families, and expand economic opportunity across every community in our state.
Most importantly, we need policies that recognize a simple truth: when more Delawareans succeed, Delaware succeeds.
Building an economy that works for everyone is not simply an economic goal.
It is a commitment to creating a stronger, more prosperous, and more inclusive future for our state.
That is the Delaware I believe in, and it is the Delaware we should continue building together.
