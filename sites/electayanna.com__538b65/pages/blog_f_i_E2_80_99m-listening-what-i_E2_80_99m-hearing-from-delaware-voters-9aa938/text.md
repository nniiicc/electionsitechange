Running for public office should never be a one-way conversation.
Over the past several weeks, our campaign has been reaching out to Delaware voters, introducing our vision for the future and, more importantly, asking people what matters to them.
Some have offered their enthusiastic support.
Some are still deciding.
Others have asked difficult, thoughtful questions about where I stand and what I would actually do if given the opportunity to serve.
I welcome all of it.
Because if I am asking for your vote, you deserve more than campaign slogans.
You deserve to know what I believe, what I will fight for, and how I intend to turn priorities into action.
Education: What Does “Investing in Education” Actually Mean?
One voter challenged me to go beyond simply saying that we need to “invest in education.” That is a fair challenge.
Investing in education cannot mean automatically spending more money without asking whether those dollars are reaching classrooms, teachers and students effectively.
We need strong teachers, safe schools, effective classroom resources, support for students with different learning needs, and greater accountability for how education dollars are spent.
Parents also deserve transparency and a meaningful voice in the decisions affecting their children.
My goal is not simply to spend more.
It is to make sure Delaware is spending wisely and producing better opportunities and better outcomes for our children.
Supporting Delawareans With Disabilities and Special Needs Families caring for children and adults with disabilities often find themselves navigating complicated systems involving education, healthcare, transportation, employment and social services.
Government should help families navigate those systems — not make their lives more difficult.
We should be working toward stronger access to services, greater opportunities for independence, inclusive education and employment, and meaningful support for caregivers and families.
Every Delawarean deserves the opportunity to live with dignity, security and purpose.
Environmental Health and Environmental Justice A community cannot truly be safe and thriving if families are disproportionately exposed to pollution, contaminated water, poor air quality or other environmental hazards simply because of where they live.
Environmental decisions must consider the people who live closest to industrial facilities, major transportation corridors and other potential sources of pollution.
Economic development matters — but so does protecting the health and quality of life of the people who already call these communities' home.
The Rising Cost of Energy One voter put the issue very plainly: household energy bills have become too expensive.
Delaware lawmakers should be asking hard questions about affordability, rate structures, infrastructure costs and what can realistically be done to protect consumers while maintaining a reliable energy system.
Energy policy ultimately shows up at the kitchen table.
When electricity costs rise dramatically, families have less money for groceries, housing, transportation, medication and everything else they need.
Affordability has to be part of the conversation.
Data Centers and Delaware’s Future Data centers can bring investment, jobs and economic development.
But they can also create legitimate questions about energy consumption, water usage, infrastructure, land use, environmental impact and the effect large projects could have on utility customers and surrounding communities.
We should not approach major development projects with an automatic “yes” or an automatic “no.” We should ask: What does Delaware gain?
What are the long-term costs?
Who benefits?
Who carries the burden?
And what protections need to be in place for taxpayers, ratepayers and nearby communities?
Responsible economic development means answering those questions before decisions are made.
College Affordability and Opportunity How do we make education after high school attainable without saddling young people with overwhelming debt?
College is one pathway, but it isn’t the only one.
Delaware should support affordable higher education while also strengthening career and technical education, apprenticeships, skilled trades, workforce training and other pathways that allow young people to build successful careers.
We should be helping students find opportunities that match their talents and ambitions — and making sure they know what financial aid, scholarship and training resources are available to them.
Where I Stand — and Why Details Matter Some voters told me immediately that I have their support.
Others essentially said: “Convince me.” I respect both responses.
One voter said being a Democrat wasn’t enough of a reason to earn their vote.
I agree.
A political party should never substitute for accountability.
I am running because I believe representation means showing up, listening, explaining your positions and being willing to answer difficult questions.
You don’t have to agree with me on every issue to deserve my attention or respect.
This Conversation Will Continue People are concerned about education.
They’re worried about energy costs.
They want better support for people with disabilities.
They’re thinking about environmental health, development, college affordability and the direction of our state.
And some people simply want to know that the person asking for their vote is actually listening.
I am.
I don’t expect to earn your vote simply because I ask for it.
I intend to earn it by listening, showing up and demonstrating that your concerns will have a voice in Dover.
I am listening to the people of District 9 — not only to the voices that agree with me, but to the questions, concerns and challenges that make our community stronger.
That is the kind of representation I will bring to Dover.
