VOTE - November 3rd 2026 Early Voting Begins October 22, 2026 Home About Ayanna Volunteer Blog More Home About Ayanna Volunteer Blog Home About Ayanna Volunteer Blog My Blog All Posts Our Seniors Deserve Fairness, Stability, and a Voice September 28, 2026 | News While campaigning, I had the opportunity to attend three separate meet-and-greets hosted by residents in their homes and communities.
Each host invited neighbors and friends to meet with me, share their experiences, and talk about the issues that matter most to them.
All three hosts live in 55+ communities, and at each gathering, I heard a consistent concern: seniors want a fair and equitable approach to property tax relief.
Many seniors are living on fixed incomes.
They have spent decades contributing to the communities they call home.
They have raised families here, paid their taxes, volunteered their time, supported local businesses, and helped build the communities we know today.
Yet today, some seniors are facing changes to senior tax relief that they feel leave them behind.
One issue that deserves serious discussion is the requirement that seniors meet a 10-year residency threshold to qualify for a tax credit.
I've spoken with seniors who have lived here for three or four years who feel blindsided by this change.
They chose to make this state their home, put down roots, and contribute to their communities, only to discover that the rules surrounding tax relief had changed.
As our senior population continues to grow, we need to ask an important question: Are we doing enough to make sure seniors can afford to remain in the communities they helped build?
This conversation should not be about simply asking someone else to pay more.
It should be about taking a hard look at the entire state budget, identifying unnecessary or lower-priority spending, and making sure our resources reflect the needs of our communities.
Our seniors should not be viewed simply as another source of revenue.
They deserve policies that recognize their contributions and the financial realities of living on a fixed income.
I believe we can be fiscally responsible while also making sure our seniors are treated fairly.
The conversations I had at these meet-and-greets reinforced something important: our seniors want to be heard.
They want policymakers to understand the challenges they face and to recognize the contributions they have made to our communities.
That conversation needs to happen, and I am committed to bringing their voices to the table.
Our seniors deserve the opportunity to age with dignity, stability, and fairness in the communities they helped build.
