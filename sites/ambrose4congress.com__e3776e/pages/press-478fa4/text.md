Media Inquiries info@ambrose4congress.com September 16, 2026 Endorsement Largest Federal Employee Union Endorses Ben Ambrose for Congress AFGE endorsement adds to growing coalition of organized labor backing Ambrose in Michigan’s 2nd Congressional District.
Close Ben Ambrose for Congress today announced the endorsement of the American Federation of Government Employees (AFGE), the nation’s largest union representing federal and D.C. government employees.
The endorsement adds AFGE to a growing coalition of labor and working-family organizations backing Ambrose in Michigan’s 2nd Congressional District, including UAW Region 1-D, Teamsters Local 406, UFCW Local 951, and the Michigan Farmers Union.
“I am incredibly honored to earn the endorsement of AFGE and the federal workers they represent,” said Ben Ambrose.
“These are the people who care for our veterans, protect our borders and airports, support our military, administer Social Security, protect our air and water, and keep essential government services running every single day.
They deserve respect, fair pay, strong workplace protections, and a voice on the job.” For Ambrose, the fight to protect federal workers is also personal.
Before running for Congress, Ambrose worked as a federal contractor supporting the Department of Veterans Affairs.
His team developed technology to identify veterans whose benefits had been disrupted because of errors involving Social Security numbers in government records.
The team was working to correct those records and restore benefits when its contract was eliminated during DOGE cuts, despite thousands of records still requiring attention.
“That experience is one of the reasons I decided to run for Congress,” Ambrose said.
“I saw firsthand what happens when Washington makes arbitrary cuts without understanding the mission or the people depending on that work.
You don’t make government more efficient by blindly cutting the people who are actually making government work.” A nearly 20-year Marine Corps veteran, Ambrose has pledged to defend collective bargaining rights for federal employees, protect earned retirement benefits, support competitive federal pay, preserve a merit-based civil service, adequately staff federal agencies, and oppose efforts to replace career federal employees with contractors simply to eliminate federal positions or circumvent worker protections.
AFGE’s endorsement further strengthens Ambrose’s support among organized labor heading into the November general election.
“Whether you work on a factory floor, stock grocery shelves, drive a truck, work on a family farm, care for our veterans, or serve the American people as a federal employee, you deserve someone in Congress who respects your work and will fight for your ability to build a good life,” Ambrose said.
“Michigan’s working people built our communities and our middle class.
I’m proud that UAW Region 1-D, Teamsters Local 406, UFCW Local 951, the Michigan Farmers Union, and now AFGE are standing with this campaign.
In Congress, I’ll make sure working people always have a seat at the table.” Ambrose is the Democratic nominee for Congress in Michigan’s 2nd Congressional District.

Read full release HESPERIA, Mich. — Ben Ambrose for Congress today announced the endorsement of United Food and Commercial Workers (UFCW) Local 951, adding the support of more than 31,000 frontline workers to the growing coalition of organized labor backing Ambrose in Michigan’s 2nd Congressional District.
UFCW Local 951 represents workers across Michigan and cited Ambrose’s commitment to working-class families, workers’ rights, fair wages, and safe working environments in announcing its endorsement.
“Our executive board and membership are impressed by your unwavering commitment to working-class families,” UFCW Local 951 President Courtney Phillips wrote in the union’s endorsement letter.
“We believe your vision aligns with our mission to protect workers’ rights, secure fair wages, and ensure safe working environments for all.” The union said its more than 31,000 frontline members deserve a strong voice in government and expressed confidence that Ambrose would be “that champion.” “I am incredibly proud to earn the endorsement of UFCW Local 951 and the tens of thousands of working people they represent,” said Ben Ambrose.
“These are the people who stock our grocery stores, serve our communities, and show up every day to keep Michigan moving.
They work hard for their families, and they deserve someone in Congress who will work just as hard for them.” Ambrose has made strengthening Michigan’s working and middle class a central focus of his campaign, including fighting for higher wages, healthcare for all, strong labor protections, fair government contracting, prevailing wages, family leave, retirement security, and the right of workers to organize and collectively bargain.
“Working people shouldn’t have to fight just to afford groceries, healthcare, housing, and a secure retirement,” Ambrose continued.
“If you put in an honest day’s work, you should be able to build a good life for your family.
That shouldn’t be controversial.
It should be the foundation of our economy.” In its endorsement, UFCW Local 951 also encouraged Ambrose to continue supporting union members when shopping at union-represented employers including Meijer, Kroger, Polly’s, and Harding’s and thanked him for his “dedication to labor and community progress.” The UFCW endorsement represents another major show of support from organized labor for Ambrose as the campaign moves toward the November general election.
A nearly 20-year Marine Corps veteran who grew up on a small family farm in Hesperia, Ambrose has centered his campaign around a commitment to service and fighting for people who too often feel forgotten by Washington.
“Michigan’s middle class was built by working people, and organized labor helped make sure those workers received a fair share of what they helped create,” Ambrose said.
“I’m honored to have UFCW Local 951 standing with us, and when I get to Congress, Michigan’s working families will always have a seat at my table.” The endorsement was formally announced by UFCW Local 951 President Courtney Phillips on behalf of the union’s executive board and membership.

“We are confident you are the only candidate who will truly represent the hard-working citizens in the 2nd Congressional,” the letter states.
“I am incredibly honored to have the support of the working men and women of Teamsters Local 406,” said Ben Ambrose.

They drive the trucks, stock the warehouses, care for our patients, serve our communities, and do the work that keeps Michigan running.” “When workers stand together and demand a fair wage, good benefits, safe working conditions, and dignity on the job, I will stand with them,” Ambrose continued.

A nearly 20-year Marine Corps veteran who grew up on a small family farm in Hesperia, Ambrose has made fighting for working and middle-class families a centerpiece of his campaign.
He supports higher wages, prevailing wage protections, fair government contracting, healthcare for all, family leave policies, retirement security, and protecting workers’ right to organize and collectively bargain.

Wood, Principal Officer and Secretary-Treasurer, on behalf of the union’s active and retired members.

Read full release Hesperia, MI — Today, Ben Ambrose officially filed a Statement of Candidacy with the Federal Election Commission, announcing his intention to run for the U.S.
House of Representatives in Michigan's 2nd congressional district.
Ambrose, a West Michigan native, Marine Corps veteran, and lifelong public servant, says he is running to restore integrity, accountability, and trust in Congress.
“Washington has forgotten who it works for,” said Ambrose.
“I’m running because the people of West Michigan deserve a representative who will fight for working families, protect our rural communities, and always do the right thing — even when no one is watching.” Rooted in Rural Michigan Ambrose grew up on a small farm in Hesperia, Michigan, where he learned the value of hard work, keeping your word, and showing up for your neighbors.
From helping shovel driveways to volunteering at local fish fries, Ambrose says farm life shaped his understanding of community and responsibility.
With limited opportunities after high school, Ambrose enlisted in the United States Marine Corps at 18 years old, shipping out just six days after graduation.
Over the course of nearly 20 years, he served as an Infantryman with multiple combat tours and humanitarian missions.
“In the Marines, I learned that integrity isn’t optional,” Ambrose said.
“You stand by your word.
You stand by your fellow service members.
And you do what’s right — even when it’s not popular.” A Campaign Built on Integrity, Commitment, and Trust Ambrose says those same values will guide him in Congress.
He points to what he calls a growing lack of accountability in Washington, where too many elected officials promise to stand with working families but instead side with powerful special interests and wealthy donors.
“I will always act with integrity,” Ambrose said.
“That means putting the people of this district ahead of party politics and corporate campaign contributions.” Priorities for West Michigan If elected, Ambrose says his top priorities will include: Bringing down costs for working families Reforming the healthcare system so rural residents can access quality care without going broke Protecting veterans and ensuring they receive the benefits they earned Reducing burdens on small businesses while holding large corporations accountable Cutting taxes for middle-class families while ensuring billionaires and the wealthiest Americans pay their fair share Protecting pensions, retirements, farms, land, and family livelihoods Contrast in Leadership Ambrose is challenging incumbent Congressman John Moolenaar.
Ambrose says current leadership has failed to adequately address the economic challenges facing families in the district.
“West Michigan deserves a representative who understands what it’s like to live paycheck to paycheck, to worry about healthcare costs, and to fight for your community,” Ambrose said.
“My commitment is to the people of this district — not to Washington insiders.” Election Dates The primary election will be held on August 4, 2026, with the general election scheduled for November 3, 2026.
Ambrose says his campaign will focus on traveling across the district, meeting voters face-to-face, and earning their trust the same way he has throughout his life — through service and accountability.
