ABOUT BRANDI
My decision to run for State Representative wasn’t made overnight.
It was shaped by some of the most challenging, humbling, and meaningful moments of my life—as a mother, a healthcare provider, a caregiver, and an advocate.
Early in my third trimester with my son, my world changed in an instant.
A serious complication meant he had to be delivered 10 weeks early.
Instead of bringing home a healthy newborn, we spent nearly three months in the NICU.
As a neonatal nurse practitioner, I cared for countless families in similar situations.
Suddenly, I was one of them.
I was fortunate.
My employer and colleagues supported me and covered my shifts while I was away.
My family helped when my son finally came home.
Even so, I had to use my paid time off to continue receiving a paycheck while caring for him.
My later pregnancies were also high-risk and required extended bed rest, resulting in even more time away from work.
Those experiences changed me.
They also deepened my understanding of what so many Indiana families face every day.
Throughout my career as a neonatal nurse practitioner, I have sat beside parents facing impossible choices.
Should they use their leave while their baby is in the NICU?
Save it for when their baby finally comes home?
Try to stretch a few weeks of leave to cover months of uncertainty?
Too many parents return to work while their child is still hospitalized because they can’t afford not to.
Too many families are forced to choose between being present for their children and keeping the health insurance that pays for their care.
No parent should have to make that choice.
I have also experienced our healthcare system from another perspective—as a caregiver for someone I loved.
Toward the end of his life, my grandfather needed around-the-clock care.
Our family did everything we could to keep him at home.
We rearranged schedules, rotated responsibilities, and showed up for one another because we knew how important it was to him.
One day, my grandfather looked at his nurse and said, “My family takes good care of me.” We didn’t have much time with him after that.
But those words have stayed with me.
They reminded me that being present matters.
Being able to care for the people we love matters.
Knowing that someone feels safe, valued, and cared for during their most vulnerable moments matters.
Every family deserves that opportunity.
No one should have to choose between caring for a newborn, supporting a sick parent, being present during a medical crisis, or keeping a roof over their head.
We can build policies that support families through childbirth, adoption, illness, caregiving, and end-of-life care.
These are not unrealistic goals.
They are priorities—and they should be treated as such.
Indiana currently has a budget surplus of approximately $2.5 billion, yet too often state leaders choose to cut programs and resources rather than invest in the services families need.
Education is another reason I am running.
Public education helped shape my family.
My husband and grandparents were public school teachers.
My mother has dedicated more than 30 years to serving students in a variety of roles.
Like so many Hoosier families, my family’s story is deeply connected to our public schools.
I loved school.
I had teachers who sparked my curiosity, encouraged creativity, and helped me discover my strengths.
They gave me the freedom to explore different interests and imagine different possibilities for my future.
Too many students today are being asked to narrow those possibilities too soon.
As early as eighth grade, students are expected to make decisions that can shape the direction of their futures.
At the same time, local schools have lost decision-making authority, and educators have lost the flexibility they need to meet students where they are.
I believe our children deserve better.
I want our communities to thrive.
I want students to have the freedom to explore different subjects, careers, and opportunities.
I want healthcare to be accessible and affordable.
I want families to have the time and support they need to care for one another during life’s most important moments.
Most of all, I want an Indiana where people come first.
That is why I am running for State Representative.
I believe Indiana can do better.
I believe every family deserves the opportunity to succeed, to care for one another, and to build a brighter future.
If you share my vision of an Indiana that puts families first, supports public education, and ensures access to affordable healthcare, I would be honored to earn your vote.
I hope you’ll join me in making that vision a reality.
The path to better begins with the priorities we choose – together.

KEY ISSUES
HEALTHCARE
As a nurse, I can tell you that we do not have a nursing shortage—we have a shortage of nurses willing to work under the current conditions.
Too many healthcare systems prioritize cutting costs over providing the staffing and resources patients need to receive the highest quality care.
Our healthcare system should be designed around patients, not profits.
The goal should be to ensure that patients receive the care and attention they deserve, not the minimum level of care that can be delivered with the fewest staff.
Far too much of healthcare spending is tied up in insurance bureaucracy and administrative costs rather than direct patient care.
Patients and families deserve transparency.
Nurse-to-patient staffing ratios should be public information and readily available.
Research has shown that as nurses are assigned more patients, the risks of complications increase, including higher rates of mortality, hospital readmissions, and longer hospital stays.
Safe staffing is not just a workplace issue—it is a patient safety issue.
We must also address Indiana’s maternal health crisis.
Indiana continues to rank among the states with the highest maternal mortality rates in the nation, and too many communities have become maternity care “OB deserts” where expectant mothers have little or no access to prenatal care, labor and delivery services, or obstetric providers close to home.
No woman should have to travel hours for basic maternity care or face increased risks simply because of where she lives.
Improving access to maternal healthcare must be a priority.
Insurance companies also wield far too much influence over a patient’s access to care.
The insurance you pay for should not be allowed to delay, deny, or prevent medically necessary treatment.
Patients deserve timely, affordable, high-quality healthcare.
My perspective comes from both sides of the healthcare system.
I have cared for patients as a nurse, and I have experienced healthcare as a patient myself.
I understand the challenges families face, and I know how much work remains to be done.
I am committed to fighting for a healthcare system that puts patients first, supports healthcare workers, and delivers the quality care every person deserves.
I will work tirelessly to make that vision a reality.
EDUCATION
Public education is one of the most important investments we can make in our future.
Every child deserves access to a high-quality education, regardless of their zip code, family income, or individual learning needs.
Public schools serve all children, providing opportunities that help every student reach their potential.
Strong public schools benefit entire communities.
They prepare students for success, attract businesses, support economic growth, and strengthen the communities we call home.
Yet in 2026, the Network for Public Education gave Indiana state leaders an “F” for their support of public schools, ranking the state 45th in the nation.
The passage of SB 1 in 2026 is projected to reduce public school funding by approximately $744.4 million over three years.
The law places new limits on local funding that supports transportation, building maintenance, and daily operations.
It also restricts school districts from pursuing consecutive referendums to finance construction projects and limits when those referendums can appear on the ballot.
Decisions that have traditionally been made at the local level are increasingly being controlled in Indianapolis.
I will work to protect our public schools, support our teachers, and strengthen our communities.
Higher education should also be affordable and accessible for those who choose to pursue it.
Students should not be burdened with decades of debt simply to earn a degree and enter the workforce.
We must create pathways to education and career success that do not leave young people struggling under the weight of never-ending student loans.
FAMILIES
The well-being of Indiana’s children has declined since 2019, yet our state has failed to make the investments necessary to support working families.
Instead of expanding access to affordable, high-quality childcare, the legislature froze childcare voucher programs, creating additional challenges for parents who are already struggling to balance work and family responsibilities.
Rather than addressing the childcare shortage with meaningful solutions, state leaders chose to lower qualification standards for childcare providers and reduce certain safety requirements.
Indiana families deserve better.
Parents should have access to safe, affordable, and enriching environments where their children can learn, grow, and thrive.
Today’s families face increasing demands.
Many adults are not only raising children but also caring for aging parents and other loved ones.
These responsibilities often require time away from work, yet too many workers lack access to paid family and medical leave.
No one should have to choose between earning a paycheck and caring for a child, parent, or family member in need.
I will support policies that strengthen childcare options, expand support for working families, and provide the flexibility families need to care for their loved ones.
Families should never be forced to choose between meeting basic necessities and fulfilling their responsibilities as caregivers.
A stronger Indiana begins with supporting the families who make our communities strong.
