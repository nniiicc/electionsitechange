Naomi Riess The votes Work should pay, and you should keep the money you have earned.
Extra hours, a second job, a growing business: that's how families here get ahead.
Your extra hours, still taxed HB25-1296 · 2025 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = Higher Taxes Starting with 2026 taxes, Colorado adds qualifying overtime back into state taxable income, even though it is now deductible on federal returns.
Colorado passed this before the federal deduction became law.
Naomi's approach When people work extra hours, they should keep more of that paycheck.
Qualifying overtime should be deductible on state taxes too.
Source: HB25-1296 The tools of work cost more, resulting in more taken from your paychecks HB26-1223 · 2026 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = Higher Taxes From Jan.
1, 2027, Colorado charges state sales tax on most software: downloads, apps and cloud subscriptions.
Naomi's approach Small businesses, remote workers and students all run on software.
Keep it out of the sales tax and lower costs for everyone.
Source: HB26-1223 fiscal note Local restaurants pay more SB25-285 · 2025 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = Higher Taxes Raised annual inspection fees for restaurants and other retail food businesses, making the cost of a meal more and everyday life less affordable..
Naomi's approach People deserve to eat out once in awhile.
Food safety matters, and so do the margins of family-run places on Main Street.
Fund inspections efficiently without new costs on the businesses many local jobs depend on.
Source: SB25-285 Care close to home In a district this size, getting to care often means a long drive.
Medical rides: payment cut by two-thirds HB26-1410 · 2026-27 budget Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = Less Healthcare The 2026-27 state budget's Medicaid rate and service cuts totaled $270 million, according to House Democrats, including a medical-ride pickup payment that fell from $36.40 to $12.15.
At least one Western Slope provider says the cuts are forcing it to scale back.
Naomi's approach A missed ride becomes a missed appointment, and a missed appointment can become an ER visit.
Root out fraud and mismanagement of funds in the program without cutting the rides rural patients rely on.
Sources: House vote $270M figure Provider HB26-1328 Fewer choices for expecting families HB26-1092 · 2026 Katie Voted No Katie's Vote = Less Healthcare Voted no in the House Health & Human Services Committee.
It would have barred public hospitals from denying full staff privileges to certified nurse-midwives and certified midwives based on their license alone.
Naomi's approach Families should be able to choose a qualified midwife and still deliver at their local hospital.
Hospitals' concerns deserve a hearing; so do expecting families with few nearby options.
Source: HB26-1092 Higher Insurance Premiums SB26-178 · 2026 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = Less Affordability Lowered the state reinsurance program's average premium-reduction target from 20% to 18% for people who buy their own coverage.
Naomi's approach Self-employed workers, ranchers and early retirees buy their own insurance.
Every point of relief counts for them.
Source: SB26-178 One more way to see a doctor HB26-1096 · 2026 Katie Voted No Katie's Vote = Fewer Patient Choices Voted no twice on letting Medicaid patients pay a direct primary care practice and keep their Medicaid coverage.
Naomi's approach Patients on Medicaid should be able to choose the primary care that works for them, especially where clinics are few and far between.
Source: HB26-1096 Local communities know best Each town in this district knows its own needs best.
Less local say on housing HB26-1001 · 2026 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = More Expensive Housing Requires covered communities to approve qualifying housing from certain public and nonprofit owners through an administrative process.
Naomi's approach Southwest Colorado needs more attainably priced housing and a diversity of housing types, built the way each community chooses.
Local officials answer to local voters.
Source: HB26-1001 State restrictions on local zoning HB25-1093 · 2025 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = More Expensive Housing Limits certain local decisions that make housing more affordable and accessible.
Naomi's approach Growth decisions belong with the people who live with the results.
Regulations cause unintended consequences and impact on adjacent owners.
Source: HB25-1093 Even the governor said no Two of these votes went further than Colorado's Democratic governor would go.
A worker vote, removed HB26-1005 · 2026 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = More Union Power Would have removed the second election workers hold before a union can negotiate a contract requiring every employee to pay fees.
Gov.
Polis vetoed it, as he vetoed the same policy in 2025.
Naomi's approach Workers deserve a direct say before fees come out of every paycheck.
Any change should come from a real agreement between workers and employers.
Source: Veto coverage A new fee on game purchases HB26-1418 · 2026 Katie Voted Yes Katie's Vote = Higher Taxes Would have added a 5% fee on qualifying in-game purchases to fund youth services.
Gov.
Polis vetoed it, so the fee never took effect.
Naomi's approach Fund youth services through the budget, not a new fee on what families buy.
