Meet Mark Issues News Volunteer Contribute Meet Mark I was born and raised in Fayetteville, North Carolina, the fourth child of five born to Ruth Marguerite Rogers, a waitress, and Samuel Penn Carver, a building contractor.
During WWII my mother worked in a defense plant in Baltimore and my father served in the Navy in the European theatre.
My mother was diagnosed with breast cancer and had a radical mastectomy in 1973.
I was 15 years old when my mom passed away and I had just started my junior year of high school.
Within four months of my mom's passing I was living on my own with a fellow classmate living in a trailer, where I continued to attend school and work at a local car wash to support myself.
After graduating from high school at 17 I went to work at Kelly-Springfield Tire Company, a subsidiary of Goodyear Tire and Rubber.
During my twenty-five years at Kelly-Springfield, I held a variety of positions working my way up to serve as a vacation replacement supervisor and later served as a shop steward and eventually was elected as a Division Chairman for the URW/United Steel Workers.
In 1981I met my wife Lori at a Halloween costume party.
We have been married 43 years.
In 1986 we celebrated the birth of our only child, Lindsay.
When Lindsay was born I remember holding her and thinking how am I, a factory worker, going to make her life better?
So, before Lindsay's first birthday, while working my full-time position at Kelly-Springfield I also became a full-time student at Methodist College.
In less than five years I graduated from Methodist College, completing my Bachelor of Science, majoring in history.
In 1995, I attended Carolina Labor School at the University of North Carolina at Wilmington and was elected to a three-year term as USW Division Chairman for Local 959.
In 1996, I attended United Steel Workers training classes in Orlando, Florida.
Later in 1996 I served on the USW contract negotiations committee as we bargained with Goodyear Tire and Rubber.
After 8 months in negotiations, we called the plant's first strike at Kelly-Springfield in Fayetteville.
The strike lasted about a month.
While striking should always be the last resort it should always be a choice.
After retiring from Goodyear in 2001, I was accepted into the graduate program at NC State University where my primary focus was American Labor history.
While attending NC State I studied the labor history of Gaston County, the Loray Mill strike, and the Uprising of '34.
I am a member of Phi Alpha Theta, a History Honor Society, and Sigma Omega Chi, a Sociology Honor society.
Upon graduating with honors, I began teaching history.
I have had the honor of teaching at seven colleges, in North Carolina and Oklahoma.
This year I have been teaching college for 22 years with 20 of those at Gaston College.
In July of 2022 Lori and I became grandparents, we are Mimi and Papa.
Our daughter, Lindsay and son-in-law Michael, brought Hazel Amelia Stockman into this world.
We are so proud of Lindsay and Michael, and cherish the family time we spend with them and our Hazel.
