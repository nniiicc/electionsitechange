Public Service Agenda
The Secretary of State’s office should be built on public service.
That is the standard we will set.
This office administers Iowa’s elections, safeguards important business records, holds sensitive information, and gives Iowans and local officials guidance they have to be able to rely on.
Those are not small responsibilities, and they demand more than just competent administration.
They demand honesty, openness, sound judgement, and a plain understanding of who the office actually works for.

No one else.
Public service was at the forefront of my career in the Navy, and it will be at the forefront of everything we do in the Office of the Secretary of State.

Protect Iowans’ Information
The Secretary of State’s office holds some of Iowans’ most personal, sensitive information: where they live, drivers license and social security numbers, the records of the businesses they’ve built.
Iowans do not surrender that information.
They entrust it to their government, on the reasonable expectation that it will be protected, not given away. tThis is not merely a technical function of the office.
It is a duty.
I will treat Iowans’ information as what it is: theirs.
As Secretary of State, I will:
- Fight back against illegal requests, and unequivocally protect Iowans’ personal information.
- Collect and keep only the information this office genuinely needs to do its job.
- Regularly review the office’s data-retention, access, and cybersecurity practices, and fix weaknesses before they become failures.
Make Transparency the Standard Practice
Iowans should not have to file a public-records request to understand what their government is doing.
Transparency should be the default, not the exception.
As Secretary of State I will:
- Root out government corruption that too often keeps Iowans in the dark
- Publish clear election guidance, directives, and significant policy changes online, with plain-language explanations of what they mean for voters, county officials, and businesses.
- Improve the speed and quality of public-records responses.
- Maintain regular, professional communication with the press, and correct inaccurate information clearly and promptly — including my own.
Listen to Iowans Across the State
This office should not be run entirely from behind a desk in Des Moines.
The people who work with this office — voters, county officials, small-business owners, veterans, students, and community organizations — understand its problems long before anyone in the capital does.
As Secretary of State I will:
- Visit each of Iowa’s ninety-nine counties during my term.
- Hold regular public meetings and listening sessions across the state.
- Use those meetings to explain changes, answer questions, and catch problems before they become crises.
Work in Partnership with Local Officials
Iowa’s elections are run in each of our ninety-nine counties by county auditors, of both parties, and their staffs, who take that work seriously.
The Secretary of State’s job is to give them the support, the guidance, and the trust they need to succeed.
As Secretary of State I will:
- Meet regularly with county auditors throughout the year.
- Provide consistent, timely guidance to every county, to ensure our elections run as smoothly as possible.
- Coordinate with state, federal, and local partners to strengthen election security, and to protect Iowa businesses from fraud.
- Consult with local officials before implementing major administrative changes, not after.
Build an Office that Responds
People should not have to fight their way through their own government to get an answer.
A voter with a question, a county official who needs guidance, a small-business owner filing paperwork — each of them deserves a straight answer in reasonable time.
As Secretary of State I will:
- Set clear standards for responding to phone calls, emails, filings, and public inquiries, and track unresolved requests so Iowans aren’t forced to start over every time they contact the office.
- Simplify forms, instructions, and website content.
- Review complaints and recurring problems.
- Publish basic performance measures, so Iowans can judge for themselves whether service is getting better.
This office exists to serve the people of Iowa.
It must be worthy of their trust.
It’s not complicated.
You do the work.
You tell people the truth.
You own it when you get something wrong.
That is what I will bring to the Secretary of State’s office.
