Angela is fighting for
Climate Action Now
The climate crisis is a daily reality in CA-34.
From polluted air and toxic runoff to the urban heat island effect, working-class and immigrant communities across our district are disproportionately impacted by environmental harm — the result of decades of environmental racism, underinvestment, and corporate influence on policy.
Boyle Heights alone carries a pollution burden about 63% above the county average.
A Green New Deal That Centers Our Communities
- Massive federal investment in renewable energy infrastructure — solar, wind, battery storage — for homes, schools, and public buildings
- Unionized, family-sustaining green jobs prioritizing communities hit hardest by industrial pollution, displacement, and job loss
- Job training and workforce development in clean energy, construction, public transit, and environmental restoration
Urban Greening for Health and Resilience
- Funding for community parks, urban forests, and green corridors to reduce extreme heat and improve air quality
- Investment in rooftop gardens, green schoolyards, and permeable infrastructure to reduce flooding and boost cooling
- Prioritizing investment in census tracts with high asthma rates, low tree canopy, and extreme heat exposure
Investing in Community-Led Climate Solutions
- Native plant installations promoting local biodiversity and traditional ecological knowledge
- Medicinal workshops and educational programs rooted in ancestral practices
- Urban agriculture and food sovereignty projects: community gardens, co-ops, composting hubs
Declare a climate emergency and hold Big Oil accountable
- This summer's Lineage warehouse fire in Boyle Heights, which sent smoke and ammonia into the neighborhood, is a direct example of what happens when hazardous operations are sited near working-class communities of color
- Support a national climate emergency declaration in Congress, in line with the National Climate Emergency Act (Ocasio-Cortez/Blumenauer/Sanders)
- A polluters-pay climate superfund, following New York's and Vermont's models
- A windfall profits tax on oil and gas companies
- End federal fossil fuel subsidies
- Stronger federal siting and health-review requirements for hazardous facilities near residential communities
- Codify Justice40 into law
- Extend clean energy savings, like community solar, to renters following Illinois' CEJA model
