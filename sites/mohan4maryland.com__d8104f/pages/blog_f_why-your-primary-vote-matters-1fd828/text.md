All Posts Why Your Primary Vote Matters June 13, 2026 Your Voice, Your Power: The Untapped Potential of Primary Elections Every four years, national elections capture headlines—but the real shaping of policy and representation often happens much closer to home, during primary elections.
In communities across Maryland, voters hold the power to determine not just who appears on the general election ballot, but which issues take center stage in local debates.
Primary elections are where party nominees are chosen, and turnout is typically low—meaning a relatively small number of engaged voters can have an outsized impact on the direction of their district.
For those passionate about education reform, affordable housing, or equitable development in Baltimore County and beyond, the primary is where your influence begins.
Unlike the general election, where choices may feel limited, the primary offers a true opportunity to shape the future by supporting candidates who align with your values.
Primary elections serve as the critical first step in shaping who will represent communities in local, state, and national government.
Unlike general elections, where the final candidates from each party face off, primaries determine which candidates earn the right to appear on the ballot.
In Maryland politics, where party affiliation often influences election outcomes, participating in the primary can be even more impactful than waiting until November.
Voters registered with a political party can help select their preferred candidate by casting a ballot in the primary.
These early contests narrow the field and often spotlight issues most relevant to party members.
Because turnout tends to be lower in primaries, each vote carries greater weight—making your participation essential.
A strong primary showing can energize grassroots support, influence policy platforms, and set the tone for the general election.
In 42A a strong Democratic turnout in the primary will help set the conversation about the kinds of legislations voters want their officials to support and spearhead, the issues they want to see championed in Annapolis and the tone of political collaboration for the next four years.
Your primary vote helps define the direction of local governance.
By getting informed, showing up, and voting early, you take an active role in shaping not just who runs, but how they govern.
Participating in primary elections isn’t just about picking a candidate—it’s a direct line to shaping your party’s core values and policy direction.
When voters turn out in primaries, especially at the local level, they help define which issues rise to the top, from education reform to housing equity and economic development strategies.
Primary outcomes signal priorities.
Candidates align their platforms with voter concerns, meaning your ballot sends a clear message about what matters most to your community.
On a personal note, I have maintained that my legislative agenda will be primarily determined by what my constituents think is most important. whether it’s advocating for transit improvements in Baltimore County or pushing for stronger environmental regulations across the state, or increased land preservation and agricultural impetus in our District.
Your vote helps determine which policies gain momentum.
So please: Research candidates’ stances before primaries Attend local party forums and town halls Encourage neighbors to participate—especially in low-turnout cycles Your involvement ensures Maryland’s political platforms reflect real community needs—not just party insiders.
You don’t need to be a political expert to make an impact.
Start by researching the candidates on your primary ballot, attending community forums, and discussing key issues with neighbors.
Verify your voter registration, request your ballot early if voting by mail, and make a plan to vote.
Even small actions—like having conversations about local representation—strengthen civic engagement across Baltimore County and beyond.
Democracy thrives when people show up.
By participating in the primary, you help ensure that leadership reflects community values, not just special interests.
This is how meaningful change begins—neighbor by neighbor, vote by vote.
Take pride in your role, and encourage others to claim theirs.
