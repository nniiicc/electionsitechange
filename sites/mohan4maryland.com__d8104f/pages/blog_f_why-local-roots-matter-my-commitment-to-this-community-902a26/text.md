All Posts Why Local Roots Matter: My Commitment to This Community August 30, 2026 When people ask me why I'm running for Delegate, I could give them a list of policy positions.
But before we get to policy, I want to talk about something more fundamental: what it means to actually be from here — and why that matters for who represents you in Annapolis.
This Isn't a Résumé Line for Me I've sent my kids to our local schools — the same ones bursting at the seams because our county hasn't kept pace with new subdivisions.
I've sat in the same backed-up traffic on the same two-lane roads that were built for a farm town, not for the commuters now packed onto them every morning.
I've watched the fields I lived near get sold off for another housing development, and I've watched longtime neighbors weigh whether they can still afford to stay.
That's not a talking point.
That's what it means to actually live in a place that's changing fast — the way exurban communities like ours are changing all over this state.
Why Groundedness Is Good Government A Delegate's job isn't to arrive in the capitol with a stack of national talking points.
It's to carry this community's specific, textured reality into rooms where decisions get made about our schools, our roads, our small businesses, and our families.
Here's what local grounding actually delivers: It catches what statistics miss.
A growth report can tell you our county added a few thousand new residents last year.
It won't tell you that the elementary school is now running classes out of trailers, that the volunteer fire department is stretched thin covering subdivisions built miles past the old response radius, or that the farmer down the road turned down a developer's offer only to watch his property taxes climb anyway because of the land around him.
For instance, a fantastic local discussion recently consisted of a long time resident, who was proud of our farming tradition and wanted above all, to preserve that tradition.
The resident was adamant that solar farming would ruin the district.
A farmer responded with the actual reality- getting paid a fraction of the fee that Solar companies were offering to lease their land in order to conserve it for agriculture.
She pointed out that the last decade had hit farmers hard economically, and few could actually afford their farms!
So while non-farming locals, with the best of intentions want to preserve our agricultural land, without local patronage, and systems in place to cushion farmers from climate disasters, crop failures, insect invasion and even the importation of cheaper produce from other states or countries, clinging to an absolute policy without recognizing the reality of farmers' lives would not be helping them.
The conversation was a wonderful and cordial one between two neighbors who both began with the belief that they each knew best, but ended with the recognition and understanding of the nuances and complications of actual life.
To me, this is how policy should be formulated- with ownership and partnership from the people who will be impacted!
You have to be here, talking to people, to know what growth actually feels like on the ground.
It builds trust that outlasts an election cycle.
This community has seen candidates before who show up in October and vanish in November.
I understand why that breeds skepticism.
The only way to earn trust is to still be answerable to you after the votes are counted — to be someone you can stop at the grocery store and hold accountable, dial on your cell-phone, email on their regular email, chat with them at school pick-up or summer camp.
It means real relationships, not just endorsements.
I didn't build a campaign from a donor list.
I built it from years sitting on the PTA, volunteering with non-profits like animal rescues and Student Support Network, from volunteering to serve in County and State Boards and Commissions, testifying for my neighbors at County Council meetings, standing alongside my neighbors who've watched zoning decisions reshape this district, and showing up at the same planning commission meetings where the future of our roads, schools, and open land actually gets decided — long before it hits the news.
It protects against one-size-fits-all solutions.
State-level policy too often treats every district the same — written for dense urban cores or for rural farm counties, with little in between.
Our exurban reality is different: we need the infrastructure investment of a growing suburb and the respect for agricultural land and rural character of the county we still are in many ways.
A Delegate who understands that balance won't import solutions that make sense somewhere else but ignore what's actually happening here.
A Word of Honesty I don't think being local is the only qualification for office, and I'm wary of candidates who use "roots" as a substitute for ideas, or as a cudgel against newer neighbors, immigrants, or younger residents who are just as invested in this community's future even if their families haven't been here for generations.
Being grounded shouldn't mean being closed off — the best representatives combine deep local knowledge with the vision to bring real resources and expertise back home.
My long career as a researcher has taught me humility about the extent of my knowledge- no one knows it all and my aim is always to learn every day.
But I do believe representation requires shared stake in outcomes.
And that's what I'm offering: not just a plan for this district, but a life built inside it.
What This Means for You When I'm in Annapolis fighting for the road funding, school capacity, and smart growth planning this district needs, I won't be guessing at what it takes to keep this a place families can afford to put down roots.
I'll already know — because I'm watching this district grow in real time, my kids are in these schools, and my future is tied to this district's future just as tightly as yours is.
That's the kind of representation I believe you deserve.
That's the campaign I'm running.
Not grand.
Not powerful.
A real public servant. — Jyoti Mohan, Candidate for Delegate, District 42A Share this post: Recent Posts Not Me, WE: a platform of service and collaboration.
