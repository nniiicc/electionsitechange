Have your ballot?
Here's how to return it Fill it out with a blue or black pen.
Follow the instructions — fill in the ovals completely.
Put the ballot in the ID envelope and complete every line: print your name and address, give your Ohio driver's license/state ID number or the last four digits of your Social Security number, and sign it .
A missing signature or ID number is the #1 reason ballots get held up.
Seal the ID envelope inside the return envelope.
Return it one of three ways: Drop box at your county Board of Elections — available 24/7 until 7:30 pm on Election Day.
Only you can put your own ballot in the drop box.
Hand it in inside the Board of Elections office during business hours.
A close family member (spouse, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, aunt/uncle, niece/nephew, or in-law) can do this for you, but they must bring it inside and sign a short form — they can't use the drop box.
Mail it with a stamp (postage is not prepaid).
We recommend mailing no later than Saturday, Oct 24 .
It must arrive by 7:30 pm Nov 3 — a postmark does not count.
Track it.
Confirm your Board received it at VoteOhio.gov ballot tracking .

Common questions Your situation, answered I moved within Ohio and didn't update my address by Oct 5.
Can I still vote?
Yes.
Go to your new county's Board of Elections during early voting (or on Election Day) and vote a provisional ballot for your new address.
It's counted once they verify your eligibility.
Bring your ID.
If you moved within the same county, you can also update your address at the Board and vote there.
Something's wrong at my polling place.
What are my rights?
My name isn't in the poll book.
First make sure you're at the right precinct (poll workers can check).
If you are, ask for a provisional ballot — you have the right to one, and it's counted once the Board confirms your registration.
I forgot my ID.
Vote a provisional ballot, then show an accepted photo ID at your county Board of Elections by Saturday, Nov 7 and it counts.
I can't get into the building.
Ask for curbside voting — send someone in to tell a poll worker, and two officials will bring a ballot to your car.
This works during early voting too.
I need help marking my ballot.
You can bring a person of your choice (not your employer or union rep), or ask two poll workers from different parties to help.
I'm in line at 7:30 pm.
Stay in line.
Anyone in line when polls close gets to vote.
Someone is turning people away, challenging voters, or making people uncomfortable.
Don't leave.
Call 866-OUR-VOTE (866-687-8683), the nonpartisan Election Protection hotline, or 844-OHI-VOTE (844-644-8683).
Your county Board of Elections can also send help.
My name changed (marriage, divorce).
Will my ID match?
If your address is the same, bring proof of the name change (marriage license or court order) and you'll sign a short form and vote a regular ballot.
Without proof, you'll vote provisionally.
Either way, you can vote.
I have a felony conviction.
Can I vote?
In Ohio, yes — unless you are currently incarcerated for a felony.
If you're on probation, parole, or community control, or have finished your sentence, you can vote.
If your registration was cancelled while you were incarcerated, you'd need to re-register before the next election's deadline.
I'm a college student.
Where do I vote?
Wherever you're registered.
If you're registered at your campus address in Ohio, vote there.
If you're registered at home, vote early in person in your home county, request a mail ballot from your home county Board of Elections (by Oct 27), or go home on Election Day.
You can't be registered in two places.
I requested a mail ballot but now want to vote in person.
During early voting, go to your county Board of Elections and bring the mail ballot if you have it — they can help you vote in person.
On Election Day you'd have to vote a provisional ballot.
Honestly, the easiest thing is to vote your mail ballot and drop it in the Board's drop box.
I have a disability or I'm homebound.
What are my options?
Every polling place and early voting site is accessible and has accessible voting machines.
You can vote curbside from your car.
If you can't travel at all, call your Board of Elections — they can send two election officials to deliver and return your ballot, or set you up with an accessible absentee ballot you mark at home.
I was told my ballot has a problem.
What now?
You have until Saturday, Nov 7 to fix it.
For a provisional ballot cast without ID, bring an accepted photo ID to your county Board of Elections.
For a mail ballot with a missing signature or ID number, the Board will send you a form (11-S) to complete.
Call them if you're unsure.
After Election Day you can check whether your provisional ballot counted at 866-OHIO-VOTE (866-644-6868).
How do I know my vote will count?
Ohio elections are run county by county by bipartisan Boards of Elections, with paper ballots and post-election audits.
You can confirm your mail ballot was received at VoteOhio.gov .
If you ever have a question about your ballot, your county Board of Elections is the best place to call — numbers above .
