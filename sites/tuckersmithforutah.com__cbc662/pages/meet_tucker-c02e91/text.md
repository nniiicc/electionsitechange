A leader for the community.
Home-Grown Having lived in Orem for 25 years, Tucker is a product of the great educational opportunities Utah County offers.
Tucker benefitted greatly from the vast array of programs at Mountain View High School, such as Band, Orchestra, Choir, Pit Orchestra, Mock Trial, and Boy’s Soccer.
Graduating from Mountain View, he then attended Brigham Young University, where he received his Bachelor’s of Music in Music Education.
Service as an educator Tucker has given back to his community through his work and service as a public educator.
Tucker has taught music for 8 years, 7 of those at Timpanogos High School in Orem.
In that time, Tucker’s band students have performed at countless memorial services, farmer’s markets, care centers, and school events.
While at Timpanogos, Tucker has also served as the union representative and on leadership teams, serving the needs of teachers and staff at Timpanogos High School Service in the arts Serving his community through music and the arts, Tucker has performed with volunteer organizations, such as the Orem Community Band, Wasatch Show Band, Utah Valley Symphony Orchestra, and Timpanogos Symphony Orchestra.
He has also acted in community theater plays at the SCERA Shell, Springville Playhouse, and American Fork Community Theater.
Tucker has dedicated his life to service in school, in his family, and in his community.
Why is it important to me?
Developing Ideals Growing up, my parents taught me the ideals of the Founding Fathers and the importance of democratic governance.
Phrases from the Declaration of Independence–such as “all men (and women) are created equal,” and “governments (derive their power) from consent of the governed,”-- instilled in me a love for equality and democracy.
My experiences in history and government classes in junior high and high school strengthened my understanding of–and appreciation for–the structure and the ideals of our government.
Seeking equality and justice As I studied at Brigham Young University (BYU), I began to realize that the ideals championed by our Founding Fathers were not being realized in our current society.
Intense economic power disparities corrupt our society and economy, and people and families of extreme wealth have an unacceptable level of influence on our government.
Children from low-income families start several rungs lower on the ladder of opportunity.
I realized that, to honor my commitment to the Founding Father’s ideals, I needed to fight for economic equality and equality of opportunity.
Most importantly, I realized my role as a teacher was ideal for moving our society towards equality.
Through providing opportunity and access for children, I could help to assuage many of these injustices.
Hence, I dedicated my life to providing meaningful opportunities for all students to create beautiful music, helping them to embrace their humanity and discover beauty.
Seeking Change In teaching, I have seen firsthand the disparity between the wealthy class and everyone else.
Students who have money receive many opportunities to engage with the academic content and with the school community, while many lower-income students do not have those same opportunities, even within public school.
The pandemic placed our economic challenges in stark contrast—thousands of Utah families and students struggled, while the billionaires padded their pockets.
This stark reality made clear to me that we need candidates and representatives who will stand up for democracy, who will stand up against the tyranny of the wealthy, who will stand up for the masses oppressed by an unelected wealthy class.
I hope to be that representative for Utah.
