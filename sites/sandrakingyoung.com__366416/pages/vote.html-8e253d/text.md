Military & Dependents College Students Off-Island Residents Sandra King Young · American Samoa Who This Is For You can still vote — from anywhere.
If you call American Samoa home but you're stationed, studying, or living somewhere else right now, you can still make your voice heard.
Whether you're living abroad, either a military service member, college student, or simply an American Samoan resident temporarily off-island but still intending to vote in local elections—registering for an absentee ballot takes a few minutes—here's exactly how.
See what it takes below.
Are you Eligible?
How to Register Download Registration Form Before You Start Are you eligible?
Most people who grew up in or claim American Samoa as home meet these four requirements.
Check them off, then see what you'll need to have on hand.
Eligibility Checklist You're a U.S. citizen or U.S. national You're at least 18 years old You've lived in American Samoa for 2 years total, at some point in your life You lived in your home voting district for at least 1 year before the election What You'll Need A valid U.S. passport or your birth certificate A valid government-issued photo ID Your Social Security card, or an official SSA letter showing your SSN How to Register Four simple steps.
No trip to a government office required.
Fill out one form, send it in, and you're on the voter rolls.
1 Download the form Get the official absentee voter registration form — free, one page, no login required.
2 Fill it out & sign Print it or fill it out digitally, then sign it.
Takes about five minutes.
3 Snap a photo Take a clear photo or scan of your signed form and your documents.
4 Email it in Send the form and your documents to the Election Office.
That's it — you're registered.
