So much so, that it tends to usurp the other branches of State Government, and has painted the Eastern Shore into a corner in some respects.
First, Nurse Practitioners and Physician Assistants must work directly under a doctor's supervision at all times with no end date.
Why?
Because it is law.
We can work to relax some of these statutes to allow for a required time period as understudy, and then allow NPs and PAs to practice family medicine in rural areas.
Second, we must work to allow those on Medicaid to access heathcare (especially specialists) in Delaware.
Medicaid is a Federal program administered by the States.
Marylanders should be able to seek treatment across State Lines, but the Legislature forbids it.
What the state Legislature doesn't appreciate is that the State of Delaware is completely enveloped by Maryland.
Many on the Eastern Shore are just a short drive from doctors and specialists in Delaware.
Yet, those on Medicaid, many of whom lack transportation, must pay an Uber, or get someone to drive them for hours, pay tolls, with high gas prices, to see doctors and specialists across the bridge in Baltimore.
It is an undue burden for many.
Lastly, with the recent closing of Union Hospital's Maternity Ward in Cecil County, there is no place in any of the four counties of District 36, where a woman can give birth outside of an emergency room.
No woman should be relegated to giving birth where people with communicable diseases are treated.
We should explore how we might increase the presence of midmives and doulas in the district to help women give birth safely and locally.
