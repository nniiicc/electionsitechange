September 22nd
Brad was pleased to attend this forum, sponsored by ARC Minnesota - SE Region, The Region 10 Quality Council, SAM (Self-Advocates Minnesota, this past Tuesday.
This event focuses on issues that impact people with disabilities in our community.
Questions asked, and addressed were questions on how candidate plan to listen to self-advocates when making decisions on waiver services/supports, Transportation, Housing, and Fraud (to make sure the fraud doesn't negatively impact people who need those services)
Brad’s answer to the fraud question was, in part:
“Don’t allow it to happen to begin with—in the first place!”
Internal controls are designed to identify and prevent fraud before it reaches the level we have witnessed.
When state employees raise concerns about potential fraud and those concerns are not adequately addressed, that demands accountability.
Brad put it this way: “When your checking account shows unusual activity, your financial institution doesn’t simply look the other way.
It will freeze the account and contact you to determine whether the activity is legitimate or whether your account has been compromised.”
The federal government took similar action when concerns arose that Minnesota funds were not being used for their intended purposes.
Minnesota has a fiduciary responsibility to safeguard taxpayer dollars and ensure public funds are being used as intended.
Brad believes that responsibility must be taken seriously.
Whistleblower warnings should be investigated, internal controls should be followed, and fraud should be stopped before it happens, not after taxpayers have already lost millions of dollars.
As Brad said, “FRAUD-FRAUD-FRAUD!” It should be a top priority to protect Minnesota taxpayers and the essential services that depend on those dollars.
This is not simply a federal issue, as our Democratic friends choose to blame.
Minnesota state agencies had responsibility for administering and overseeing many of these programs, and the state’s own Legislative Auditor has found significant failures in oversight.
In the Feeding Our Future case, for example, the Legislative Auditor concluded that inadequate oversight by the Minnesota Department of Education created opportunities for fraud.
The people of Minnesota deserve accountability from those responsible for overseeing these programs, including Governor Tim Walz and state agency leadership.
Minnesotans also deserve to know where elected officials stand when serious failures occur, and whether they are willing to publicly demand accountability, stronger safeguards, and meaningful reform.
Brad's opponent has mostly been silent.
Brad's opponent has spoken publicly about fraud in a newsletter, and a few media outlets, and supported anti-fraud legislation, but we have not found one, not one public press release specifically condemning the scale of Minnesota's fraud or publicly assigning responsibility for the failures to Governor Walz and his administration, nor her party, for allowing this massive fraud to occur.
Some Democrats have called for Minnesota to look forward and avoid politicizing the issue.
But accountability requires more than simply moving forward, it requires asking how these failures happened, who was responsible for oversight, and what must change to prevent them from happening again.
Minnesotans deserve transparency and accountability from their state government.
As your elected State Representative, Brad Trahan will not hesitate to ask tough questions, hold government accountable, and call out fraud, waste, and abuse when it occurs.
As many people know, Brad is the proud father of Reece, a 26-year-old with severe autism.
For the past 23 years, Brad has been not only a dedicated parent and advocate for his son, but also a passionate self-advocate for countless individuals and families facing similar challenges.
His personal experience has given him a firsthand understanding of the importance of ensuring that people with disabilities have a strong voice, access to needed services, and the opportunity to live with dignity and support.
In 2016, Brad led an initiative for 13 years, 8 months, for Mayo Clinic to provide autism therapy, benefits for their employees' dependents, diagnosed with autism.
Just recently in 2026, Brad led initiative to expand access to specialized oral care, in hospital, under anesthesia for individuals with cognitive and intellectual disabilities.
There were only five (5) hospitals in the State of Minnesota that provided this case for adults with cognitive and intellectual disabilities, and Brad was able to add a sixth, Olmsted Medical Center.
A project he started in January 2025, and completed in June 2026.
Also, recently in late August 2026, Brad identified a gap in Mayo Clinic Insurance Policy, regarding oral anesthesia care and provider coverage.
Brad worked closely with Mayo, Medica (Plan Administrator), and MN DHS.
May0 agreed that there was a gap, and effective October 1st, they were going to add OMC as an approved Provider for this Oral anesthesia care for adults with cognitive and intellectual disabilities.
Brad achieved this task in just one-weeks time!
