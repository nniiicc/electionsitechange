11th) A Family, a Community, and a Commitment to Protect Our Most Vulnerable For Brad, this moment is deeply personal.
Standing alongside his family—Brenden, Reece, Joanie, and Payton—and with HHS Secretary Robert F.
Kennedy Jr. during the announcement of a nationwide Autism Alert system for individuals with autism who go missing was a powerful reminder of why this work matters.
Brad has spent years advocating for programs that help protect individuals with autism and other vulnerable members of our community.
One of those efforts began right here in Rochester and Olmsted County with Project Lifesaver, a program designed to help law enforcement locate individuals who may wander or become missing.
Brad helped bring Project Lifesaver to Rochester and Olmsted County by raising $15,000 to launch the program.
He then continued that effort, raising another $25,000 to help seven additional Minnesota counties implement Project Lifesaver.
In 2015, Brad led an initiative that resulted in an $80,000 Minnesota state grant to help additional law-enforcement agencies establish Project Lifesaver programs.
Brad emphasizes that none of this happened because of one person.
“This has always been about collaboration.
So many people have stepped up and made a difference.
When it comes to protecting our most vulnerable, we have to work together and do everything we can.” Brad is especially proud of the Rochester Police Department and Olmsted County Sheriff's Office Project Lifesaver teams, whose dedication continues to make a difference for families throughout our community.
He also recognizes the continued efforts of Rochester Autism Advocacy, including its work helping families navigate the application process and identify individuals who may benefit from the program.
Brad is grateful as well for the past support of RTAAF, and for the leadership and administrative support provided by Sheriff Borchardt, Sheriff Torgerson, and Police Chief Franklin, whose belief in the program helped make this community effort possible.
For Brad and his family, this isn't simply about policy.
It's about people.
It's about families.
And it's about making sure that when a vulnerable person goes missing, every possible resource is available to help bring them home safely.
The announcement of a nationwide Autism Alert system represents an important step forward—and Brad is proud to have been part of the local effort that helped demonstrate what is possible when families, advocates, law enforcement, government, and communities work together.
