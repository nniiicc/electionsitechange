Paul, MN 55155 (651) 296-6153 sen.nick.frentz@senate.mn Minnesota State Senate Dear Friends and Neighbors, Thanks for the chance to serve you in the Minnesota Senate.
I’m proud to work for every person in our district, regardless of who they are, where they live, what they do for a living, or who they voted for.
We are all in this together.
The 2018 legislative session adjourned on May 20.
Overall, this session saw some accomplishments for Minnesota — including several that are good for our area.
Our region gets a lot of respect from other legislators in St.
Paul, and if you ask me, it’s deserved.
The good news included passage of a bipartisan bonding bill that contained over a billion dollars in public projects, passage of a pension bill, and approval of state employee contracts, among other things.
We worked hard on agriculture, education, local and state government, transportation, and more.
When the two political parties worked together, we saw progress.
Unfortunately, when the two parties didn’t work together, we got almost nothing done.
We failed to agree on a tax bill, and we owe it to the people of Minnesota to get that done either in a special session or immediately in January when we reconvene.
There was also no progress in the areas of our opioid crisis, distracted driving, or gun violence prevention— despite public support for action.
One obstacle: the use of massive “omnibus” bills which try to force subjects together like transportation, agriculture, education, etc. that don’t belong together.
This approach causes good legislation to fail when it is paired with bad policy.
The use of a huge omnibus supplemental budget bill, instead of individual or “stand alone” bills, failed Minnesotans this session.
I hope we see a lot less of that tactic in the future.
Bottom line: the legislature needs to do better.
We live in a great state, and we should try to keep it that way.
We have a good overall economy, great schools, good roads, and good communities – both large and small.
Most of all we have lots of hard working Minnesotans that are just good neighbors and great people that I’m honored to represent here in District 19.
Please let me know if you have any questions, concerns, or just want to chat.
Thanks again for the chance to represent you.
Sincerely, State Senator Nick Frentz, Senate District 19 Here to Serve You can contact my office at any time with questions or concerns.
My staff and I are happy to help and we pride ourselves on being responsive and understanding.

14/Bonding bill passed The final bonding bill signed into law contained important provisions for District 19, including: vital funding of $174 million for Hwy.
14 from Owatonna to Dodge Center, $2.2 million in upgrades for the St.
Peter Regional Treatment Center, and $4.3 million for Phase II for the Clinical Sciences building project at MNSU-Mankato.
Bonding equals jobs.
Pension bill passed Sen.
Frentz, proudly appointed to the Pension Commission in January, voted “yes” on the pension bill.
It represented shared sacrifice—reforms in this bill include COLA reductions and increased employee/employer contributions.
This also strengthens our state credit rating, which will save us money in the long run.
This bill was a big win for our SD 19 retirees, state, county, and city employees, teachers, and their families.
Helping police, firefighters, and first responders who suffer from PTSD Sen.
Frentz was the chief author of SF125, which classifies post traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) as a presumptive work illness for police, paramedics, and firefighters.
This bill was a top priority for Sen.
Frentz, and was passed in the last week of the session as part of the Worker’s Compensation bill.
North Mankato Infrastructure Grant for North Port Industrial Park The Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development (DEED) awarded North Mankato a $339,000 infrastructure grant, benefiting the North Port Industrial Park.
This will promote growth, which in turn will bring additional jobs to the community.
Health Care This is the number one problem people want the legislature to fix.
Sen.
Frentz supports fundamental change that will allow all Minnesotans to get basic access to medical care.
This would include allowing any Minnesotan to sign up for MinnesotaCare, which could significantly reduce their monthly premiums.
Agriculture As a member of the Senate Agriculture Finance Committee, Sen.
Frentz knows agriculture is the backbone of Southern Minnesota’s economy.
To support farmers, he co-authored a bill to provide tax credits for buffer strips, and will work with Minnesota Corn, Minnesota Pork Producers, and Minnesota Soybean Growers to ensure passage.
Sen.
Frentz is also a member of Minnesota Farm Bureau and Minnesota Farmers Union and will work with them to strengthen Minnesota family farms.
Transportation As a member of the Senate Transportation Committee, Sen.
Frentz has spent two years working towards stable long term funding for our roads, bridges, and transit.
There is no dispute that we are short of the funding necessary; the question is whether to take money away from existing programs, or increase revenue through other means like a gas tax.
Regardless, we should effectively budget long term so MnDOT, our counties, cities, and townships can all plan accordingly.
Opioids Last year, 395 Minnesotans lost their lives to opioid abuse.
Big pharmaceutical companies – which have profited to some extent from the opioid crisis – should be held accountable.
Sen.
Frentz supported the Senate bill which required those companies to contribute a small portion of their profits to curbing opioid abuse.
This common-sense proposal did not become law.
Education Education is a cornerstone of the District 19 area’s success.
Sen.
Frentz is committed to making sure our schools receive a fair share of education funding.
This promotes strong economies, communities, and neighborhoods.
Education and school funding has always been Sen.
Frentz’s top priority.
Supplemental budget and tax bills vetoed Senator Frentz has always fought to protect Minnesota’s economy from future instability and to avoid future budget deficits.
Unfortunately, the tax bill proposals that were offered (and vetoed) threatened Minnesota’s fiscal integrity.
The tax bill was projected to leave the state with a $500 million deficit in just a few years, and would have given the wealthiest Minnesotans the biggest tax cuts while working families received far less.
The Supplemental Omnibus Budget bill was a problem in more than one way – it was 995 pages long, was brought to the floor very late in the session, and it contained a number of questionable policy proposals.
Instead of passing separate bills based on budget areas, this bill combined every budget and a ton of policy into one huge bill that forced lawmakers into a take it or leave it position.
This move killed many good provisions that would have passed on their own.
Senator Frentz serves on three standing committees: Transportation Finance and Policy, Agriculture Rural Development and Housing Finance, and Aging and Long-Term Care Policy.
He also serves on the Legislative Commission on Pensions and Retirement, Legislative Audit Commission (LAC), the Governor’s Task Force on Housing, and the Senate Select Committee on Health Care.
