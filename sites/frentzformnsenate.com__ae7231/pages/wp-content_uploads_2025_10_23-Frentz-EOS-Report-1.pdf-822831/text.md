How I Voted On the Budget and Omnibus Bills — And Why Tax Bill (HF1938) YES Vote I voted “yes” mostly because of the tax cut provisions.
The bill provides a $1.1 billion Child Tax Credit and $1.1 billion in Social Security tax cuts over the next four years.
It also provides property tax relief and a new renter’s credit.
Further, the $300 million in public safety funding will help make our communities safer statewide.
There was also a $6 million extension of a local sales tax option allowing North Mankato to fund and develop local projects.
Agriculture, Broadband, and Rural Development (SF1955) YES Vote Agriculture supports 431,000 jobs and provides a $116 billion total economic impact for our state.
The bill appropriates $1.5 million for soil health programs, $800,000 for pollinator research, and $300,000 for invasive plant species removal. $200,000 was included for GreenSeam, a Greater Mankato Growth based coalition of more than 1,000 agriculture related entities across southern Minnesota.
Veterans (SF2247) YES Vote Education Finance (SF2684) YES Vote Education was the top investment we made this session – over $5 billion in new funding to help our kids, teachers, and schools.
This bill provides millions in grants for STEM course offerings in Greater Minnesota, financial literacy courses, and more.
Judiciary and Public Safety (SF2909) YES Vote The bill provided more money for public defenders, and funded law enforcement priorities.
Probably the most notable provisions adopted were on gun violence prevention: we passed universal background checks and red flag provisions.
The intent is simple: to help keep Minnesotans safe.
Transportation (SF3157) YES Vote Providing the biggest boost in transportation funding in many years, this bill included increases for road and bridge construction, and for transit.
The increase of 5 cents per gallon in the gas tax to 33 cents was OK with me, given that the 2008 level of 28.5 cents (the last time it was increased) would be about the same as 40 cents per gallon today in inflation adjusted dollars.
I was happy to vote “YES” on the bill because it provides $14 million for the design and construction of a Minnesota military museum at Camp Ripley, $50,000 yearly for grants to cover retreats for veterans, and $540,000 yearly for Metro Meals on Wheels to provide homedelivered meals to veterans.
Local Project Successes As chief author of these bills, I want to thank area advocates and local and state officials for their help in making these projects a reality. $35 million for the Mankato Wastewater Treatment Plant Because of its age and a high risk of facility failure, this was an urgent district need.
The project will overhaul and update several plant components.
This will increase safety, capacity, and protect local ratepayers. $21.56 million for the St.
Peter Security Hospital including the Phase II improvements on the lower campus and improvements to the Sunrise and Tomlinson buildings.
This will improve workplace safety. $1.6 million for the MSU Rural Behavioral Health Center This bill establishes and funds a first of its kind rural mental health clinic at MSU Mankato.
It will offer training for our students and provide needed mental health treatment and counseling for area residents. $8.46 million for MSU Mankato to renovate space in the lower level of the Clinical Sciences building, to design the demolition and replacement of Armstrong Hall, and make other campus renovations. $4.2 million for prep work for a new Bureau of Criminal Apprehension lab in Mankato.
When completed, this $45 million project will support southern MN criminal prosecution work.
Bonding Bills (HF669 And HF670) YES Vote A big bipartisan win for Minnesota.
We passed $2.6 billion in borrowing for dozens of statewide infrastructure improvements.
These projects create hundreds of good paying local jobs, too.
