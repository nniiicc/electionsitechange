Office Email: sen.nick.frentz@senate.mn justin.emmerich@senate.mn Dear Friends and Neighbors, Thanks for allowing me to serve our area in the Minnesota Senate.
I hope this letter finds you and your family well.
As I write this, we just finished the 2021 legislative special session and passed a $52 billion two-year budget.
This brochure is my annual report to let you know what happened, what votes I took, and why.
Overall, I was pleased with the session and I think the budget deal is a good one.
We dealt with the public health challenges of the pandemic.
Many Minnesotans and businesses made sacrifices that helped save lives.
I am proud of the work I did in the areas of clean energy, agriculture, and getting COVID-19 relief funds to workers and small businesses.
My job remains pretty simple: to give my constituents a voice at the Capitol, whether they voted for me or not.
This session, as always, we were glad to get thousands of emails, calls, and letters (on just about every subject, too).
I still love this work as much as ever, and my favorite part is working directly with people and trying to make things better.
I am glad to have moved back to North Mankato now that the session adjourned, and to see all of Minnesota getting back to normal at work, school, and play.
This is a great state and I hope you have a great rest of 2021.

State Senator Nick Frentz 2021 CAPITOL REPORT Leadership How I voted on Budget Bills and Why: Agriculture: YES vote This bipartisan bill promotes and protects our farmers and farm economy.
It expanded meat processing capabilities, fully funded the Dept. of Agriculture, and expanded the Board of Animal Health to include one additional livestock producer.
We also extended the farmer-lender mediation act to 2027, and renewed funding to two of my focal points – Greater Mankato Growth’s GreenSeam, and MN farm safety grants.
Energy & Commerce: YES vote This was my first year as the Ranking Member on the Energy and Utilities Committee, and I worked closely negotiating the final bill language.
I’m satisfied with what was in it – solar on schools and colleges, the Natural Gas Innovation Act, and my bill establishing a $5 million state building effieciency revolving loan fund.
What was missing was obvious: stronger action to fight climate change.
Education: YES vote This was the largest funding increase in the last 15 years - $462 million over 2 years - that included the continuation of 4,000 voluntary PreK slots across the state, funding for the Teachers of Colors Act, and maximizing school nutrition.
Assistant DFL Leader I was proud to be elected again as an Assistant Minority Leader in the Senate.
I bring a voice from southern Minnesota to represent our interests at the Legislature.
Higher Education: YES vote This bill supports Minnesota State Mankato, South Central College, Gustavus, and Bethany Luthern.
It includes a $56.4 million increase for the MinnState system to deal with budget shortfalls due to COVID-19, and a tuition cap of 3.5% through the next two academic years.
Legacy Finance: YES vote Legacy funding - $645.62 million - is for the four ‘legacy’ funds: Arts, Cultural Heritage, History, and clean water.
Provisions included are Forever Green, Veterans Voices, Appetite for Change, Minnesota Historical Society, MN State Arts Board, and especiallly the Children Museum of Southern Minnesota in Mankato.
Taxes: YES vote Provided over a billion dollars in tax cuts, including full federal conformity tax forgiveness for PPP loans for small businesses and for unemployment compensation (up to $10,200) for our working men and women.
It was made possible by our strong state economy and the federal funds we received in the American Rescue Plan.
I strongly support the distribution of $250 million additional funds to our frontline workers.
We made adjustments during the pandemic, including holding committee and constituent meetings over Zoom.
Committee Assignments I proudly serve on the Senate Agriculture and Rural Development Finance and Policy Committee, the Energy and Utilities Finance and Policy Committee (ranking DFL member), Rules and Administration Committee, Pensions Committee, and the Legislative Audit Commission.
