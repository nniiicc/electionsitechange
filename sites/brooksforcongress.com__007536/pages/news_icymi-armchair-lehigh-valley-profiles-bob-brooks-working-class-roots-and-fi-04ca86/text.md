The story shows how his life as a working man has shaped his campaign to bring down costs for Lehigh Valley families and beat Ryan Mackenzie.
Some highlights from the story include: On why Bob is running : “It’s time we had someone representing the Lehigh Valley in Congress who actually understands the challenges working people are facing.” On Bob’s story : “I understand because I lived them.
I’ve lived in public housing.
I’ve relied on food stamps when times were hard.
I had to choose between paying the medical bills and paying the mortgage.
I worked hard and I made it out of the fire.” On Bob standing up for workers : “I fought for better wages, better healthcare and better working conditions for my members.
Now I want to take that experience with me to the halls of Congress where we can actually pass laws that actually work for working people.” On affordability : “Frankly, it’s the reason I got into this race.” On SNAP : “[Mackenzie] completely pulled out the rug from thousands of our neighbors who count on SNAP to survive.
The cruelty of it all is deeply sickening.” On Ryan Mackenzie’s record : “He is the reason why it costs more than $4 a gallon to fill up at the gas pump.
He campaigned on a promise to lower costs and invest here at home.
Instead, he’s voted for a cost-spiking war of choice in Iran that has made everything more expensive.” On Mackenzie voting for the One Big Beautiful Bill and then sponsoring a discharge petition to reverse its health care cuts , Bob compared him to “an arsonist calling the fire department after he already lit the match.” Read the full profile here . – Share About Bob Brooks Bob spent 20 years as a firefighter in the City of Bethlehem.
He’s held nearly every position on the truck and every post in his union, from shift rep to president of the Pennsylvania Professional Fire Fighters Association, where he now represents over 8,000 firefighters across the Commonwealth.
Among Bob’s proudest victories is helping lead the fight to pass mental health coverage for firefighters struggling with PTSI — a ten-year battle he refused to give up on.
Bob was raised primarily by a single mom who worked as a bartender to keep food on the table.
By the time he turned 30, Bob had already held more jobs than most people do in a lifetime: dishwasher, short-order cook, bartender, landscaper, warehouse worker, pizza delivery driver.
He moved to Pennsylvania more than #ago and never looked back.
Bob also runs Brooks Lawn Care, a small family-operated snow removal and lawn care business he started in 2013.
Whether plowing driveways at 4 a.m. or mowing lawns after a firefighting shift, Bob has never stopped hustling to provide for his family.
When he’s not working, you’ll find Bob on the baseball field, where he’s coached youth and varsity baseball for over two decades.
Or maybe you’ll spot his tattoo — a bulldog in a fire helmet — which says just about everything you need to know about his grit, his loyalty, and his love for the job.
Bob’s never had a silver spoon — just calloused hands, a deep sense of duty, and an unshakeable commitment to do what’s right.
In Washington, Bob won’t only talk about fighting for working people — he’ll live it every day, just like he always has.
