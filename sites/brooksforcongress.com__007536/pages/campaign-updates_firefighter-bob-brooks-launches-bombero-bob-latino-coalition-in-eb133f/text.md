Bethlehem, PA – Today, firefighter and Democratic nominee for Pennsylvania’s 7th Congressional District Bob Brooks launched Bombero Bob, a coalition of Latino community leaders and local elected officials from across the Greater Lehigh Valley working to send him to Congress.
Watch footage of the full press conference here.
“I’m honored to have the support of an incredible coalition of Latino leaders across the Lehigh Valley.
My pledge to our Latino community is simple: I promise to show up, listen, and fight for you and your family just as hard as I fight for my own,” said firefighter Bob Brooks.
“Whether it’s been over a meal at El Tipico, at Fiesta on Hamilton, or celebrating Dominican Independence Day, I’ve had the privilege of speaking with so many members of our local Latino community on the campaign trail about what issues matter most.
What I have heard consistently is that too many families here are getting squeezed by rising costs while career politicians like Ryan Mackenzie sell us out.
I understand the challenges that working Pennsylvanians are facing because I’ve lived them myself, and I’m ready to fight for hardworking Latino families in the halls of Congress.
I’m proud to stand with these leaders today, and I’m committing to fighting for this community every single day I’m in Congress.”
Since day one, engaging Latino voters has been a top priority of Bob’s campaign.
Latino and Hispanic Pennsylvanians make up nearly 23% of the district, and they will be key in electing him to Congress.
Bombero Bob coalition members include:
- Matt Tuerk, Mayor of Allentown
- Cynthia Mota, Allentown City Council Vice President
- Cristian Pungo, Allentown City Council Member
- Lou Jimenez, President of Bethlehem IAFF Local 735
- Claudia & Juan Parrales De Leon, Community Activists from the Lehigh County Democratic Committee
- Nestor Tatis, community activist
- Cesar Nieves, SEIU Local 32BJ
- Victor Martinez, La Mega 101.7FM
- Julian Guridy, former candidate for the Pennsylvania House of Representatives
- Patty Torres, Co-Executive Director of Make the Road Action Pennsylvania
The Bombero Bob coalition will work alongside Bob to reach Latino voters across PA-07 on the issues that are front and center to the community: lowering the rising cost of living, expanding economic opportunity for local small businesses and workers, boosting access to health care, repairing our broken immigration system, and pushing back on the culture of corruption in Washington that has left working families behind while the rich get richer.
In conjunction with the announcement of Bombero Bob, Bob joined Victor Martinez at 9:30 AM on Wednesday morning for an interview on La Mega 101.7FM.

Bob spent 20 years as a firefighter in the City of Bethlehem.
He’s held nearly every position on the truck and every post in his union, from shift rep to president of the Pennsylvania Professional Fire Fighters Association, where he now represents over 8,000 firefighters across the Commonwealth.
Among Bob’s proudest victories is helping lead the fight to pass mental health coverage for firefighters struggling with PTSI — a ten-year battle he refused to give up on.
Bob was raised primarily by a single mom who worked as a bartender to keep food on the table.
By the time he turned 30, Bob had already held more jobs than most people do in a lifetime: dishwasher, short-order cook, bartender, landscaper, warehouse worker, pizza delivery driver.
He moved to Pennsylvania more than 30 years ago and never looked back.
Bob also runs Brooks Lawn Care, a small family-operated snow removal and lawn care business he started in 2013.
Whether plowing driveways at 4 a.m. or mowing lawns after a firefighting shift, Bob has never stopped hustling to provide for his family.
When he’s not working, you’ll find Bob on the baseball field, where he’s coached youth and varsity baseball for over two decades.
Or maybe you’ll spot his tattoo — a bulldog in a fire helmet — which says just about everything you need to know about his grit, his loyalty, and his love for the job.
Bob’s never had a silver spoon — just calloused hands, a deep sense of duty, and an unshakeable commitment to do what’s right.
In Washington, Bob won’t only talk about fighting for working people — he’ll live it every day, just like he always has.
