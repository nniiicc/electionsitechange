Nazareth, PA – Tonight, firefighter Bob Brooks won the Democratic nomination for Pennsylvania’s 7th Congressional District and will take on vulnerable Republican Congressman Ryan Mackenzie in November.
Bob issued the following statement on his primary victory:
“I spent 20 years running into burning buildings as a Bethlehem firefighter because nothing is more important to me than protecting the community I love.
I’ve lived the challenges of regular people, and I know personally what it’s like to work multiple jobs at a time just to get by.
I’ve worked hard to make it out of the fire, and today I’m honored to stand tall as the Democratic nominee to represent the hardworking men and women of the Lehigh Valley and Carbon County in Congress.
This is a victory for every person across our community who has felt left behind, is tired of a broken Washington and is ready to send more working class voices to Congress to fight for us.
“My opponent Congressman Ryan Mackenzie is a career politician who lied to our faces when he campaigned on a bs promise to lower costs.
The price of everything from gas to groceries to health care has skyrocketed while Mackenzie’s has been in Washington, and he has voted to spend billions of dollars in Iran while our community gets left behind, gas prices rise, and American lives are lost.
Working people across the Lehigh Valley are struggling as Mackenzie does the bidding of Donald Trump, his D.C. party bosses, and the corporate donors that own him.
Enough.
It’s time to send one of us to Congress to actually fight for our families and make life more affordable.
In November, we’re going to get the job done.”
In his victory speech remarks, Bob thanked Lamont McClure, Ryan Crosswell, and Carol Obando-Derstine for running spirited campaigns focused on holding Mackenzie accountable.
Bob looks forward to uniting support behind the shared mission of defeating Mackenzie in November.

Bob spent 20 years as a firefighter in the City of Bethlehem.
He’s held nearly every position on the truck and every post in his union, from shift rep to president of the Pennsylvania Professional Fire Fighters Association, where he now represents over 8,000 firefighters across the Commonwealth.
Among Bob’s proudest victories is helping lead the fight to pass mental health coverage for firefighters struggling with PTSI — a ten-year battle he refused to give up on.
Bob was raised primarily by a single mom who worked as a bartender to keep food on the table.
By the time he turned 30, Bob had already held more jobs than most people do in a lifetime: dishwasher, short-order cook, bartender, landscaper, warehouse worker, pizza delivery driver.
He moved to Pennsylvania more than 30 years ago and never looked back.
Bob also runs Brooks Lawn Care, a small family-operated snow removal and lawn care business he started in 2013.
Whether plowing driveways at 4 a.m. or mowing lawns after a firefighting shift, Bob has never stopped hustling to provide for his family.
When he’s not working, you’ll find Bob on the baseball field, where he’s coached youth and varsity baseball for over two decades.
Or maybe you’ll spot his tattoo — a bulldog in a fire helmet — which says just about everything you need to know about his grit, his loyalty, and his love for the job.
Bob’s never had a silver spoon — just calloused hands, a deep sense of duty, and an unshakeable commitment to do what’s right.
In Washington, Bob won’t only talk about fighting for working people — he’ll live it every day, just like he always has.
