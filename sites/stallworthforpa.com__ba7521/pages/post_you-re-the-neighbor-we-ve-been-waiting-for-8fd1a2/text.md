People are tired of being talked at instead of talked to .
They grow weary of hearing promises that sound good during campaign season and disappear once the election is over.
They are exhausted from watching leaders fight for attention while families try to afford groceries, pay rent, get their kids safely through school, and care for aging parents.
They’ve lost belief that government even tries to work for regular people.
So when someone tells me they stopped voting, I don't start by shaming them.
I start by listening.
Most people care deeply, especially about what happens in their community.
Apathy isn’t intentional.
At its core, it’s human nature to care about one another.
People don’t usually disconnect from politics because they don't care.
On the contrary, many are exhausted because they care deeply .
They showed up to town hall meetings, talked to their neighbors, and worked for change: they felt like they did all they could…and in the end, they still walked away disappointed.
Maybe you looked at politics and saw people arguing for attention while your family was trying to keep up with rent, child care, gas, and everything else getting more expensive.
Maybe you wanted to vote, but you were working, parenting, caregiving, grieving, or simply trying to get through the week.
Or maybe you thought, with politicians just lying to get ahead, there wasn’t really anything worth voting for.
Many of us have felt that for too long, the people asking for your vote weren’t really seeing your life.
That’s completely understandable.
However, I also believe this: your community still needs you.
This campaign has never been about political performance.
It has been about relationships.
That’s what we saw in the primary.
People came out to vote because Stallworth wasn’t ever just a name on a ballot.
Voters turned out to vote for a neighbor.
They voted for Dan’s daughter.
Wanda’s daughter.
Heath’s sister.
Chris’s wife.
They voted for a neighbor, a friend, a familiar face, and someone who understands that the 199th District is not just a district number.
This is home.
We’re people.
Neighbors.
Family.
Community.
One of the stories I’ve been told that really resonated with me came from a man I spoke with at the gym.
He told me it was his daughter’s first time voting, and she was happy to vote for someone she actually wanted to vote for.
I have thought a lot about that and what it means for this campaign.
I believe this story illustrates what it looks like when hope starts to come back.
When people start to believe that there’s a candidate who will actually listen and support them.
Sometimes hope comes back quietly, through one person deciding to register, one young voter feeling proud to participate, one neighbor telling another neighbor, “I think this time could be different.” That’s also how trust begins to rebuild.
Trust comes back when people feel seen and listened to.
It comes back when a candidate knows the streets, the schools, the families, the churches, the small businesses, the worries, and the hopes of the people she wants to represent.
There are people in the 199th District who care deeply about this community but are not sure whether their vote helps.
Some are newly registered and still trying to understand where they fit.
Some used to vote regularly but stopped because they felt ignored.
Some believe in service, fairness, working families, safe schools, clean water, affordable housing, and opportunity, but they’re tired of feeling like politics doesn’t have room for them.
I want to say this clearly: There is room for you here.
Bring your disappointment and your questions.
Bring your hope, even if it is only a little bit of hope right now.
Bring your people and most importantly, bring your voice.
Come back to the table.
The table belongs to all of us.
Politics doesn’t belong to the people who already feel powerful.
Democracy shouldn’t feel like a locked room where regular people are expected to wait outside.
Public service should never become so far removed from real life that people stop believing their voice can make any difference.
But we have to change this by claiming it together.
Voting is part of that work, but it is not the only part.
So is volunteering.
So is donating if you are able.
So is knocking on one door, making one call, sharing one post, talking to one neighbor, bringing one friend to vote, or helping one person understand what is at stake.
You don't have to do everything…but this community needs you to do something.
I am running for the Pennsylvania House because I believe the 199th District deserves leadership rooted in service, accountability, and real life.
We deserve representation that knows us before asking for our vote.
We deserve someone who understands that our families, our schools, our water, our housing, our roads, our farmland, our jobs, and our future all share one thing in common… They are our lives.
Lives which deserve to be represented with care, courage, and honesty.
I know politics can be disappointing.
I also know there are many valid reasons for stepping away from it.
However, I am asking you to consider coming back because your community is worth fighting for and it deserves your vote.
It’s not just names on the ballot.
Your community is on the ballot, too.
The people you love are on the ballot.
The future of our schools is on the ballot.
The safety of our neighborhoods is on the ballot.
The cost of living is on the ballot.
Whether people in Harrisburg listen to communities like ours is on the ballot.
We have already seen what happens when people decide to show up.
We saw it in the primary.
People participated because they believed this campaign was rooted in something real.
Voters felt connected, not because of political noise, but because of relationships.
We saw proof that when people recognize themselves in a campaign, they are willing to come back.
Now we have to keep going.
With love for this community and a commitment to one another.
If you believe, as I do, that Pennsylvania’s 199th District deserves better, I am asking you to be part of helping us build it.

Bring your disappointment, your questions, your hope, and your people.
Vote.

Donate.

You don't have to do everything, but this community needs you to do something.
