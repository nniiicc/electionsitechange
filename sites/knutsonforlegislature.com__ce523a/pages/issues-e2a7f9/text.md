Nebraska is facing a $471 million - and growing - deficit.
Prioritizing corporate tax giveaways, tariffs, and federal cuts has landed us here.
Common sense says we need to re-examine incoming revenue streams, closely look into places we can trim, and spend our money where we receive the greatest return - our people.
I am suspicious of proposals that suggest we can currently afford a 50% property tax reduction.
The math just does not add up.
I will work towards sensible budgeting that prioritizes people, not corporations.
Like the majority of Nebraska voters, I strongly support keeping public funding for public schools.
Our public schools accept EVERY child, something I am particularly passionate about as a special education teacher.
All children deserve a quality education across our state, and I will be a strong advocate for them.
Research has shown technology, such as one-to-one Chromebook use, has not been an effective tool in our schools.
Students retain and perform much better with tangible learning- whether that is pen, paper, reading from a physical book, creating a diorama, etc.
Growing concerns about attention span, fine motor skills, emotional regulation, and reduced social engagement also accompany high-tech use.
Technology has a place and a time, but we must ensure its adoption and use serve students' best interests and support learning, not detract from it.
As new technology continues to emerge, such as AI, it is all the more important we provide guardrails.
I propose we adopt a bill tasking our state department of education with crafting policies and guidelines for data-backed technological use in the classroom for each grade level.
