Meet Caitlin Issues Volunteer Contribute ISSUES Skyrocketing valuations and property taxes are hitting our wallets and hurting our ability to buy homes, keep our homes, or run the family farm.
Between 2020 and 2024, the media property tax bill in Cass County rose 14.5%, while the median property tax bill in Lancaster county rose 25.6%, while the median home value climbed 44.8%.
Nebraskans can't afford more empty promises when it comes to property taxes.
It's time for change.
Here's my common-sense, fiscally conservative plan: we must expand the Homestead Exemption because it is working.
We can and must deliver broad-based relief for all homeowners by closing special interest tax loopholes that primarily benefit the wealthy and well-connected.
Hold the line on spending and do more with less and laser in on waste, fraud, and abuse of our money.
In my household and at my kitchen table, affordability ins't just a talking point, it's part of our life.
My husband and I are working middle class raising our two boys in the community we love.
It is unsustainable that utilities are skyrocketing, gas, diesel, and groceries are more expensive, and property taxes and home insurance in Nebraska are among the highest in the nation.
Nebraskan is presently ranked as the most unaffordable state for child care.
It doesn't have to be this way.
This is the result of out of touch politicians that deliver for the wealthy and well-connected and don't center middle-class families, seniors, and family farmers.
Child tax credits should be established to empower families and support our workforce challenges.
Childcare workers should be able to utilize the child care program in Nebraska, which is a proven policy tool in other states.
We should cap medical debt and shelter the family home from medical bills that pile up for non-elective care.
We should repeal the misguided new state tax on diesel.
There are things that we can and should do to make life more affordable for everyday Nebraskans.
Big tech data centers are causing our utility bills to skyrocket over 20%, and their massive demand is causing the problem, and yet their rates remain flat.
That's not right in a state that has public power.
Data centers are also threatening our land, water, and way of life.
Luckily, local citizens are organizing and speaking out to get moratoriums to protect themselves.
But we need to and can do more.
I am the only candidate who has not wavered in demanding an end to NDAs to ensure full transparency so local communities can act faster with all the necessary information.
I will fight to end sweetheart tax deals for big tech data centers that have cost our state budget over $5 million.
That money should go to real property tax relief and balancing the budget instead.
I will fight to make sure data centers pay for their power and don't pass the cost onto us.
Like the majority of Nebraska voters, I strongly support keeping public funding for public schools.
Our public schools accept EVERY child, something I am particularly passionate about as a special education teacher.
All children, whether they live in Manley, Louisville, Elmwood, Lincoln, or anywhere else, deserve a quality education across our state, and I will be a strong advocate for them.
In a democracy, when we vote on an issue, it's not a suggestion, it's the law.
The people of Nebraska are the second house.
Politicians in Lincoln have tried to overturn and undermine the will of the people on critical issues like medical cannabis, private school vouchers, paid sick leave, and modest minimum wage increases to keep pace with inflation.
One of the most sacred obligations of elected officials is to respect the will of their constituents.
As your State Senator, I would vow to uphold the will of the voters in our District and work to implement a safe and accessible medical cannabis program for suffering patients, reject the spending of our tax dollars on private schools, and work to implement paid sick leave and minimum wage increases as the voters intended.
Research has shown technology, such as one-to-one Chromebook use, has not been an effective tool in our schools.
Students retain and perform much better with tangible learning- whether that is pen, paper, reading from a physical book, creating a diorama, etc.
Growing concerns about attention span, fine motor skills, emotional regulation, and reduced social engagement also accompany high-tech use.
Technology has a place and a time, but we must ensure its adoption and use serve students' best interests and support learning, not detract from it.
As new technology continues to emerge, such as AI, it is all the more important we provide guardrails.
I propose we adopt a bill tasking our state Department of Education with crafting policies and guidelines for data-backed technological use in the classroom for each grade level.
Many of us worry about the world our children and grandchildren will inherit.
In an age of discord, one commonality among us is the desire for a better future for them.
Nebraskans work hard to provide for our families.
In exchange, our families deserve access to healthy food, healthcare, high-quality childcare, clean air and water, and an economy that provides freedom and opportunity.
I've knocked on more than 10,000 doors during this campaign, and I've heard YOUR concerns.
We have so much more in common than what divides us.
