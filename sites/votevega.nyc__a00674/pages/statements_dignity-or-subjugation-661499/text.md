Donate Now → To my neighbors in the Bronx: This is Jose Vega, Independent Congressional Candidate running in New York’s 15th Congressional District against Ritchie Torres.
By now, you have all probably heard the story of Maricarmen Abascal, an 87-year-old woman in Spain who was forcibly evicted from her apartment (where she had lived for over 70 years!) after an investment fund, which had purchased her building, demanded a 230% rent hike!
Her story has unfortunately become commonplace in Spain, Germany, and the United States, with many people seeing their rents and the cost of homes surging in recent years.
A video of Abascal, who is wheelchair-bound, showing her being forcibly removed from her apartment on a stretcher circulated worldwide.
She appealed from her hospital bed: “Please fight!” She said, “I haven’t managed to defend my home, but I have fought so the others can learn to fight for what is theirs!“ People in Spain responded.
Large numbers of people showed up two days later outside Abascal’s building to protest.
The long-simmering anger against rent hikes and unaffordable housing ignited.
Soon, there were protests in over 50 cities in Spain, bringing hundreds of thousands of people out into the street, with signs that read, “Not one more eviction, not one less neighbor, we are all Maricarmen” and “right to housing at a fair price.” Soon, the whole world was watching.
As a result, Maricarmen Abascal has returned to her apartment.
A power greater than money had prevailed.
A Spanish reporter asked Pope Leo XIV to comment about Abascal’s case on his flight back to Rome from France, and he responded, “How is it possible for something like this to happen?
What is wrong with a system that allows an elderly woman, who is also disabled or facing some difficulty, to be evicted from her home?” People heard his message.
After all, 1.2 million people had assembled to hear the Pope in Madrid on June 7, and hundreds of thousands more in other Spanish cities.
At the time of this writing, Maricarmen Abascal has passed away in a hospital.
She was to be returned to her apartment following the justified anger by the Spanish population against her eviction.
This was not an inevitable death; it was murder caused by depraved indifference.
Yes, “we are all Maricarmen.” We too, wherever we may be in the world, are victims of depraved indifference.
Many living in the South Bronx, where I live, are the poorest in what is termed [the poorest Congressional district in the nation.] ( https://www.cityandstateny.com/politics/2019/12/ny-has-the-richest-poorest-smallest-most-unequal-congressional-districts/176658/)35.8% of Bronx residents pay out half of their income on rent.
If you’re spending 50% just on rent every month, you don’t have money for food, clothing, and other necessities.
You can’t save anything.
You’re paying way more than you can handle.
You are being forced to become a debt slave, a criminal, or both.
You know the reality of Maricarmen Abascal’s own experience.
You know not only “the costs of living,” but the toll it takes on human life and dignity.
Evictions in Spain and Germany, rats in the schools in France, are also a daily experience in America, and in my district in particular.
But our so-called “representatives” in Congress, including Ritchie Torres, who himself came out of Bronx public housing, won’t deal with the root cause of the housing, immigration, and poverty crises—the Epstein class and their commitment to a dying, 500-year-old imperial system, with their unpayable debt, and endless wars.
