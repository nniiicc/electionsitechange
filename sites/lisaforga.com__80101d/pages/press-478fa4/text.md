May 6, 2026
KSU hosts Civil Political Dialogue event
Kennesaw State University’s School of Government and International Affairs hosted a Civil Political Dialogue event on April 29.
The event brought together politically diverse students and two local elected officials of opposing parties for a structured conversation on political extremism, political violence and the importance of civil discourse.
Held in the Academic Learning Center on KSU’s Kennesaw campus, the event featured State Rep.
Lisa Campbell, a Democrat, and State Sen.
Ed Setzler, a Republican.
The discussion was moderated by former Republican Georgia State Sen.
Don Balfour and Dr.
Eric Castater, a Professor of Political Science at KSU.
Sen.
Balfour currently serves as a Senior Advisor for the Georgia Democracy Resilience Network, a cross-partisan initiative launched and supported by the Carter Center to promote peaceful political engagement, trust in elections and community resilience against political violence.
Dr.
Castater ran as a Democrat for the Georgia State House in Cobb County in 2024.
Approximately 40 students attended the event, which was designed as a listening and learning experience with both academic and civic purposes.
The dialogue focused on the causes of rising political extremism and violence in America, the meaning of civility in politics, the responsibilities of leaders from both political parties and ways elected officials can model constructive disagreement.
Students also had the opportunity to ask questions during a moderated Q&A session.

February 5, 2026
On February 5, 2026, I joined GPB’s Lawmakers to discuss several pieces of legislation focused on addressing real challenges facing Georgia families.
As the State Representative for House District 35, I am committed to advancing practical solutions that strengthen financial stability, healthcare access, and opportunities for children.
I discussed the Medical Debt Protection Act (HB 765), which would help protect patients from predatory collection practices, establish reasonable payment plans, and give families time to recover without risking their homes, credit, or livelihoods.
I also spoke about legislation addressing prior authorization practices by insurers, Prohibit Prior Authorization (HB 654), aimed at ensuring that medically necessary tests, procedures, and prescriptions ordered by a healthcare provider are not delayed or denied, so that medical decisions remain between patients and their doctors.
In addition, I highlighted the proposed Georgia Children First Trust Fund (HR 396), which would invest a portion of the state’s reserves to create a sustainable funding source for early learning and childcare—supporting families, providers, and Georgia’s workforce.
These are kitchen-table issues affecting families in every community.
I remain committed to working with colleagues on both sides of the aisle to advance policies that strengthen families, expand opportunity, and build a healthier, more prosperous Georgia for everyone.
