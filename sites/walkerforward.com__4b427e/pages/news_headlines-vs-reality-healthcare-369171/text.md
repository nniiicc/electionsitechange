Reality: Healthcare Jul 22 Written By Brian Walker I wanted this one to be about all of us.
Instead, the first thing Republicans lead with on healthcare is opposing transgender medical procedures for anyone under 18, and keeping biological males out of girls' sports.
I left the party over things like this.
Not because I don't have opinions on either topic, I do, but because when "healthcare policy" opens with two culture war fights before it says one word about the cost of your insulin, you've told me what the priority actually is.
Next up: parental rights, home state jurisdiction, and making sure a woman sees an ultrasound before an abortion.
Again, not what I'd call healthcare.
That's control dressed up as policy.
Then, buried at the bottom, they finally get to something real.
Tax-exempt premiums.
Tax-deductible health expenses.
I could get behind both of those on their own.
But then they say they want to scrap the ACA, and I'm out.
Competition alone doesn't fix this.
I'd point anyone curious to a piece out of Stanford on how health insurance turned from protecting patients to chasing profit.
Worth ten minutes.
I've got no problem with a company making money.
They employ people.
They have payroll to make.
Here's what I do have a problem with: families in this district whose paycheck goes straight to a premium, and then when they actually need to use it, get told no.
There was a time Blue Cross kept a nickel of every premium dollar for overhead.
Today, under the ACA's own rules, insurers can keep up to 15 to 20 cents of every dollar before it has to go to actual care.
That's not a scandal, that's the legal ceiling.
Meanwhile rural hospitals are closing wings and cutting services, and the bill lands on the people who live here.
While that's happening, the money at the top keeps climbing.
Mayo Clinic posted a record $1.5 billion in profit in 2025.
Sanford Health, expanding into Minnesota, posted $1.1 billion.
UnitedHealth just posted $5.48 billion in net income for a single quarter.
Somebody's getting rich off this system.
It isn't the family paying the premium and it isn't the hospital in a small town trying to keep its doors open.
And this is the part that gets me.
While all that money moves around at the top, the loudest thing coming out of St.
Paul is a fight over ultrasound pictures and who gets to play girls' sports.
I understand people have strong feelings on both.
I'm not asking anyone to drop those feelings.
I'm asking why that's the headline instead of the henhouse.
Bring healthcare back to people, not shareholders.
That's not a radical idea.
That's just what I think we owe each other in this district.
