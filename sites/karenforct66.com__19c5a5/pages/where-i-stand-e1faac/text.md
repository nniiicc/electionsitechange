Broad based tax reforms are needed to provide relief for middle-class families and inspire the entrepreneurial spirit that once gave Connecticut a booming economy.
Excessive and ever increasing fees must be reduced or eliminated.
Tax incentives rewarding job-creators attract investment and build a self-sufficient, tax-paying workforce.
Less government intrusion equals more innovation and opportunity.
JOBS, not promises, pay bills.
Building relationships with “Main Street” businesses creates stronger communities.
Public Safety The only honest conversation about public safety begins with acknowledging the lawful public should not have to fear for their safety and security and criminals should not have to fear bias based on physical traits.
The judicial system must ensure the balance between victims’ and perpetrators’ rights.
Law enforcement officers must feel free to meet the public’s expectations while respecting the limits of their authority.
Punishment needs to be commensurate with the crime regardless of who is judged to have broken the law.
Building relationships with Law Enforcement officials creates stronger communities.
Local Land-use Authority Connecticut’s current definition of “affordable housing” protects neither access to home ownership nor the environment.
State mandated one-size-fits-all regulations requiring deed restrictions and allowing high-density building defy reason given Connecticut’s environmental and housing-stock diversity.
Contrast small towns with no water or sewer infrastructure to cities with established sanitary and community water systems.
Whether Colonial-era homes or modern single and multi-family dwellings, it should not matter WHEN a unit was built or whether it is “deed-restricted,” only that it meets the economic definition of “affordable”.
Building relationships that protect local interests creates stronger communities.
The Second Amendment Federal and State Constitutional protections guarantee law abiding citizens retain their right to keep and bear arms for all lawful purposes, including self defense.
The State of Connecticut is among those with the most restrictive gun laws in the country.
Creating more gun laws is not the answer to crime.
Enforcement of existing gun laws, REQUIRING punishment of criminal firearm misuse, more accessible mental health care, and securing our schools in the same manner we do our government buildings should be our goals.
Building relationships based on equal rights creates stronger communities.
Bias in Education History is fact.
Interpretation of historical fact is subjective.
History in all its glory and ugliness should be taught without bias.
Human evolution is more than biological because we have the ability to reason.
Students struggling with reading and comprehension lack the ability to understand social constructs that are part of every current discussion about history and its presentation.
Unbiased teaching of historical fact is required to ensure we progress toward the open and inclusive society that protects us all equally.
Building relationships with our neighbors stronger communities.
Small Business & Farms Supporting Connecticut Farms and Small Businesses For more than 30 years, Karen Reddington-Hughes has experienced firsthand the challenges and responsibilities of owning a small business.
She understands what it means to serve customers, manage rising costs, comply with regulations, and make the difficult decisions necessary to keep a business operating.
Karen also understands the importance of tourism to Connecticut’s local economy.
Her work promoting our region was recognized with a Governor’s Award for Tourism in 2017.
She knows that our farms, village centers, restaurants, shops, historic sites, and recreational destinations are not separate economic interests.
Together, they create local jobs, attract visitors, preserve the character of our communities, and keep money circulating close to home.
Connecticut’s farms and small businesses do not need more mandates from Hartford.
They need a state government that listens, responds promptly, and allows them to grow.
Karen will continue to champion policies that: Cut red tape and simplify permitting.
State agencies should provide clear requirements, reasonable deadlines, and a single point of contact whenever possible.
Business owners and farmers should not have to navigate multiple agencies or wait indefinitely for routine permits and approvals.
Control taxes, fees, energy costs, and unfunded mandates.
Connecticut cannot claim to support small businesses while continually increasing the cost of operating them.
Karen will oppose policies that place disproportionate financial and administrative burdens on family farms and locally owned businesses.
Protect working farms and agricultural land.
Farmland preservation must be paired with policies that help farming remain economically viable.
Karen supports improving access to farmland, protecting the farm-property tax classification, and helping the next generation of Connecticut farmers enter and remain in agriculture.
Strengthen Connecticut Grown and farm-to-market opportunities Schools, institutions, restaurants, retailers, and consumers should have greater opportunities to purchase products grown and produced in Connecticut.
State programs should expand markets for local farmers without imposing excessive paperwork or favoring large operations over small family farms.
Support agritourism and the visitor economy.
Farm stands, orchards, vineyards, seasonal events, historic destinations, inns, restaurants, and locally owned shops bring visitors into our communities and support employment throughout the region.
Karen will promote responsible agritourism while protecting local character and respecting municipal authority.
Invest in local jobs and workforce development.
Career and technical education, agricultural education, apprenticeships, and partnerships between schools and local employers can prepare young people for good careers while helping businesses find qualified workers.
Give small businesses a voice before regulations are adopted.
Proposed laws and regulations should be evaluated for their cost and practical effect on small employers and farms.
Karen supports requiring meaningful small-business impact reviews and giving affected owners a genuine opportunity to be heard.
Preserve local control.
The people closest to a community usually understand its needs best.
Decisions involving land use, economic development, farming, and community character should not be unnecessarily centralized in Hartford.
Karen believes Connecticut should measure economic policy by a straightforward standard: Does it make it easier or harder for a farmer, tradesman, shopkeeper, restaurant owner, or family business to remain here, create jobs, and pass that business to the next generation?
As a longtime small-business owner, Karen will continue to be a practical and independent voice for the people who work, invest, take risks, and create opportunity in our communities.
Karen Reddington-Hughes: Your voice for the district.
