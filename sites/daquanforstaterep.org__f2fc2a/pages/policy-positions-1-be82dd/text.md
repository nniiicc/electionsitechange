Rising rents, mortgages, and property taxes are pushing families to the brink while development struggles to keep up with rising material costs, zoning barriers, and slow permitting.
As State Representative, I will fight for increased state funding for District 39 through the biennial budget, prioritizing both renters and homeowners.
That funding would strengthen the Ohio Housing Trust Fund, which supports new construction, rental assistance, homelessness prevention, and emergency repairs, especially for low-income families, veterans, seniors, and people with disabilities.
It’s also smart economics, generating $9.50 for every dollar invested.
I will also expand the Welcome Home Ohio Program and support local organizations like Miami Valley Community Action Partnership, United Way of Greater Dayton, The Foodbank, and the YWCA.
Finally, I will push to repeal or amend House Bill 430 to restore basic protections against unchecked rent increases.
Housing should be attainable, not a pipe dream, for the next generation .
Universal health care Health care should be a right, not a privilege.
I support creating a statewide health care plan that uses existing federal funding from Medicare, Medicaid, and the Affordable Care Act to guarantee a universal standard of care for Ohioans.
This plan would work alongside or compete with private insurance, offering coverage at a deeply reduced cost or completely free, depending on available revenue. ​ By leveraging federal legislation such as the State-Based Universal Health Care Act, Ohio can apply for waivers that allow us to reallocate existing tax dollars without creating new taxes.
Working with our federal partners, we can build a 10-year plan to reach at least 95% coverage within five years, with a strong focus on preventive care.
No one should be denied care or priced out so corporations can post record profits.
Your life and your health are not for sale.
Protect and expand social safety nets Programs like TANF, SNAP, Medicaid, and Social Security are lifelines for Ohio families.
Without them, Ohio’s poverty rate would jump from about 10.5% to nearly 28%.
Nearly one in four Ohioans relies on these programs, and it is our responsibility as leaders to protect them, not demonize the people who need them.
I will stand firmly against attempts to weaken these programs under the guise of preventing waste, fraud, and abuse, when the real impact is to cut off support for those already struggling.
I will also fight to expand funding for critical services like Head Start, which is operating on emergency funds and facing closure, putting tens of thousands of children at risk.
Supporting families isn’t a handout; it’s an investment in Ohio’s future.
Policies that reduce wealth inequality ​ Wealth inequality is crushing working Ohioans, and it has been ignored for far too long.
Right now, the top 1% in Ohio earns as much as the entire bottom 50% combined.
While top earners average about $850,000 a year, the average Ohioan earns about $46,000, and that gap continues to widen under policies such as Ohio’s flat tax.
This flat tax overwhelmingly benefits the wealthy, delivering nearly 96% of the gains to the top earners while offering little to no benefit to everyone else, all at a cost of nearly $1 billion in lost state revenue.
That is not fair and not sustainable.
As State Representative, I will introduce legislation to repeal these harmful policies and replace them with a truly progressive tax system that ensures the wealthiest Ohioans pay their fair share without placing the burden on working and middle-class families.
Fair, livable wages tied to inflation ​ Why is it that working Ohioans still can’t afford to live in Ohio?
Our state minimum wage is $11 an hour, better than the federal $7.25, but still nowhere near enough to keep up with inflation.
Today, nearly 25% of Ohioans spend half or more of their income just on housing.
Meanwhile, state assistance programs are effectively subsidizing corporations that refuse to pay livable wages.
Ohioans are tired of being told we can’t afford to pay workers enough to survive.
A business that can’t pay its employees a livable wage is not a sustainable business.
I will work with economists and use tools like the MIT Living Wage Calculator to establish a statewide minimum wage tied to inflation, one that reflects the real cost of food, housing, childcare, health care, and transportation.
Last year, Ohio’s minimum wage was at the poverty level.
That is unacceptable.
We must restore the purchasing power of working Ohioans.
Four-day work week Universal Free School Meals Protect and Restore Remote-Work Options Paid Work-Commute Education & Workforce Development ​​​​​​ Protection and restoration of remote-work options ​Ohio has moved backward on remote work.
Recent mandates requiring state employees to return to five-day, in-person workweeks were justified as productivity measures, but the data tells a different story.
Remote work has been shown to increase productivity, reduce burnout and turnover, and improve overall health and well-being, while also cutting transportation costs, childcare burdens, and environmental impact.
Where remote work is possible, it should be protected and expanded.
I will champion policies that restore flexible work options across Ohio, because supporting families, improving quality of life, and reducing our environmental footprint should be priorities, not afterthoughts.
Education should open doors, not create debt and barriers. ​ Daquan supports: ​ Free or low-cost trade schools Free or low-cost community college Expanded apprenticeships and vocational programs Accessible workforce certifications Expanded school-to-employment programs and pipelines Universal Free School Meals Ohio offers free school lunches only through limited income-based programs, but we do not have a universal standard for feeding our children.
Think about that.
A state that claims to value life and childcare still allows kids to go to school hungry based on their parents’ income.
I will introduce legislation modeled after Minnesota’s Free School Meals for Kids program to provide every public school student with one free breakfast and one free lunch, no paperwork, no stigma, no exceptions.
This policy is overwhelmingly popular, improves student outcomes, and ensures every child has what they need to learn and thrive.
Feeding our children should not be controversial; it should be the standard.
A four-day work week to improve quality of life​ A four-day workweek may sound ambitious, especially at a time when wages have stagnated, and many Ohioans are working more than 40 hours or multiple jobs just to get by.
But paired with a livable wage, it gives us a real opportunity to restore balance to people’s lives.
The average worker should not spend more than half their waking hours working just to survive.
Countries such as Belgium, Iceland, Japan, the United Kingdom, and Germany have demonstrated that a four-day workweek increases productivity, reduces burnout, lowers turnover, and improves overall well-being.
Ohio’s workforce has increased productivity by more than 200% over the last 30 years without a comparable rise in wages.
That’s not sustainable.
I will introduce legislation to allow Ohio to adopt a four-day workweek so workers can earn more, work less, and finally have time to live.
Paid work-commute policies​ Ohio workers give hours of their lives every week just getting to and from work, yet that time is treated as if it has no value.
I support paid work-commute policies that compensate workers for their travel time, even at a reduced rate, because commuting is time dedicated to employment, not personal leisure.
Right now, only certain workers, like contractors traveling between job sites, are compensated for job-related travel.
I will introduce legislation to reclassify work commutes as job-related travel, making them eligible for employer compensation.
This policy would not only respect workers’ time but also encourage more people to commute into Dayton for work, strengthening our local economy and supporting business growth.
