On Monday, February 2, 2026, my colleagues and I returned to the Georgia State Capitol for the fourth week of the legislative session.
The first three weeks of the session included Governor Kemp's State of the State, followed by a week of budget hearings in preparation for the Fiscal Year 2027 budget.
Each legislative session, the Georgia General Assembly’s sole constitutional obligation is to pass a balanced budget, which underscores the importance of our work thus far.Gov.
Kemp’s Amended Fiscal Year 2026 budget proposal is set by a revised revenue estimate of $42.3 billion.
Along with expected revenue growth, the governor’s AFY 2026 budget estimate includes $3.3 billion in undesignated surplus funds, resulting in a total revenue adjustment of $4.5 billion—or an 11.9 percent increase—over the original FY 2026 budget estimate.
The governor’s FY 2027 budget is set by a revenue estimate of $38.5 billion, representing an increase of $738 million—or two percent—over the original FY 2026 budget.
Highlights of Governor Kemp's budget presentation:
- Since 2021, more than $9.7 billion has been returned to Georgia taxpayers through rebates, income tax cuts and various relief measures.
The AFY 2026 budget proposal includes an additional one-time tax rebate that would return $1 billion to Georgia families to help offset the increased cost of living and ongoing pressure from higher prices in recent years.
This rebate would average approximately $250 for single filers, $375 for head-of-household filers, and $500 for married couples filing jointly.
- Allocation of $625 million to provide a one-time salary supplement of $2,000 to full-time, regular state employees and school personnel, recognizing their continued service to Georgia.
Of those funds, $384 million is designated for K-12 teachers, school nurses, nutrition workers and bus drivers, as well as Pre-K lead and assistant teachers.
- Timber Industry recovery support of $11 million to establish the Georgia Forestry Innovation Initiative, in partnership with the Georgia Institute of Technology, aimed at supporting the timber industry’s expansion into emerging markets through research, product testing and private partnerships.
As part of his focus on supporting the forestry sector and rural economic development, the governor’s AFY 2026 budget proposal also includes $35 million to establish a natural gas infrastructure pipeline program aimed at improving access and supporting the infrastructure needed for sustaining and expanding key industries across the state.
- The Technical College System of Georgia (TCSG) would receive an additional $34 million in formula funds to reflect an enrollment increase of 9.4 percent.
In addition, the Governor's FY 2027 budget proposal allocates an additional $58 million to fully fund the Helping Outstanding Pupils Educationally (HOPE) scholarship to 100 percent of its promise, which helped nearly a quarter of a million students attend post-secondary institutions last year.
- Investment of $325 million one-time endowment in the AFY 2026 budget proposal to establish the Dedicating Resources to Educationally Advance More Students (DREAMS) scholarship program—the first need-based aid program of its kind in Georgia history.
- The Department of Corrections is in desperate need of additional security upgrades, equipment, and vehicle replacements, and has proposed $194 million in its AFY 2026 budget.
In addition, $5 million will be designated to establish a defined contribution plan for State Law Enforcement Officers.
- Infrastructure improvements of $2.4 billion to expand and improve critical freight and commuter corridors.
These investments would go toward state-level projects, such as interchange conversions on Highway 316–University Parkway and the I-75 Henry County Express Lanes, as well as local transportation projects and the repair and replacement of rural bridges.
Georgia's Future Looks Bright
Georgia’s State Economist, Dr.
Robert Buschman, briefed legislators on the state’s revenue outlook, outlining various factors behind recent growth, as well as potential risks ahead.
- State revenues have increased by approximately $9 billion—about 36 percent—over the past five years, with nearly all of that growth occurring in FY 2021 and FY 2022.
- Georgia’s three largest revenue sources—personal income, corporate income and sales taxes—accounted for roughly 90 percent of 36% increase, highlighting that personal income and wage growth in Georgia have outpaced national averages, while sales tax revenues have remained strong through FY 2025.
- Georgia has outpaced the national unemployment rate for nearly six consecutive years, with a rate of 3.5 percent as of November 2025.
- Emphasized that consumers continue to feel the strain of rising costs for everyday necessities, explaining that incomes have not kept pace for the average worker and that any renewed inflationary pressures are likely to further challenge household budgets.
Property Tax Relief
Exciting news is the introduction of the Georgia HOME Act.
House Republicans have introduced the Homeownership Opportunity and Market Equalization Act of 2026 (Georgia HOME Act).
This legislation will provide property tax relief for homeowners across the state.
Additional details on the Georgia Home Act:
- Phase in an up to 100% exemption by 2032.
- Enable local grants to homeowners.
- Permit repurpose and use of existing sales tax and limited use of assessments for critical services as offsets.
- Permits the use of capital assessments subject to a referendum.
- Establishes guardrails for taxes on non-homestead properties.
This legislation is still under review and debate.
Once finalized and approved by the General Assembly and signed by the Governor, the Georgia HOME ACT will be placed before the voters as a ballot referendum.
