Some dispatch centers struggle with after‑hours staffing, and there are known cellular dead zones along state and county roads.
This Act directly targets those weaknesses with national minimum standards, federal funding, and enforceable accountability.
1.
Impact on 911 Reliability in Indiana Current situation: Multi‑hour 911 outages during storms and power failures.
Single‑path routing in some counties, where one fiber cut can disable 911.
Inconsistent backup power at towers, especially in rural areas.
Impact of the Act: Carriers serving Indiana must implement dual‑path routing for 911 traffic.
Towers and routing centers must maintain 8–24 hours of backup power.
Counties gain access to federal support for CAD, GIS, and NG911 upgrades.
Result: A more resilient 911 system that is less vulnerable to storms, fiber cuts, and power loss.
2.
Impact on Rural Counties in Southern Indiana The 9th District includes rural counties such as Washington, Orange, Crawford, Harrison, Lawrence, and parts of Monroe, Clark, and Floyd.
These areas face long response distances, limited staffing, and known dead zones.
Impact of the Act: Likely designation of several counties as 911 Priority Zones , unlocking remediation funds.
Carriers must map and fix dead zones along rural corridors and valleys.
Counties can access EMS staffing grants to maintain 24/7 coverage.
PSAPs receive support to replace outdated equipment and software.
Result: Rural residents gain more reliable 911 access and faster EMS response.
3.
Impact on EMS Availability Current situation: Some rural EMS agencies operate with only one or two units.
Overnight coverage often depends on mutual aid or volunteers.
Staffing shortages and burnout are common.
Impact of the Act: Each county receiving federal funds must maintain at least one fully staffed EMS unit 24/7.
Federal grants can be used for hiring, overtime, training, retention bonuses, and equipment.
Result: More consistent EMS coverage, especially in rural counties where “no one is available after 5 PM” has been a real‑world problem.
4.
Impact on PSAP Staffing Current situation: Some PSAPs in southern Indiana have only one dispatcher on duty overnight.
After‑hours coverage may rely on part‑time or on‑call staff.
High turnover and low pay contribute to chronic understaffing.
Impact of the Act: PSAPs must maintain 24/7 on‑site staffing with at least two trained telecommunicators.
Counties can apply for federal staffing grants to meet these requirements.
FECOO oversight and reporting requirements increase transparency and accountability.
Result: Faster call‑handling, safer workloads, and reduced risk of missed or dropped emergencies.
5.
Impact on Cellular Coverage and Dead Zones Current situation: Persistent dead zones in and around Hoosier National Forest.
Coverage gaps along SR‑135, SR‑37, SR‑56, SR‑64, SR‑66, and other rural routes.
Residents in valleys and hollows often cannot complete mobile 911 calls.
Impact of the Act: Carriers must map these dead zones and submit remediation plans.
New or upgraded infrastructure is required where coverage is inadequate.
Backup power and redundant routing are mandatory for critical sites.
Result: Improved mobile 911 reliability in areas that currently lack dependable service.
6.
Impact on Severe Weather Response Indiana regularly faces tornadoes, ice storms, derechos, flooding, and widespread power outages that stress emergency‑communications systems.
Impact of the Act: Backup power requirements keep towers and routing centers online during outages.
Redundant routing prevents multi‑county 911 failures from single points of failure.
Public outage notifications inform residents when 911 is impaired and provide alternatives.
Staffing requirements ensure EMS and dispatch coverage during severe weather.
Result: More resilient emergency communications when Indiana is under the greatest stress.
7.
Impact on Local Governments Costs Upgrading PSAP and EMS infrastructure to meet new standards.
Hiring and retaining sufficient staff for 24/7 coverage.
Meeting reporting and compliance obligations.
Federal support Grants covering up to 80% of eligible project costs.
Potential waivers of matching requirements for small or high‑need counties.
Technical assistance from FECOO for planning and implementation.
Savings Reduced liability exposure from 911 failures and delayed response.
Fewer emergency overtime spikes during outages and storms.
Improved mutual‑aid coordination and resource sharing.
Result: Local governments gain clearer standards, more predictable funding, and tools to stabilize critical services.
8.
Impact on First Responders More reliable 911 call delivery and dispatch.
Better caller location through improved GIS and NG911 systems.
Stronger cybersecurity and system resilience.
More consistent EMS and dispatch staffing to support field operations.
Result: Safer working conditions and more effective emergency response across Indiana.
9.
Impact on Indiana Residents More reliable access to 911 from both landline and mobile phones.
Faster and more accurate EMS and law‑enforcement response.
Public notifications and clear alternatives when 911 is impaired.
Greater confidence that someone is available after 5 PM, even in rural areas.
Bottom line: For Indiana, especially its rural and small‑town communities, the Act is designed to turn fragile, patchwork emergency‑communications systems into dependable, transparent, and enforceable public safety infrastructure. © # Floyd Taylor for Congress.
