The bill establishes national minimum standards for 911 uptime, routing redundancy, backup power, PSAP staffing, EMS availability, outage reporting, and public transparency.
It also creates a new federal oversight office to enforce compliance and administer modernization grants.
Purpose of the Legislation The legislation responds to persistent issues affecting communities nationwide, including: 911 outages during storms and power failures Rural dead zones where mobile 911 calls cannot be completed Dispatch centers that lack after‑hours staffing EMS units unavailable overnight in rural counties Inaccurate or delayed caller‑location information Lack of public notification during 911 outages Insufficient federal oversight and enforcement The bill aims to ensure that every 911 call is answered , every county has 24/7 EMS coverage , and the public is informed when 911 is impaired .
Key Provisions of the Bill National 911 Reliability Standards Requires 99.999% uptime for 911 routing systems Mandates dual‑path redundant routing Requires 8–24 hours of backup power at towers and routing centers Rural and Underserved Coverage Establishes 911 Priority Zones in areas with poor coverage Requires carriers to map and remediate dead zones Conditions federal telecom funding on compliance with reliability standards Next‑Generation 911 Modernization Provides $1 billion for CAD, GIS, NG911, and location‑accuracy upgrades Improves vertical and horizontal caller‑location accuracy Enhances cybersecurity protections for 911 systems After‑Hours EMS and Dispatch Availability Requires 24/7 on‑site staffing at all PSAPs Requires at least one fully staffed EMS unit on duty at all times Establishes federal staffing grants for rural and underserved counties Outage Transparency Requires carriers to report 911 outages within five minutes of detection Requires public alerts with alternative emergency contact numbers Creates a national 911 Reliability Dashboard for public transparency Enforcement and Oversight Establishes the Federal Emergency Communications Oversight Office (FECOO) Authorizes civil penalties up to $1,000,000 per incident Creates criminal penalties for willful negligence resulting in injury or death Allows suspension or termination of federal funding for noncompliance Funding and Appropriations The bill relies on existing federal telecommunications funding sources, including: Universal Service Fund (USF) Rural Digital Opportunity Fund (RDOF) Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) program No new federal taxes or fees are created.
Civil penalties collected under the Act help offset implementation costs.
Implementation Timeline Immediate: Penalties, outage reporting, and public notification requirements 1 year: PSAP staffing and EMS availability requirements 2 years: Uptime, redundancy, backup power, and NG911 upgrade requirements Extensions: Available for rural and tribal communities facing financial or logistical hardship Impact on Rural Communities and States The bill is expected to significantly improve emergency‑response reliability in rural regions, including areas with long EMS response distances, limited dispatch staffing, known cellular dead zones, and aging 911 infrastructure.
Communities in southern Indiana and similar regions nationwide stand to benefit from improved coverage, faster response times, and more resilient emergency‑communications systems.
