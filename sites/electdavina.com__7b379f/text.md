Davina Williams Duerr grew up in upstate New York, earned a Bachelor of Arts in architecture with a minor in environmental geography from Syracuse University, and moved west to build a career in design.
A licensed architect, she brings a builder's eye for how housing, land use, and climate fit together to every bill she writes.
She stepped into civic life through the Bothell Landmarks Preservation Board — ten years of service, eight as chair.
She co-founded the M.I.L.K.
Money campaign through the Northshore Schools Foundation, a program that raises money for students experiencing homelessness and still serves thousands of kids each year.
In 2016 she ran for Bothell City Council and won.
Her colleagues elected her deputy mayor.
When state Rep.
Derek Stanford was appointed to the Senate in 2019, the King and Snohomish County Councils appointed Davina to fill his seat.
Voters elected her to a full term in 2020 and re-elected her in 2022 and 2024.

HB 2502 2026 Permitting reform to speed up housing Davina’s 2026 permitting bill — streamlines local review and cuts delays so homes get built faster and at lower cost.
Signed into law HB 1183 2025 Housing affordability and innovative housing products First law of its kind in the country.
Eliminates parking minimums for affordable, modular, mass timber, and passive buildings.
Drafted with architects and builders.
Signed into law HB 1462 2025 Cutting industrial refrigerants Two years of work with NRDC and the refrigeration industry.
Phases out super-pollutant refrigerants used in grocery coolers, heat pumps, and ACs.
Signed into law HB 1154 2025 Landfill enforcement Gives the Department of Ecology the power to step in on solid-waste facilities.
One Washington landfill had been out of compliance for 20 years.
Signed into law HB 2418 2025 Streamlined local permits for housing Most consequential permitting reform.
Allows cities to streamline review processes and reduce approval timelines for housing projects.
Signed into law HB 1282 2022 Buy Clean, Buy Fair Washington Act Takes on the embodied carbon in steel, concrete, and wood for state-owned public buildings.
Among the first such laws in the country.
Signed into law HB 1099 2022 Climate and the GMA (first attempt) Passed the House.
Died in the Senate at the end of session by a handful of votes.
Came back in 2023 as HB 1181.
