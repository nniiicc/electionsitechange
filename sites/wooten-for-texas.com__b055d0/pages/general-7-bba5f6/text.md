Mental Health in Texas MENTAL HEALTH NEEDS, CHALLENGES, AND SOLUTIONS IN TEXAS (Texas Ranks Near the Bottom of all States in Access and Quality of Care) THE SCOPE OF THE NEEDS--EPIDEMIOLOGY (Texas Health Data: Mental Health): *Approximately 1 in 5 adults and 1 in 3 children have mental health disorders per year in Texas *Almost 4 million Texans (that we know of) experience some form of mental illness each year *Chronic and severe forms of mental health diagnoses, called Serious Mental Illness (SMI), afflict a million Texans for years, substantially limiting the quantity and quality of their lives (Schizophrenia and Bipolar Disorder); some of these individuals have so impacted that they qualify as having Disabilities *Almost half of Texans report some symptoms of anxiety and depression *Suicide incidence in Texas is 4500 per year, with younger and rural populations more vulnerable *Substance abuse/addiction affects around 17% of Texans aged 12 years and up, which is almost 4 million people a year *Families, loved ones, and communities are impacted by caring for those who suffer from disorders, so the effects are extremely widespread THE CHALLENGES IN MENTAL HEALTH CARE—INADEQUACY AND INEQUITY: *Chronic workforce shortages in public health agencies, private practice, and the legal system: -170/254 counties have zero psychiatrists; 163 have no MH NPs -143 counties have zero psychologists -91 counties have no licensed clinical social workers -84 counties have no licensed chemical dependency counselors -29 counties have no licensed professional counselors *School districts also have chronic shortages in mental health staffing: -only 25% of needed school psychologists/social workers -only 64% of needed school counselors -Texas state officials do not obtain the available Medicaid payments for in-school services * Psychiatric hospitals and long-term care and support for those with SMI and/or disabilities are woefully inadequate: -State hospitals have 1600 patients waiting for an opening -Private psychiatric and substance abuse hospitals and programs and inaccessible financially and do not have enough slots per capita -The group home/community care waiting list (CLASS list) for those with developmental and/or psychiatric disabilities is 15-20 YEARS -Social Security Disability Income and other state or federal provisions for support are sparingly given after overcoming numerous barriers *The most vulnerable people, given the influence of poverty and marginalization in worsening mental health outcomes, are also the least likely to be able to afford or access mental health services POTENTIAL SOLUTIONS TO IMPROVE MENTAL HEALTHCARE ACCESS IN TEXAS: *Address the provider, services, and facility shortages: -increase funding for graduate degree programs and loan repayment -develop a Train in Texas, Stay in Texas grant to student loan program -increase funding for schools and state agencies to hire staff -increase Home and Community-based Services (HCS) providers *Improve affordability and access to care: -fund an increase in services across all state programs (i.e., MH/MR, TWC, CPS, public health centers, state hospitals, legal systems) -require insurance companies to provide parity of m.h./medical care -fix and then expand Medicaid to attract providers to serve indigent -incentivize private providers who accept insurance or offer a sliding- fee payment scale instead of only doing full-fee out of pocket services, so that not only the wealth can be served by the already limited number of m.h. providers -state subsidies for insurance or programs for mental health -increase funding for mental health services and special education programming in schools -establish preventative programs across schools and the state -work with large companies to expand EAP services for employees -this is not an exhaustive list of possible remedies, so more research and proposals from others will be heartily welcomed The motivation for me to run for office came out of my knowledge from 30+ years as a clinical psychologist, that there are so many unmet mental health needs in Texas.
Texas is lousy and pathetic at taking care of people, ranking around 45th out of 50 states in per capita expenditures on human services.
Yet, Texas is second highest in wealth as measured by the Gross Domestic Product (GDP).
So where is the money going?
Not to those who need it.
This must change, which is why I and 13 other Democratic candidates for the Texas House of Representatives must flip our seats and take over the State House.
Affordability Every week I hear from HD 63 families who are working hard and still falling behind on groceries, insurance, property taxes, and childcare.
That shouldn't happen to people doing everything right.
The Texas Democratic Party's 2026 platform puts affordability first, with proposals to lower costs through infrastructure investment, housing access, and tax relief for working families.
The Texas Republican platform doesn't address affordability or inflation directly.
Its main cost related proposals are tax cuts that would help some Texans while cutting the public services many families rely on.
Extremist politicians have had 30 years to act on affordability and have instead focused on culture war fights that don't put money back in anyone's pocket.
I will fight for affordable healthcare, childcare, housing, and human services.
As your State Representative, I will: Push for property tax relief targeted at homeowners and renters, not just a talking point Support expanding affordable childcare so parents can work without losing a paycheck to daycare Bring the same fiscal discipline I've used running a household and a private practice to how the state spends taxpayer money Healthcare Access and Affordability As a clinical psychologist with over 30 years treating patients, I've seen firsthand what happens when people can't afford care.
A manageable problem becomes a crisis.
4.3 million Texans lack insurance, including over a million children.
The Texas GOP platform explicitly opposes any further Medicaid expansion and calls for repealing the Affordable Care Act.
Texas is one of the few states that has never expanded Medicaid, even though the federal government would cover almost all of the cost.
The Democratic platform supports expanding Medicaid, building toward universal coverage with a public option, and expanding rural healthcare access and mental health funding.
Affordable healthcare, including mental health treatment, should be available to everyone.
I will fight for affordable healthcare, childcare, housing, and human services.

My daughter is a special education teacher in Lewisville ISD.
Most of my psychology practice clients with learning differences go to public schools and need special education services.
For my part, I have taught in the public sector at the local community college.
Public education from pre-kindergarten to college is the equalizer of opportunity across all income levels.
Every kid in HD 63 deserves that same chance.
I support public education for every student.
School funding, especially funding for learning needs and teacher compensation, will be drastically reduced if school vouchers are allowed in Texas.

I will not support school vouchers that reduce opportunity for all Texas students while perpetuating systemic elitism and bigoted world views.
As your State Representative, I will: Oppose voucher schemes that drain funding from public schools Fight for competitive teacher pay and manageable class sizes Support full funding for public education, including special education and mental health support in schools Immigration & Border Security Texas has always been shaped by immigrants — the workers, entrepreneurs, and families who help power our farms, our hospitals, our tech companies, and our construction sites.
I believe in an immigration system that reflects that reality: one that is secure, orderly, and fair, and that strengthens our economy instead of undermining it.
Our system is broken, and Republican policies are making it worse.
Right now, immigration courts are buried under millions of pending cases, with families waiting years for a resolution.
Outdated visa and guest-worker rules don't match what Texas employers actually need, and needlessly restrictive rules push people toward dangerous, illegal crossings instead of legal pathways.
Rather than fixing these problems, the current administration has pursued an approach that is chaotic, cruel, and often unconstitutional — detaining U.S. citizens by mistake, deporting legal residents with no criminal record, and using force that has injured and endangered innocent people.
These abuses don't make us safer.
They make our communities more fearful and our country less trusted around the world.
They also hurt our economy.
Mass deportation efforts threaten to eliminate millions of jobs nationwide, with construction, childcare, health care, and agriculture — industries Texas depends on — hit hardest.
Immigrants, including those without documentation, contribute billions in taxes every year while remaining ineligible for most of the benefits those taxes fund.
An immigration policy that drives out workers and shrinks our tax base is bad economics, not just bad policy.
Here's what I support instead: Real border security.
No serious candidate supports open borders, and I don't either.
Border security matters, and we should invest in smart infrastructure and technology while addressing the reasons people cross illegally in the first place — chiefly, a broken legal immigration and asylum process.
Focused, lawful enforcement.
Interior enforcement should target people who pose an actual safety risk, not sweep up long-time residents, taxpayers, and U.S. citizens in the process.
Everyone in this country — citizen or not — is entitled to due process under our Constitution.
An asylum system that follows the law.
People fleeing genuine danger have a legal right to seek asylum here.
We should meet our legal obligations, clear the backlog, and stop policies that shut the door on people with legitimate claims.
Modernized guest-worker and visa programs.
Texas employers need reliable, lawful ways to hire the workers they can't find domestically, with real protections against abuse for both immigrant and American workers.
An earned path to legal status.
For undocumented immigrants who have lived here for years, working hard and raising families, we need a realistic, earned pathway to legal status — not endless limbo.
An end to for-profit detention.
Immigration enforcement shouldn't be a profit center.
We need transparency, oversight, and humane alternatives to incarceration.
I'm encouraged that bipartisan efforts like the Dignity Act show that Republicans and Democrats can come together on real solutions that pair strong enforcement with earned legal status.
That's the kind of practical, values-driven leadership I'll bring to this office — securing our border, respecting the rule of law, and building an immigration system that works for Texas families and our economy.
Gun Violence and Safety Let's keep our communities and children safe.
No child should be terrified in their own school.
Gun deaths are now the leading cause of death for children age 1 to 17.
Texas gun deaths have jumped over 50% since Governor Abbott took office, from 2,848 in 2014 to 4,389 in 2024.
On an average day, 12 Texans die from gun violence.
Texans overwhelmingly support common sense gun laws: 89% support universal background checks, 88% support Red Flag laws, 83% support raising the purchase age to 21, and 70% support banning the sale of assault style rifles.
The Republican platform supports permitless "Constitutional Carry," legalizing armor piercing ammunition, and eliminating gun free zones.
The Democratic platform supports universal background checks and restricting large capacity magazines and bump stocks, while respecting the 2nd Amendment.
I will fight to increase the age that assault rifles can be purchased and for the ban of high capacity magazines.
I will also fight for increasing gun safety measures to keep our communities safe.
As your State Representative, I will: Support raising the minimum purchase age for semi automatic rifles to 21 Support banning high capacity magazines Support red flag and safe storage laws, while upholding law abiding Texans' 2nd Amendment rights GUN SAFETY: SANE AND SENSIBLE SOLUTIONS We have a gun violence epidemic In the United States, which is even worse in Texas.
The gun death rate is more than 10 times that of other developed countries.
Over 4000 Texans are killed by gun violence each year, and this is the leading cause of death for children.
The epidemic, though, isn’t just about deaths, but the injuries and PTSD lead to chronic problems in the victims and those around them.
In Texas, gun violence has doubled since Gov.
Abbott took office a decade ago.
In the past 20 years, more than 100 laws have been passed by the Republican-led legislature that loosen gun regulations.
Further, my opponent (Bumgarner), the incumbent for TX House District 63, co-owns a gun manufacturing company.
He has twice authored legislation (HB 3580 in 2023 and HB 920 in 2025) aimed at exempting firearms from sales tax to make it more affordable for people to buy his guns.
Corruption?
Self-interest?
We need him out of office, replaced with a candidate like me who cares about the well-being of people, not just profit and donors.
It is past time for more safety in gun laws.
All people, but especially children, deserve to be safe from gun violence that comes from mass shootings, accidents, murders, domestic violence, and suicides.
The statistics could not be clearer, that sensible gun regulation improves outcomes for people and states.
Therefore, we need to: 1.
Increase the age limits for purchase of any gun to at least 21, but ideally to 25 for greater prefontal cortex maturation.
Between 21 and 25, a comprehensive risk assessment can be completed, also requiring letters of recommendation from two or more community experts in addition to the standard criteria.
2.
Ban assault-style weapons and high-capacity magazines, with exceptions for military and law enforcement, and at controlled gun ranges.
3.
Increase Red Flag Laws and mandatory reporting from more entities, including gun dealers, pawn shops, social media outlets, clergy, school personnel, and medical and mental health professionals.
The “Duty to Warn” legal criteria may need to be expanded given the lethality of firearms.
4.
Continue universal background checks but conduct more extensive checking on those who are flagged, to assess functional stability, with review of mental health, school, employment, and legal records.
5.
Train specialized law enforcement personnel and/or mental health professionals to conduct Risk Assessments.
Protocols from forensic specialists already exist, so use them.
6.
End gun show loopholes that avoid regulations and repeal liability protection for gunmakers and dealers.
7.
Require an extensive training process to increase safer use and storage.
8.
Require mandatory waiting periods to reduce “heat of the moment” actions.
9.
Increase the legal penalties for black market sales for both sellers and buyers.
10.
Provide therapy and support for people and communities traumatized by gun violence, including victim assistance programs to help with the financial costs.

As this is not an exhaustive list, other solutions and strategies can be developed based on research, experience, and innovation.
