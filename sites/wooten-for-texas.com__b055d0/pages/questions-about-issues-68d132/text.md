DONATE SUBSCRIBE Frequently Asked Questions about Denise Wooten's Policy Positions What does Dr.
Wooten see as her top priority?
Dr.
Wooten says her top priority is changing the direction of state government so it benefits everyone in her district and across Texas, not just the wealthy and powerful. ​ What is Dr.
Wooten's professional background, and how does it inform her campaign?
Dr.
Wooten has practiced as a clinical psychologist for 35 years and has taught as an adjunct professor at North Central Texas College for 7 years.
She says this work with everyday people shapes her focus on public education, healthcare access, and equal opportunity.
Where does Dr.
Wooten stand on public education?
Dr.
Wooten lists public education among her core issues and says she is committed to protecting and funding it.
She cites research showing Texas schools remain underfunded relative to the rest of the country: state per-student operating spending trails the national average by thousands of dollars, and the share of uncertified teachers in Texas classrooms has climbed sharply in recent years.
Recent state budget increases directed some new money to public schools, but much of it came with restrictions, and the state has also begun steering public dollars toward private-school tuition through education savings accounts.
Based on this research, Dr.
Wooten supports raising core per-student funding, investing in teacher pay and retention, catching struggling readers and math students earlier, fully funding services for students with disabilities.
She supports repealing the vouchers if possible, otherwise requiring clear reporting on their use.
What is Dr.
Wooten's position on healthcare?
Dr.
Wooten supports expanding access to medical and mental healthcare, and lists healthcare access and affordability among the key issues she is running on.
She points to Texas's comparatively high number of residents without insurance and supports policies to expand coverage, including Medicaid expansion, which research shows would extend coverage to more Texans, improve health outcomes, strengthen the economy, and help keep rural hospitals open.
She also supports action to protect and shore up Medicare's long-term finances and to reduce Texas's maternal mortality rate, which research shows remains above the national average.
Does Dr.
Wooten support reproductive rights?
Yes.
Dr.
Wooten says she is committed to preserving a woman's right to make her own reproductive choices, and lists women's healthcare choices as one of her key issues.
She cites research showing that Texas's abortion ban has not stopped abortions but has instead pushed patients to travel out of state or use telehealth, has left doctors uncertain about when miscarriage and pregnancy-emergency care is legally protected, and has fallen hardest on Texans with the fewest resources.
Based on this research, she supports restoring abortion access, especially in cases of rape, incest, and serious health risks; removing criminal penalties on doctors and patients; requiring clear hospital protocols for pregnancy emergencies; and expanding access to contraception and family planning to reduce unintended pregnancies.
What is Dr.
Wooten's stance on gun policy?
Dr.
Wooten supports what she describes as sensible assault rifle legislation, and lists gun violence and safety among the issues she is focused on.
She cites research showing that Texas's gun death rate is well above the national average and has risen sharply over the past decade, and that states with stronger gun laws see substantially lower rates of gun deaths than states with fewer restrictions, including Texas.
Consistent with this research, she supports measures such as expanded background checks, keeping guns away from people under domestic-violence restraining orders, allowing courts to temporarily remove firearms from people found to be a danger to themselves or others, raising the minimum age for gun purchases, and greater investment in mental health care, while continuing to respect Second Amendment rights.
Where does she stand on property taxes and affordability?
Dr.
Wooten says she wants to reduce the property tax burden on the middle class, and lists affordability as one of her key issues.
She points to research showing that state and local taxes in Texas fall disproportionately on middle- and lower-income households, and supports ensuring corporations and wealthy individuals pay their fair share.
She also cites the state's housing affordability crisis: more than a third of Texas households spend over 30 percent of their income on housing, and the typical first-time homebuyer is now significantly older than a few years ago because prices have outpaced wages.
In response, she supports continuing to streamline zoning and building rules, protecting renters' rights, allowing more accessory dwelling units, and increasing public investment in affordable housing.
What is Dr.
Wooten's position on voting rights?
Dr.
Wooten says she supports fair voting rights and opposes what she calls egregious gerrymandering.
She also lists voting accessibility among her key issues.
She cites research ranking Texas among the hardest states in which to vote, pointing to limited mail-in voting, polling-place closures, and restrictive registration and ID rules, while noting that documented voter fraud is extremely rare.
Consistent with this research, she supports making voter registration easier, expanding early voting, protecting mail-in voting, and establishing an independent redistricting commission in Texas to address gerrymandering.
Does Dr.
Wooten address climate change?
Yes.
Dr.
Wooten says she wants to address climate change to help keep the planet livable, and lists climate change and renewable energy among her key issues.
She cites research showing broad scientific agreement that climate change is real and human-caused, and that Texas is already experiencing its effects through rising temperatures, more days above 100 degrees, worsening flooding and drought, and an increase in costly weather disasters.
She also cites research finding that renewable energy is now cost-competitive with fossil fuels and that Texas's clean energy sector already employs a large and growing workforce.
Based on this, she supports expanding renewable energy and grid modernization, encouraging electric vehicle adoption, improving building efficiency, reducing methane emissions from oil and gas operations, and developing a statewide climate adaptation plan to prepare for flooding, drought, and extreme heat, none of which currently exists in Texas.
What is Dr.
Wooten's approach to equality and rights for marginalized groups?
Dr.
Wooten says she is committed to promoting individual rights, especially for marginalized groups, and lists equality and diversity among her key issues.
This respect for diversity and belief in equity/equality is consistent with Dr.
Wooten's personal and professional ethical standards.
Does Dr.
Wooten address housing and immigration?
Dr.
Wooten lists housing affordability and immigration and border security among her key issues.
On immigration, she cites research indicating that the large majority of immigrants are in the United States legally, that immigration has little negative effect on wages for native-born workers, that immigrants pay more in taxes than they draw in public benefits, and that immigrants are not associated with higher crime rates.
She supports comprehensive immigration reform paired with responsible border security.
On housing, she points to research identifying an inadequate supply of affordable units, rising construction and insurance costs, and labor shortages tied to strict immigration enforcement as key drivers of the state's affordability gap, and supports the zoning, renter-protection, and housing-investment approaches described above.
What is "Informed Decision Making," as Dr.
Wooten describes it?
Dr.
Wooten lists informed decision making among her key issues on her campaign site, reflecting her broader view that medical and policy decisions should be made by qualified professionals rather than politicians without relevant training.
Where does Dr.
Wooten's research on these issues come from?
Dr.
Wooten's her issue positions draw on her personal and professional experience as well as nonpartisan policy research and sourcing from the Center for Policy Progress, a nonprofit that provides fact-based policy papers to candidates at no cost, along with U.S.
Census Bureau data and Texas Legislative Council district demographic data.
What is Dr.
Wooten's guiding mission statement?
Dr.
Wooten's stated mission is "Equality Meets Opportunity." She says all Texans deserve the same rights and chances for health, growth, education, career options, and livability.
What office is Dr.
Wooten running for?
Dr.
Wooten is running for Texas State Representative, House District 63.
