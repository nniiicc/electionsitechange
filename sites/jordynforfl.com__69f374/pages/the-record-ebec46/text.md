Your septic bill
Your septic bill is coming.
Where is the help?
State law now requires about 28,000 Indian River County properties in the Lagoon watershed to connect to sewer or install an advanced septic system by July 1, 2030.
Depending on the home, that runs from $5,000 to $25,000 or more.1,2
Next door, Brevard County gives homeowners at least $6,000 apiece for Lagoon-area septic upgrades.
Indian River County has no program like it.3,1
In December 2023, Vero Beach's water and sewer director said the city would seek a state appropriation for its new wastewater plant and had already discussed it with Brackett.5 The state money that came arrived through agency grant programs, and by 2026 the city was asking Congress for earmarks instead.6,7
Of the 13 budget requests Brackett filed for 2024, the ones that made the news were a $3 million boat ramp, a $2 million jetty project, and baffle boxes in Palm Bay.4
Ask Robbie Brackett what he has asked Tallahassee to do about your septic bill.
Jordyn will make that request on day one.
Your wallet
His waterfront project.
Your water bill.
As Mayor, Brackett backed Three Corners, the redevelopment of the old power plant site on the Lagoon.22,27 Moving the city's wastewater plant off that land is what makes the project possible.8,9
The new plant was first approved at $82 million.
By 2025 the total had more than doubled to $178 million.8,9,10
To pay for it, the city passed water and sewer increases of 15%, 18%, and 18%.
The average monthly bill climbed from $51 to about $83, and another 47% in increases is already approved through 2029.8,11
He sold the waterfront dream.
You pay for it every month.

A landlord voting on his own tax bill.

Brackett was one of two council members to vote no.14,15

A commercial landlord voted against the Lagoon fee his own buildings would have helped pay, then backed cutting it to pocket change.
