New Hampshire lawmakers gave final approval to a business tax cut and a ballot question to cap property taxes, two measures championed by House Majority Leader Jason Osborne (R-Auburn), according to the New Hampshire Union Leader.
The reporting frames the pair as central pieces of the House GOP’s agenda heading into the fall, linking the policy push directly to the party’s political strategy.
Both measures cleared their final procedural hurdles together, the outlet noted.
The dual approval marked the culmination of a months-long effort by House Republicans to pair tax policy with a direct appeal to voters.

According to the New Hampshire Union Leader, the business tax compromise in HB 155 would raise the no-tax floor for the Business Enterprise Tax so that firms with payroll, dividends and interest below $400,000 would not owe it, up from a current threshold just under $300,000.
The outlet reported that an actual cut in the BET rate would depend on triggers tied to the state’s Rainy Day Fund and revenue surpluses, and the package also included new dollars aimed at raising reimbursement rates for nursing homes in the state.
