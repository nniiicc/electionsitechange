In 2014 at age 20, Becca ran for Select Board and has not stopped serving Hartford since.
What does Becca have to offer?
Sen.
Becca White has 10 years of elected leadership under her belt, a college education from the University of Vermont, and has worked fulltime for highly regarded companies and organizations.
Her public service background includes:
- Three years of service as your State Senator for the Windsor County District
- Assistant Majority Leader (aka the Whip) for the Vermont Senate Democratic Party
- Serving on two committees - Senate Transportation and Government Operations
- Former Clerk of the Natural Resources & Energy Committee
- Co-Chair of the Climate Solutions Caucus (CSC) the largest nonpartisan caucus in Vermont
- Four years at the end of 2022 serving as one of the State Representatives for Hartford, Vermont
- Member of the Transportation Committee
- Member of the leadership for team the Climate Solutions Caucus (CSC)
- Co-Chair of the Futures Caucus (tri-partisan led)
- Lead sponsor of the Transportation Modernization Act of 2021 and the Transportation Innovation Act of 2022
- House Communications Committee member, and House Campaign team member
- Member of the Rainbow Caucus, Women’s Caucus, and Rural Caucus
- Four years on the Hartford Selectboard, elected to Vice Chair
- Serving as the liaison to various committees including the School Board, Energy Commission, Tree Board, Hartford Committee on Racial Equity and Inclusion, and as an EC Fiber delegate for the town.

My vision statements on pressing issues:
The Climate Crisis
We are facing an existential threat greater than any other that the human race has seen before.
As a young person in Vermont, I know my life is and will be to an even greater extent, shaped by how we respond.
Income Inequality
I grew up in a “no-frills” household but was able to get a quality public education, have stable housing despite multiple home foreclosures, and live in a safe healthy village.
Income inequality stops these types of opportunities, and everyone deserves dignity.
Housing Affordability
My husband Dylan and I live in an affordable housing home through Twin Pines Housing Trust, and I see the value of secure housing as it impacts employers and workers in our region.
Vermonters should never be torn between choosing to house over other necessities.
Health Care as a Right
When my mother injured her back and was laid up for months, I saw what it meant to not have health insurance that covered costs.
I also experienced the benefit of being on Dr.
Dynasaur and Medicaid, and I believe healthcare is a right.
Equity
We must root out systemic racism in all facets of institutions, including criminal justice and police reform.
Accountability for people in positions of authority, including elected officials like myself, is paramount.
Moving Forward
There are lots of other values-driven issues I am focused on, and they range from responding to surveillance capitalism and data protection to how we fund our public education system to ensure all Vermonters have high-quality lifelong learning opportunities.
