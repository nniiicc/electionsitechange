Meet Ihor Semko
Ihor Semko was born in Ukraine and came to the United States at eight years old with a family that had nothing but a belief in hard work.
He moved from Oregon to New Jersey to Herkimer, New York, learning to adapt to new communities, new cultures, and a new language each time.
Along the way, he learned that fitting in was not the goal.
Making a place for himself was all that was needed.
Ihor became the first in his family to earn a college degree in the United States, attending Herkimer County Community College and Utica University before earning his MBA.
He built a career in human resources and organizational leadership, working directly with employees and business owners to solve real problems in real workplaces.
As Head of HR for a manufacturing plant near Syracuse, he created safety committees, built in-house training programs that saved hundreds of dollars per employee, and modernized operations from the ground up.
He has also served as a commissioner on the Oneida City Water Board.
Today, Ihor and his wife Andriana, a SUNY Poly professor, are raising their two young sons in Central New York.
The same conversations keep coming up with neighbors, coworkers, and local families: everything costs more, young people are leaving, and the district's Republican assemblyman is not paying attention.
Ihor is running for the 122nd Assembly District because he is tired of waiting for someone else to step up.
He is bringing a practical, community-focused voice to Albany, one that prioritizes affordability, supports local workers and small businesses, and fights to make sure Upstate families can build a life and stay.
I got into this race because I kept having the same conversation.
At work, at the grocery store, at my kids' library story time.
People in this district are frustrated.
Property taxes keep going up.
Young people are leaving because they cannot afford to stay.
Small businesses are struggling under costs they did not create.
And the response from our Republican assemblyman has been silence.
I spent my career in human resources working between employees and employers, figuring out what is broken and how to fix it.
That is how I think about government too.
The 122nd District does not need more politicians talking past us.
It needs someone who will listen, identify the real problems, and push for solutions that actually work for the people living here.
This district leans conservative, and I respect that.
I am not running to bring Albany's politics to Central New York.
I am running to bring Central New York's priorities to Albany.
Affordability.
Jobs.
Healthcare access.
Better infrastructure.
These are not partisan issues.
They are kitchen table issues, and they have been ignored for too long.
I am also running because of what public matching funds make possible.
In this race, every dollar donated by someone in our community gets matched by up to twelve to one.
That means this campaign is not built on special interests or big money from downstate.
It is built on neighbors investing in their own representation.
I am asking for your support because I believe our community deserves leadership that shows up, listens, and gets to work.
