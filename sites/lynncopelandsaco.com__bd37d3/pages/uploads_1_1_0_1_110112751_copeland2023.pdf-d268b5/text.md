It has impacted lower-income folks the hardest.
I was proud to vote for legislation funding the creation of the Housing First Program, a game-changing approach that will prioritize providing permanent housing and on-site services to those who have been chronically homeless.
We also passed measures to accelerate housing production, including a more than $100 million investment to help incentivize the development of affordable housing.

COPELAND SUPPORTING FAMILIES This session, the Legislature enacted major initiatives to support Maine families at every stage of life and provide relief to alleviate the pressure of rising costs.
We passed legislation that will establish a comprehensive paid family and medical leave benefits program, ensuring that Mainers will no longer have to choose between putting food on the table and caring for themselves or a loved one.
We created a $300 state child tax credit, which will help more young families make ends meet and reduce food insecurity and child poverty.
We increased eligibility for the child care affordability program, so more income-eligible families can pay for child care – which is a necessity, not a luxury.
And we passed measures to continue providing free school lunch for all students and meet the state’s obligation to fund 55% of K-12 public education costs.
I also supported legislation to increase the annual income tax pension deduction from $30,000 to $35,000 and expand eligibility for the Medicare Savings Program, which will help more older Mainers pay their bills and make it easier to age with peace of mind.

GROWING MAINE’S ECONOMY Maine’s economy is employee-driven, and our people are our greatest resource.
This year, we prioritized legislation that will empower workers and make progress toward creating an economy that enables everyone to reach their full potential.
Too many of Maine’s working parents can’t access affordable child care.
To address this, we raised wages for child care providers – our workforce behind the workforce – to improve retention, make child care more available and reduce barriers for parents to participate in our economy.
We also enacted measures that will expand career pathways for more Mainers by strengthening apprenticeship and pre-apprenticeship programs, improving access to skills training for new workers and broadening free community college tuition to include the high school classes of 2024 and 2025.
While these are important steps, I am disappointed that legislation allowing the Wabanaki to access the same rights as other federally recognized tribes did not become law.
However, I remain committed to moving toward a future where we truly recognize and honor the Wabanaki Nations.
COMBATING CLIMATE CHANGE The climate crisis poses a threat to every corner of Maine, from our coastline to our inland communities.
Addressing it requires bold, thoughtful leadership – and this year, the Legislature took significant steps to transition away from fossil fuels and invest in climate resiliency.
I was proud to back legislation that will further the development of offshore wind energy in federal waters in the Gulf of Maine, putting our state in a position to substantially increase clean energy production and take advantage of a major economic development opportunity.
We also invested $200 million over the next two years to continue reducing carbon emissions, which will unlock additional federal infrastructure grants for our state to fight the climate crisis.

EXPANDING ACCESS TO HEALTH CARE Every Mainer deserves access to affordable, comprehensive health care regardless of age, race, gender or income.
Enacting policies that ensure all Maine people can receive high quality care will always be one of my top priorities.
This includes reproductive care.
The decision to have an abortion should be made by a patient and their medical provider, not politicians.
This session, we passed measures that will expand abortion access and strengthen protections for both patients and providers.
We also blocked proposals to significantly restrict abortion access, ensuring Mainers can continue to receive the care they need when they need it.
Additionally, we established the Dirigo Business Incentive Program, which will offer businesses tax credits to train workers and invest in improvements – helping to reduce Maine’s labor shortage, attract new businesses and make our state more competitive.
Fighting for equity and equal opportunity for all Mainers is central to my work in the Legislature.
Harmful disparities exist based on race, ethnicity and tribal status here in Maine.
While there is significant work to be done on both racial equity and tribal selfdetermination, this year, we focused on taking steps forward for the Wabanaki Nations.
