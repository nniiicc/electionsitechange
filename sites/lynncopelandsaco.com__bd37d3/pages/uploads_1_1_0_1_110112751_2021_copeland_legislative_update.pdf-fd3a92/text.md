COPELAND Dear Neighbor, The people of Maine have shown remarkable resilience over the last year and a half.
We have adapted to the changing course of the pandemic with grit, protecting the lives and livelihoods of our fellow Mainers along the way.
Especially during these challenging times, I am proud to serve our community in the Maine House of Representatives.
The first session of the Legislature wrapped up in July after eight months of hard work.
Despite all of the logistical challenges of lawmaking during a pandemic, we delivered monumental victories for the people of Maine.
We passed a two-year budget with overwhelming bipartisan support that will stabilize property taxes, fulfill the state’s commitment to fund 55% of K-12 public education costs and protect our natural resources.
We also voted to allocate federal relief funds to help small businesses rebound from the pandemic, attract qualified workers to fill workforce shortages and expand access to child care and public preschool.
Mainers will gain access to regular dental care.
We also approved funding to help older Mainers and people with disabilities access long-term care.
To address workforce shortages in health care, we passed measures to recruit more people into those fields.
We made much-needed investments in our state’s infrastructure.
Over $50 million will be put towards the construction of new affordable housing.
We dedicated $21 million for the expansion of affordable broadband.
Funding will also go towards improving our drinking water and wastewater infrastructure to protect public health and our environment.
While we are between legislative sessions, I am still working hard for the people of District 14.
If there are any questions I can answer or support I can provide, please feel welcome to reach out to me.
As always, it is an honor to serve you in the Maine House of Representatives.

ADVANCING RACIAL EQUITY STRENGTHENING OUR ECONOMY Historically disadvantaged racial, Indigenous and tribal populations in Maine experience higher rates of incarceration, lower pay and increased barriers to education, safe housing, food security and health care.
In order to make Maine a more just place, we must address the causes of racial inequity.
COVID-19 prevention protocols, supply chain issues and workforce shortages have put a lot of stress on our economy.
I am proud that the Legislature was able to pass several measures to help businesses rebound from the pandemic, support workers and fill critical workforce needs.
This year, the Legislature passed a historic measure to include racial impact statements in the legislative process and provided funding for the Permanent Commission on the Status of Racial, Indigenous and Tribal Populations to support its work.
We also passed bills to advance fair chance employment, preserve fair housing, require data collection on health care disparities and improve voter accessibility to ensure that all eligible voters can cast their vote.
With federal relief funds, the Legislature created additional economic recovery grants for businesses and organizations, targeting ones that had not been eligible for previous COVID-19 relief.
To protect the industries that have powered our state’s economy for generations, we allocated $50 million to support growth and sustainability in agriculture, food processing, the working waterfront and the forest products industry.
While I am proud of the progress we made this year, there is a lot more work to do to remove structural barriers that perpetuate racial inequities in Maine.
I look forward to continuing this important work in the months and years to come.
