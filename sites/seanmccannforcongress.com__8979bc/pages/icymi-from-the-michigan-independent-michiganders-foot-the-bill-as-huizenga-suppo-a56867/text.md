From $700 in additional fuel costs per Michigan household and $221 million in higher gas expenses across Michigan’s 4th Congressional District, Huizenga’s votes in Washington have continuously stained Michigan families’ budgets.
Read more about what Michigan voters are seeing and reading about Huizenga’s actions in Washington causing them to foot the bill: President Donald Trump’s policies have led to all-around higher prices for Michiganders, including at the gas pump.
Michigan households have paid nearly $700 in extra fuel costs since the Trump administration launched military attacks on Iran in February.
Michigan Republican Rep.
Bill Huizenga has voted against multiple resolutions to remove U.S. military forces from the conflict.
Michigan has experienced the largest year-over-year gas price increase of any state, up nearly 57% , according to Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy.
Households in Huizenga’s 4th Congressional District have paid an additional $221 million combined to fill their gas tanks since the war began.
The average price of regular unleaded gas in Michigan hit a new high for 2026 of $4.93 per gallon and diesel reached a high of $6.89 per gallon last month, according to AAA.
Michigan Ag Today reported in April that the average grain farmer could pay $12,000 this year in additional diesel fuel and fertilizer costs.
Huizenga voted for a $95 billion House budget package in late July with up to $73 million in additional military spending tha t passed over Democrats’ objections that the money could be better spent helping to lower everyday costs for Americans.
Huizenga is now locked in a tight reelection race against Democratic state Sen.
Sean McCann , who represents Michigan District 19.
In July 2025, Huizenga backed the One Big Beautiful Bill Act , the budget bill signed into law by Trump.
The law was estimated to increase Michigan household energy bills by more than $910 million annually in 2030 and more than $2.1 billion by 2035 due to reduced clean energy investments and greater reliance on fossil fuels, according to an analysis by Energy Innovation in June 2025.
The analysis also estimated the law will cost Michigan 21,300 jobs in 2030 d ue to reduced investment in domestic energy and manufacturing.
Changes in eligibility requirements for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program included in the law have already caused more than 138,000 Michiganders to lose their food benefits , including more than 46,000 children, according to the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities.
Huizenga has also championed Trump’s tariff policies, which have r aised consumer prices and led to increased costs for Michigan’s auto industry.
An August 2026 report from the National Taxpayers Union Foundation shows Michigan has paid an estimated $23 billion in tariffs since Trump imposed tariffs on goods imported into the United States, the third-highest total in the country.
Data show that’s equivalent to $5,619 per Michigan household, more than any other state.
While Michiganders struggled with high prices, the Michigan Independent reported in February, Huizenga remained the only U.S.
House member from Michigan not to have requested federal funding known as earmarks for community projects in his district; meanwhile, he has tripled his total assets and increased his personal wealth as much as fivefold since he took office in 2011.

About the race for Michigan’s 4th Congressional District: MI-04 is one of the country’s key 2026 battlegrounds.
The R+3 district has been named a top “Red to Blue” race by the DCCC and rated a toss-up by Sabato’s Crystal Ball.
Cook Political Report has consistently moved this race in Democrats’ direction, calling the district “ a bright spot for Democrats .” Poll after poll has shown Sean McCann leading Bill Huizenga, and McCann continues to post strong fundraising numbers.
According to Cook, Sean McCann’s State Senate seat (MI-19) “makes up about a third of the 4th District and includes 45% of the 4th’s primary electorate.” Governor Gretchen Whitmer and Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson each carried MI-04 with more than fifty percent of the vote in 2022.
MI-04 covers Southwest Michigan and includes the cities of Kalamazoo, Battle Creek, Holland, St.
Joseph, Benton Harbor and the outer suburbs of Grand Rapids including Ottawa County.
Sean has also received endorsements from Michigan Governor Gretchen Whitmer, Michigan Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson, Senator Elissa Slotkin, Congresswoman Hillary Scholten, former Congressman Mark Schauer, over 15 labor groups, and over 70 local elected officials and community leaders.
