Bill Huizenga’s votes show he put data centers and coal plants over constituents” Press Release The ‘Gander “Rep.
Bill Huizenga (R-Holland) has voted to support Trump’s AI race—and data shows it could cost Michiganders a lot of money.” KALAMAZOO, MI – In case you missed it, The ‘Gander reported on Congressman Bill Huizenga’s support of policies that favor AI data center expansion and keeping aging coal plants running, that leave Michigan families footing the bill.
Huizenga backed legislation scaling back clean energy investments and supported keeping the J.H.
Campbell coal plant open, an effort reportedly costing more than $260 million, while experts warn that his energy policies could drive household utility bills up by $300 a year.
Read more about what Southwest Michigan families are reading about Huizenga’s record of putting the interests of AI data centers ahead of the constituents he was elected to represent.
In 2025, alongside other Michigan Republicans, Huizenga supported the Trump administration’s reconciliation bill, which cut Medicaid coverage for 15,000 residents in Michigan’s 4th Congressional District—the one Huizenga represents—which includes Allegan, Van Buren, northern Berrien and Kalamazoo counties, and portions of Ottawa and Calhoun counties.
T he “One Big Beautiful Bill,” or OBBBA, also scaled back clean energy investments established during the Biden administration and paved the way for new data center construction nationwide—including in Michigan —as part of the Trump administration’s ongoing plan to have the US lead the “golden age” of artificial intelligence.
Experts say this data center construction, along with powering the data centers once they’re built, won’t just strain the area’s electrical supply.
It’ll also strain Michiganders’ wallets, with expected yearly utility bills rising by $300.
This is expected to further impact low-income Michiganders, who are already reeling after Huizenga and other Republicans supported a 2025 appropriations bill that eliminated federal funding for a Department of Energy program that helped their households increase energy efficiency and reduce energy bills.
The push for data center construction has also narrowed jobs and investment into the state’s clean energy infrastructure.
New data shows that, since Trump was re-elected in 2024, over 8,000 jobs and over $1.7 billion in investment in Michigan’s clean energy sector have been cancelled or delayed .
Huizenga has also publicly supported the Trump administration’s executive order to keep the J.H.
Campbell coal plant in West Olive open despite a state-approved plan to close the facility to expand renewable energy and pushback from his constituents and other Michiganders.
Reminder: Sean has a long record of fighting to lower energy costs: Sean is calling for a three-year freeze on utility rate increases to give Michigan families relief from repeated rate hikes.
Sean is a cosponsor of SB 768, legislation that would freeze utility rates for three years and prevent utility companies from raising rates during that period.
Sean helped pass SB 353 , 880 , and 881 , measures that expanded eligibility for the Michigan Energy Assistance Program (MEAP) and addressed utility participation in the program.
Sean helped pass HB 5028 , which limited homeowners associations’ ability to prohibit certain energy-saving home improvements.
The contrast in their records is clear: Huizenga has consistently backed federal energy policies that could increase costs for Michigan households, while Sean McCann has supported legislation aimed at limiting utility rate hikes, expanding energy assistance, and helping families lower their energy bills.

About the race for Michigan’s 4th Congressional District: MI-04 is one of the country’s key 2026 battlegrounds.
The R+3 district has been named a top “Red to Blue” race by the DCCC and rated a toss-up by Sabato’s Crystal Ball.
Cook Political Report has consistently moved this race in Democrats’ direction, calling the district “ a bright spot for Democrats .” Poll after poll has shown Sean McCann leading Bill Huizenga, and McCann continues to post strong fundraising numbers.
According to Cook, Sean McCann’s State Senate seat (MI-19) “makes up about a third of the 4th District and includes 45% of the 4th’s primary electorate.” Governor Gretchen Whitmer and Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson each carried MI-04 with more than fifty percent of the vote in 2022.
MI-04 covers Southwest Michigan and includes the cities of Kalamazoo, Battle Creek, Holland, St.
Joseph, Benton Harbor and the outer suburbs of Grand Rapids including Ottawa County.
Sean has also received endorsements from Michigan Governor Gretchen Whitmer, Michigan Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson, Senator Elissa Slotkin, Congresswoman Hillary Scholten, former Congressman Mark Schauer, over 15 labor groups, and over 70 local elected officials and community leaders.
