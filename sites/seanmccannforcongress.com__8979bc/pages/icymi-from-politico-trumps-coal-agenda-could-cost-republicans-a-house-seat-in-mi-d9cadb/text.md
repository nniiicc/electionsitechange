Read more below: The Trump administration’s actions to rescue an imperiled coal-fired power plant could cost Republicans a Michigan congressional seat, one crucial to holding their House majority.
Energy Secretary Chris Wright has tried to keep the 64-year-old J.H.
Campbell Generating Plant in southwest Michigan running, a costly effort that is expected to raise utility rates in the area, In an election season that’s increasingly about rising prices at the pumps and elsewhere, incumbent Republican Rep.
Bill Huizenga is taking heat for the Trump administration’s energy policies in his district, including the costs to keep the plant running and its loss in the court case over the issue.
As Democrats nationally sharpen their focus on affordability issues and electricity rates in their midterm drive, Campbell is a concrete example that Trump’s policies are why life is getting more expensive.
In a poll published this month by Suffolk University, 27.4% of Michigan voters said inflation and the economy was their top election priority, more than double any other issue.
And a survey released earlier this year by Climate Power found that 58% of Michiganders think President Donald Trump’s policies have made energy more expensive.
The U.S.
Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit ruled this month these reasons simply do not constitute an emergency in line with the law that Wright used to keep the plant open .
“People have seen their electric rates go up substantially in a very short order of time, and the plant operates at a loss — it’s costing $600,000 a day to operate.
The whole thing is a terrible situation that needs to be ended,” McCann told POLITICO, citing an estimate based on the operator’s disclosures.
“It’s a huge factor in affordability.” “There are a lot of people in Ottawa County who have wanted and planned for its shutdown, not only from the operational point of view of costs and coal, but also from the polluting aspects, dealing with the extensive emissions that go east from the shores of Lake Michigan,” McCann said “It’s a very real and present factor in increased utility costs to customers.” Reminder: Sean has a long record of fighting to lower energy costs: Sean is calling for a three-year freeze on utility rate increases to give Michigan families relief from repeated rate hikes.
Sean is a cosponsor of SB 768, legislation that would freeze utility rates for three years and prevent utility companies from raising rates during that period.
Sean helped pass SB 353 , 880 , and 881 , measures that expanded eligibility for the Michigan Energy Assistance Program (MEAP) and addressed utility participation in the program.
Sean helped pass HB 5028 , which limited homeowners associations’ ability to prohibit certain energy-saving home improvements.
While Huizenga supported the federal intervention to keep Campbell operating, McCann has focused his work on utility affordability, ratepayer protections, and lowering energy costs for Michigan families.

About the race for Michigan’s 4th Congressional District: MI-04 is one of the country’s key 2026 battlegrounds.
The R+3 district has been named a top “Red to Blue” race by the DCCC and rated a toss-up by Sabato’s Crystal Ball.
Cook Political Report has consistently moved this race in Democrats’ direction, calling the district “ a bright spot for Democrats .” Poll after poll has shown Sean McCann leading Bill Huizenga, and McCann continues to post strong fundraising numbers.
According to Cook, Sean McCann’s State Senate seat (MI-19) “makes up about a third of the 4th District and includes 45% of the 4th’s primary electorate.” Governor Gretchen Whitmer and Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson each carried MI-04 with more than fifty percent of the vote in 2022.
MI-04 covers Southwest Michigan and includes the cities of Kalamazoo, Battle Creek, Holland, St.
Joseph, Benton Harbor and the outer suburbs of Grand Rapids including Ottawa County.
Sean has also received endorsements from Michigan Governor Gretchen Whitmer, Michigan Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson, Senator Elissa Slotkin, Congresswoman Hillary Scholten, former Congressman Mark Schauer, over 15 labor groups, and over 70 local elected officials and community leaders.
