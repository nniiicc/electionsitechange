As his war in Iran and trade wars have pushed farmers to the brink, Gov.
Pritzker has stood with them.
Gov.
Pritzker has delivered for Illinois farmers by signing an executive order to provide farmers needed relief from rising diesel prices, calling on Trump to suspend federal import taxes on fertilizers, and securing more than $4 billion in commitments from overseas purchasers of Illinois corn and soybeans.
Meanwhile, Darren Bailey defended Trump’s tariffs and repeatedly described them as “short-term pain for a long-term gain,” even as Illinois farmers struggled to make ends meet.
Bailey also supports Trump’s war in Iran, which drove up the cost of essentials like fuel and fertilizer, proving his loyalty lies with Trump, not Illinois farmers.
Read excerpts from the story below: On Gov.
Pritzker’s new low-interest loan program: “This month, Pritzker toured Halpin Farms, a family grain and cattle operation about 70 miles (113 km) southwest of Chicago, promoting a pilot program of low-interest state loans to build grain silos.
He talked about how agriculture remained a critical industry in Illinois, even as he looks to quantum computing and advanced manufacturing to drive economic growth in the state. “‘Family farms are going bankrupt.
It’s near impossible to make a profit now because of the high price of diesel, because of the war in Iran, and because of the trade wars that are causing the farmers not to be able to sell their goods abroad,’ Pritzker told Reuters. ‘Right now, we need to make sure our farmers stay alive and stay in business.’” On Illinois farmers’ disdain for Trump’s failed leadership: “The Trump administration is expected to send more than $50 billion to farmers this year, the second-largest relief package in US history.
Yet many farmers say the checks don’t make up for the high input costs. “‘We’re getting beat up,’ said John Bartman, 48, a corn and soybean farmer in Marengo, Illinois, adding that ‘a bailout doesn’t make us whole.
It helps the banks out more than the farmer.’ “Bartman, who plans to vote for Pritzker, said he thinks farmers will value the governor’s efforts to salvage important trade relationships.” On Gov.
Pritzker securing international trade deals: “In August, Pritzker signed an agreement with Indonesia to expand agricultural trade with the fourth-largest market for US soybeans.
He previously led ⁠trade missions to Canada and Japan in 2024.
And in April 2025, as the Trump administration imposed tariffs on imports from most trading partners on what it called “Liberation Day,” Pritzker was in Mexico City to promote Illinois corn.
“That visit to Mexico – the top market for US corn – landed a $55 million manufacturing deal, but did not bag new deals to buy Illinois corn.” Full story is available here .
