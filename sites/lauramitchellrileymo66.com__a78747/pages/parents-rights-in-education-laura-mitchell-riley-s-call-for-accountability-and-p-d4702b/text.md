The Importance of Parents Rights in Education
Current Challenges Facing Parents in Education
Parents today are increasingly confronting significant obstacles that undermine their ability to actively guide their children's educational journey.
Among the most pressing challenges is the widespread lack of transparency from schools about what exactly is being taught in classrooms.
Parents frequently find themselves excluded from crucial decisions affecting their children's education and healthcare, particularly when controversial or sensitive topics are introduced without any parental consent.
These challenges not only limit parents’ capacity to safeguard their family values but also create mistrust between families and educational institutions.
The encroachment on parents' authority in their children’s educational experiences runs contrary to foundational civil liberties and calls for urgent attention.
- Limited transparency from schools about curriculum content
- Exclusion of parents from critical decisions regarding their children’s education and healthcare
- Introduction of controversial topics at young ages without parental consent
Why Parents Rights in Education Matter
Laura Mitchell Riley, candidate for Missouri House District 66, emphasizes, "The person should be involved in every area of their child's school life, and they're not doing that.
Parents should know everything." This statement underscores the vital role of parental involvement as a cornerstone of responsible education and child welfare.
Parents possess the primary legal and moral right to know and influence how their children are taught, ensuring that education aligns with their values and principles.
Upholding these rights preserves family integrity, reinforces community values, and strengthens the trust essential for positive educational outcomes.
The system becomes fairer for every student when parents are empowered to actively engage with schools and educators.

Laura Mitchell Riley highlights several controversial educational policies currently impacting parental rights, expressing strong opposition to practices she believes undermine family authority and child welfare.
She objects intensely to transgender policies that exclude parents from being notified when their children express gender transition intentions, emphasizing that at a minimum "parents should be made aware and involved.
" This, she argues, is fundamental to protecting children from irreversible decisions made without appropriate guidance.
Additionally, Riley criticizes Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) programs in schools, describing them as a form of discrimination and indoctrination that conflicts with her community’s values.
She also advocates for the reintroduction of Christian teachings, including the Bible and Ten Commandments, into educational curricula to provide students with a moral compass and clear ethical guidance.

The Role of Parents Versus Educators
- Parents as primary decision-makers in education and healthcare
- Schools as facilitators, not gatekeepers, of information
- Necessity of parental consent for sensitive topics
Laura Mitchell Riley advocates strongly that parents retain the primary role in deciding what is appropriate for their children's education and healthcare.

She stresses the necessity that schools obtain parental consent before addressing sensitive topics such as gender identity.
This respect for parental rights ensures children receive guidance consistent with family beliefs and protects the trust fundamental to educational success.
The risks to families grow when schools withdraw parents from the process.

Laura Mitchell Riley believes protecting families starts with keeping parents informed and involved.

People Also Ask
What are parents rights in education?
Parents rights in education refer to the legal and moral entitlements that parents hold to be informed about and involved in their children's educational experiences.
This includes knowing the curriculum content, participating in healthcare decisions at school, and influencing school policies to reflect family values.
How can parents influence their child's education?
Parents can influence their child's education by actively communicating with teachers and school administrators, participating in school governance such as school boards or committees, choosing appropriate educational settings like homeschooling or school choice, and advocating for transparency and parental consent on sensitive matters.
Why is transparency important in schools?
Transparency ensures that parents are fully aware of what their children are learning, allowing them to make informed decisions and protect their children from exposure to content that may conflict with their family’s values or beliefs.
Transparent communication fosters trust and collaborative environments between families and educators.
