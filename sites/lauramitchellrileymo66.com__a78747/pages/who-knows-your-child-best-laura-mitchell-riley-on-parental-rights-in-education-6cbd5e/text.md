Who Knows Your Child Best?
Laura Mitchell Riley on Parental Rights in Education
In an era where families increasingly question the role of schools in shaping their children’s values and wellbeing, the topic of parental rights in education has become a pressing concern for many communities.
For Missouri’s District 66 candidate, Laura Mitchell Riley, these rights are not just political talking points—they are personal convictions rooted in her faith and life experiences.
“The person should be involved in every area of their child’s school life and everything, and they’re not doing that,” Riley emphasizes, highlighting the critical need for parental involvement and transparency.
Understanding Parental Rights in Education and Healthcare
- Definition and scope of parental rights in education: Parental rights in education encompass the authority and responsibility parents have to make decisions about their children's schooling, including curricula, extracurricular activities, and healthcare decisions administered through schools.
- The role of parents in decisions about their children's schooling and healthcare: Parents are the primary guardians, entrusted with guiding their children's moral, educational, and physical development.
This includes being informed and consenting to policies and treatments their children face in educational settings.
- Legal frameworks supporting parental rights, including references to the bill of rights: The U.S.
Constitution and various Supreme Court rulings affirm that parents hold fundamental rights to direct the upbringing and education of their children.
State laws further reinforce these protections, aiming to balance parental authority with the functions of public education systems.
But do these legal protections reflect the day-to-day reality for families in Missouri House District 66?
Laura Mitchell Riley, a candidate for Missouri House District 66, emphasizes, "The person should be involved in every area of their child's school life and everything, and they're not doing that.
Parental rights in education form the cornerstone for families seeking to ensure their children grow in environments that respect their values.
Riley highlights that when these rights are diminished, it fractures the trust between families and schools.
Her stance echoes a wider concern among Missouri families, especially in District 66, about how school policies can sometimes bypass parents altogether.
Current Challenges in Public Education and Parental Involvement
- Concerns over transparency in school districts and board of education decisions: Parents are often left unaware of the details of curriculum changes, policy updates, or sensitive issues addressed in schools, undermining their ability to guide their children effectively.
- Issues with policies on transgenderism and lack of parental notification: Riley criticizes policies where schools do not inform parents when children express gender identity changes, emphasizing the need for parental knowledge and participation in these profoundly personal matters.
- The impact of DEI (Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion) programs on parental rights: Programs aimed at fostering inclusivity can sometimes conflict with family beliefs, raising questions about the appropriate boundaries of educational content and parental consent.
- The debate over religious content in public education, including calls to reinstate Bible teachings and the Ten Commandments: For many in District 66, including Riley, reintroducing Christian values into education is seen as crucial for moral guidance and community cohesion.
Riley states, "Parents should know what their children are being taught.
If the schools cannot be more open and transparent about what they’re teaching the children, then they have something to hide."
These challenges underline a growing divide between school systems and families.
Riley contends that schools’ lack of openness about curricula and policies erodes parental trust and participation.
The controversy over teaching gender identity changes without parental consent particularly strikes a nerve; Riley explains, “Parents should really be making a big deal out of that because you take care of that child. ” The tension surrounding Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion initiatives highlights the delicate balance schools must strike to respect varying family beliefs while promoting acceptance.
Moreover, Riley advocates for a return to Christian teachings in schools as a foundation for students' moral development.
The Role of Parents Versus School Districts in Education Decisions
- The balance of authority between parents and school districts: While school districts administer public education, ensuring standardized and quality instruction, parents seek to retain ultimate decision-making power regarding their children's moral upbringing and healthcare.
- Parental rights in school district policies and curriculum choices: Parents advocate for involvement in creating curricular content and influencing policies that affect their children directly.
- The importance of parental consent in healthcare decisions within schools: Medical and psychological services offered in schools require parental knowledge and explicit consent, reinforcing trust and respect for family autonomy.
- School district accountability and the need for parental engagement: Raising parental engagement in school governance through council participation and transparent communications strengthens community ties and educational outcomes.
Board of Education's Influence on Parental Rights
- How boards of education shape policies affecting parental rights: Boards have significant influence over school curricula, disciplinary policies, and health services, often making decisions that impact families without direct parental consultation.
- Calls for increased transparency and parental input in board decisions: Advocates like Riley push for school boards to prioritize openness and encourage parents to have a seat at the policymaking table.
Laura Riley asserts, "No teacher should be able to tell you about your child better than you do.
Parents should know their children better than anybody else."
The school board holds the formal power to enact policies that directly influence parental rights.
Riley’s call for transparency and greater parental input reflects her belief that family voices are too often sidelined.
She insists, “Your parents should know you better than anybody else. ” This perspective urges a collaborative approach where schools do not usurp parental authority but partner with families in children's upbringing.

Frequently Asked Questions About Parental Rights in Education
What are parental rights in education?
Parental rights in education refer to the legal and moral authority parents have to make decisions about their children's schooling, including curriculum, healthcare, and participation in school activities.
How can parents influence school district policies?
Parents can attend school board meetings, participate in parent-teacher organizations, and advocate for transparency and policies that respect their rights.
What legal protections exist for parental rights?
Legal protections include constitutional rights, state laws, and Supreme Court rulings that uphold parents' authority over their children's education and healthcare decisions.
How do parental rights affect healthcare decisions in schools?
Parental rights ensure that parents are informed and consent to any healthcare services or interventions provided to their children in schools.
What can parents do if they disagree with school curriculum?
Parents can request curriculum transparency, advocate for changes through school boards, or consider alternative education options such as homeschooling.
