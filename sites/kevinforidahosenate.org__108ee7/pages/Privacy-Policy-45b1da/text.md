Housing Housing needs look different across the district, but the challenge is real in most communities.

1) Tell us about yourself -- include information about your family, career, education, volunteer work, and any prior experience in public office.
East Idaho has been home to me for most of my life.
I moved here as a teenager, grew up in Ririe, and graduated from Ririe High School in 2005.
Today, I live in Rigby with my husband, Justin, and I am proud to call this community home.
My career has always been rooted in working directly with people.
In high school, I worked at Heise Hot Springs, which gave me an early appreciation for hard work and working with people.
Today, I am a REALTOR® with Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices Silverhawk Realty East Idaho.
My work puts me in direct conversation with people across our region, hearing fi rsthand about housing, rising costs, and what it takes to build stability here.
I currently serve on the Snake River MLS Board of Directors and have been involved locally through the Political Involvement Committee, the Ririe Coalition for Community Development where I served as Vice President, and the Ririe Chamber of Commerce.
I have not held elected office before.
What I bring is real-world experience, a strong connection to the community, and a clear understanding of the pressures people are facing.
2) Why are you seeking political office within your community?
Briefly explain your political platform.
I am running because of what I see and hear from people every day.
Families are working hard, but it is getting harder to keep up.
Costs continue to rise, and many people feel like the pressure is increasing faster than the solutions.
My campaign is grounded in a simple idea: working together for stronger communities.
That means focusing on practical issues that come up consistently, including cost of living, water, schools, support for first responders, and making sure local communities still have a real voice.
As a REALTOR®, I see how policy decisions play out in real life.
I am not running to build a political career.
I am running because I believe those real conversations should be reflected more clearly in the decisions being made.
Good public service requires listening, being honest about tradeoff s, and being willing to follow through.
3) What are the greatest challenges facing people in your district?
What is your plan to meet and overcome those challenges?
The biggest challenges I hear about are affordability, housing access, water, and keeping up with growth without putting too much pressure on local communities.
Costs are rising across the board, including property taxes, insurance, utilities, and everyday expenses.
For many families, that is making it harder to stay stable, let alone get ahead.
We need to take a close look at what is driving those costs and make sure state decisions are not adding unnecessary pressure.
That includes reviewing policies that impact housing, supporting infrastructure that keeps pace with growth, and being more deliberate about long-term planning.
We should also take a serious look at whether current policies are making it harder to build the kinds of homes people can actually afford.
If we want to improve housing access, we need to be willing to address those barriers directly.
Water is also central to our future.
Eastern Idaho depends on responsible management of both groundwater and surface water.
That means continuing conservation eff orts, supporting aquifer recharge, and making sure state and local planning reflects the real limits of our water supply.
We also need to ensure the state is doing its part to support schools, roads, and first responders so local communities are not continually asked to carry more of the burden.
4) How will you best represent the views of your constituents – even those with differing political views?
How do partisan politics play into your role as an elected leader?
My responsibility, when elected, is to represent the people of District 31 as a whole.
That starts with being accessible and willing to listen, especially when conversations are not easy.
I believe most people want practical solutions, accountability, and leaders who are willing to work in good faith.
There will always be differences in perspective, but that should not stop us from working toward outcomes that benefit the community.
I am a Democrat, and I am clear about that.
At the same time, I do not believe party affiliation should get in the way of solving problems or working with others to get results.
If something is going to move our communities forward, I am willing to work with anyone who is serious about making it happen.
5) What parts of the Legislative budget could use more funding?
Where are places in the budget where cuts could be made?
The state budget should reflect what matters most in people’s daily lives.
That includes schools, infrastructure, water management, and support for first responders.
At the same time, we need to be honest about what is happening at the local level.
When state funding does not keep pace, the difference does not disappear.
It shows up in bonds and levies, and that shifts more pressure onto local taxpayers.
I am not opposed to tax relief.
But if we continue reducing state revenue, we need to be clear about how we will maintain the services people rely on without pushing those costs somewhere else.
I would start by looking at administrative overhead, long-term vacant positions that remain unfi lled, duplication across agencies, and programs that cannot show clear public value.
Before asking communities to pay more locally, we should be making sure the state is operating as efficiently as possible.
If we get this right, families should feel relief, not more pressure showing up closer to home.
6) Do you believe ethics investigations in the Idaho Legislature should be kept secret from the public, or do you believe they should be open and transparent?
Why or why not?
Public trust depends on accountability.
I believe ethics investigations should be handled with as much transparency as possible while still protecting due process.
The public should have confidence that concerns are being taken seriously and addressed appropriately.
At the same time, there needs to be a fair process that protects against claims being used for political purposes before the facts are clear.
A strong system is one that is consistent, transparent when appropriate, and fair to everyone involved.
That balance is essential if we want people to trust the process.
7) Water availability and management are critical for eastern Idaho agriculture and growth.
What specific strategy or legislation would you support to protect groundwater and surface water resources, and what is your timeline for measurable improvements?” Water is one of the most important long-term issues facing eastern Idaho, and it needs to be treated that way.
We need to protect Idaho water for Idaho families, Idaho farms, and Idaho’s future.
That starts with practical management, accurate measurement, and coordination among the people who rely on it.
One clear example is aquifer recharge.
Idaho has invested in recharge efforts to strengthen the Eastern Snake Plain Aquifer, and those eff orts have produced measurable results.
At the same time, those gains are not guaranteed, and in some cases have not held over time.
We cannot afford to manage water reactively.
We need to stay ahead of the problem.
That means continuing recharge, improving measurement, supporting conservation where it works, and making sure growth decisions reflect the water we actually have.
This is about long-term responsibility, and getting it wrong has real consequences for agriculture, communities, and the future of eastern Idaho.
8) In March 2026, the Idaho House passed House Joint Memorial 17, requesting the U.S.
Supreme Court to overturn the 2015 Obergefell v.
Hodges ruling that legalized same-sex marriage nationwide.
Do you agree with the majority opinion of the Idaho House?
Do you oppose same-sex marriage in Idaho?
No, I do not support efforts to roll back the freedom to marry.
I believe committed couples should have the freedom to build a life together, make legal decisions for their families, and be treated equally under the law.
Marriage is a deeply personal commitment, and I do not believe government should take away rights that families already rely on.
This issue is not abstract to me.
My husband and I have built our life here in East Idaho.
We care about the same things our neighbors do: strong communities, stability, and a future where people are treated with dignity and respect.
I respect that people have different personal or religious beliefs.
But my role as a legislator would be to uphold fairness under the law and protect the rights of Idaho families.
