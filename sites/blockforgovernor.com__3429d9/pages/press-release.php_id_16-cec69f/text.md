"Rhode Island should continue its efforts to fight climate change and move towards a clean energy future.
However, our current trajectory is ambitious to the point of dangerous," Block said.
"If we don’t slow down the process of converting to renewable energy, we are going to drive up energy costs so high that they damage our economy, place enormous strain on our public finances, and make life even more unaffordable for Rhode Islanders.
We need to find the right balance.
Right now, we don’t have it."
Block called for slowing down the current timeline that requires Rhode Island to achieve 100% renewable energy by 2033.
He proposed pushing back the timeline to 2040, noting that Rhode Island is the only state with a goal of 100% renewable energy earlier than 2040.
"If we maintain the 2033 clean energy goal that is required by Rhode Island's Renewable Energy Standard, compliance costs will increase at an unsustainable rate and significantly drive up energy costs for Rhode Island households and businesses," Block said.
Block also wants to end the practice of using Rhode Island ratepayer funds to subsidize out-of-state renewable energy.
"When we subsidize clean energy, those projects must be within our borders so that we can fully realize the economic benefits of these investments, including job creation and improved energy infrastructure," said Block.
The plan also includes the following provisions:
– Requiring total transparency regarding the purchase of Renewable Energy Credits by Rhode Island electricity suppliers pursuant to the Renewable Energy Standard;
– Establishing a streamlined process, called the "Build It Here" program, for developing in-state renewable energy projects;
– Directing more funds from the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative to bill relief for low-income households; and
– Removing local property taxes from gas and electric bills.
"Right now, when it comes to clean energy, Rhode Island is acting like a state with a thriving economy, healthy finances at the state and local levels, and a disproportionately large carbon footprint.
We have none of those things," Block said.
"We should continue our transition to renewable energy, but we must do so with a sensible and realistic approach that accounts for the fact that climate change is not the only challenge facing Rhode Island."
