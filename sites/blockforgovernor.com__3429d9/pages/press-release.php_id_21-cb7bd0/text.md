★ November 3, 2026 Countdown to Election Day # Days # Hours # Minutes # Seconds Home About Issues Appearances Media & Press Ken I Be Honest Polls Donate Donate ← Media & Press Official Press Release Rhode Islanders Deserve Answers from Foulkes on CVS Opioid Case, Not a Spokesperson's Statement CVS has agreed to pay $315 million to resolve federal allegations of illegally filling opioid prescriptions and seeking improper payment from Medicare and other federal healthcare programs Release Date October 15, 2026 Location Barrington, Rhode Island Media Contact michelle@blockforgovernor.com Barrington, Rhode Island — BARRINGTON, RI – Independent candidate for Governor Ken Block today called on Democratic nominee Helena Foulkes to provide more details regarding her actions at CVS in light of the company’s decision to reach a settlement with federal prosecutors in a major opioid case.
“Helena Foulkes ran CVS Pharmacy during a period when serious violations allegedly occurred, and Rhode Islanders deserve more than a short statement from her campaign spokesperson,” said Block.
“The federal complaint says, among other things, that pharmacists faced pressure to fill prescriptions and CVS failed to respond adequately to warnings.
Given the devastating toll of the opioid crisis on families across Rhode Island, she owes the public more transparency and it should come directly from her.” On Tuesday, the U.S.
Department of Justice announced that CVS will pay $315 million to resolve allegations that it illegally filled opioid prescriptions and billed federal health programs for them.
The government's case covers conduct beginning in October 2013 and ending in December 2025.
Foulkes was president of CVS Pharmacy from January 2014 to February 2018, with responsibility for the company's retail business including pharmacy operations.
Foulkes responded to the settlement with a short written statement from a spokesperson .
Yesterday, she declined an interview with NBC 10 .
In early September, before the primary election, NBC 10 asked Foulkes whether CVS, under her leadership, ignored red flags as the lawsuit alleged.
She replied: “No.
CVS will defend itself at some point.
I think the record will show that CVS was a leader in the industry.” CVS agreed to settle the federal case and resolve the government's allegations without defending itself in court.
According to the complaint the Justice Department filed in December 2024 , CVS: (1) filled prescriptions for “pill mill” doctors; (2) dispensed dangerous combinations of opioids, benzodiazepines, and muscle relaxants; (3) ignored red flags raised by its own pharmacists and internal data; and (4) used performance metrics, incentive pay, and staffing levels that put profits ahead of patient safety.
“Six weeks ago, she told Rhode Islanders that CVS would defend its record,” Block said.
“Instead, CVS agreed to pay $315 million to resolve the federal case and accepted additional federal oversight of its controlled-substance dispensing through August 15, 2032 .
I think Rhode Islanders deserve to know what role Helena Foulkes played in approving and overseeing the performance metrics, incentive pay, and staffing policies that allegedly pressured pharmacists to fill prescriptions despite warning signs.” The complaint also describes certain decisions CVS made while Foulkes was in charge.
In 2015 and 2016, CVS tested a program requiring pharmacists to document every prescription they refused to fill because they doubted it was legitimate.
Most participating pharmacists said the program had value and it would have let CVS quickly identify and block high-risk prescribers.
CVS cancelled it .
Of the seven doctors named in the complaint, the alleged misconduct for six fell mostly within Foulkes's tenure, according to a Boston Globe review .
Among them is Dr.
Raymond Kraynak, Pennsylvania's top opioid prescriber in 2014, 2015 and 2016.
According to the complaint , CVS pharmacists and staff escalated concerns about Kraynak 17 times beginning in April 2014, more than any other prescriber in the country.
CVS did not block him until June 2017.
He was indicted that December, and in 2021 he pleaded guilty and admitted that opioids he prescribed resulted in the deaths of five patients.
He was sentenced to 15 years in federal prison .
Asked about Kraynak by name during an August 12 News debate , Foulkes did not address his case.
“Why did CVS keep filling Dr.
Raymond Kraynak's prescriptions for three years after its own pharmacists first raised concerns?” Block asked.
“Was Helena Foulkes aware of this at the time?” In the final days of her primary campaign, Foulkes told the Providence Journal that questions about opioids were “totally fair game.” “She said it herself, these questions are fair game,” Block said.
“Anyone who wants to serve as Governor must be willing to answer difficult questions about their professional record.” CVS admitted no wrongdoing in the settlement, which resolves allegations without a determination of liability.
Foulkes was not named as a defendant in the case.
