Chamber of Commerce.
Block also fires back at statement from Rhode Island legislators on abortion policy BARRINGTON, RI – Independent candidate for Governor Ken Block today criticized his Democratic opponent, Helena Foulkes, for skipping a gubernatorial candidate forum held this morning by the Northern Rhode Island Chamber of Commerce Release Date October 7, 2026 Location Barrington, Rhode Island Media Contact michelle@blockforgovernor.com Barrington, Rhode Island — "We need a Governor who isn't afraid to debate their ideas," said Block.
"Helena skipped today's forum and has already cancelled on the East Bay Chamber of Commerce as well.
This is not the behavior of a serious candidate for Governor." Foulkes has told the East Bay Chamber of Commerce that she will not attend their forum on October 28.
"Helena feels entitled to be our next Governor," Block said.
"That sense of entitlement has led her to believe that she is not obligated to prove to voters why she deserves to hold that office." Block and Foulkes both attended last night's AFL-CIO convention.
"Helena showed up for labor but bailed on the business community," Block said.
"I show up for everyone because that's what our next Governor should always do." Tonight, NBC 10 and Coastal ABC will host a televised debate.
The next debate of the campaign is WPRO's gubernatorial debate on Tuesday, October 13.
"Given her increasing habit of skipping gubernatorial forums, Helena should fully commit to the WPRO debate on Tuesday," said Block.
The Block campaign also responded to a statement issued yesterday by Democratic members of the Rhode Island General Assembly criticizing his position on reproductive healthcare.
"I am pro-choice and I've always been pro-choice," said Block.
"At a recent forum, I said I would work with the General Assembly on legislation relating to abortion.
I am surprised that comment would upset Democratic legislators." The statement on Tuesday cited Block's recent comments at the Women’s Fund of Rhode Island and the League of Women Voters Rhode Island Gubernatorial Forum on September 30.
"This is what desperate politicians do," Block added.
"They enlist their allies to engage in performative politics and create controversy where none exists.
Helena doesn't want to have an honest debate over the policies that the next Governor can actually impact.
