Barrington, RI —
Rhode Islanders are paying hundreds of dollars every year in government charges through their utility bills.
Some appear as line items.
Others are buried in rates after state laws and regulations cause utilities to incur new costs that are passed on to RI Energy's customers.
Many customers are often called ratepayers, but most have no idea these charges are part of their bill.
Independent gubernatorial candidate Ken Block announced the Honest Utility Bill Initiative, a proposal that targets a 25 percent reduction in residential and business electric bills.
The proposal would seek to eliminate unnecessary government charges, make remaining charges transparent, and move appropriate taxes from utility bills to the state budget.
Gas bills and other utility bills will be discussed in future announcements.
“Government has turned your electric bill into a second tax
bill.
Your utility bill should tell you what electricity

Making utility charges transparent
According to Block, more than 30 percent of a typical Rhode Island electric bill consists of taxes, fees, surcharges, and other government-mandated costs rather than the direct cost of producing and delivering electricity.
Some costs are clearly identified while others are not.
In both cases, Rhode Islanders pay.
The issue is not whether every program is worthwhile.
The issue is how those programs are funded and whether utility bills are the appropriate place to collect those costs.
A comprehensive review of utility charges
As Governor, Block plans to direct a comprehensive review of every state-imposed utility bill charge.
Each charge would have to justify its continued existence.
Charges that no longer make sense would be considered for elimination.
Programs that continue to serve an important public purpose would be reviewed to determine whether they should be included in the state budget instead of monthly utility bills.
Moving these programs into the state budget is not only more
transparent — it is also generally less regressive.
A regressive tax takes a larger percentage of income from lower-income earners than from higher-income earners.
Financing broad public policy through utility bills requires families of all income levels to pay these charges every month.
Funding those same programs through the normal budget process can spread the burden more fairly and make public spending easier to review.
The Block Test
As part of the Honest Utility Bill Initiative, every government-imposed utility charge would be evaluated using four questions:
- Does it make sense?
- Does it work?
- Is it cost-effective?
- Is it easy for Rhode Islanders to understand and live with?
“Throughout my career, I have asked a simple question: Why are we paying for this?” Block said.
Families do not need another politician agreeing that utility bills are unaffordable.
They need someone focused on lowering the bills permanently.
