Opinion Diagnostic View results “Ken Block doesn’t just have a path to winning the November gubernatorial election, he has a runway.” — Brian Wynne, President, Opinion Diagnostics Opinion Diagnostics surveyed 802 likely 2026 Rhode Island general-election voters about the gubernatorial race.
In initial matchups, independent Ken Block places second behind either Gov.
Dan McKee or Democratic challenger Helena Foulkes (with Aaron Guckian third), but after voters receive brief additional information about Block, he moves into the lead over McKee and pulls even with Foulkes.
Block is also the only tested candidate with a net-favorable image.
What voters told us Key insights ✓ Top voter concerns are affordability (38%), followed by infrastructure (12%)—notably driven by Washington Bridge impacts—and eliminating waste, fraud, and abuse in state government (12%). ✓ In terms of favorability, Block holds a positive rating of +10 and has significant potential for increased recognition among voters. ✓ McKee’s favorability is markedly negative at -33 across all demographic groups. ✓ Foulkes maintains a slightly negative overall rating at -3, while Guckian remains predominantly unfamiliar to the electorate. ✓ The analysis suggests that Block could unite independent voters, attract more Republicans as his chances improve, and gain support from many undecided voters. ✓ This positions him as a strong candidate who might even become the frontrunner, depending on the results of the Democratic primary.
Head-to-head results Ballot tests Two hypothetical matchups were tested: McKee vs.
Guckian vs.
Block, if McKee survives the Democratic primary, and Foulkes vs.
Guckian vs.
Block, in the event she prevails over McKee.
Each matchup identified the party affiliation of each candidate.
The relevant Democrat and Guckian were randomized in the first or second position with Block appearing third to mimic the order candidates will appear on the ballot in November.
How the research worked About the survey Respondents were presented with three pieces of information about Ken Block: why an independent like Block would be best positioned to address issues that partisan politicians have failed to fix; Block’s experience as an outsider with a record of exposing and fixing waste in government; and Block’s track record as a successful business executive, positioning him to address issues like the Washington Bridge failure and Rhode Island’s payroll processor.
Each piece of information was portrayed as a campaign message from Block’s supporters, not as a hard fact respondents should necessarily accept at face value. ★ After hearing these statements, Block takes the lead in a matchup versus McKee and is tied in a matchup against Foulkes.
