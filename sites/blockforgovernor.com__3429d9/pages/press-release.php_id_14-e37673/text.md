Barrington, Rhode Island —
BARRINGTON, RI — A new poll of 800 likely Rhode Island voters finds that independent Ken Block would defeat Democrat Helena Foulkes in a two-way race for Governor, 44 percent to 39 percent, and that Republican Aaron Guckian is headed for the weakest finish by any Republican nominee for Governor since the founding of the Republican Party in 1856.
“The poll says the same thing that most voters tell me,” said Block.
“Rhode Island faces serious challenges and they wish it was a two-person race.”
The survey, conducted September 23–25 by Opinion Diagnostics for the Block for Governor campaign, shows Foulkes leading the current five-way field with 41 percent, Block second at 24 percent and Guckian third at 19 percent, with 16 percent undecided.
Block has run ahead of Guckian in every public poll since June.
The results align with the September 24 poll conducted by the University of New Hampshire, which found Foulkes leading with 44%, Block second at 25%, and Guckian at 17%.
The new poll finds that Block is the only candidate in the race whom voters view favorably: 37 percent favorable to 23 percent unfavorable, a net of +15.
Foulkes is at 42 percent favorable and 43 percent unfavorable, with 29 percent of likely voters holding a very unfavorable view of her two weeks after she won the Democratic primary.
Guckian is at 25 percent favorable and 27 percent unfavorable, and nearly half of voters cannot rate him.
Asked how they would vote in a two-way race between Foulkes and Block, voters chose Block 44 to 39 with 17 percent undecided.
Block would carry independents, the largest bloc in the state, 59 to 22, and Republicans 78 to 4.
Foulkes would carry Democrats 73 to 13.
The margin holds under every turnout model tested, including the larger and more Democratic electorates of presidential years.
Guckian’s 19 percent would be the lowest share for a Republican nominee for Governor in the 104 elections since 1856; the current low is James Nugent’s 21.5 percent in 1974.
Guckian holds only 62 percent of registered Republicans on the five-way ballot, and when his supporters are asked to choose between Foulkes and Block, they pick Block 76 to 4.
Fifty-nine percent of likely voters are not voting for Foulkes today, and 40 percent cannot yet rate Block; among those who can, 62 percent view him favorably.
“Rhode Island voters have told us two things,” said Brian Wynne of Opinion Diagnostics.
“They do not want Helena Foulkes as their next governor, and the candidate positioned to beat her is Ken Block.
The vote against Foulkes is a majority of the electorate.
Today it is split between a candidate who leads her one-on-one and a candidate on track for the worst Republican showing in the history of the office.
Guckian’s own voters have already made their choice: given Foulkes or Block, they choose Block nineteen to one.”
Methodology
Opinion Diagnostics surveyed 800 likely November 2026 general election voters in Rhode Island from September 23 to 25, 2026.
Respondents were randomly selected from the state voter file and contacted by text message with a unique link to a web survey, and were screened to confirm they are registered and definitely or probably voting in November.
Results were weighted to the likely November 2026 electorate on party registration, region, gender, age and race, modeled on the average of the 2018 and 2022 general elections.
The margin of error is ±3.5 percentage points at the 95 percent confidence level; subgroups carry a wider margin.
The survey was conducted for Ken Block for Governor.
The full memorandum, including crosstabs by party, gender, age, region and education, is available on request.
