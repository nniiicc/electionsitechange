I want to make my position clear.
I am not a “gun-control guy,” but I am also not a “gun guy.” Throughout this campaign, I have consistently said that gun issues are not among my primary concerns.
They are not.
I am not ideologically opposed to firearms in any way; I strive to not be ideological about most issues.
I believe law-abiding citizens have a right to own firearms, and nothing in my campaign materials or interviews indicates otherwise—because that is my position.
The Supreme Court has made clear that Second Amendment rights are to be broadly interpreted.
Cases involving AR-15-platform semi-automatic rifles and similar firearms are currently before the Court, and many firearms-related questions will ultimately be resolved through the courts.
Rhode Island faces many urgent challenges.
If we do not address them soon, businesses will continue to leave, jobs will disappear, and more of our young adults will depart the state.
Those issues, not gun policy, will consume the vast majority of my time and political capital as governor.
My priorities will include: ✔️ .
Our infrastructure is in poor condition, and DOT management needs fundamental reform.
We need to rebuild the department around accountability, competence, and results. ✔️ Ending costly failures in state technology .
The state has repeatedly spent enormous sums on computer systems that fail to deliver.
We wasted roughly a billion dollars on UHIP and experienced a payroll-system fiasco costing hundreds of millions more.
I built and managed large computer systems, and I know how to approach these problems. ✔️ Improving our schools .
For decades, some of our urban school systems have performed poorly.
When students graduate without the skills they need to succeed, they become disadvantaged adults, and their children often enter the same struggling schools.
That cycle must end.
Breaking it will require a great deal of political courage, accountability, and a commitment to improving our schools as quickly as possible. ✔️ Reducing waste and fraud .
I helped the State of Texas identify and save a billion dollars through efforts targeting waste and fraud.
I will bring that same approach to Rhode Island.
Our roughly $15 billion state budget is not sustainable without significant reforms. ✔️ Lowering electricity costs .
Nearly 40 percent of our electric bills consists of taxes and fees imposed by municipalities, fire districts, water authorities, and the state.
We need to reduce those costs and lower the burden on ratepayers. ✔️ Fixing healthcare .
I have a detailed plan to address Rhode Island’s healthcare challenges and bring down costs while improving the system. ✔️ Stabilizing our cities and towns .
Providence, Cranston, Warwick, East Providence, Johnston, Pawtucket, Woonsocket, and other communities face serious financial challenges.
As governor, I will provide firm guidance and leadership on the state level to help these communities restore their financial stability.
As you can see, I will have more than enough work to keep me busy.
My goal is straightforward: put Rhode Island on a better path, make government work, and reduce costs for the people who live here.
