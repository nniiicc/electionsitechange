Let’s Talk RI Videos coming soon Cost of Living Relief Click here to download a PDF of Ken's Cost of Living Relief Plan Government shouldn't be allowed to hide the cost of government My plan targets hidden and regressive taxes, fees, and government-imposed costs that are driving the affordability crisis in Rhode Island.
My "Cost of Living Relief Plan" is aimed at reducing the taxes, fees, surcharges and other government-imposed costs buried in the everyday bills Rhode Islanders pay.
Rhode Islanders are being nickel-and-dimed to death, and too often government makes it difficult for us to even see what we're paying.
That's not just an affordability problem.
It's a transparency problem.
And there's another problem with the way we collect this money.
Many of these taxes and fees are regressive.
They fall on necessities like electricity, cell phones, insurance, and transportation, and they hit people who are struggling to make ends meet much harder than people who aren't.
A pair of bills introduced in the General Assembly this year provides a striking example of the transparency problem.
Senate Bill 2020 and its House companion, House Bill 7465, proposed a new 0.25% assessment on property-and-casualty insurance premiums to fund fire-service training.
The legislation provided that the assessment "shall not be passed onto policyholders as a specific line-item surcharge." Think about that.
The government was proposing a new cost on insurance and specifically preventing it from appearing as a separate surcharge on your bill.
If the government imposes a cost that Rhode Islanders ultimately pay, people have a right to know about it.
Thankfully, these bills didn't pass.
Electric bills provide an even bigger example.
The state's own analysis says state programs and taxes account for approximately 24% of the average residential electric bill.
But even that doesn't tell the whole story.
Municipal taxes and other government-imposed costs are embedded elsewhere in utility rates and aren't identified to customers as taxes.
When those costs are included, government-driven costs approach 40% of a typical electric bill.
And these hidden costs aren't progressive.
The electric company doesn't ask how much money you make before collecting them.
A family struggling to pay the rent has to pay them just like a wealthy household does.
The difference is that the family struggling to get by feels every dollar.
Rhode Island cannot control inflation, interest rates or the national economy.
But it can control the taxes, fees and mandates imposed by state and local government.
Government shouldn't be allowed to hide the cost of government.
If the General Assembly wants to impose a tax or fee, disclose it.
If government wants to spend money on a program, put it in the budget.
Debate it publicly.
Debate it annually.
Make legislators vote for it.
And let Rhode Islanders see what they're paying.
And while we're making these costs visible, let's ask a second question: why are we financing so much of our government with regressive taxes on things everyone needs?
That's backwards.
Rhode Island has an affordability crisis.
Government should stop making it worse.
Block said the five proposals would be the beginning of a broader review of the taxes, fees, surcharges and mandates embedded in the cost of living in Rhode Island.
Rhode Island government can't fix every cause of the affordability crisis.
But it can stop adding unnecessary and regressive costs to the things people need every day.
Make the costs visible.
Make the government justify them.
Make them fair.
And get the costs down.
Block's "Cost of Living Relief Plan" includes the following policies: 1 Reduce government-imposed costs on electric bills My administration would target a significant reduction in residential and business electric bills by eliminating unnecessary government-imposed costs and moving appropriate and necessary taxation from electric bills to other, fairer mechanisms available to the state.
Our electric bills have become hidden tax bill.
Electricity is a necessity.
We shouldn't use everyone's electric bill as a backdoor way to raise revenue, especially when those charges bear little relationship to someone's ability to pay.
If the state needs the revenue, let's raise it transparently and fairly.
2 Prohibit auto insurers from using credit-based insurance scores to set rates Rhode Island allows auto insurers to use credit-based insurance scores when determining rates.
My administration would work with the General Assembly to prohibit the practice.
Your auto insurance should be based on how you drive.
We should not penalize a good driver because they struggle financially.
In fact, charging someone more because they're already having trouble making ends meet just makes it harder for them to get ahead.
3 Reduce taxes and fees on cell-phone bills According to the Tax Foundation, Rhode Island has the highest state and local wireless tax and fee burden in New England at 15.75%, compared with 10.75% in Massachusetts and 8.42% in Connecticut.
Rhode Island applies its 7% sales tax to cell-phone service, imposes a separate 5% telecommunications gross-receipts tax, and adds a monthly E-911 surcharge.
These taxes and fees increase the cost of providing wireless service and ultimately contribute to what consumers pay.
My administration would work to significantly reduce the taxes and fees imposed on wireless service.
Where the revenue is necessary to fund state services, I would seek to replace it through fairer and more transparent sources of revenue.
A cell phone isn't a luxury anymore.
It's a necessity.
Yet we pile taxes and fees onto everyone's cell-phone bill without regard to their ability to pay.
If government needs the money, it should raise it in a fair and transparent way - not hide regressive taxes in the bills for things everyone needs.
4 Stop the return of truck tolls and oppose passenger-vehicle tolls My administration would request a full stop on the planned return of Rhode Island's truck-tolling program and oppose new tolls on passenger vehicles.
Groceries…gasoline…almost everything we buy has to get to Rhode Island somehow, and much of it arrives by truck.
The truck toll doesn't stop with the trucking company.
It becomes another cost of bringing goods into Rhode Island and ultimately another cost paid by consumers.
Truck tolls are another tax you pay - you just don't see it.
5 Eliminate RIPTA fares for one year and measure the results RIPTA currently collects roughly $9 million a year in passenger fares.
My administration would implement a one-year fare-free trial, replacing that revenue with savings identified elsewhere in the state budget, and measure the effect on ridership and transportation costs.
Finding $9 million in a $15 billion state budget to fully subsidize RIPTA fares should be like finding a dime in your seat cushion.
If we can't find savings of that magnitude and redirect them toward something that could meaningfully lower transportation costs for Rhode Islanders, we're not looking hard enough.
Truck Tolls As your Governor, I will suspend the truck toll program because it makes no sense.
When we last tolled trucks, we took in over $100 million from the trucking industry - much of it from Rhode Island businesses.
We only kept about $35 million, sending much of the rest of it out-of-state to pay for - wait for it - the tolling program.
What genius decided that pulling $100 million from our economy to net $35 million was a good idea?
It is a TERRIBLE idea, and one that we all pay for, since trucking companies pass the cost of tolls on to consumers.
Rhode Island imposed a major new cost on commerce, spent tens of millions building and administering the collection system, and then spent millions more defending it in court.
Now the state wants to resume tolls.
Doubling down on such a bad idea is not how we are going to fix our state.
I will veto any effort made to expand the tolling program to automobiles.
The Social and Cultural Issues Prioritizing Effective Governance Over Social Disputes Social issues are undeniably important and frequently ignite strong arguments and disagreements among Rhode Islanders.
These discussions often become passionate and personal, yet it is crucial to understand that such matters are not the ones threatening the state’s financial stability or future.
Ken’s campaign is not about forcing his own views on citizens or allocating political or financial resources to issues that will ultimately be settled by courts at the state and federal levels.
Instead, he is committed to fixing a government that has grown wasteful and dysfunctional.
Residents of Rhode Island do not need to agree on every social issue; what truly matters is the ability to come together and build a government that operates efficiently and effectively for all.
Understanding Division and the Importance of Unity Social and cultural issues tend to be the most divisive, as they touch on deeply held personal values.
Although these topics are significant, at this pivotal moment, Rhode Islanders must unite—even in disagreement—to address and solve the state’s most urgent challenges.
There is broad agreement that the state government wastes billions of taxpayer dollars due to dysfunction, inefficiency, and misplaced priorities.
Political views on social issues are not a factor in the push to resolve these government shortcomings.
Personal Opinions and the Quest for Common Ground Ken acknowledges that he has his own perspective on contentious social and cultural issues and recognizes that achieving consensus can be challenging.
Nevertheless, he finds reassurance in the fact that reaching agreement on every issue is not necessary to move forward and address Rhode Island’s problems.
Focus on Governance, Not Ideology Ken is running for Governor, not for King, Dictator, or Tsar, and he does not intend to impose his own beliefs on others.
Although many single-issue voters understandably want to know a candidate’s position on every subject, the reality is that debates over topics such as abortion and firearms will likely continue for decades.
Meanwhile, Rhode Island faces a governmental crisis that requires immediate attention.
The Root of Rhode Island’s Challenges Rhode Island’s most serious issues stem from an inefficient and ineffective government.
Taxpayers are investing large amounts in a government that fails to deliver results, and the situation continues to deteriorate each year.
State Budget The state budget has nearly doubled in ten years while incomes have not even begun to keep pace.
Our state employees are crippled by poor, outdated computer systems and obsolete equipment.
Rhode Island has spent billions on these systems and have not got the results that were paid for.
Ken will stop wasting money on poorly run projects while making our state workforce better trained and more effective.
Millionaires Tax If passed by the legislature, Ken will veto a Millionaires Tax.
Ken’s mission is to reduce expenses, not add more taxes. $15 billion should be more than enough.
Government Efficiency Ken’s Department Heads will be leaders who understand efficiencies and customer service.
State government will focus on proper training and equipment for employees.
The Block administration will systematically review operations, systems, services, and personnel for every state agency.
Bottlenecks, missing resources, and root causes of dysfunction will be identified and fixed.
Housing Ken understands that all state policies affect housing, directly or indirectly.
We struggle to build affordable homes while promoting solar projects that take thousands of acres of buildable land.
The Block administration will review state-owned land and buildings for affordable housing opportunities.
All legislation should include a 'Housing Note' identifying its impact on housing availability.
We need more housing – legislation should not make that harder.
RIDOT (Rhode Island Department of Transportation) Ken will revamp RIDOT's management and change how the department operates.
Make the department fair, transparent, and efficient.
Ensure contracts are awarded based on merit.
Judges and Magistrates We have made progress in diversifying judges but more work is needed.
The judicial selection process remains highly political.
Ken will prioritize judicial and legal merit in appointments.
The administration will push to move magistrate appointments away from the General Assembly.
Use the same fair process as judicial appointments.
Election Choices Elections should allow for real choice, not political entrenchment.
Rhode Islanders deserve to vote for something, not against someone.
Voting for Ken Block means choosing a government focused on fixing problems.
