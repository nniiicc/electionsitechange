“The truck tolls make no sense,” said Block.
“We took more than $100 million out of the trucking industry and our economy, much of which came from Rhode Island businesses, while only a fraction of that money ultimately benefited taxpayers.
In fact, most of it went out-of-state to cover the costs of the tolling program itself.” Rhode Island's truck tolls operated from 2018 until a federal court shut them down in September 2022.
State records show approximately $103 million in toll revenue during that period.
However, Rhode Island paid approximately $53 million to toll-system contractor Kapsch, incurred at least $7 million in documented RITBA toll-processing expenses, and reported approximately $8 million in legal fees and costs defending the program.
That leaves no more than roughly $35 million of the original $103 million after those documented costs.
“Rhode Island collected more $103 million from truck tolls but after the costs of building, operating, and defending the toll system in court, the state appears to have netted just $35 million,” Block added.
“That is certainly not worth the economic harm of this policy.” The Rhode Island Trucking Association has said that 94% of the tolls collected would come from local businesses upon reinstatement of the program, which can no longer include daily caps on tolls due a ruling by the First U.S.
Circuit Court of Appeals.
“These tolls ultimately raise costs on both businesses and consumers,” said Block.
“Rhode Island companies that move significant quantities of goods by truck, such as Ocean State Job Lot and our supermarkets, operate in an economy where transportation costs matter greatly.
Those costs eventually show up elsewhere in the price of goods, deferred investment in new equipment, or lower profitability.
Compounding the cost of tolls, local truckers are paying higher diesel fuel and insurance costs.” Block said his administration would suspend the tolling program and veto any legislation that establishes tolls on passenger vehicles.
“Rhode Island imposed a major new cost on commerce, spent tens of millions building and administering the collection system, and then spent millions more defending it in court,” Block said.
“Now the state wants to start up the tolls again.
Doubling down on such a bad idea is not how we are going to fix our state.” Download Fact Sheet Media Contact For questions, interviews or additional information: ken@blockforgovernor.com About Ken Block Ken Block is an independent candidate for Governor of Rhode Island.
