It’s the second independent survey this fall to put Blake in front, following an August WNYT/Survey USA poll that had Blake ahead 43% to 39%.
The numbers cap a week in which Constantino did everything but campaign in the North Country.
He paid private investigators to spy on Blake’s family dairy farm with drones, then staged a phony press conference in Colonie to level false accusations about what they supposedly found.
From there he went to Canada to visit a YouTuber, and today he’s in Syracuse for a rally with President Trump.
It didn’t work.
The same week Constantino tried to smear Blake’s character, Siena found that voters see Blake as the more honest candidate and the one more in touch with their lives.
North Country voters know their neighbors, and they know a manufactured scandal when they see one.
While Constantino chases headlines, North Country residents across party lines are moving toward Blake: independents are breaking his way, a meaningful share of Republicans are crossing over, and voters trust him more to bring down costs, the issue weighing hardest on families here.
Rather than accept the results, Constantino’s campaign is picking a fight with a second highly rated independent pollster, after threatening to sue WNYT over a SurveyUSA poll that also showed Blake leading.
During the primary, Constantino released internal poll after internal poll.
In the general election, he hasn’t released a single one.
“While Anthony was flying drones over my barn, I was in small businesses and union halls across the North Country listening to people who can’t afford diesel, groceries, or a trip to a hospital that keeps cutting services,” said Blake Gendebien.
“That’s the difference in this race.
He’s running a reality show.
I’m running to lower costs, protect rural healthcare, and clean up Washington.
Republicans, independents, and Democrats are coming along because they’re tired of the circus.” “If Anthony’s team thinks Siena got it wrong, they should put their own numbers on the table,” said Michael Waller, Campaign Manager.
“We already know what’s coming instead: they’ll call this a Democrat scam and demand Siena go door to door apologizing to every voter.
If anyone owes the North Country an apology, it’s Anthony, for running a campaign that puts his gripes over their needs.” ### Blake and his wife Carmen have raised their three sons on Twin Mill Farms in Lisbon, milking 500 cows and farming 1200 acres.
Blake and Carmen also started The Jules of Life Foundation, which provides resources and support to North Country families battling pediatric cancer.
For the past 10 years, Blake has served as Vice Chair of the Cooperative Board of Agri-Mark, representing the interests of farmers from across the region.
He is a former member of the Lisbon Central School Board and was a longtime junior varsity basketball coach.
Blake is committed to being a bipartisan leader who will lower costs, save our rural healthcare, and end the corruption in Washington.
