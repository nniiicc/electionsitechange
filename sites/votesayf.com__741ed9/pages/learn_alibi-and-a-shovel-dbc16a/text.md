🚀 Before You Start
This song uses only 4 chords — all the same moveable shape.
Once you learn one, you know all four.
Step 1
Sit with the guitar across your lap.
Keep your fretting wrist relaxed — your thumb behind the neck, fingers curled.
Step 2
Index finger on the low E string.
Ring finger two frets higher on A.
Pinky on D at the same fret as ring.
That's it — one shape for the whole song.
Step 3
This song uses fingerpicking on strings E, A, and D (the three thick strings).
The thin strings aren't played.
Less to worry about.
Step 4
The recording is 128 BPM.
Start at half speed.
Clean and slow beats fast and sloppy every time.

🎤 How to Sing While You Play
This is the hardest part — but it's also where the magic happens.
Take it in stages:
1
2
Forget the words.
Just hum along to the recording while you play.
This gets your voice and hands working at the same time without the added challenge of remembering lyrics.
3
Speak the words in time with your picking, like spoken word poetry.
This ingrains the rhythm of the lyrics with the rhythm of the chords before your voice takes over.
4
The ▸ breath markers above show you where the natural pauses fall.
Always breathe at the end of a lyric phrase, not mid-line.
This also gives you a split-second to prepare the next chord.
5
Real music doesn't stop.
If you miss a chord change or lose your place in the lyrics, keep the picking hand moving.
The rhythm is the backbone — protect it above everything else.
You can fix notes; a stalled rhythm kills the whole feel.
6
