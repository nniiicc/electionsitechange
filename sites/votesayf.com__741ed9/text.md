Why "Good Enough" Isn't Good Enough for Indie Artists
The math behind why I'm asking ASCAP to modernize.
The Old Way: Sampling
ASCAP cannot listen to every radio station 24/7.
So they listen to a few stations for a few hours (a "sample") and use math to guess what was played everywhere else.
The Modern Reality: Census
With digital fingerprinting (like Shazam technology) and logs, it is technically possible to track 100% of plays rather than guessing.
The Flaw That Hurts Indie Artists
If my song plays on a local station in Pompano Beach on a Tuesday at 2 AM, and ASCAP wasn't listening at that exact moment, I get paid $0.
It's a lottery system.
Continuing to use "Sampling" for radio in 2026 disproportionately hurts independent artists who get sporadic airplay, while major label artists (who get played every hour) are guaranteed to be caught in the sample.
The Legal Foundation
The Consent Decree: Second Amended Final Judgment (AFJ2)
Case: United States v.
ASCAP, 41 Civ. 1395, S.D.N.Y.
(1941, amended June 11, 2001)
The Obligation (Section VII): ASCAP must distribute royalties collected in a manner that is "fair and non-discriminatory" among its members.
Where The Violation Occurs:
- Sampling methodology systematically under-represents independent artists with sporadic airplay
- Major label artists (hourly rotation) are guaranteed to be captured; indie artists are not
- Technology exists (digital fingerprinting, broadcast logs) to achieve census-level accuracy
- Continued use of outdated sampling when better methods exist = discriminatory distribution
"Under the Consent Decree, we are owed a fair and non-discriminatory distribution system.
I am calling on ASCAP to move from 'Sampling' to 'Census' tracking for all mediums.
It's not just about money; it's about the poetic justice of being counted."
