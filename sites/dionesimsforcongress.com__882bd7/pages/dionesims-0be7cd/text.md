Harris Endorses Dione Sims for Congress Read the official announcement The Torchbearer Dione Sims The Listener | The Builder | Granddaughter of Dr.
Opal Lee Freedom is not a word Dione Sims learned from a slogan.
She learned it as a responsibility, something you protect with your hands, your time, and your choices.
In her family, freedom was never abstract.
It was measured in whether a neighbor could keep their home, whether a child had a real chance to succeed, and whether a community had enough food, safety, and opportunity to make it through the week with dignity.
From an early age, Dione watched what it looked like to stand up for others, not for applause, but because it was the right thing to do.
Her compass was shaped by her grandmother, Dr.
Opal Lee, the "Grandmother of Juneteenth," whose life's work showed that freedom is not something you wait to be handed down.
It is something you walk toward, sometimes literally, and something you defend for the next generation.
Dione learned that progress requires patience, courage, and consistency.
She learned that freedom is incomplete if it only reaches a few, and that leadership is not about titles, but about showing up, again and again, even when the work is hard and the outcomes are uncertain.
That belief has guided Dione's life's work.
In 2000, she founded Unity Unlimited, Inc., a Fort Worth based nonprofit dedicated to unity, educational opportunity, and community understanding.
Her work has never been about performance or politics.
It has always been about building practical pathways that help people thrive, especially those who have been overlooked or pushed to the margins.
Through Unity Unlimited, Dione has created programs that expose young people to vocational and cultural opportunities, support families, and bring communities together around shared humanity and common sense solutions.
Her leadership style is rooted in listening first, identifying what people actually need, and then doing the work to meet those needs with integrity.
Dione is often called the "Granddaughter of Juneteenth," not as a title, but as a reflection of the responsibility she carries.
Since 2015, she has co-produced the Annual Juneteenth Fort Worth Celebration alongside Dr.
Opal Lee.
She helped engineer the Opal's Walk campaign that moved the nation toward recognizing Juneteenth as a federal holiday, and she stood beside her grandmother at the White House for the historic bill signing.
Later, as the Founding Executive Director of the National Juneteenth Museum, Dione worked to ensure that the story of freedom is not just commemorated, but understood and carried forward.
"Juneteenth is not just history to me, it is a living assignment, to keep widening the circle of freedom." For Dione, freedom is not symbolic.
It is deeply personal and deeply practical.
That is why she sponsors Opal's Farm, an urban farming initiative that reduces food insecurity while offering second chance training and employment opportunities.
It reflects her belief that people deserve more than survival.
They deserve stability, opportunity, and the dignity of being able to provide for themselves and their families.
By profession, Dione is a Software Quality Manager, a role that mirrors her mindset.
She believes in accountability, systems that work, and follow through.
People trust her because she says what she means and does what she says she will do.
She is known for being honest, sometimes blunt, but always rooted in care.
When conflict arises, Dione does not inflame it.
She brings people together, names the truth, and works toward resolution.
Dione's service extends across civic, faith, and community spaces.
She has held leadership roles in women's and service organizations, serves on advisory boards, and brings her faith into her work as a minister of the Gospel.
She is a wife and grandmother who understands the hopes families carry for their children and grandchildren, and the fear that comes when those hopes feel increasingly out of reach.
Dione is running for Congress because she believes freedom must protect people, not just power.
Today, too many Texas families are working hard and still falling behind, not because they failed, but because the system keeps pulling the floor out from under them.
Costs rise, wages lag, and decisions made far away fail to reflect the realities of everyday life in Texas communities.
"Hard work should pay off, and nobody should have to live one emergency away from losing everything." In the 25th Congressional District, which stretches from Fort Worth through rural Texas, Dione sees something rare and powerful.
She sees a district that reflects the country itself, urban and rural, diverse in race and background, connected by shared needs.
People want stability.
They want opportunity.
They want leaders who respect their work, their faith, and their future.
Dione Sims talks about freedom the way families in Texas live it, as something practical.
Freedom is being able to work one job and still pay the bills without needing a second shift, a side hustle, or a constant prayer that nothing breaks.
Freedom is not wondering whether a doctor's visit will turn into debt, or whether your child's future will be smaller than your own.
And it is not watching Washington protect the powerful while telling everyone else to "work harder." Dione is running because she believes freedom has to mean security, dignity, and the right to build a good life, not just for a few, but for everyone.
