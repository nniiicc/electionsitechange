Harris Endorses Dione Sims for Congress Read the official announcement Back to the Platform Issues Freedom to choose data centers Innovation That Serves Texans — Not the Other Way Around Position brief Responsible Technological Innovation Innovation That Serves Texans — Not the Other Way Around Dione Sims believes America should lead the world in artificial intelligence and emerging technology.
But technological leadership must be measured by more than how quickly we build, automate, or deploy.
Progress should improve people's lives, strengthen communities, expand economic opportunity, and protect the resources Texans depend on.
Artificial intelligence has the potential to transform healthcare, education, agriculture, manufacturing, government, transportation, energy, and small business.
It can increase productivity, create new industries, and strengthen America's global competitiveness.
But innovation without responsible oversight can also create serious consequences—from workforce displacement and misuse of personal data to increased demands on electricity, water, land, and infrastructure associated with large-scale data-center development.
Dione supports a balanced approach: encourage innovation, protect American competitiveness, and establish responsible guardrails so technological progress does not come at the expense of workers, families, taxpayers, communities, or future generations.
Texans Should Decide What Gets Built in Texas Communities Dione believes one principle should be clear: Texans—not billionaire technology executives, distant corporations, or private political interests—should ultimately have a meaningful voice in determining whether major data-center developments are appropriate for their communities.
Data centers can bring substantial economic benefits.
They can attract billions of dollars in private investment, expand local tax bases, create construction and technology jobs, strengthen digital and electrical infrastructure, and position Texas as a global leader in the rapidly expanding AI economy.
Dione believes there should be another important benefit: meaningful investment in the communities that host these facilities.
When major technology companies choose Texas communities for large-scale data centers—particularly when they receive public incentives or infrastructure support—Dione believes they should be prepared to become genuine community partners.
That means making significant, measurable investments that directly benefit local residents.
Depending on the needs of the community, those investments could include: College and technical scholarships for local students, particularly those pursuing technology, engineering, cybersecurity, skilled trades, energy, and other high-demand careers; Technology and equipment for public schools, including computers, laboratory equipment, robotics programs, broadband infrastructure, cybersecurity resources, and modern classroom technology; AI and technology education for students, teachers, workers, entrepreneurs, and small businesses; Internships, apprenticeships, and workforce-development programs that create pathways for local residents to participate in the technology economy; Support for local nonprofit and civic organizations addressing education, youth development, food insecurity, veterans, seniors, parks, recreation, and other community priorities; Investments in community infrastructure and public amenities that improve the quality of life for residents; and Partnerships with community colleges and workforce organizations to develop the skilled workforce necessary for the next generation of technology jobs.
Dione's position is straightforward: If a corporation benefits from a Texas community, that community should meaningfully benefit from the corporation's presence.
These investments should not replace taxes or allow corporations to purchase political influence.
They should represent an additional commitment to responsible corporate citizenship and measurable community partnership.
Balancing the Benefits and Costs of Data Centers Dione recognizes that data centers present both opportunities and legitimate concerns.
They can generate major capital investment, construction activity, infrastructure improvements, tax revenue, technology-sector growth, workforce-development partnerships, educational investments, scholarships, charitable contributions, and new economic opportunities.
At the same time, large facilities can create substantial electricity demands and, depending upon their technology and cooling systems, significant water requirements.
Communities may also face questions involving transmission infrastructure, backup generators, noise, land use, construction traffic, environmental impacts, utility costs, emergency preparedness, and compatibility with nearby residential development.
Property owners also deserve credible information about whether proposed industrial development and related infrastructure could affect surrounding communities and property values.
The question should not simply be whether Texas can build more data centers.
The question is whether they are being built responsibly, transparently, and in genuine partnership with the communities hosting them.
No Progress Without Transparency Dione Sims will oppose backroom arrangements that commit taxpayer resources or significantly affect communities without appropriate transparency and public accountability.
When corporations seek tax incentives, infrastructure commitments, special regulatory treatment, or other public benefits, taxpayers deserve to know what they are receiving in return.
Dione supports appropriate public disclosure surrounding major technology and data-center developments, including electricity and water requirements, infrastructure needs, public subsidies and incentives, anticipated employment and economic benefits, environmental and community impacts, and commitments made to the communities hosting these projects.
For projects receiving substantial public benefits, Dione will advocate for transparent Community Benefit Agreements or comparable enforceable commitments when appropriate.
Instead of vague promises, communities should be able to see what companies have committed to provide—from scholarships and workforce programs to educational investments and infrastructure improvements—and whether those commitments have been fulfilled.
Public resources require public accountability.
Preparing Texas Small Businesses for the AI Revolution Responsible innovation is not only about regulating technology.
It is about making certain Texans have an opportunity to benefit from it.
Artificial intelligence could become one of the most consequential productivity technologies available to small businesses.
Yet large corporations have significantly greater resources to acquire technology, employ specialists, train workers, and redesign their operations.
Dione does not want Texas small businesses left behind.
She will support practical AI education and workforce training for entrepreneurs, small businesses, workers, and community organizations through partnerships with community colleges, universities, workforce-development organizations, chambers of commerce, libraries, and qualified training providers.
AI training can help small businesses automate routine administrative work, improve customer service, strengthen marketing, analyze business information, manage inventory and scheduling, prepare proposals, enhance cybersecurity awareness, identify new customers, and allow employees to devote more time to higher-value activities.
Training must also address responsible AI use, including privacy, cybersecurity, intellectual property, verification of AI-generated information, and understanding when human judgment must remain in control.
The AI economy should not belong exclusively to America's largest corporations.
Texas entrepreneurs and small businesses deserve the knowledge and tools necessary to compete.
Protecting Workers While Building the Workforce of the Future AI will change some jobs, create others, and transform many existing occupations.
Dione believes government should neither attempt to stop technological progress nor tell displaced workers that they are simply on their own.
She supports investments in AI literacy, technical education, apprenticeships, reskilling, community-college partnerships, and employer-supported workforce development so Texans can compete for the opportunities technological advancement creates.
Her objective is clear: technology should expand human potential—not make working people economically disposable.
Independent Leadership: No Big Tech Corporate PAC Money Congress will make consequential decisions involving artificial intelligence, data privacy, technology competition, infrastructure, energy, and data centers.
Texans deserve representatives whose judgment is independent of the corporations affected by those decisions.
Dione Sims pledges not to accept campaign contributions from Big Tech corporate PACs.
Her decisions will be guided by what protects Texans, strengthens American competitiveness, encourages responsible innovation, and serves the public interest—not by the campaign priorities of corporations Congress may be called upon to regulate.
The Sims Standard for Responsible Innovation Dione's position is not anti-technology or anti-business.
It is pro-innovation, pro-worker, pro-small business, pro-community, and pro-accountability.
Texas can lead the next technological revolution while establishing a higher standard for how innovation strengthens the communities that make that growth possible.
Innovate boldly.
Train our people.
Protect our resources.
Invest in our communities.
Empower Texans.
Demand transparency.
Dione Sims believes technological progress should not simply make corporations more valuable.
It should make Texas communities stronger.
