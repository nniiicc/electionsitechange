Hannah Sabio-Howell submits over a thousand signatures to qualify for the ballot and earns key endorsement from sitting senator, signifying momentum
Seattle, WA – In a rare moment, especially for someone challenging Democratic Party leadership, State Senator and Labor Committee Chair Rebecca Saldaña has endorsed Hannah Sabio-Howell.
This latest endorsement, alongside support from 43rd Legislative District State Representative Shaun Scott and Seattle City Councilmember Alexis Mercedes Rinck, paints a clear picture of growing momentum.
In a video announcing her endorsement of Hannah available online, Saldaña notes their long history together.
Nearly 10 years ago, Hannah was an intern in her Senate office.
Since then, they’ve worked together both inside and outside of the legislature on progressive wins for everyday people like the Domestic Workers Bill of Rights and the tax on Capital Gains to fund childcare.
She officially kicked off her campaign last Thursday in a room of over 250 people at Massive in Capitol Hill, raising over $8,200 in a single night.
Hannah has secured support from the Working Families Party, Progressive Victory, Tech 4 Housing, Tech 4 Taxes, Port Commissioner Toshiko Hasegawa, and more, viewable on her website (hannahforwashington.com).
Candidates for legislative office can appear on the ballot either by paying a $676 fee or by collecting an equivalent number of signatures from registered voters in the district.
A life-time organizer, Hannah opted for the rarely utilized option of collecting signatures.
She believes more candidates should organize to get on the ballot this way because it offers the campaign an early opportunity to connect with voters at their doors, farmers markets, and street corners—and demonstrates grassroots power and volunteer energy.
“For many of my constituents in the 43rd Legislative District, an unexpected $700 expense is the difference between making rent or putting food on the table.
It shouldn’t be a barrier that prevents everyday people from engaging in our democracy,” said Hannah to supporters and volunteers that joined her in Olympia to submit over 1500 signatures to the Secretary of State.
“This campaign believes in getting money out of politics.
That starts by showing what it looks like to get on the ballot through support from our community, not personal wealth.”
