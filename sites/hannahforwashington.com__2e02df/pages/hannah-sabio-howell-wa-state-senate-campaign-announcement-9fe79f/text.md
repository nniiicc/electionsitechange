Seattle, WA – Hannah Sabio-Howell, former Communications Director at Working Washington, has announced her candidacy for state Senate in Washington’s 43rd legislative district, which spans neighborhoods such as Capitol Hill, First Hill, the University District, Fremont, Belltown, Downtown, Montlake, Eastlake and Laurelhurst.
She is challenging Senate Majority Leader Jamie Pedersen, a 20-year incumbent and corporate attorney.
Since beginning to fundraise on March 1, the campaign has raised over $# in individual donor dollars, demonstrating strong grassroots support for a new generation of leadership ready to take on an inadequate status quo.
“Washington state could be the best place in the nation to build a life if people can afford to do that—but that’s not the Washington we live in right now,” Sabio-Howell said.
“We need leaders who are going to deliver on affordability, taking on entrenched corporate interests to pass legislation that makes it easier to build more housing and bring rents down, win transformative social safety net programs like universal childcare, and raise taxes on ultra-wealthy corporations to truly invest in the future we deserve.” For the last three years, Sabio-Howell has served as the communications director at Working Washington, a statewide organizing home for building worker power in underpaid and underrepresented industries.
In this role, she led communications campaigns to organize and mobilize alongside workers, taking on big fights with multi-billion dollar corporations and powerful special interests to successfully defend gig workers’ right to minimum wage, expand wage and workplace protections with domestic workers, and equip farmworkers and restaurant workers with the tools to be the strongest spokespeople and champions of their own industries.
If elected, Sabio-Howell will bring this experience and a pro-worker agenda to Olympia.
“We know that winning a more fair economy where we can afford the lives we dream of is possible.
I’ve stood alongside organized workers who do it every day,” she said.
“We live in the home of the Fight for $15—we know how to fight corporate interests and win, and that’s what this race is about.
I believe in taking on the corporations that try to rig the rules at our expense and will never back down from any opportunity to organize with and uplift everyday people.” Prior to her role at Working Washington, Sabio-Howell worked first in the Washington State House of Representatives and then in the Washington State Senate, serving as part of the team of legislators and staff advancing policies to make Washington’s economy more fair, expand healthcare access and make meaningful progress toward climate justice.
As a communications specialist in the Washington state Senate Democratic Caucus, Sabio-Howell worked on successful policies such as the capital gains tax, postpartum health equity and pay transparency for workers.
Her job, both as a legislative aide and later as a communications specialist, was about opening lines of access for everyday people to the state legislature, ensuring people across Washington state understand the legislature and how to shape it.

“We can do better than catering to corporations in our state, and nowhere is that leadership more possible than the 43rd district.” As a renter, a cyclist and a professional photographer, Sabio-Howell’s experiences have shaped her connection to and love of the 43rd district’s community of urbanists and artists.
For the past four election cycles, she has been a member of The Urbanist Elections Committee and the ProChoice Washington PAC board.
A proudly out queer woman and a woman of color, she would join both the Senate Members of Color and Senate LGBTQ Caucuses if elected.
