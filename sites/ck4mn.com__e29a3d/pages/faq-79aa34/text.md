- This is the one worth reading twice.
A postmark does not count.
Your ballot must be received by election officials by Election Day, so a ballot postmarked November 2 that arrives November 5 does not get counted.
- By mail: use the postage-paid envelope that came with your ballot, and send it as early as you can.
Within a week of Election Day, drop it off instead of mailing it.
- Drop it off yourself: return it to the office that sent it, during office hours, no later than 5 p.m. on Election Day.
Some jurisdictions have a 24-hour ballot drop box.
- Have someone drop it off: one person may return ballots for up to three other voters, and they'll need ID showing their name and signature.
- Do not bring your absentee ballot to a polling place on Election Day.
If illness or a hospital stay makes it hard to get out, Minnesota's agent delivery process lets someone you know pick up and return your ballot starting seven days before the election — and that route runs until 8 p.m. on Election Day.
- You apply for a ballot, and you do not need to be registered to apply.
The fastest way is online at MNVotes.gov — you'll need an email address and either your Minnesota ID number or your Social Security number.
You can also download a paper application and return it to the Scott County election office.
The Secretary of State recommends applying before Tuesday, October 20 so there's time for your application to be processed, your ballot to reach you, and your ballot to get back.
One step people miss: you need a witness.
Before you mark your ballot, find someone who is a U.S. citizen and will be 18 by Election Day — or a notary — and have them watch you receive the blank ballot.
You and your witness both sign the white signature envelope.
You can follow your ballot the whole way with the Track Your Ballot tool at MNVotes.gov.
- Polls are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3.
If you're in line at 8 p.m., you get to vote.
Find your polling place with the Secretary of State's Poll Finder at pollfinder.sos.mn.gov — polling places in 54B are assigned by address, and yours may have changed.
You can also see your sample ballot there before you go.
Not registered?
Minnesota has same-day registration.
Pre-registration closes Tuesday, October 13, but you can register at your polling place on Election Day with proof of residence — a driver's license, a utility bill, or a registered voter in your precinct who can vouch for you.
