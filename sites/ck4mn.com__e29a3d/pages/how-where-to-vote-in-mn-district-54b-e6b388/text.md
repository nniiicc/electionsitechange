Three Ways to Vote IN PERSON ON ELECTION DAY Go to your assigned polling place on Tuesday, November 3 , between 7:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m.
If you're already registered, you don't need to bring anything — Minnesota has no photo ID requirement for registered voters.
If you're not registered, you can register right there with proof of residence.
As long as you're in line by 8:00 p.m., you get to vote.
EARLY, IN PERSON You can vote in person before Election Day at a county or city location — no appointment, no excuse needed.
This has been open since September 18 , and the last day is Monday, November 2 .
Starting Friday, October 16 , Minnesota adds a new option called Early Voting : instead of sealing your ballot in an envelope, you sign a short certificate and feed your ballot straight into the counter, exactly like Election Day.

BY MAIL (absentee) Anyone can request a mail ballot for any reason, or no reason at all — and you do not need to be registered to apply.

Same-day registration available If you're not registered by October 13, you have not missed anything that matters — Minnesota lets you register when you vote, in person, with proof of where you live.
Am I registered to vote?
Check in about fifteen seconds at mnvotes.org .
You'll need to re-register if you've moved, changed your name, or gone several years without voting.
It takes under a minute to confirm either way.
Can I Register on Election Day?
Yes.
Minnesota allows same-day registration at your polling place — you do not have to register in advance.
Bring proof of where you live, such as a Minnesota ID with your current address, or a photo ID plus a recent utility bill.
A registered voter from your precinct can also vouch for you.
Where do I vote?
Your polling place depends on your exact address, and it can change between elections.
Look it up at pollfinder.sos.mn.gov .
If you live in a mail ballot precinct, Poll Finder will tell you that too.
When can I vote?
Election Day, early in person, or by mail.
All three are explained below.
Two elections in 2026: the Minnesota State Primary on Tuesday, August 11 , and the General Election on Tuesday, November 3 .
Polls are open 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. for both.
Some townships with fewer than 500 residents may open later — check Poll Finder for your precinct.
How do I vote early or by mail?
Any registered Minnesota voter can vote absentee — by mail or in person — without giving a reason.

Does my township vote by mail?
It might.
Minnesota townships of any size, and cities with fewer than 400 with registered voters can hold elections entirely by mail.
District 54B includes four townships: Jackson, Louisville, Sand Creek, and Spring Lake Townships, so this affects a real number of voters here — and most people don't know it until a ballot shows up.

Does my mail ballot need a witness?
Yes — and this is important.
Your ballot envelope must be signed by a witness: any U.S. citizen who is at least 18 on Election Day, or a notary.
A missing witness signature is one of the most common reasons a mail ballot is rejected.
Read the envelope instructions before you seal it.
How do I return my mail ballot, and when is it actually due?
The deadline that catches people: a postmark does not count.
Your ballot has to be received by election officials — not just mailed — by Election Day.
A ballot postmarked November 2 that arrives November 5 does not get counted.
You have four ways to return it.
Drop it off yourself, before Election Day Bring your completed ballot to either Scott County location during business hours, any time through Monday, November 2 : · Scott County Government Center — 200 Fourth Ave West, Shakopee · Scott County Public Works — 600 Country Trail East, Jordan Drop it off on Election Day On Tuesday, November 3 , hand-delivered ballots go to the Scott County Government Center , 200 Fourth Ave West, Shakopee, no later than 5:00 p.m.
Do not bring your absentee ballot to a polling place — they cannot accept it.
By mail or courier Use the postage-paid envelope that came with your ballot and send it as early as you can.
Mailed and couriered ballots must be received at the Government Center by 8:00 p.m. on November 3 .
Inside the last week, don't mail it — drop it off.
Have someone return it for you One person may return ballots for up to three other voters, and will need to show ID with their name and signature.
If illness, disability, hospitalization or a care facility keeps you from delivering your own ballot, a designated agent can do it for you starting seven days before the election, with a deadline of 8:00 p.m. on November 3 at the Government Center.

If you're already registered: nothing.

Do I need a photo Id to vote in Minnesota?
No.
Minnesota has no photo ID requirement for registered voters.
You'll only need to show documentation if you're registering at the polls, and even then, a photo ID is not required if you have other accepted proof of residence.
What will be on my ballot?
Minnesota House District 54B plus statewide, congressional, and county races.
View your personalized sample ballot by entering your address at mnvotes.org — worth doi ng before you go, since down-ballot races are where people get stuck.
What if I have a Special Situation or Circumstance?
I just moved.
Update your registration at your new address.
If you moved recently, you can re-register at your new polling place on Election Day.
I'm a college student.
You can register and vote using either your home address or your school address — your choice, but only one.
I can't get into the building, or I need help voting.
Every polling place offers curbside voting — an election judge will bring a ballot out to your car.
You may also bring someone to assist you, or ask an election judge for help.
I'm in the military or living overseas.
You can register and request a ballot through the state's absentee process for military and overseas voters .
I have a past felony conviction.
In Minnesota, your right to vote is restored as soon as you are no longer incarcerated — including while on probation or parole.
If that's you, you can register and vote.
I don't have a ride to the polls.
Let us know and we'll help you figure something out.
Can I vote if I've been convicted of a felony?
Yes.
In Minnesota your right to vote is restored as soon as you are no longer incarcerated — including while on probation, parole, or supervised release.
You will need to register at your current address.
How does Minnesota's primary work?
It's an open primary — you don't register with a party and you don't have to declare one in advance.
But you must vote within a single party's column .
If you mark candidates in more than one party's column, that portion of your ballot won't count.
Nonpartisan races on the same ballot are unaffected.
