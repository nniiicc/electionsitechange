David Johnson is a lifelong Kentuckian, public school teacher, and community advocate who believes government should work for everyday people—not just the wealthy and well-connected.
With deep family roots in Estill County and a lifetime in Madison County, David has dedicated himself to serving families, students, and communities across Eastern Kentucky.
Having taught in both Madison County and Estill County Schools, David has worked closely with students and families from diverse backgrounds and understands the challenges facing Kentucky's public schools.
As a special education teacher, he works every day with students with disabilities and sees firsthand how decisions made in Frankfort affect real people.
His commitment to service extends beyond the classroom.
As a former member, appointed by Mayor Blythe to the Richmond Human Rights Commission, David became a strong advocate for fair housing, equal employment opportunities, and ensuring that every citizen is treated with dignity and respect.
He has worked alongside people of all political backgrounds, earning a reputation as someone more interested in solving problems than engaging in partisan politics.
David has also been an active voice in the Kentucky General Assembly, advocating for issues that matter to working families.
In 2020, he played a key role in helping pass Kentucky’s Insulin Copay Cap, making life-saving medication more affordable for thousands of Kentuckians.
He has consistently spoken out for affordable healthcare, strong public schools, government transparency, and policies that put people before special interests.
Known throughout the region as a neighbor you can count on, David believes that no community should be forgotten and every voice deserves to be heard.
Whether in the classroom, the community, or the halls of government, he remains committed to fighting for the people of Kentucky and building a stronger future for the next generation.

I support strengthening rural clinics, protecting local hospitals, and ensuring healthcare providers have the resources they need to serve patients close to home.

Rural Kentuckians deserve stability—not uncertainty—when it comes to their healthcare.
