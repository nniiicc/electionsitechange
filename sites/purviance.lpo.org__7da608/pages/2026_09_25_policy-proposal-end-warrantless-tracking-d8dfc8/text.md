Vote for Jason Donate 2026 September 25 POLICY PROPOSAL – End Warrantless Tracking POLICY PROPOSAL – End Warrantless Tracking 25 Sep 0 POLICY PROPOSAL – End Warrantless Tracking Jason Purviance Uncategorized Protecting everyday travel, your likeness, and your data if I am elected September 25, 2026 In my Guiding Principles I said I would oppose mass tracking tools like Flock systems and government data collection without warrants.
This is that plan in plain language.
I am not trying to take a useful tool away from a cop looking for a stolen truck or a missing persons.
I am trying to stop the government, and private companies working with the government, from keeping a record of lawful people driving to work, school, church, and the store.
Americans have a constitutional right to travel freely throughout the United States.
I will not treat that right as optional.
If every is being logged, stored, and searched without a warrant we do not have free movement.
We have temporary permission.
The rule I will fight for A cop may check a plate against stolen, wanted, and missing-person lists in the moment.
The state may not keep a diary of every lawful trip and search it later without a warrant.
No third party may collect or store that data.
What officers could still do Police and city vehicles could use a plate reader the old way: The device belongs to the agency, not a vendor network An on-duty officer is using it It checks a plate right then against stolen cars, wanted people, missing people, or an active warrant If it is not a hit, the read is thrown away That is a tool.
It is not a catalog of an entire population.
What will be stopped Saving a searchable history of everyday driving Private companies collecting or storing those plate reads, locations, or images Government paying a vendor for a map of where people have been Buying that same information from a data broker instead of getting a warrant Using your face, walk, or voice to track the public without a judge A company can sell a camera or fix a camera.
It should not own a file of who drove down the road.
What I would offer in Columbus If elected, I will work to advance a package I am calling the Ohio Digital Warrant Acts .
It is one idea in a few parts: Ask a judge first .
Historical location records, face searches, and bought data should need a warrant.
Plate readers the old way .
On the cop car.
Live hot list only.
No vendor travel diary.
Your body is not a barcode .
No warrantless public tracking by face, gait, voice, or likeness.
No middleman database .
If police cannot collect it themselves without a warrant, they cannot pay a company to collect or keep it for them.
No catalog of innocent people .
Do not build permanent files on people who did nothing wrong “just in case.” Don’t ship Ohio’s data everywhere .
A confirmed hit on a stolen car can be shared for that case.
A dump of everybody’s driving history cannot.
Real consequences .
If someone builds the diary anyway, that data should be deleted, kept out of court, and the people who did it should be accountable.
This is a working proposal, like my property-tax and data-center posts.
If a piece of it can be clearer or more practical, I want to hear it.
