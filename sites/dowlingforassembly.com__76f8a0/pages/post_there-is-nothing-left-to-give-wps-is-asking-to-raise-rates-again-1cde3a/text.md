There Is Nothing Left to Give | WPS is Asking to Raise Rates Again
There is nothing left to give.
That's what kept running through my head when I started reading about Wisconsin Public Service's proposed rate increases for 2027 and 2028.
WPS is asking the Public Service Commission of Wisconsin to approve another increase in electric and natural gas rates.
According to WPS, a typical residential electric customer would pay about $11 more per month in 2027 and another $5 more per month in 2028.
For natural gas customers, WPS projects about another $7 per month in 2027.
Maybe someone looks at $11 and thinks...
"It's only $11."
But that's the problem.
It's $11 here.
Another $20 there.
More at the grocery store.
More at the gas pump.
More for insurance.
More for housing.
More when the car breaks.
More when an appliance dies.
And eventually, families run out of places to find "just a little more."
I know what that looks like.
In May of 2022, our dryer broke.
Josh tried fixing it himself, and he actually got it working again.
For about a month.
Then another part broke.
We couldn't afford to run out and buy a new dryer.
So we didn't.
For FIVE MONTHS, our family hung our clothes outside.
Six people.
Four boys.
Five months of laundry hanging outside because buying a new dryer simply wasn't in the budget.
We saved.
And then, as usually happens when you're raising a family... life happened.
Something else needed the money.
And then our water heater broke.
Eventually, we found an old cream-colored dryer from the 1980s on Facebook Marketplace for $50.
We bought it.
And wouldn't you know it... that ugly little dryer is still going strong.
That's what affordability actually looks like for a lot of families.
It isn't a spreadsheet.
It's fixing something one more time.
It's waiting another month.
It's buying used.
It's putting something on a credit card.
It's hanging your family's laundry outside for five months because there simply isn't money for another expense.
And I don't think things have gotten easier since 2022.
Then I read something that stopped me.
Major companies are now openly talking about how financially stretched their customers have become.
Kraft Heinz has reported consumers trading down, waiting for promotions and choosing smaller package sizes at lower price points.
McDonald's has talked about financial pressure on lower-income customers.
Whirlpool is seeing people postpone buying appliances.
Think about that.
Companies aren't just looking at inflation statistics.
They're watching what people actually do when the money runs out.
Buy less.
Wait longer.
Fix the old one.
Choose the smaller package.
Go without.
I understand why a company might make a smaller package so someone can afford the purchase.
But that doesn't make the underlying affordability problem disappear.
It just makes the package smaller.
And we've all had enough of "shrink-flation."
And now we're talking about another utility increase.
WPS says these increases are necessary to support investments in reliability, Wisconsin's energy grid and other infrastructure.
The company also says its proposal incorporates $132 million in savings from federal tax credits and earnings sharing.
Those arguments deserve to be part of the conversation.
But so does this:
Can customers afford it?
That's one of the reasons we have the Public Service Commission.
WPS has made its request.
The Commission has not made its final decision.
And right now, customers have an opportunity to put their experiences into the public record.
If another $11 a month matters to your family, tell them.
If you're already choosing between expenses, tell them.
If you've cut everything you reasonably can, tell them.
If you're fixing appliances instead of replacing them, buying less at the grocery store, turning down the thermostat or watching every mile you drive because you can't absorb another increase...
Tell them.
You don't need to understand hundreds of pages of utility filings.
You don't need to be an energy expert.
You are the person paying the bill.
That matters.
