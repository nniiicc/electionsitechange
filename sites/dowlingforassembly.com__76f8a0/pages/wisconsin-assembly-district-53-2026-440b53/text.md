What Are the Main Issues in the District 53 Election?
One of the biggest issues I'm hearing about right now is data centers. ​ Residents have a lot of questions about what large-scale data center development could mean for our communities.
They're concerned about Wisconsin's water and natural resources, enormous electricity demands, potential impacts on utility costs, noise, and the tax exemptions being offered to qualifying data centers.
I share many of those concerns. ​ I'm not anti-technology.
I'm actually an FAA-certified drone pilot, and I believe technology and innovation are important to Wisconsin's future.
But there's a big difference between supporting technology and giving giant corporations a blank check to build whatever they want without asking who ultimately pays the price. ​ My position is pretty simple: Yes to innovation.
No to cutting corners. ​ If a massive data center wants to build in Wisconsin, I believe we should be asking serious questions before approving it.
How much electricity will it require?
Who pays for the new infrastructure necessary to provide that power?
What are the impacts on our water and environment?
How will noise affect neighboring properties?
What tax exemptions or incentives are being provided, and what does the community actually receive in return? ​ Those aren't anti-business questions. ​ They're responsible-government questions. ​ Affordability is another major issue throughout District 53.
Residents continue to talk with me about property taxes, housing costs, utility bills, fuel, groceries, and the everyday cost of raising a family.
Housing availability also comes up frequently, particularly how land-constrained communities like Menasha, Neenah, and Fox Crossing can create more housing choices while using the land we already have responsibly. ​ Government transparency continues to be important to me as well.
Whether we're talking about data centers, surveillance technology, development, taxes, or state spending, I believe residents deserve to know what their government is doing, how decisions are being made, and who ultimately benefits from those decisions.
You don't have to agree with me on every issue.
I don't expect you to. ​ But I will tell you where I stand, explain why, listen when you disagree... and keep asking questions.
How Do I Register to Vote in Wisconsin?
Wisconsin residents can check their voter registration, register to vote, request an absentee ballot, and find other election information through MyVote Wisconsin . ​ For the November 3, 2026 General Election, Wisconsin allows several methods of voter registration, including online registration for eligible voters, registration through your municipal clerk, and registration at your polling place on Election Day. ​ Because election deadlines and individual circumstances can vary, I recommend using the State of Wisconsin's official MyVote website rather than relying on campaign information for your registration status.
Where Do I Vote in Wisconsin Assembly District 53?
Your polling location depends on your home address and municipality. ​ Instead of guessing based on a district map, enter your address into Wisconsin's official MyVote system to find your current polling location. ​ Polls for the November 3 General Election are open from 7:00 AM until 8:00 PM.

What Does a Wisconsin State Assembly Representative Do?
Wisconsin has a bicameral state legislature consisting of the State Assembly and State Senate.
Assembly representatives introduce, debate, amend, and vote on legislation affecting people throughout Wisconsin. ​ But I believe there's another part of the job that's just as important: representing the people who live in your district. ​ That means listening to residents, helping constituents navigate state government, attending community meetings, asking questions, communicating what is happening in Madison, and making yourself accessible to the people you're supposed to represent.
I've said this throughout my campaign: I answer to my community, not a party.
