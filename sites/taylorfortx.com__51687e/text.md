About Taylor
Taylor Rehmet is a U.S.
Air Force veteran, aircraft mechanic, union president, working-class Texan, and the State Senator for District 9.
Born and raised in Garland, Taylor grew up in a working-class family.
His dad was an aircraft mechanic, and his mom worked in a local salon.
He learned the value of hard work early, working as a plumber’s assistant and on a ranch before joining the Air Force.
After his service, Taylor returned home and put those skills to work as a machinist building F-35s.
Taylor’s commitment to fairness and dignity on the job led him to union organizing.
His coworkers elected him president of his local chapter of the International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers, and he later became president of the union’s Texas State Council.
He has spent his career bringing people together to fight for better pay, safer workplaces, and a fair deal for working families.
In January, Taylor won a historic special election because voters were ready for a leader focused on lowering costs for the middle class, not favors for special interests or divisive culture wars.
He got to work immediately, holding the district’s first town hall in years, closing over 1,000 constituent cases in his first 100 days, and talking with hundreds of community members across Senate District 9.
Now, Taylor is working across the aisle to lower property taxes and everyday costs, make insurance more affordable, protect families from rising utility bills, repeal the private-school voucher scam, and fully fund public schools.
He isn’t a career politician.
He is a working-class leader who listens, shows up, and delivers real results.
A Texas You Can Afford Agenda
Senator Rehmet knows that Texans are working harder than ever and still struggling to get by.
Ahead of the 90th Legislative Session, he announced his “A Texas You Can Afford” agenda with concrete solutions to lower property taxes, rein in rising healthcare costs, stop unfair pricing practices, and make housing and childcare more affordable.
Working Texans should be able to afford a home, see a doctor, buy groceries — and get a fair deal along the way.
