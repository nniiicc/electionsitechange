A young couple who grew up here can’t find a place they can afford.
A retired teacher has paid off her house but watches her tax bill climb faster than her Social Security check.
Employers from the school to the marina can’t hire, because workers can’t find anywhere to live.
These aren’t separate problems.
They’re one problem, and too often Concord has made it worse.

Why Property Taxes Keep Rising New Hampshire pays a smaller share of public school costs than almost any other state, so towns raise most of the money through property taxes.
When Concord cuts aid or passes costs down to towns, your local bill goes up.
Politicians call it a tax cut at the State House.
You pay for it at town meeting.
At the same time, housing hasn’t kept up.
When there’s nowhere affordable to live, young families leave, schools shrink, and local businesses can’t find workers.
A smaller, older tax base means the same costs are spread over fewer people.
Property Tax Relief for Local Homeowners Relief for people who live here year-round I support a homestead exemption for primary residences, so part of the value of the home you actually live in is shielded from the statewide education property tax.
A 2026 bill, HB 1648, would have exempted up to $300,000 of a primary residence.
It was sent for further study.
I’ll work to bring it back and get it right.
Help for the neighbors who need it most New Hampshire already has a Low and Moderate Income Homeowners Property Tax Relief program, but too few people know it exists.
I’ll push to raise its income limits, and to make sure every eligible senior and working family in our towns applies.
Stop passing the bill down to towns The state should pay its fair share of public education, including special education, which towns are required to provide but can’t control the cost of.
I’ll vote against unfunded mandates and against budget tricks that cut state taxes by raising yours.
Affordable Housing & Local Control Reward towns that choose to build I’ll fight to restore funding for the Housing Champions program, which gives towns that update their zoning a leg up on grants for water, sewer and roads.
I’ll also support homes working families and seniors can afford, including accessory dwelling units that let a family add a small apartment for a parent or an adult child.
The Housing Champions Program Housing Champions started as a bipartisan idea: instead of forcing towns to change, reward the ones that choose to.
It worked.
Then the majority in Concord turned on it.
2023 — Program Created The Legislature creates the program with $5 million to help towns that remove barriers to housing.
2024 — Housing Progress The 28 Housing Champion communities approve 2,643 new homes, 45% of all housing units approved statewide that year.
2025–2026 — Funding & Future 2025 — The state budget stops funding the program.
February 2026 — The House votes 185 to 166 to repeal it entirely (HB 1196).
April 2026 — The Senate votes to keep the program alive.
It still has no new money.
I believe in local control.
That’s why I’d rather reward towns that choose to build than force every town to do the same thing.
Incentives respect local decisions.
Mandates from Concord don’t.
Questions I Hear Would a homestead exemption raise taxes on seasonal homes?
It would shift some of the statewide education tax from year-round homes toward other property, including second homes.
Seasonal residents are part of our community and our economy, and I don’t support punishing them with a special surcharge.
A homestead exemption simply makes sure the people who live, work and send their kids to school here aren’t taxed out of their own towns.
Isn’t zoning a local decision?
Yes, and it should stay that way.
Housing Champions doesn’t force any town to change anything.
It offers help to towns that decide on their own to make room for more homes.
Is there any help I can get right now?
If your household income is under the program limits (currently $37,000 for a single person or $47,000 for a married couple or head of household), you may qualify for the state’s Low and Moderate Income Homeowners Property Tax Relief.
Applications are filed with the NH Department of Revenue Administration between May 1 and June 30.
Ask at your town office about local exemptions for seniors, veterans and people with disabilities, too.
