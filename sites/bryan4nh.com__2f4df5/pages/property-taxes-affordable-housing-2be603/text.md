Skip to content Home About FAQ Contact Get Involved Issues Endorsements X Donate Property Taxes & Housing Home Property Taxes & Housing Fair Property Taxes & Homes Our Families Can Afford In my work as a mental health clinician, I hear the same worries every week.
A young couple who grew up here can’t find a place they can afford.
A retired teacher has paid off her house but watches her tax bill climb faster than her Social Security check.
Employers from the school to the marina can’t hire, because workers can’t find anywhere to live.
These aren’t separate problems.
They’re one problem, and too often Concord has made it worse.
Why Property Taxes Keep Rising New Hampshire pays a smaller share of public school costs than almost any other state, so towns raise most of the money through property taxes.
When Concord cuts aid or passes costs down to towns, your local bill goes up.
Politicians call it a tax cut at the State House.
You pay for it at town meeting.
At the same time, housing hasn’t kept up.
When there’s nowhere affordable to live, young families leave, schools shrink, and local businesses can’t find workers.
A smaller, older tax base means the same costs are spread over fewer people.
Property Tax Relief for Local Homeowners Relief for year-round residents I support a homestead exemption for primary residences, so part of the value of the home you actually live in is shielded from the statewide education property tax.
Help for neighbors who need it most New Hampshire already has a Low and Moderate Income Homeowners Property Tax Relief program, but too few people know it exists.
I’ll work to expand eligibility and make sure more qualifying seniors and working families know how to apply.
Stop passing costs down to towns The state should pay its fair share of public education, including special education.
I’ll oppose unfunded mandates that leave local property taxpayers paying the bill.
Affordable Housing & Local Control Families who grew up in Carroll County should have the opportunity to build their lives here.
But rising housing costs are making it harder for young families, workers, and seniors to find homes they can afford.
Reward towns that choose to build I support restoring funding for New Hampshire’s Housing Champions program, which helps communities that voluntarily update their zoning compete for grants for water, sewer, and road improvements.
Support more housing options I’ll support housing options that work for local families and seniors, including accessory dwelling units that allow homeowners to create a small apartment for a parent or adult child.
Respect local decisions I believe zoning decisions should remain local.
I’d rather reward towns that choose to make room for more housing than impose one-size-fits-all mandates from Concord.
The Housing Champions Program The Housing Champions program was created to encourage communities to address local housing needs without imposing statewide zoning mandates.
It offered financial incentives to towns that voluntarily removed barriers to housing development.
Here’s how the program has evolved and why its future matters to Carroll County.
2023 — Program Created New Hampshire established the Housing Champions program with $5 million in funding to encourage communities to remove barriers to housing development.
The program offered financial incentives rather than requiring towns to change their zoning regulations.
2024 — Housing Progress According to Bryan’s policy statement, 28 Housing Champion communities approved 2,643 new homes in 2024, representing 45% of all housing units approved statewide that year.
The program was designed to encourage housing development while allowing towns to make their own zoning decisions.
2025–2026 — Funding & Future New funding for Housing Champions was discontinued in the 2025 state budget.
In February 2026, the New Hampshire House voted to repeal the program, but the Senate voted to preserve it in April.
Although the program survived those votes, its future funding remains uncertain.
