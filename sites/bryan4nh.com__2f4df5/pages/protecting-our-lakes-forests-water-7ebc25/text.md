Before I worked in mental health, I studied outdoor education and adventure leadership and worked in outdoor programs for young people.
In recent summers, cyanobacteria warnings have become more common on lakes across the Lakes Region, and that worries me, because our lakes are central to how we live here.
This isn’t an abstract issue for District 3.
Clean water is the reason people move here, the reason visitors come, and a big part of what supports property values and local jobs.
Moultonborough — Protecting Our Shoreline According to the town’s community profile, Moultonborough has 89 miles of shoreline, more than any other town in New Hampshire.
That includes frontage on Lake Winnipesaukee and Lake Kanasatka.
It’s an extraordinary asset and a big responsibility.
Aging septic systems and runoff from roads and lawns are leading sources of the phosphorus that feeds algae blooms, and a town can’t fix those alone.
In Concord, I’ll support state funding for shoreland protection and septic system upgrades, so a town with this much shoreline isn’t left to carry the cost by itself.
Madison — Protecting the Water Under Our Feet Madison sits over the Ossipee Aquifer, a shared source of drinking water for six towns: Madison, Tamworth, Sandwich, Ossipee, Effingham and Freedom.
Madison has an aquifer protection ordinance that other towns in the watershed have used as a model.
The town also worked with the Upper Saco Valley Land Trust and The Conservation Fund to permanently protect the 626-acre Chain of Ponds Community Forest, which holds forest, ponds and wetlands.
Pressure to develop makes long-term groundwater protection more urgent.
I’ll support regional protection of the aquifer and oppose unfunded mandates that make it harder for small towns like Madison to do this work.
Tamworth — Forests, a Lake, and a Shared Aquifer Tamworth borders the White Mountain National Forest and is home to Chocorua Lake, which has been protected through the work of the Chocorua Lake Conservancy.
Tamworth also shares the Ossipee Aquifer with Madison and four other towns, and its planning board and conservation commission have taken part in the regional effort to protect it.
Much of this work is done by local volunteers.
State government should support them with resources, not red tape.
What I'll Fight for in Concord.
Lake & Watershed Protection Programs that help towns protect water quality shouldn’t depend on one-time federal money.
I’ll fight for dependable funding for lake and watershed protection.
Local Conservation Commissions I’ll support local conservation commissions in Madison, Moultonborough, and Tamworth, which are run largely by volunteers.
These commissions play an important role in protecting our natural resources.
Shoreland Protection I’ll work to protect New Hampshire’s Shoreland Water Quality Protection Act from efforts to weaken it.
This law helps safeguard the lakes, rivers, and shorelines that are so important to our communities.
Septic System Upgrades I’ll support programs that help homeowners near lakes and over the aquifer upgrade aging septic systems to protect our water quality.
Cyanobacteria Blooms I’ll support a science-based response to cyanobacteria blooms, which have become more frequent on Lakes Region waters.
Clean Energy and the Bigger Picture Protecting our lakes and forests also means smart energy policy.
I support letting New Hampshire residents, businesses and towns invest in larger local renewable-energy projects, as long as net-metering rates pay fairly for the energy they provide without shifting unreasonable costs onto other ratepayers.
A healthy environment isn’t separate from a healthy community.
