Bryan believes good policy starts with listening to the people who live and work in our communities.
His priorities focus on the challenges he hears about every day from families across Madison, Moultonborough, and Tamworth — and on practical solutions that make New Hampshire a better place to live, work, and raise a family.
Strong communities begin with giving children and families the opportunity to thrive.
Bryan has spent his career working with children and families and has seen firsthand how much of a difference the right support at the right time can make.
Bryan supports strong public schools, affordable childcare, access to healthcare and mental health services, and adequately funding the Office of the Child Advocate so vulnerable children have strong, independent oversight.
He'll work to strengthen services for families and help make our communities places where young families can afford to stay, raise their children, and build a future.
The rising cost of housing, property taxes, childcare, healthcare, groceries, and other everyday expenses is putting real pressure on families throughout New Hampshire.
Bryan opposes a broad-based tax on earned income.
He supports restoring a modified Interest and Dividends Tax with substantially higher exemptions, such as $25,000 for individuals and $50,000 for couples, as one way to reduce the state's reliance on property taxpayers.
He also supports helping communities remove unnecessary barriers to new housing while preserving local decision-making, environmental protections, and community character.
Access to quality healthcare and mental health services is especially important in rural communities, where finding a provider or getting an appointment can be difficult.
Bryan has spent more than a decade as a licensed social worker specializing in anxiety and trauma, and he understands how important timely care can be.
Bryan believes people should be able to get the care they need without unnecessary barriers.
In Concord, he'll bring his professional experience to discussions about strengthening healthcare and mental health services for New Hampshire families.

Our local businesses are an essential part of what makes Madison, Moultonborough, and Tamworth great places to live.
Bryan wants New Hampshire to support a strong local economy where businesses can succeed, people can find good jobs, and working families can afford to live in the communities where they work.
Bryan notes that New Hampshire has repeatedly reduced business tax rates over the past decade.
He does not support additional across-the-board cuts without clear evidence that the economic benefits would justify the lost revenue.
He believes state tax decisions should also consider their effect on local services and property taxpayers.
Strong public schools give children the foundation they need to succeed and help keep our communities strong.
Bryan believes New Hampshire relies too heavily on local property taxes to fund public education, creating significant differences among communities and putting added pressure on homeowners.
Bryan believes the state should take greater responsibility for funding public education.
He supports parental choice, but opposes an unlimited Education Freedom Account program that directs public funds to private education without the same accountability expected of public schools.
He also believes local voters and school districts—not a statewide mandate—should determine their school budgets.
He believes educators, students, and families should have the support and resources they need to succeed.
Bryan will listen to parents, teachers, and community members and work to keep quality educational opportunities available to New Hampshire children.
Good government starts with listening to people, respecting local communities, and focusing on practical solutions.
Bryan believes state government should work with towns rather than impose one-size-fits-all solutions on communities with different needs.
Bryan supports protecting the right of eligible citizens to vote while maintaining election integrity.
He believes voting requirements should verify eligibility without creating unnecessary barriers for lawful voters.
He'll bring the same approach to Concord that he's used throughout his career: listening first, finding common ground, and working with people who may see things differently.
