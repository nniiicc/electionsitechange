Contact Bryan Answers to Common Questions Who is Bryan Randolph?
Bryan is a Moultonborough resident, husband, father, and licensed social worker who has spent more than a decade helping people navigate difficult challenges.
He's running to bring that experience—and an approach centered on listening, common ground, and practical solutions—to the New Hampshire House.
What district is Bryan running to represent?
Bryan is running to represent New Hampshire House Carroll County District 3, which includes Madison, Moultonborough, and Tamworth.
He wants to make sure the people and communities of District 3 have a strong voice in Concord.
Why is Bryan running for State Representative?
Bryan is running because he believes government works best when representatives listen to the people they serve.
He wants to bring his experience working with individuals and families to Concord and focus on practical solutions to the challenges facing communities in District 3.
What are Bryan's priorities?
Bryan is focused on helping children and families thrive, strengthening our communities, addressing the rising cost of living, improving access to healthcare and mental health services, and creating a future where people can afford to live and work in the communities they call home.
What is Bryan's political experience?
Bryan isn't a career politician.
His experience comes from more than a decade as a licensed social worker helping people work through complex problems.
He believes those same skills—listening, finding common ground, and working toward practical solutions—are needed in Concord.

Where Bryan Stands Answers to common questions about Bryan's positions on issues facing New Hampshire.
Does Bryan support a state income tax?
No.
Bryan does not support a broad-based tax on earned income in New Hampshire.
He believes the state's revenue and property-tax challenges can be addressed without creating a new tax on people's wages.
How does Bryan think New Hampshire should fund public education?
Bryan believes New Hampshire relies too heavily on local property taxes to fund public education.
The result is large disparities between communities and puts homeowners in the position of choosing between adequately funding their schools and keeping property taxes affordable.
He believes the state needs to take greater responsibility for meeting its constitutional obligation to fund public education.
Does Bryan support statewide public school open enrollment?
Bryan believes school districts should have a meaningful say in whether and how they participate in open enrollment.
He opposes a statewide mandate that could create financial and enrollment challenges for receiving districts without giving local communities adequate control.
What is Bryan's position on Education Freedom Accounts?
Bryan supports parents having choices in their children's education, but he does not support an unlimited Education Freedom Account program that sends public dollars to private education without the same accountability expected of public schools.
He believes New Hampshire should prioritize meeting the state's obligation to adequately fund every child's public education.
What is Bryan's position on the Interest & Dividends Tax?
Bryan supports restoring a modified Interest & Dividends Tax with substantially higher exemptions—such as $25,000 for individuals and $50,000 for couples—so that it primarily affects substantial investment income.
He believes this would be fairer than continuing to place so much of New Hampshire's tax burden on homeowners through property taxes.
Does Bryan support state-mandated caps on school budgets?
No.
Bryan believes local voters should continue to approve school budgets through their local budget process.
He believes the state should take greater responsibility for funding education and reducing property taxes rather than impose a one-size-fits-all cap on communities while leaving them responsible for costs they cannot always control.
What is Bryan's position on housing development?
Bryan believes the shortage of affordable housing is a serious problem in District 3.
He supports the state helping communities remove unnecessary barriers to housing development while preserving local decision-making, environmental protections, and community character.
Does Bryan support further business tax cuts?
Bryan does not support additional across-the-board business tax cuts without clear evidence that they would produce enough economic benefit to justify the lost revenue.
He believes decisions about business taxes should also consider their effects on state revenue, local services, and property taxpayers.
Energy, Rights & Public Safety Does Bryan support expanding net metering?
Bryan supports allowing New Hampshire residents, businesses, and communities to invest in larger local renewable-energy projects.
He believes net-metering rates should fairly compensate the energy these projects provide to the grid without shifting unreasonable costs onto ratepayers who do not have renewable-energy systems.
What is Bryan's position on gun laws?
Bryan supports the Second Amendment and the rights of responsible gun owners.
He also believes reasonable steps can be taken to keep firearms away from people who pose a demonstrated danger to themselves or others, strengthen protections for victims of domestic violence, and improve firearm safety.
He believes protecting individual rights and public safety are not mutually exclusive.
What is Bryan's position on abortion?
Bryan supports protecting access to abortion before 24 weeks and opposes earlier abortion bans.
Later in pregnancy, these decisions can involve serious and complex medical circumstances, and Bryan believes they should be made by patients and their medical providers rather than politicians.
Does Bryan support funding the Office of the Child Advocate?
Yes.
Bryan has spent his career working with children and families and believes strong, independent oversight is essential to protecting vulnerable children.
He supports adequately funding the Office of the Child Advocate and continuing to improve New Hampshire's child welfare system.
Does Bryan support nuclear energy development in New Hampshire?
Bryan believes nuclear power should be part of the conversation as New Hampshire works to diversify its energy supply and reduce reliance on fossil fuels.
He supports exploring new technologies such as small modular reactors, provided they meet rigorous standards for safety, waste management, and environmental protection.
Does Bryan support legalizing recreational marijuana?
Yes.
Bryan supports legalizing and regulating adult recreational cannabis.
He favors a state-controlled retail model similar to New Hampshire's liquor system rather than an unrestricted private retail market.
He believes a portion of the revenue should be dedicated to prevention, education, substance-use treatment, and addressing the public-health costs associated with legalization.
Anti-discrimination protections Bryan opposes allowing discrimination based on biological sex in settings such as bathrooms and locker rooms.
He believes laws requiring separation in these settings would be difficult to enforce and could contribute to stigmatization and harassment.
What is Bryan's position on proof of citizenship for voter registration?
Bryan believes only U.S. citizens should vote in our elections.
He also believes eligible citizens should not lose access to the ballot simply because they don't have a passport or birth certificate readily available.
He supports protecting election integrity without creating unnecessary barriers for eligible voters.
