- Advocating for Rural Texans Texas is as vast as it is beautiful, and District 86 is no exception.
With miles of farm and ranch land, our small local communities often face difficulty with funding.
Without the tax base of big urban areas, and agriculture exemptions that are crucial to our farmers and ranchers, small communities must often rely on grant funding to provide the basic infrastructure and hazard mitigation efforts that are necessary for their community.
That is why the panhandle of Texas has more volunteer fire departments than we do paid fire departments.
The federal funding is hard to come by for these folks, as many of the determining factors for granting funding are rooted in population density which puts our rural communities at a further disadvantage.
This funding is used to purchase fire response vehicles, equipment for firemen in the field, and training, all of which is costly, but necessary to keep our communities safe.
When our communities are not granted the additional funding, they have to stretch an already limited budget, making decisions to forgo repairs or update equipment.
It is time to level that playing field by instituting policy that changes the determining factors for grant awards.
Increasing the competitiveness of rural and small community applications should increase the opportunity and amount of funding granted to our district.
