Data Centers
Data centers are becoming a major part of modern infrastructure and they are bringing a lot of concern for Oklahoma communities.
We cannot stop them but we can put legislation in place that requires that they build them responsibly.
These facilities place heavy demands on electricity, water usage, and land, which can impact local residents and neighborhoods if not properly managed.
Oklahoma should adopt legislation that requires responsible development while setting clear standards for energy use, water conservation, and siting requirements to ensure these facilities do not place unnecessary costs and strain on local communities along with ensuring that they do not decrease home values or cause hardships for those living nearby.
Protecting Small Businesses
Oklahoma prides itself on being a pro-business state, but too often programs designed to support small businesses end up favoring large corporate conglomerates instead.
Complex application processes and loopholes make it difficult for small business owners, who are already working long hours, to access the help they were promised.
At the same time, we are seeing massive tax incentive packages, sometimes reaching into the billions, directed toward large, often foreign-backed industrial projects like the aluminum smelter in Inola.
That raises serious questions about priorities and long-term return on investment for Oklahoma taxpayers.
We should not be pushing out local ownership or limiting opportunity for Oklahoma entrepreneurs while concentrating incentives in a handful of large entities.
Instead, we need a more balanced approach that supports small businesses, strengthens homegrown industry, and ensures incentives are transparent, accountable, and tied to real, long-term job creation.
Smart, strategic economic policy can do both: help Oklahoma’s small business community grow while still welcoming responsible large-scale investment that benefits our state as a whole.
Fixing the Childcare Crisis & DHS
Childcare is one of the most over regulated private businesses in the country making it difficult for providers to operate profitably.
While regulations are necessary to protect children, excessive regulations from DHS have led to underpaid workers, high costs for families, and limited availability.
I have been warning numerous politicians, including our former Senator, about the mismanagement of DHS funds for years and was called a "bureaucrat" amongst other names.
In Sept 2025 the State Auditor found that the childcare side of DHS mismanaged over $63 million dollars in funding.
Money that was supposed to help childcare was used to buy Cadillacs and remodel houses.
One employee of DHS embezzled over $2million dollars into her husbands curriculum business.
Now that state must pay that money back out of state tax dollars.
Furthermore DHS has 1 supervisor for every 3.6 employees.
That's a drastic amount of wasteful spending.
Rather than look into their own books DHS chose to do funding cuts that have only worsened the problem, leaving two-thirds of Oklahoma—including Owasso and Tulsa—in a childcare desert, where three children compete for every available spot and in many counties there are ten or more waiting for each spot.
This shortage forces many parents out of the workforce, creating hiring challenges for businesses.
While smart investments in childcare could boost Oklahoma’s economy by over $1.2 billion annually.
Aside from their horrible bookkeeping, there are also numerous no-cost solutions within DHS that would allow these private businesses to expand, improve wages, and serve more families—while saving the state money.
Childcare isn’t just a family issue; it’s an economic one.
By cutting red tape and implementing common-sense reforms, we can make childcare more accessible, increase our state's workforce, and help Oklahoma's economy thrive.

Eliminate the State Property Tax & Income Tax
Phasing out Oklahoma’s state income tax and lowering our excessive property taxs will keep more money in the hands of hardworking families, attract businesses, and fuel economic growth.
States like Florida have thrived without an income tax, and they are working to eliminate property tax, proving that smart fiscal policy leads to prosperity.
This transition won’t happen overnight, but with responsible budgeting and strategic economic policies, we can reduce reliance on these taxes while maintaining essential services.
Lowering and eventually eliminating these taxes will empower individuals, grow local economies, and keep Oklahoma competitive.
Now is the time for bold action to secure a stronger economic future.
