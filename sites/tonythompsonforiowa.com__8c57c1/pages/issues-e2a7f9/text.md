Every policy decision comes back to one basic question:
Does it make life better for Iowa families and our communities?
My approach is grounded in three simple commitments:
Strong Families.
Strong Communities.
Strong Futures.
These priorities shape how I look at every issue — from taxes and schools to water, energy, and economic growth.
These are the lens I will look through when making decisions at the State Capitol.
If you work full-time — or worked for a lifetime — you should be able to afford a simple, decent life here.
Our economy should reward work, not just wealth.
Property taxes, healthcare, childcare, housing, and everyday expenses are putting real pressure on Iowa families.
We need honest tax policy that provides essential services without continually shifting costs from one pocket to another.
I'll work for a tax system that treats working families and seniors fairly, demands transparency about what we're paying for, and stops using short-term fixes that simply create bigger problems later.
Families shouldn't have to choose between getting the care they need and paying the bills.
Iowa needs affordable healthcare, stronger rural healthcare systems, better access to mental health services, and policies that help families afford quality childcare.
We also need to make Iowa a place where healthcare professionals, childcare providers, and other caregivers can afford to build lives of their own.
A strong economy starts with people who can thrive, not just survive.
Iowa needs skilled workers in construction, healthcare, education, agriculture, manufacturing, technology, and the trades.
I'll support apprenticeships, career and technical education, community colleges, the liberal arts, and other pathways into good careers.
And I'll stand up for workers' ability to earn a fair wage, support their families and have a meaningful voice in their workplace.
Some things work best when we build them together: good schools, safe roads, clean water, reliable infrastructure, and thriving local economies.
Strong public schools are the backbone of strong communities.
Every Iowa child deserves a great education, regardless of their ZIP code.
That means properly funding our public schools, investing in teachers and education professionals, restoring strong support systems for students, and making sure rapidly growing districts have the resources to keep up.
Public dollars should first fulfill our responsibility to public education.
I oppose an unlimited taxpayer-funded private-school voucher system that diverts resources while our public schools are being asked to do more with less.
Education should prepare students not just for a job, but for life and citizenship — whether their next step is a university, community college, apprenticeship, the military or the workforce.
Strong communities need locally rooted businesses and real competition.
I've built a small business.
I know how hard it is to compete when large companies have advantages that local businesses don't.
Iowa should make it easier to start and grow businesses, encourage entrepreneurship, invest in our downtowns and local economies, and promote competition instead of consolidation.
Economic development shouldn't just be about attracting the biggest company with the biggest incentive package.
It should help Iowans create businesses, jobs and wealth that stay in our communities.
Growth requires infrastructure that can keep up.
Safe roads and bridges, reliable water and wastewater systems, broadband and an affordable, resilient electric grid are foundations of a strong economy.
We should make smart investments before systems fail, plan for growing communities, and expand Iowa's energy opportunities while protecting landowners and respecting local communities.
Saving a dollar today isn't a bargain if it costs us ten dollars tomorrow.
Iowa has enormous advantages: some of the world's best farmland, abundant energy resources, hardworking people, and communities worth investing in.
Our responsibility is to leave those opportunities stronger than we found them.
Farm profitability and clean water don't have to be competing goals.
As the 4th generation on our family's farm, I believe Iowa can produce food, support profitable farms and dramatically improve our water and soil.
That means investing in conservation practices that produce measurable results, helping farmers reduce risk as they adopt new practices, expanding markets and opportunities for more diverse crops, and creating an agricultural economy where farmers capture more of the value they create.
We also need honest measurement.
If we're spending public dollars on water quality, Iowans deserve to know whether it's working.
Good stewardship isn't just environmental policy.
It's agricultural policy, economic policy and an investment in the next generation.
Our kids should inherit opportunities, not the tab.
I balance a family budget and a business budget, and I'd bring the same basic discipline to the state: honest numbers, sustainable spending, and investments that pay dividends for generations.
We shouldn't use one-time money to disguise ongoing obligations or make promises today that simply push costs onto local governments and property taxpayers tomorrow.
At the same time, responsible budgeting doesn't mean refusing to invest.
Schools, infrastructure, water and a skilled workforce are assets that make Iowa stronger.
The question shouldn't simply be, "What can we cut?"
It should be, "What investments can we make today that will make Iowa stronger ten, twenty and fifty years from now?"
None of these issues exists in isolation.
A good school helps a child, a family, an employer and an entire community.
Clean water affects our health, our farms, and our economy.
Affordable childcare helps parents work and businesses find employees.
That's why I don't start with a political talking point.
I start by asking what problem we're actually trying to solve.
Listen first.
Ask good questions.
Follow the evidence.
Work with people who see things differently.
I don't expect us to agree on every issue.
But you should always know how I'll approach the job: make decisions based on what's best for Iowa families, our communities, and the future we're building together.
That's what Building a Better Tomorrow, Together means to me.
