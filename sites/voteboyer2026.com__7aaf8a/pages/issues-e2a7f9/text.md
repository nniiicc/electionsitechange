District 82 Primary Issues and Our Plan of Action
Housing and Affordability: “The Issue People Feel First”
District 82 is powered by working people—and the promise of our local economy should be simple: if you work hard, you should be able to build a stable life and a better future.
From Immokalee to LaBelle, Clewiston, Ave Maria, and Everglades City, families are facing rising housing and insurance costs, long commutes, and the daily need for dependable schools, healthcare, and safe infrastructure.
This platform focuses on three connected priorities: expanding attainable and workforce housing and confronting affordability pressures; strengthening public schools, improving healthcare and mental-health access, supporting smart public safety, and protecting due process and equal protection; and safeguarding our water, land, and future through Everglades restoration and stronger resilience to hurricanes and flooding.
These issues are linked—because a family cannot thrive without a stable home, a community cannot grow without strong schools and reachable care, and our district cannot prosper without clean water and infrastructure that can withstand storms.
The goal is straightforward: make District 82 work for the people who work for District 82.

Schools, Healthcare, and Rights: “A Family-First Agenda”
District 82’s “Family-First Agenda” centers on the everyday institutions that determine stability and long-term opportunity: strong public schools, accessible healthcare, and respect for rights and dignity.
The platform describes public schools as community anchors—especially for working parents and multilingual families—providing structure and support that help children thrive and allow parents to maintain employment.
It calls for stronger support for educators, expanded career and technical education pathways, improved English learner services, and focused efforts to recruit and retain qualified teachers to match the district’s multicultural needs.
On healthcare, the agenda highlights rural access gaps and provider shortages that force long drives and delays in care.
It promotes earlier care closer to home by strengthening community clinics, responsibly expanding telehealth, and building workforce pipelines for nurses, counselors, and primary care providers.
Mental health is treated as essential to community stability, with an emphasis on prevention, timely treatment, and reducing crises that disrupt families, schools, and work.
The platform also places civil rights at the center of a family-first approach, calling for due process, equal protection, and personal dignity in a multicultural district shaped by immigrant contributions.
It supports a fair and humane immigration approach that upholds the rule of law while rejecting dehumanization and protecting families.
Finally, it advances “smart,” community-based public safety—supporting first responders while prioritizing prevention, mental-health response options, and trust-building to keep neighborhoods secure.
Water, Land, and Resilience: Everglades Restoration and Storm Readiness
Water and resilience form the platform’s third priority because, in District 82, clean water is both an environmental responsibility and an economic necessity.
Water quality affects public health, agricultural productivity, tourism, fishing, property values, and the overall quality of life families depend on.
The agenda places Everglades restoration at the center of the district’s future, supporting efforts tied to the Comprehensive Everglades Restoration Plan (CERP) to improve water flow and timing, protect wetlands, reduce harmful discharges, and strengthen natural systems that help buffer flooding and storm impacts.
The platform also calls for measurable progress, transparency, and accountability so restoration delivers real results—not just promises.
Alongside restoration, the platform emphasizes year-round hurricane, flooding, and sea-level rise preparedness through practical infrastructure investments: stronger drainage and canal systems, hardened utilities, safer roads, and emergency planning that reaches every neighborhood, especially communities with fewer recovery resources.
The guiding principle is clear: one storm should not wipe out years of progress for families and small businesses.
By linking environmental stewardship to economic security and fairness, the platform argues that investing in clean water and resilient infrastructure protects livelihoods, reduces repeated disaster costs, and keeps District 82 stable, competitive, and ready for the future—so the district works for the people who work for it.
