Affordability- Gas Prices, Why Are Gas Prices So High in Illinois?
Why are Illinois gas prices so high compared to nearby other states?
If you've traveled out of state, or even locally, especially in Chicago and the collar counties, you've noticed that we pay a higher cost per gallon.
Look no further than the gas taxes imposed on us.
In 2019, the Rebuild Illinois Act, not only doubled the base gas tax, it also for the first time established "automatic annual increases tied to the CPI-U" index, effective July 1, 2019, and every July 1, every year thereafter.
What is the "CPI-U" index?
The CPI-U is the inflation index provided by the Department of Labor, each month based on a "basket of goods" representing a typical family's purchases, including food, housing, transportation, medical care, and yes, gas or fuel prices.
Given the sharp increase in the gas tax imposed on us, you may be wondering, what percentage of the CPI-U used to raise gas taxes is made of up of "gas or fuel" prices?
The answer: less than 3% on average.
In other words, Illinois ties its annual fuel tax increase to a measure that is 97% unrelated to fuel gas prices.
So, you may now be asking, why tie gas and fuel prices to the overall CPI-U, when the underlying product being taxed may actually decrease in a given year?
(Per DOL, gas and fuel prices actually decreased by -9.5%, YOY as of July 1, 2025) The simple answer, a fuel tax tied to the broad CPI-U index brings in more revenue without having to go back to the citizens to ask for more money, and unlike the "gas and fuel" CPI, the board index almost always increased year over year.
In short:
The impact of tying Illinois's gas sales tax to overall CPI-U is that during high inflation periods, the gas tax is supercharged to higher rates, without direct tie into the underlying cost of fuel which the tax is tied to.
And as a bonus, the tax increase never goes down!
Net result: a cash infusion to the state coffers, though gas prices may have declined, and the funds are fungible (that is, the gather funds can be used for other purposes than for which they were collected).
Tying the gas sales tax to the CPI-U index, largely unrelated to the cost of filling up your truck, car or motorcycle, is automatically making it harder for Illinois families to make ends meet.
The broad CPI-U tie in should be discontinued and our politicians should have to explain each year why they want more money, as well as explain what was spent with what was collected.
