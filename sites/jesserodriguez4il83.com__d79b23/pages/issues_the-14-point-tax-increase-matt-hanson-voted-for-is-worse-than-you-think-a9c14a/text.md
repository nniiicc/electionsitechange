The 1/4-point tax increase Matt Hanson voted for is worse than you think.
The Chicago-area transit bailout under SB 2111 is a raw deal for suburban taxpayers.
Had Enough?
While the Collar Counties (DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry, and Will) are being asked to shoulder the full burden of the new 0.25% RTA sales tax increase, they get zero control over how it is spent.
How did our suburban based Illinois House Representative Matt Hanson (D) vote?
Despite the fact Illinois families already carry the highest tax burden of any state in the county, he voted yes. https://www.illinoispolicy.org/report-illinoisans-pay.../ Here is the truth about the newly formed NITA board structure and the politicians who pushed it through: The Representation Disadvantage: The 5 Collar Counties represent roughly 3.3 million residents—comparable to Chicago and suburban Cook County.
Yet, they get only 5 out of 20 seats on the governing board.
Chicago, Cook County, and Springfield can easily team up and outvote our local interests 15 to 5.
Rubber-Stamped by Local Reps: To make matters worse, our own State Representative Matt Hanson (D-Batavia/Aurora) of the 83rd House District fell right in line and voted YES to tax his own constituents.
It is no surprise to see him rubber-stamp this package; he continues to vote exactly as he is directed by his bosses in the Democratic Party Leadership rather than standing up for the hard-working families in his district.
Siphoning Road Funds: The state is also masking the true cost of this bailout by sweeping "interest" income generated from gas tax revenues.
Instead of fixing our suburban roads and bridges, that interest cash is being funneled directly into propping up city transit operations.
The Bottom Line: By continuously hiking sales taxes, diverting infrastructure funds, and on top of Tollway increases to cover unnecessary spending plans, Springfield ensures that Illinois remains stuck with one of the highest tax burdens in the entire country.
Suburban families in Aurora, Oswego, Yorkville, North Aurora, Batavia, Geneva and St.
Charles pay the price, while Chicago calls the shots.
