⚠️ ILLINOIS START-UP BUSINESSES ARE BEING PUT AT A HUGE DISADVANTAGE! ⚠️
The practical impacts of this bill (Senate Bill 3019) on venture capital efforts and businesses in Illinois include:
- Increased State Tax Liability: Gain from a liquidity event (such as a sale, merger, or other disposition) that was previously excluded from state income tax under federal Section 1202 will now be subject to Illinois income tax at a flat rate of 4.95% for individuals.
This change applies to tax years ending on or after December 31, 2026, and primarily affects Illinois resident taxpayers.
- Shifted Investor Behavior: Because the Section 1202 exclusion serves to offset the inherent risks of early-stage investing, its elimination at the state level may cause investors to seek larger equity positions to compensate for the lost tax advantage.
- Altered Exit Timelines: Existing Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) shareholders may be forced to reevaluate their exit structures and timelines in order to mitigate their exposure to the new Illinois tax.
- Changed Negotiating Dynamics: Deal terms during transactions may shift as both buyers and sellers begin to factor the incremental state tax cost into their negotiations.
- Missed State-Level Enhancements: Although federal law under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) has expanded and made QSBS more attractive, Illinois residents will miss out on these expanded benefits at the state level, even though they will still receive the federal exclusion.
- Potential Changes in Investor Domicile: Individual investors may evaluate changing their state residency to conforming states prior to a major exit event to avoid the incremental Illinois tax cost.
- Impact on Passthrough Arrangements: Stakeholders in passthrough entities will need to assess how this tax addback impacts economic arrangements among partners and whether existing tax distribution provisions are adequate.
