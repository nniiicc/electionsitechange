Recognized Named a 2026 session “winner” Leadership Elected House Republican Floor Leader In November 2024, House Republicans elected April Connors as Floor Leader — the No.
3 post in the caucus and one of the highest‑ranking Republican roles in the chamber.
It is a position normally reserved for senior members; her colleagues chose her in only her second term.
The Floor Leader manages debate strategy on the House floor and speaks for the caucus in the moments that decide whether a bill moves or dies.
It is a direct measure of how much trust she has earned in Olympia in a short time.
More on April’s leadership → Housing A housing win with zero opposition April’s manufactured‑home mortgage bill (HB 1191) passed the House 97–0 and the Senate 49–0 before being signed into law — a rare show of unanimous, bipartisan agreement, praised by The Pew Charitable Trusts.
It reclassifies manufactured homes in resident‑owned communities as real property so owners can access conventional mortgages instead of high‑interest chattel loans — about 1,700 homes across roughly 30 communities to start, with more expected to follow.
For those families, it’s the difference between building equity and being locked out of ownership.
More on April’s Housing Work → Workforce Career training, written into law April’s career & technical education bills, HB 1414 and HB 1722, were signed into law in 2025.
Together they clear the way for trained 16‑ and 17‑year‑olds to earn professional licenses and step into real careers instead of waiting on the sidelines.
For the Tri‑Cities — where skilled trades, healthcare, and technical work drive the economy — it’s a direct line between local classrooms and local family‑wage jobs.
More on April’s CTE work → For the district Delivering for the Tri‑Cities April has helped secure tens of millions of dollars in capital investment for the 8th District: behavioral‑health care capacity, college and school facilities, and infrastructure that families and employers depend on.
These aren’t abstract budget lines — they’re recovery beds, classrooms, and projects that keep the Tri‑Cities growing.
Delivering them takes a seat at the table and the relationships to use it.
More on Tri‑Cities investments → Holding the line Stopping a state income tax.
April coordinated a 24-hour, 33-minute House floor effort against SB 6346.
The Democratic majority ultimately forced the bill through on a narrow 51–46 vote — eight Democrats joining all Republicans in opposition — enacting Washington’s first state income tax.
The House Clerk could not recall a longer floor debate in 26 years.
From her seat as Assistant Ranking Member on House Appropriations, she also led caucus opposition to the multi-billion-dollar 2025–27 operating-budget tax package and voted no on gas-tax and hunting/fishing-fee increases — keeping the line on costs Tri-Cities families and small businesses already pay.
More on the tax fight → Recognized Named a 2026 session “winner” The nonpartisan Washington State Standard named April a “winner” of the 2026 legislative session for leading the longest floor debate in modern state history against a proposed state income tax.
That recognition came on top of a 2025 Legislative Superhero Award honoring her work expanding career and technical education.
Independent observers — not the campaign — flagged her as one of the most effective members of the session.
