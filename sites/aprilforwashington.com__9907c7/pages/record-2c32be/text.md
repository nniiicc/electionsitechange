1 / 1 ← → Energy & Hanford Defending the energy that powers the Tri-Cities.
From Hanford’s federal cleanup mission to the Columbia River hydroelectric system to a first-term bill answering rural complaints about wind-farm light pollution — April’s energy record reflects a district whose economy is built on reliable, affordable power.
Signed into law Wind‑turbine light‑pollution fix Connors‑sponsored · Signed May 2023 · Partial veto April’s first-term signed bill: aviation-safety lighting on Washington wind farms now activates only when an aircraft is detected nearby, instead of blinking constantly through the night.
Bipartisan support; answered long-running rural Eastern‑Washington complaints about night-sky pollution from utility-scale wind installations.
Read the coverage → Co‑sponsored Hanford cleanup‑mission defense With the Mighty 8th delegation · Multiple sessions Hanford supports roughly 13,000 federal-contract workers and a $3.35 billion FY2026 federal cleanup budget — one in ten Tri‑Cities jobs and the single largest economic engine in the 8th District.
April co‑sponsors caucus work defending the federal obligation and the family-wage jobs it anchors.
Defended Columbia River hydroelectric system Federal dam network · Ongoing The federal hydropower system on the Columbia and Snake rivers anchors irrigation for over a million acres of 8th‑District farmland and provides the bulk of the region’s baseload electricity.
April has opposed dam-removal proposals and consistently supports continued federal investment in the hydroelectric network.
Opposed Premature retirement of reliable baseload 2025–26 sessions April consistently votes against legislation that would shutter reliable baseload generation — hydro, nuclear, natural gas — before Washington has affordable, dependable replacements in place.
Reliability isn’t a partisan issue in the 8th District: it’s what keeps farms irrigated, families employed, and the regional clean-energy economy growing.
