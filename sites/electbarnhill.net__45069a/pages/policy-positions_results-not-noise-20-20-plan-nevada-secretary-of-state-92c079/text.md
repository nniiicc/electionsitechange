A Ready-to-Go Plan for Voter ID
The Secretary of State’s office gets six bill draft requests (BDRs) each legislative session — a real, limited tool for shaping law.
My first priority is implementation legislation that’s ready the moment Question 7 (Voter ID) passes this November and amends the Nevada Constitution.
Voters already backed it by a wide margin in 2024; this is the second required vote.
As Secretary of State, I’ll ensure a clean, efficient rollout — photo ID for in-person voting, Free ID for those who can’t afford it, and real verification for mail ballots — protecting the process while keeping access straightforward for lawful voters.
I’ve gone into the practical implementation questions in greater detail in Voter ID Is Coming: Let’s Talk About Making It Actually Work for People, including how the state could address identification access, cost, provisional ballots, rural access, and other implementation concerns.
For more detail on what the office can do using its existing administrative authority, see What an Independent Secretary of State Can Accomplish Under a Democratic-Controlled Legislature.

Cutting the Commerce Tax
My second BDR targets repeal of the Commerce Tax, a gross-receipts tax on any business with more than $4 million in Nevada revenue.
It’s become an unnecessary drag: new and growing companies feel it immediately, established firms have absorbed rising costs, and it undercuts the one advantage Nevada holds over every income-tax state.
Legislative action is required to fix it, and the Secretary of State’s office — as Nevada’s chief business-filing authority — is the right office to lead that charge and keep Nevada open for business.
Closing Nevada’s Privacy Gap
Nevada is also losing ground on business privacy.
Wyoming keeps member and manager names off public filings entirely.
Colorado offers strong formation privacy protections.
Nevada still requires managers or managing members to appear on the public annual list.
Companies that value confidentiality are choosing to form elsewhere.
I’ll use this office’s existing authority — and remaining BDRs if needed — to close that gap and stop the outflow of business formations to our competitor states.
Overseeing the ORION Migration
The conversion from SilverFlume to ORION is happening right now.
Phase 1 (UCC filings) launched in December 2025.
Phase 2 — full business licensing, trademarks, and notary services — is scheduled for this summer, with planned downtime and new forms.
Large government IT replacements always carry risk: data integrity, user training, vendor performance, and edge-case failures.
Twenty years of systems analysis experience means I know how to supervise a migration like this, hold vendors accountable, and protect the data that businesses and voters rely on.
No other candidate brings that hands-on technical depth to this office.
