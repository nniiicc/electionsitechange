The Honest Answer Is You. electbarnhill Oct 08, 2026 0 Comments Every federal lawsuit in Nevada comes with a short form most people never read: the Certificate of Interested Parties.
Its job is simple.
It tells the judges who has a financial or corporate stake in the case (parent companies, subsidiaries, insurers, anyone standing behind a party) so a judge can step aside if there’s a conflict.
In most cases, it reads like a corporate org chart.
Mine doesn’t.
Two names.
That’s the whole list.
The certificate I filed in Barnhill v.
Aguilar names exactly two parties: me, a Nevada voter, and Francisco V.
Aguilar, sued in his official capacity as Nevada Secretary of State.
Under the court’s rules, I certified that: I’m a natural person, not a corporation, partnership, or LLC.
No parent company, subsidiary, affiliate, or corporate owner stands behind this case.
No insurer is involved.
I’m the real party in interest.
Nobody else is bringing this case through me.
From my Certificate of Interested Parties, filed under LR 7.1-1 and Fed.
R.
Civ.
P.
7.1.
One voter, not a national committee That matters because of what the Secretary of State has asked the court to do.
Instead of answering my complaint, he asked the court to pause my case indefinitely while a different voter-roll lawsuit plays out, one brought by the Republican National Committee.
That case is between a national party committee and the state.
Mine is between one Nevada voter and the official responsible for keeping the voter rolls accurate.
No national committee, corporation, or advocacy group is behind it.
I asked the court to consolidate or coordinate the two cases rather than set mine aside, because a voter’s claim shouldn’t have to wait behind a party’s.
The part the rules don’t let me write down There’s one more paragraph in that certificate, and it’s the one I care about most: “Plaintiff is aware that accurate voter-list maintenance under the National Voter Registration Act is a matter of public concern to Nevada electors generally.” The rule exists to flag judicial conflicts, not civic ones, so I couldn’t list every registered Nevada voter as an interested party.
It wouldn’t help the judge, and it isn’t what the rule is for.
But that’s who this case is really for.
My analysis found 9,607 Nevadans with duplicate active voter registrations, which produced 12,465 matched registration pairs.
Each pair shows a 2024 General Election vote recorded under both registration numbers.
I’m not alleging fraud.
I’m asking for an audit, a reconciliation, public reporting, and a statewide duplicate-detection program, because accurate rolls are how every lawful vote keeps its full weight.
So if you’re a Nevada voter, you won’t find your name on that certificate.
You have the biggest stake in the case anyway.
What comes next The Secretary of State’s motions to stay and to extend time, my opposition and motion to consolidate, and my request for electronic filing access are all pending before the court.
