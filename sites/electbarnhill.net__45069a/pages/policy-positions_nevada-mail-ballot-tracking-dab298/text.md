How Nevada mail ballot tracking works
- Sign up: go to MyBallot.NV.gov.
It’s free, and you can choose text, email, or phone call updates.
- Watch the statuses: you can see when your ballot is sent, when the county receives it, when it’s accepted for counting, and when it’s flagged because something needs your attention.
- Keep your contact information current: update it at VOTE.NV.gov.
If your county needs to reach you about a problem, that is how they will do it.
- Confirm you’re registered: tracking only works if your registration is correct.
You can look yours up on the Secretary of State’s voter search or read my guide on how to check your Nevada voter registration.
“Received” is not the same as “counted”
Your ballot can be received and still need a second look.
The most common reason is a missing or mismatched signature on the return envelope.
Nevada lets you fix that, and the process is called curing your ballot.
For the November 3 election, the deadline to cure is 5 p.m. on November 9.
The state explains the options, including fixing it electronically, on its signature cure page.
If you see a flagged status, don’t panic and don’t wait.
Contact your county election office, which you can find on the state’s county clerk contact list, and follow the instructions.
Fixing a problem early is far easier than fixing it on deadline day.
The dates that matter
- October 20: last day to register or update your registration and be mailed a ballot.
- October 27: the Secretary of State recommends mailing your ballot at least one week before Election Day.
- November 3: Election Day.
Your mail ballot must be postmarked by this date, or returned to an official drop box in your county.
- November 7: the last day for the county to receive a ballot that was postmarked on time.
The U.S.
Supreme Court ruled this summer that states can keep this kind of grace period.
- November 9, 5 p.m.: the last chance to cure a signature problem.
If you would rather vote in person, you still can.
Bring your mailed ballot to surrender, or sign the required affirmation, and vote only once.
Deadlines and locations can change, so check VOTE.NV.gov for the latest official information.

What I would do as Secretary of State
Nevada already has a tracker.
My job would be to make it work better for every voter, in every county.
- Plain-language statuses: every county should use the same definitions, and every notice should say what happened, what the deadline is, and how to fix it.
- Early testing: test the system and its messages before ballots go out, not after voters start calling county offices.
- Clear reporting: publish how many ballots were sent, received, flagged, and cured, so voters see facts instead of rumors.
- Rural and hard-to-reach voters: keep phone and mail options for voters without fast internet, and get notices to seniors, students, military families, and voters with disabilities.
See my plan for rural voting access.
- Consistent standards: a voter in Ely and a voter in Henderson should get the same information.
That is the idea behind consistent county election standards.
