O’Mara visits Seneca County businesses now designated on the NYS Historic Business Preservation Registry: Recognizing businesses that have supported local communities for over 50 years
Updated: Sep 25
Longstanding local businesses are the foundation, and the heart and soul of so many of our local communities across the Finger Lakes region.
Seneca Falls, N.Y., September 23—State Senator Tom O’Mara (R-C, Big Flats) visited four Seneca County businesses on Wednesday to help celebrate their inclusion on the New York State Historic Business Preservation Registry.
O’Mara, who nominated each of the businesses for a place on the registry, said, “Longstanding local businesses are the foundation, and the heart and soul of so many of our local communities across the Finger Lakes region.
I’m proud to have the opportunity to help recognize and pay tribute to these local businesses in Seneca County.
They have been operating locally for generations and have taken their place as cornerstones of the culture and economy in Seneca Falls and Waterloo.
They are sources of enormous pride in the community and have earned and deserve this designation on New York State’s Historic Business Preservation Registry.”
The Registry is operated by the New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation (OPRHP).
According to OPRHP, it recognizes New York-based businesses over 50 years old that have made contributions to their communities.
Beginning in 2022, the online registry “highlights historic businesses that have helped craft the very culture of New York State and support local communities and their people.”
On Wednesday, O’Mara visited each of the following businesses to present them with a certificate and a window decal designating their place on the Historic Business Preservation Registry:
-- Connie’s Diner, 205 East Main Street, Waterloo.
Established in 1966, a favorite among locals, Connie’s Diner in Waterloo began as the go-to neighborhood spot for hearty homemade meals with a side of genuine hospitality.
The diner grew into a community hub and became known for its classic recipes, retro charm, and friendly service.
All of which inspired Connie Caratozzolo in 1989 to leave her chef position at Hobart College and purchase the diner from Sharon Podgorny (the original owner of Chick’s Diner).
The diner reopened as “Connie’s” and continues its legacy with the addition of family recipes handed down to Connie from her mother.
-- Mull Funeral Home, 113 Virginia Street, Waterloo.
Opening in 1959, Mull Funeral was established at 113 Virginia Street.
It was the family’s home and Russell V.
Mull, owner, and licensed Funeral Director, at that time, called it “a family business.” It remains so today.
The funeral home is in a historic Victorian home which is over 150 years old.
Russell V.
Mull passed away on August 15, 2005.
His son, William, continues the traditions of the Mull family in serving the area when their funeral needs arise.
-- Robinette Jewelers, 90 Fall Street, Seneca Falls.
George Robinette opened the doors to Robinette Jewelers (then called George S.
Robinette Jeweler) on July 10th, 1944, at 92 Fall Street in Seneca Falls.
Today the store, now located at 90 Fall Street, is owned and operated by his son Mark.
Mark’s son, Daniel, has also returned to Robinette Jewelers to work alongside his father.
In late 2020, Robinette Jewelers welcomed 4th generation family members to the business.
-- Sinicropi Florist, 64 Fall Street, Seneca Falls.
Sinicropi Florist was established in 1975 by the founder Joyce Sinicropi and currently owned and operated by Andew Allen of Allen Floral Group LLC.
They are celebrating their 50th year in business and are a well-known presence in the town of Seneca Falls.
They have provided exceptional customer service and stunning floral arrangements for all occasions to the town of Seneca Falls and surrounding areas.
In July, OPRHP announced that the Registry now includes more than 300 businesses statewide.
OPRHP Commissioner Kathy Moser said, “The Historic Business Preservation Registry is a meaningful way to recognize businesses that have become cherished institutions in communities across New York.
Reaching the milestone of 300 designated businesses reflects the strength and enduring legacy of these local establishments.
We look forward to continuing to celebrate the family-owned businesses and community anchors that help define the places New Yorkers call home.”
Find out more about the Historic Business Preservation Registry at: https://parks.ny.gov/preservation/programs/business-registry.
