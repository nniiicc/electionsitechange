How Colorado Keystone Accounts work:
1.
The "Open and Fund" Phase:
- Opening: A taxpayer opens a "Colorado Keystone Account" at their preferred financial institution.
- Funding: The taxpayer contributes money from their paycheck or savings into this account.
Up to $10,000 a year for the individual and up to $20,000 a year for a couple (Joint).
- Benefit: The taxpayer lists their total annual contributions on their annual Colorado State Income Tax return.
This amount is subtracted from their "Adjusted Gross Income" (AGI), which lowers the total amount of income that the state taxes.
Example: If you earn $75,000 and contribute $10,000 to your Colorado Keystone Account, the state only taxes you as if you earned $65,000.
You pay less state income tax immediately.
So, yes, this is also a tax cut for every Coloradan.
2.
The "Growth and Deployment" Phase:
- Growth: The money inside the account is not sitting idle.
It can be invested in index funds, bonds, or high yield savings accounts, allowing the down payment to grow alongside the market.
- Tax-free Usage: As long as the money is used for a "Qualified Housing Expense," the withdrawal is free of tax.
- Withdrawal: This is the keystone moment.
When the account holder reaches a life milestone like buying a home, needing a new roof, or installing an accessibility ramp, they withdraw the funds.
It is that simple.
3.
Staying Compliant with my Campaign's Accountability and Transparency Promise.
- No State Middleman: The state government does not "run" the accounts.
Financial institutions provide the account infrastructure.
This guarantees these accounts can be launched immediately in 2027.
- Reporting: The taxpayer simply keeps records (receipts/invoices) for their housing expenses, similar to how they keep medical receipts for a Health Savings Account (HSA).
They report the total spent on their tax return, and the Department of Revenue audits it randomly, just like any other tax deduction.
- The TABOR Advantage: In Colorado, any policy that lowers the amount of income tax the state collects actually helps the state under the Taxpayer's Bill of Right (TABOR) revenue cap.
By lowering the taxable income of hardworking Coloradans, the Colorado Keystone Account respects TABOR.
We are not creating a new taxpayer-funded government housing agency; we are creating a private, tax-free lane for you to build and maintain your own financial wealth here in Colorado.
