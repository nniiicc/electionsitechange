Real Solutions to Public Safety
Let's talk public safety.
We need a proactive legislation package designed to restore law and order and protect our suburban communities here in Jefferson County.
I have heard you.
I share the same frustrations.
Here are some of the promises and policies I will advance.
Coloradans deserve legislators that seek real, tangible solutions, not simply talk about how they will fix them, and in most cases never follow through or put forward actual solutions.
These are my core objectives for making Edgewater and Lakewood safer:
- Anti-Theft Measures: We need to repeal weakened penalties for repeat retail theft and property crimes.
It is time to restore strict felony thresholds for stolen goods by increasing penalties for organized retail crime rings in our commercial corridors of HD30.
- Commuter Safety and Transit Accountability: Colorado has the foundation of great alternative commuting options, but they are not safe, secure, or clean.
We need to demand structural security, cleanliness standards, and a zero-tolerance policy for crime and open substance abuse across RTD light rails and bus transit hubs here.
- Enhanced Penalties for Critical Infrastructure Theft: We need to increase mandatory criminal penalties for individuals and criminal groups that engage in stealing copper wire, catalytic converters, and public utility infrastructure.
These thefts are rising and disrupt suburban communications, travel, and our power grids.
- Judicial Accountability: We need to reform Colorado's bond and sentencing framework to ensure judges face regular performance transparency when releasing career criminals or violent offenders on low-cost recognization bonds.
We can also expand the protections of the Colorado Victim Rights Act ensuring transparent public tracking of lenient plea bargains, and introduce more accountability measures.
- Drug and Human Trafficking: We have to strengthen the penalties and close the loopholes in state drug and theft laws.
We have to establish a zero-tolerance for human trafficking and not put forward bills, like my opponent, decriminalizing prostitution which would encourage these criminal acts in our neighborhoods.
- Community First Mental Health: We can redirect state behavioral health funds away from ineffective, top-down administrative programs and into local, community-run clinical care centers that are the ones actively treating addiction and mental health challenges here in HD30.
This focuses the needed funds to the frontline staff at treatment facilities, crisis stabilization units, and builds on the ground support systems that combat public encampments and substance abuse.
Addiction reform can happen, but we need to focus on the long term help of recovery, not the short term use of funds simply managing this growing crisis.
- Emergency Services Support: We have to ensure adequate state funding and regulatory backing is given to our local emergency services as well as our volunteer emergency services.
These services keep us safe and are the requirement of our government to fund and fully support.
I will work alongside them, ensuring they are funded, supported, and respected.
That way they can be equipped to focus on successfully doing their jobs.
My main focus is building more accountability, training support, and aiding these teams for their primary, specified focus on achieving rapid response timing across our suburban and urban areas.
When you call for help, the systems need to be in place and enactable alongside the manpower to be there in a timely matter.
