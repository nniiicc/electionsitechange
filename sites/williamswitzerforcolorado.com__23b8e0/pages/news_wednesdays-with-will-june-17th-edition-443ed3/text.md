My Father Taught Me Something Politics Forgot.
One of my favorite days of the year is Father's Day.
While my wife Megan and I do not have children of our own yet, we have been blessed by incredible parents and mentors throughout our lives.
We have also been fortunate to give back to our communities and serve as mentors ourselves.
With Father's Day approaching, I have been reflecting on the fathers and father figures who helped shape the person I am today.
The truth is, some of the most important work happening in our communities doesn't happen at the State Capitol.
It happens around the dinner table.
It happens while teaching a child how to ride a bike, volunteering together as a family, coaching a youth sports team, or simply showing up day after day for the people who depend on you.
My father taught me that being a father is not about being perfect.
It is about being present.
It's about putting your family first, even when nobody is watching.
Growing up, some of my life's most important lessons came from him.
My siblings and I learned the value of hard work, keeping our word, treating others with respect, and helping neighbors when they needed a hand.
Looking back, those lessons became the foundation not only of strong families, but of strong communities.
One story that often comes to mind is from my high school years.
As my interest in public policy and politics grew, I spent countless hours talking with my father about current events, government, and the issues shaping our country.
What made those conversations special was that he never tried to tell me what to believe.
Instead, he encouraged me to think for myself, ask questions, seek truth, and carve out my own path.
That encouragement extended beyond our conversations at home.
My father would often join me as we drove hours sometimes to hear presidential candidates and political leaders speak including people like Bernie Sanders, Hillary Clinton, and Barack Obama despite knowing full well they were candidates he would never vote for.
We waited in lines and among crowds for many hours, because if you have ever attended a political event, you know first hand politicians are always late.
My father's willingness to invest in my search for knowledge, desire to engage in public policy conversations, and understanding helped shape who I am today.
I believe he understood something that I have come to believe and talk about often.
Culture leads politics, no matter how much politics tries to legislate culture.
The values we teach at home, the way we treat our neighbors, and the example we set for the next generation will always have a greater impact than any law passed under the Capitol dome.
That is why strong families and engaged communities are so vital in protecting and growing the neighborhoods we love.
The conversations my father and I had on those drives home are ones I still remember today.
They were never lectures.
They were conversations.
They challenged me to think critically, understand different perspectives, and question not only what I believed, but why I believed it.
More importantly, they taught me a lesson that continues to guide me today: everyone deserves to have a voice.
Listening does not always mean agreeing.
It means respecting others enough to understand where they are coming from.
In a time when politics often encourages us to retreat into our own corners, my father taught me the importance of seeking understanding first.
I have found that some of the most valuable lessons come from listening to and engaging with people you may disagree with even politically.
Most days, it feels like politics forgets these lessons.
We hear endless debates about what government should do, but not enough about the people who are already doing the work every day to make our communities better.
I see that spirit throughout House District 30.
I have met so many of you while canvasing and volunteering.
This is what makes our communities so special.
Neighbors are looking after neighbors.
Families are investing in the next generation.
Volunteers are giving their time to strengthen the places they call home.
That is why I still believe Colorado's future will not be determined by politicians alone.
It will be shaped by parents raising good kids, mentors investing in young people, neighbors helping neighbors, and individuals choosing to serve their communities.
The values that build strong families are the same values that will build a stronger more united Colorado.
This Father's Day, let's celebrate the dads, grandfathers, stepfathers, mentors, coaches, and father figures who have helped guide the next generation.
Their impact reaches far beyond their own families and into every corner of our communities.
That deserves respect.
To my father and the many mentors who have invested in me throughout my life, thank you.
You helped shape the person I am today.
You taught me to be creative, to seek understanding, and, most importantly, to listen to all sides of a conversation.
I am grateful for those lessons, which continue to guide me every day on the campaign trail and in life.
The love, support, and example you have provided to me and my family have left a lasting impact.
They have inspired my wife and me to give back by serving as mentors ourselves, investing in others, and encouraging the next generation.
Your influence extends far beyond your own families.
It lives on in the lives of those you have guided, supported, and inspired.
For that, I am deeply thankful.
This is how we truly Move Colorado Forward.
