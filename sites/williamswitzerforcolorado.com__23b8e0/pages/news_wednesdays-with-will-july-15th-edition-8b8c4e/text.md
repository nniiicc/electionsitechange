True creativity is an act of human struggle, discipline, and meaning.
It is not a prompt fed into an algorithm.
When we prioritize making art over merely modifying it, we honor the hard work, the mistakes, and the unique lived experiences.
This is something I believe a machine can never replicate.
Art and music are part of our collective human connection.
These emotions are often forged through pain, joy, and the messy reality of living life.
To replace this with generative output is not progress, it is a theft of the very thing that makes us human.
Without consent, these AI models treat culture as a resource to be extracted rather than a canvas to be painted.
This ultimately minimizes the labor of artists.
We need to stop selling out to the convenience of automated content.
When we trade the depth of human expression for the efficiency of a machine, we are not just losing jobs.
We are slowly walking into a cage of our own making and losing the connective fibers of our community.
In Lakewood and across Colorado the expression of art is the heartbeat of our identity.
If we allow “good enough” AI generated slop to saturate our public squares and means of connecting we will close the door on the next generation of human creators.
This will deny them the space to learn, fail, and eventually master their craft.
As someone deeply invested in the future of our HD30 community, I believe we must stand for the creator, not the codex.
We need a society that values the person behind the canvas and instrument, protecting them from a corporate driven race to the bottom.
If we abandon the hard work of creation, we forfeit our right to claim our own culture.
We are building a future where nothing is truly new, only a hollow echo of what we have already stolen from ourselves.
It is time we choose to protect the human element.
It is time to support local talent and reject the sterile convenience that threatens to turn our vibrant culture into a predictable, automated product.
I have spent over 20 years in the creative world.
I will always defend this industry.
In a world so disconnected and divided, I truly believe art is one of the ways we can reconnect our culture and save our state.
That is why you see me actively calling on other political candidates and elected leaders to be themselves and be creative.
We can support local artists and fellow creators, instead of watering down a message with the use of AI.
Voters can see right through this.
We see a person seeking influence over our lives that doesn’t take the time to craft their own message.
They are listening to the machine drafting their speeches and issues verbiage, not the actual neighbors or voters in the community that wants to be heard.
I predict campaign decisions like these may very well be a factor when voters cast their ballot.
It is also why we are seeing our state lean towards candidates putting in the work, out in the neighborhoods, and putting forward actual solutions (some good and some bad).
People want transparency and leaders that have direction.
Not just fancy words, or another terrible political event poster created with AI.
We do not have to accept this as the new normal.
