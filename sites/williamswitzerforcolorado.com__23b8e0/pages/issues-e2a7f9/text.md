The Colorado Credit Union Innovation Act
We have the local tools and infrastructure to solve one of the biggest issues facing the housing crisis here in Colorado.
While Washington D.C. has the potential to speed this idea up (and I'm optimistic about the proposed Move Act), our state legislators should be trying to solve these problems here locally (even if it is a national issue as well).
We can pioneer this legislation.
Our State can pioneer a voluntary, portfolio-held, portable mortgage option by empowering state chartered credit unions through a targeted regulatory framework.
This will give relief to the thousands of Colorado families that are trapped in their homes by an unprecedented economic hurdle of being interest rate locked in.
We should not penalize Coloradan homeowners for moving, whether upgrading or downsizing their living situation.
This will also create a massive opening of available homes for first-time buyers to enter the market, and ultimately keep Coloradans here in Colorado.
This policy bypasses the federal roadblock that dictates standard conventional loans by opening up our local state opportunities in lending methods.
- Establish the Pilot Program: Our legislator body can authorize and incentivize state chartered credit unions to design voluntary, portfolio held mortgage products that include a portability rider.
This would allow participating credit unions to maintain and keep loans on their own balance sheets (rather than selling them to federal secondary markets) which would allow homeowners to port their existing mortgage balance and rate when moving to a new primary residence within Colorado.
To ensure institutional finance safety, the porting of the currently held mortgage applies strictly to the remaining principle balance.
Any additional purchase price required for the new home is financed at the prevailing portfolio rates, creating a secure, blended rate calculation for the borrower.
- State Depository Alignment and Incentives: The state of Colorado manages significant public funds and treasury deposits.
Under this proposal, the state will prioritize placing public operational deposits and cash management accounts into state chartered credit unions that actively participate in this pilot program.
This rewards community-first lenders.
This will also have market incentives.
Legislators will align state financial partnerships to reward local, member-owned institutions that prioritize housing liquidity, community mobility, and consumer first lending over rigid secondary market conformity.
It is time to put Colorado and its residents first.
- Streamlined Processing and Assumable Government Backed Loans: This proposal will unlock existing tools.
FHA, VA, and USDA loans are legally assumable, but transactions routinely collapse because conventional services are slow and can often take up to 6 months to process.
This can be refocused to state credit union hubs that provide administration backing and coordination support through the Colorado Division of Housing to help state chartered credit unions act as specialized servicing connection, which then can fast track and audit local loan assumptions.
This will cut red tape and processing times down to weeks instead of months.
Coloradans believe housing is foundational to economic security, community stability, and personal freedom.
When our citizens are prevented from moving freely, the entire state suffers.
I believe in practical, localized innovation.
As a entrepreneur, this Credit Union Innovation Act bypasses the federal gridlock and uses our state regulated financial ecosystem to benefit Coloradans.
It returns the flexibility they deserve and gets our housing market moving again.

The Food Freedom and Micro-Enterprise Act
A truly sustainable Colorado starts on the local level.
We need to strengthen and defend our state's localized supply chains, food sovereignty, and true economic independence for small scale producers.
For too long our state has legislated restrictive hurdles and over regulated these industries.
This comprehensive legislation proposal merges multiple agricultural and culinary freedom frameworks into a single unified act.
It also builds upon recent successful acts which focus on sustainability and food sovereignty here in our state.
1.
Direct-to-Consumer Raw Dairy Access and Choice
- Direct Farm-Gate and Market Access: Authorizes registered, independent dairy farmers to sell unpasteurized (raw) milk directly to informed consumers at the farm, roadside stands, and farmers' markets, modernizing legacy herd-share limitations.
- Safety Standards and Transparent Labeling: Implements common-sense temperature controls during transport and mandates clear container labeling displaying herd health standards, testing results, and prominent unpasteurized health warnings.
2.
Cottage Food Expansion and Micro-Enterprise Scaling
- Modernizing Home-Kitchen and Micro-Bakery Rules: Builds upon the framework of the passed Tamale Act (HB26-1033) to expand home-kitchen access, allowing the direct-to-consumer sale of small-batch prepared foods.
This includes perishable items, baked goods, and local specialty items.
This would eliminate the heavy commercial licensing hurdles.
- Scaling Revenue Caps and Safety Training: Elevates annual net revenue caps for home-based food producers to account for inflation and business growth, backed by required food safety training covering time and temperature control for safe handling.
3.
Localized Meat Processing and Butchery Infrastructure
- Strengthening Regional Supply Chains: Directs state agricultural development grants toward building localized, independent meat processing and community locker facilities reducing reliance on consolidated national supply chains.
- Streamlining Inspection and Regulatory Hurdles: Creates flexible, state-supported inspection pathways for small-scale livestock producers to process and market locally raised meat directly to consumers, restaurants, and neighborhood markets.
4.
Direct Farm-to-Institution and School Sourcing Integration
- Growing Institutional Local Procurement: Expands state-backed local food incentive grants to encourage schools, hospitals, and public facilities to source fresh fruits, vegetables, grains, meats, and dairy directly from Colorado producers.
- Cutting Institutional Red Tape: Streamlines procurement regulations so independent regional farms and ranches can easily bid on public institution supply contracts without getting bogged down by bureaucratic paperwork.
5.
Urban Agriculture, Backyard Produce, and Community Garden Protections
- Protecting Local Urban Growing: Safeguards the rights of homeowners and urban gardeners to cultivate backyard vegetable gardens, keep small-scale laying hens, and utilize residential rainwater collection barrels for localized food production.
- Preempting Overreaching Municipal Bans: Prohibits local municipal and HOA ordinances from enacting unreasonable blanket bans on residential food-producing gardens and low impact backyard micro agriculture.
6.
Community Seed Sovereignty and Heirloom Preservation
- Protects the traditional rights of Colorado gardeners, small farmers, and regional seed libraries to save, trade, and sell open pollinated, heirloom, and non-commercial seed varieties free from state commercial licensing laws.
7.
Colorado Local Food and Produce Immunity
- Shields small-scale farmers, homesteaders, and roadside market operators from arbitrary civil liability when selling whole, unprocessed agricultural products directly to consumers who assume normal inherent risks.
8.
On farm Poultry Processing Freedom
- Expands the "Ranch-to-Plate" framework by enabling small-scale poultry raisers to process and sell a higher annual volume of farm raised birds directly to local consumers and neighborhood restaurants without mandatory multi-thousand-dollar USDA facility installations.
9.
Craft Beverage and Winery Direct Shipping Expansion
- Supports Colorado's independent farm wineries, cideries, craft breweries, and small-batch distillers by breaking down barriers to direct to consumer distribution and interstate shipping without monopolistic wholesale middlemen.
10.
Municipal Community Garden and Livestock Protection
- Empowers urban and suburban residents to utilize municipal plots and private residential spaces for sustainable food self-sufficiency, prohibiting local bans on low-impact backyard laying hens (excluding roosters) and standardizing community garden access on public utility easements.
Protecting the sustainability of our local food sources should be a priority for all legislators in Colorado.
This comprehensive legislation puts our state first in defending the way of life so many Coloradans not only depend on, but participate in.
Legislators should encourage and simplify the process, not restrict it in our state.
