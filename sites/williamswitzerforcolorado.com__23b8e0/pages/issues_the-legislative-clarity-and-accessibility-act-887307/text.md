The Legislative Clarity and Accessibility Act
We should not have a General Assembly producing hundreds of pages of legislation loaded with legal jargon.
Massive omnibus bills strategically tie up dozens of unrelated issues together, allowing special interest groups and elected leaders to secure hidden provisions and political wins at the expense of everyday Coloradans.
We must change this.
I have a Masters degree in Public Policy and I fully intend on simplifying this process and making our local government more easily accessible so that everyday citizens can not only be a part of the process, but fully understand it.
You should not have to have a degree or be a part of their club.
Our government belongs to you, the people, and I fully intend on returning it back to that ideal.
Why the Legislative Clarity and Accessibility Act is needed:
1.
The Implementation Strategy:
- This is a vehicle for reform: This Act would be implemented through modifications to the Joint Rules of the House and Senate.
- Drafting partner: Collaboration with the Office of Legislative Legal Services (OLLS) to certify executive summaries and oversee drafting standards.
- Coalition Building: This Act would advance public accountability by securing commitments from fellow candidates and legislators via a Plain Clear Language Pledge.
Voters can visibly see who intends on working for the people.
This also allows voters to hold their elected leaders accountable when they favor their party or special interest above their constituents.
2.
The Four Pillars of Reform:
- The 25-Page Limit: This enforces a strict, 25-page limit on all standard policy bills.
This matters because it forces to the point, legislated solutions and focuses on completely dismantling the era of broad sweeping legislation and hidden riders.
- Plain Language Standards: This mandates that all legislation be drafted using clear, accessible terms.
This matters because it ensures that if a bill is written for the people, the people can actually read, understand, and evaluate it without needing to be a policy expert.
- Certified Executive Summaries: This requires a certified, plain-language executive summary (not exceeding 2 pages by OLLS) for any complex technical or fiscal supplement bill.
This matters because it guarantees that every legislator and citizen knows exactly what is in a bill and understands its fiscal impacts and primary changes before a final vote is cast.
- Citizen Accessibility: This requires that Coloradans have adequate time to review legislation before it passes or is voted on.
3.
Overview of Policy Exceptions:
- Standard Policy Bills: These Bills will be enforced to the 25-page max.
They will require summaries only if necessary due to potential complexity.
This will create a new standard of legislating.
One that stops hidden provisions and unrelated riders.
- The State Budget: The Long Bill is exempt from the 25-page limit.
The Summary is required.
This exception accommodates operational complexity while enforcing clear language standards being proposed.
- Technical and Fiscal Supplements: These are exempt from the 25-page limitation.
These require a 2-page OLLS Summary.
This outlines primary changes, intent, and fiscal impact clearly, but does not limit legislators.
