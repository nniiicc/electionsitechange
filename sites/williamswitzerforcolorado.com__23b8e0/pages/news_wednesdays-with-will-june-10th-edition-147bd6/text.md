Home My Story Public Policy Goals News Connect Get Involved Join an Event Contribute News / Wednesdays With Will (June 10th edition) 10 Jun Wednesday, 6:02 PM · 2026 Wednesdays With Will (June 10th edition) A Colorado Love Story This fall, my wife, Megan, and I will celebrate 11 years of marriage.
Like many families in District 30, our story is one built on hard work, sacrifice, and the pursuit of the American Dream.
We met during our first semester of college in Nashville.
Megan was a freshman, and I had just transferred in.
We were both new to the city and happened to attend the same orientation dance class after being invited separately by campus representatives.
We share this photo as our first official meeting, one that at the time was simply a photo being taken of likeminded college students.
Over that first year, we crossed paths from time to time, but it wasn't until the last day of finals before summer break that everything changed.
I asked her to join me for a date at a mini-golf tournament hosted by the graphic design school.
The students had designed and built an 18-hole mini-golf course on campus.
Afterward, we grabbed dinner and spent the entire night walking around Nashville, talking until the sun came up.
It was a walk I'll never forget.
Very quickly, I realized she was someone special.
We shared the same values, the same belief in family, hard work, and building a meaningful life.
Looking back, I knew within the first week that I wanted to spend my life with her.
After graduation, I asked Megan to marry me.
To make the proposal special, I had a putter engraved and recreated the mini-golf date that brought us together a year earlier at our alma mater.
Like many young couples, we weren't wealthy.
We wanted a small wedding and were focused on saving for our first home.
Instead of an expensive honeymoon, we packed our bags and spent two and a half weeks exploring Colorado.
That trip changed our lives.
Colorado had always been a dream of mine.
Growing up in Arizona, I imagined someday living in the mountains.
For Megan, it was her first experience with the state.
By the time we returned home, we both knew that one day Colorado would be where we wanted to put down roots.
Years later, when COVID shut down my industry, we were faced with a difficult choice.
Rather than wait for someone else to solve our problems, we took a leap of faith.
We packed up our lives and headed west.
We moved into a small studio apartment near Edgewater and Sloan's Lake while searching for a permanent home in Lakewood.
Three months later, we found the house we live in today.
This community became our home.
Over the last five and a half years, I've watched District 30 change.
I've seen families struggle to keep up with rising costs.
I've watched small businesses face growing challenges.
I've seen public safety concerns increase while politicians spend more time arguing than solving problems.
The people of Lakewood and Edgewater are hardworking, practical people.
They aren't consumed by partisan talking points.
They're focused on paying their bills, raising their families, building their businesses, and creating a better future for their children.
Yet too often, it feels like the voices of everyday people are ignored while political insiders, special interests, and well connected organizations dominate the conversation.
That's what motivated me to run.
I didn't enter this race because I wanted to become a politician.
I entered because I believe government should work for the people who pay for it.
I believe families deserve to keep more of what they earn.
I believe small businesses deserve leaders who understand the challenges of building something from the ground up.
I believe public safety should never be a partisan issue.
I believe government should be transparent, accountable, and focused on results instead of political theater.
Most importantly, I believe the people of District 30 deserve a representative who remembers who they work for.
My wife and I chose Colorado because we believed it was a place where ordinary people could build a good life through hard work and determination.
We still believe that today.
This is our home.
It is where we hope to raise our future family.
It is where we have invested our time, our resources, and our future.
The political class may see District 30 as a collection of voting blocs and statistics.
I see it as home.
I see the families trying to get ahead, the small business owners taking risks, the seniors who built these communities, and the next generation that deserves the same opportunities we were given.
That's why I'm fighting for District 30.
Together, we can Move Colorado Forward.
