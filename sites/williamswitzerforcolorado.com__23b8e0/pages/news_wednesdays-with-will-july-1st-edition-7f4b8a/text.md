If Colorado wants to be an example of vibrant neighborhoods with thriving small businesses and a strong arts community, why isn’t the political establishment focused on making it easier, not harder, for people to participate in local culture?
This week, an issue was brought to my attention that I take personally.
Over the weekend, Denver City Council members Sarah Parady and Flor Alvidrez proposed a new tiered tax of 5% to 15% on tickets for live events, including concerts and sporting events.
At least they are being honest enough to call it a tax this time instead of hiding behind the word "fee." The reality is simple: live entertainment is already expensive.
Families are already making difficult choices about where to spend their money.
This proposal would make it even harder for working people, young families, and retirees to enjoy a concert, attend a game, or support local entertainment.
This is another example of government deciding that taxpayers and consumers can always afford to pay a little more.
I've spent over two decades working in and advocating for the creative economy.
I've seen firsthand how artists, venues, promoters, and small businesses are struggling to survive.
The live events industry was one of the first sectors shut down during COVID and one of the last to reopen.
Full disclosure, it has yet to fully recover.
Many businesses never came back; others are still fighting to regain their footing.
Now, instead of helping the industry grow, our elected officials want to make attendance more expensive and place another burden on the businesses still fighting to keep their doors open in our arts districts.
Initial supporters argue the revenue would fund programs such as "Safe Routes to School" and "Denver Moves Everyone." Those may be worthwhile goals, but government should not treat every challenge as an excuse to create a new tax.
Denver venues are already subject to existing taxes and fees.
Rather than reforming spending priorities, city leaders continue searching for new pockets to pick in the private sector.
That's not leadership.
That's lazy governing.
I have reached out to Mayor Mike Johnston, and to his credit, he has expressed opposition to this proposal.
Given my background in the music and entertainment industries, I would welcome a continued conversation with the Mayor and his team.
Colorado needs leaders who listen and understand how business actually operates, how communities are built, and how culture contributes to economic growth.
We need fewer politicians looking for the next tax increase and more leaders focused on solving problems, not creating them.
The creative economy deserves advocates, not obstacles.
I have been, and will always be, one for the state of Colorado.
