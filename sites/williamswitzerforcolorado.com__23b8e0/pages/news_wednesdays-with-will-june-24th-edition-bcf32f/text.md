Home My Story Public Policy Goals News Connect Get Involved Join an Event Contribute News / Wednesdays With Will (June 24th Edition) 24 Jun Wednesday, 7:00 AM · 2026 Wednesdays With Will (June 24th Edition) Friends, As June 30th approaches, I ask a simple favor.
If you know who you're voting for, please return your ballot as soon as you can.
You may not know this, but campaigns can see when a ballot has been received.
Do not worry, we do not see who you voted for, but the confirmation of submission can make a big difference, especially for grassroots movements like ours.
As a candidate, I can tell you firsthand every dollar and every volunteer hour matters.
That's especially true during the final days of an election.
When you vote early, you help us use resources where they're needed most.
But this isn’t just about the allocation of resources.
For me, there's an even bigger reason to vote early: the opportunity to spend Election Day serving others.
One of the things I love most about our community is the people who step up when they're needed.
Volunteers.
Neighbors.
Friends.
By voting early, you free yourself up to do exactly that.
I urge you.
Volunteer for a local campaign.
Remind friends and family to vote.
Offer a ride to someone who needs transportation to the polls.
Spend a part of your day encouraging others to participate in our democracy.
I've always believed citizenship is about more than casting a ballot.
It's about showing up for one another.
I still remember the first time I voted.
I was just a kid.
My parents and I stood in line at our local library with everyone else.
Even at my younger age, I felt it.
It was a specific energy.
Something was about to change.
People were excited and nervous.
They were all participants in something bigger than themselves.
While my parents waited to vote, a poll worker asked if I wanted to vote too.
Arizona had a program called Kids Voting USA, where students could cast their own ballots and learn about the democratic process.
At the time, I didn't think much about it and naively voted for the candidate that shared my first name.
I was excited to participate.
That experience taught me something important.
Voting isn't just about choosing candidates.
Voting is about taking ownership of your community and your future.
Like many of you, I've cast votes over the years I later regretted.
I've supported candidates on each side of the political divide, who promised one thing on the campaign trail and delivered something entirely different in office.
I know I am not alone in feeling that frustration.
Despite the disappointment, I am not discouraged.
I still believe in the importance of voting.
Democracy only works when people participate.
Our vote is our voice.
Policies, platforms, and an individual's character matters.
Leadership is relational.
It takes trust.
It requires listening to the people you represent and putting them ahead of politics.
We vote to be represented by the best person on the ballot.
That is why I'm asking you to vote early.
Not just because it helps campaigns like mine work smarter, but because it creates an opportunity for all of us to turn Election Day into something bigger than politics.
A day of service.
A day of participation.
A day of community.
June 30th is the last day to vote in the Colorado primary election.
I want to thank you for your support throughout this campaign and ask one more favor.
If you have a friend, neighbor, or local candidate you believe in, reach out and offer to help during these final days.
Many races are won or lost because ordinary people decided to make one more phone call, knock one more door, or encourage one more voter to participate.
If you are like me and believe every vote matters, every volunteer matters, and every voice matters, let’s not only make June 30th a day of service, but a blueprint for November 3rd.
Thank you for your support, your involvement in this movement, and your commitment to Colorado.
Together, we can Move Colorado Forward. -William Switzer Home Voter Information Contribute Join an Event Connect Get Involved Privacy Policy Paid for by Committee to Elect William Switzer Registered Agent: Tyler Linnebur Powered by CampaignPartner.com - Political Campaign Websites Home My Story Public Policy Goals News Contribute Get Involved Join an Event Connect Voter Information Close Menu
