Third Major announcement: On the Campaign trail.
I want to start by saying thank you.
As I’ve continued meeting so many of you at speaking events, while canvassing neighborhoods, and through the phone calls we’ve shared, your responses and conversations have been incredibly meaningful during these early stages of my campaign.
Hearing your concerns, priorities, and frustrations firsthand was something I wanted to prioritize from day one.
I truly believe that a leader cannot properly represent a community if he or she is not actively engaged in it, listening directly to the people.
When this campaign started, we adopted two simple phrases: “Ready for Leadership that Listens.” And “Listen Before Legislating.” That should be common sense for anyone seeking elected office.
But after numerous conversations and more than a few laughs together, it’s clear that many of us feel our current leadership either missed that memo or simply no longer values public input.
I share that frustration with you, and as this campaign grows, we do not plan on changing this approach even after Election Day.
Listening to the community will remain the foundation of how I would legislate because if we cannot listen to one another, how can we possibly move forward together as a state?
At a time when political division is exhausting so many Americans, communication and human connection must become a priority again.
Unfortunately, today politics has become the place where people search for belonging and identity.
I believe our real connection should come from our communities, our neighbors, and the people living alongside us, not blind loyalty to a political party.
I believe together we can fix this.
Colorado’s problems are solvable, but it will take bipartisan solutions, honest conversations, and a new era of leadership willing to put people ahead of political agendas.
The truth is, many of our current politicians are part of the problem.
They are too disconnected from everyday life to repair the system they helped create.
Our current representative is part of that.
So how do we fix it?
This responsibility falls to us.
Together, we can replace leaders who seek more power and control with leaders who actually want to serve the people they represent.
Over these first months, my team and I have focused on laying the foundation for this campaign the right way by connecting with voters, listening carefully, and learning what matters most to the people of House District 30.
That rarely happens in modern politics because too often candidates enter races acting like they already have every answer.
I didn’t want to be just another politician contributing to the cycle.
We are choosing a different path.
We have also seen election after election, voters are left disappointed, ignored, or flat-out lied to.
We do not intend to continue this.
Our next step is putting forward practical solutions to the concerns we’ve heard directly from you and developing policies that protect the priorities of the people living in our district.
Unlike many politicians, we didn’t skip the listening part.
Very soon, we will begin rolling out those plans publicly as we finalize turning your feedback, concerns, and ideas into actionable policy proposals for our community.
That is how we intend to earn not only your vote this fall but also, more importantly, your trust.
We may not agree on every issue or every decision.
That’s okay.
Healthy communities are built through honest dialogue, not forced conformity, but there is one promise I can make to you.
I will never lie to you.
We have kept this team small, we understand the odds, and we accepted the challenge anyway.
We fully intend to flip this controversial House District 30 seat, but not for a political party.
Just like my campaign, we do things a little different, this seat will be flipped for the future of our community and our state, and we are inviting you to join us in this victory.
I hope to see many of you soon out in the district.
Until then, stay connected and follow our campaign on social media for updates and upcoming events.
