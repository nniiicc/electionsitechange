Meet Your Candidate
I’m Archie Thomas, running as an independent RINO‑CRAT in House District 86 because thoughtful Republicans and Democrats have more in common than what divides us.
Together, we make up the Hamilton community and America.
Billionaires’ dark money, lockstep media control, and political extremism have no place in Hamilton.
This is not red versus blue; it is people and community versus outside money and a dysfunctional political and government. system.
I am an orphaned Republican, filed as a Democrat for Hamilton HD86.
What is a RINO‑CRAT?
It reflects the reality that the Republican Party has left many of us.
Even reasonable voices within the party are sidelined by infighting and loyalty tests.
“Republican in Name Only” now applies more accurately to more of today’s GOP than to those who still hold broader, traditional values.
If unchecked corruption and political subversion continue, our country risks becoming a “Republic in Name Only” eliminating our democracy, Montana’s control of state government, elections and the rule of law.
I was raised as an Eisenhower Republican—fiscally conservative, socially responsible, and committed to effective governance.
It is beyond time to return normalcy to Montana government.
Montanans are sleepwalking as we lose healthcare, livelihoods, public lands, and states’ rights to modern‑day billionaire interests reminiscent of the old copper kings.
Waste, fraud, and abuse in Montana mean taxpayer dollars spent on fabricated election claims, blind adherence to national agendas, and threats to our public lands (such as Sheep Creek mine), the judicial system, Montana Constitution, and federally supported agencies.
Filing as a Democrat does not make me a Democrat rather it reflects where I can best serve the people of Hamilton—speaking honestly, reaching across the aisle, and governing competently.
It is as important to know what you support, as it is to know what you are against These views are echoed by thoughtful Republicans, Democrats, and independents alike.
For almost 50 years I made payroll while making financial ends meet to support my family.
I decided to come out of retirement to work for middle-class Montanans whose needs have for too long been overlooked.
My competence is based in my life experience not my party affiliations.
My goal is to legislate by being the most competent representative for Hamilton’s House District 86 in Helena.
- Housing and heath care are attainable
- Taxes are fair
- Education funding is a future investment
- Hamilton community values are the cure not politics
- A Hamilton business owner of 50+ years has the needed life experience
- I want to give Hamilton voters a competent choice for State representative supported on my life experience in the community.
- I believe in fiscal and social responsibility for the common good.
A dysfunctional majority party legislating culture wars, adhering to national agendas and internal fighting can not look out for Hamilton’s community needs and interests. .
- The “Montana Option” initiative limiting corporate dark money in Montana elections is in a unique position to lead the way with national implications.
It is my hope to insure it is supported by the legislature once passed by the voters.
- I believe the Montana Constitution is a “real Montana treasure” requiring protection during the upcoming review process.
- I find most residents rightly disenchanted with either party, and view party influence more of a hindrance to responsive government than a help.
- I view my responsibility to represent the interests of Hamilton residents as job one.
I was raised as an Eisenhower Republican.
With reasonable adaptation for current times, those principles remain my core values.
Simply put, that means being fiscally conservative, socially responsible, and committed to effective, limited government without interference in our daily lives.
Over the decades, however, the Republican Party has left me.
When compared to the values of an Eisenhower Republican, the phrase RINO, “Republican in Name Only,” now applies far more entirely to the modern GOP than to those of us who still hold those broader values.
Due to Montana state law, I cannot run as an independent because of precinct work; therefore, I am filed as a Democrat.
I believe in focused, effective governance limited to actions best undertaken for the common good, as defined by the Constitution, and completed without the costly inefficiencies introduced by extremist culture wars.
Millions of Montana taxpayer dollars are being spent by the State defending unconstitutionally enacted laws, fighting citizen initiatives, and addressing so‑called election problems that do not exist.
This is Montana‑style fraud, waste, and abuse wrapped in cross‑clutching moralism and culture wars.
This extremism has no place in the “live‑and‑let‑live” Montana culture of the past, nor does it enhance why residents live in Hamilton, Montana.
- I am a second‑generation Montanan, raised on a farm/cow agricultural unit.
I moved to Hamilton in 1965, graduated from Hamilton High School, attended the University of Montana, and was licensed separately as a master electrician and master plumber for more than 40 years.
- I completed Sanitary Engineering curriculum, E‑Myth private business certification, Leadership Bitter Root, and ongoing multiple educational classes in business, construction, leadership, and related disciplines.
- I am blessed with the support of an independent‑minded wife, Merry Schrumpf, of 49 years, and Cody and Lindsay—two educated, enlightened, and locally employed professional adult children.
- In my 47‑year business career, I owned and/or managed an electrical/mechanical corporation, a refrigeration business, a water treatment business, and a small commercial real estate LLC.
I learned to navigate1970’s-80’s double‑digit inflation, multiple recessions including the 2008 mortgage crisis.
- During this time, among many lesser projects, I had total project control or was instrumental in the construction of a pathological waste incinerator; 1,000 KW backup generators and electrical services; and 100‑foot boiler‑plant chimney replacement and temporary steam plant for Rocky Mountain Laboratories.
My company also completed 3 projects in the Three Mile area totaling more than 20 miles of gravity‑flow irrigation systems.
- My final business of 25 years installed multiple residential, industrial, hospital water‑treatment and purification systems.During this period, I owned a retail electrical and plumbing store and developed a mini‑mall in Hamilton.

The Montana State tax system must be reviewed to insure that the highest to the lowest income Montanan are paying their fair share.
- The federal pressure on the State DEQ to fast track Sheep Creek mine with current MOU(5/5/2026) and any future federal pressures must be stopped.
- A I data centers must be completely transparent projecting consequences of their construction, operation , community and state costs prior to beginning construction.
The State tax structure for these faculties must be reviewed to insure tax structure will support the total cost to Montana tax payers
- A perfect tax storm is brewing in the next legislative session.
Historically, Montana’s tax base relied on a natural‑resource extraction property‑tax system.
In addition, Montana has typically received approximately $1.40 from the federal government for every $1 paid in federal taxes by Montanans.
The federal government’s “One Big Beautiful Bill” is expected to reduce this cash inflow, resulting in an estimated $300‑million shortfall in the next legislative budget.
- Today, Montana is no longer dominated by natural‑resource extraction industries, and the associated industrial property‑tax revenue has been significantly reduced or eliminated.
As Montana’s economy has shifted to a service‑based model, the state is left with a revenue structure that is both unworkable and unfair for the average Montanan.
I believe I can contribute meaningfully to help the state navigate its way out of this tax storm.
- I have the advantage of having lived through where Montana has been, and I have a unique appreciation for current economic growth—growth that should enable the average Montanan to afford a home, access healthcare, educate their children, and, with a little luck, still have the time and money to enjoy Montana’s public lands and natural beauty.
- We do not need to relive the past, but we must be informed by it.
There is little difference between the Anaconda Company, which dominated Montana until the late 1960s, and modern forces such as A I Centers or the Yellowstone Club.
The key is separating culture‑war divisions manufactured by billionaires—divisions that allow them to keep the wheat while convincing average Montanans to keep fighting over the chaff.
- I have been an owner, manager or workman in several small businesses in Hamilton for over the last 40 plus years.
- I am a retired small businessman not an ideologue or a party hack.
I know how to listen, develop compromises, and I am constantly surprised by what can fall into place with an open mind.
My opponent regardless of how well meaning— is entangled and hampered by loyalty oaths, conservative culture wars, and party drama rather than focused on legislation for Hamilton
- At present, the national, state, and local Republican Party operates more like a back‑alley dogfight than a political organization.
I will work to insure that national government dysfunction and chaos is not pushed downward into Montana.
- This is not to say Democrats are a stellar example of organized governance, but without their willingness to compromise in the last session, Republicans would still be scratching each other’s eyes out over an unbalanced budget.

That filing does not make me a tax and spend Democrat—it reflects the reality of where I can stand, speak honestly, reach across the aisle, compromise, and serve the people of Hamilton.
