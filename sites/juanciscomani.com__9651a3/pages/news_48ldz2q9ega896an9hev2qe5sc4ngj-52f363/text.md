NEW AD: Local Mayors and Leaders Highlight Ciscomani's Record of Results in Every Corner of Arizona’s Sixth District
TUCSON - Local leaders from across Arizona's Sixth Congressional District come together to tout Congressman Juan Ciscomani’s record of results for the district in a new 30-second ad.
The ad features Thatcher Mayor Jenny Howard, Sahuarita Mayor Tom Murphy, Eloy Mayor Andy Sutton, Graham County Supervisor John Howard, and former Casa Grande Mayor Craig McFarland.
Representing communities from one end of AZ-06 to the other, these leaders have firsthand experience working with Ciscomani to deliver on local priorities.
"I've made it my mission to show up for every corner of this district, and no one knows these communities better than the people who live and work in them," said Congressman Juan Ciscomani.
"That's why I turn to our local mayors, supervisors, and leaders to find out what they need and deliver results from the ground up.
I couldn't do this job without them, and I'm grateful for their support.
I'll keep working hard, keep delivering, and keep proving that being from AZ-06 means fighting for AZ-06."
Ciscomani has earned broad, bipartisan support by working alongside members on both sides of the aisle to get things done, not just because it's a job, but because he is a member of the community.
That approach has made him Arizona's most effective member of Congress, ranked first in the state by both the nonpartisan Center for Effective Lawmaking and Bridge Grades.
The ad highlights more than $9 million in direct investments Ciscomani has delivered to communities across the district, including $1 million to repair local roads, more than $5 million for parks, recreation, and public safety, and $3 million for schools, child care, and job training.
Those investments are part of Ciscomani’s broader record of delivering more than $61 million in Community Project Funding across all five counties in AZ-06 during his time in Congress.
With five local leaders from five different communities speaking with one voice, the ad makes clear that Ciscomani's record isn't measured by Washington headlines but by results at home.
