The first is the criminal network : pedophiles, child predators, human traffickers, and those who knowingly facilitate, conceal, or profit from these crimes.
The Jeffrey Epstein case exposed how wealth, influence, and institutional failures can allow horrific crimes against children to continue for years while victims are ignored.
The second is the influence network : individuals, corporations, lobbyists, institutions, and special interests that use extraordinary wealth and political power to shape laws, regulations, public policy, and public opinion for their own benefit.
While exercising influence is not inherently criminal, democracy is threatened when money and access become more powerful than the voice of ordinary citizens.
These systems are distinct, but they share a common feature: the belief that power can purchase immunity from accountability.
One corrupts justice through criminal acts; the other can corrupt democracy by concentrating economic and political power in the hands of a privileged few.
The solution requires addressing both.
We must relentlessly investigate and prosecute child exploitation, trafficking, and every person who knowingly enables those crimes.
At the same time, we must strengthen ethics laws, increase transparency, limit corruption, reduce the undue influence of concentrated wealth in government, and ensure that every person and institution is accountable under the same laws.
No child should be exploited.
No public office should be for sale.
No one should be above the law.
