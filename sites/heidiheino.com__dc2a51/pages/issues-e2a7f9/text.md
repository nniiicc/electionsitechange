Protect Taxpayers.
Apply the proven anti-fraud rules of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act to ALL organizations receiving taxpayer dollars.
One of my top priorities is to put an end to governmental fraud and protect the investment taxpayers have made into the great state of Minnesota.
The recent fraud cases we’ve heard so much about recently weren’t accidents.
They involved retaliation against whistleblowers and failure to investigate.
That’s not incompetence, it is a system that is broken and easily skirted.
My plan is to apply the rules set forth in the Sarbanes-Oxley Act to ALL entities receiving taxpayer dollars.
In short, the Sarbanes-Oxley Act was signed into law in July 2002, in the wake of the Enron scandal.
One of the strongest anti-fraud laws ever, Sarbanes-Oxley prevents hidden fraud and fake accounting, requires independent, strict audits, demands proof for all spending, and blocks misuse of funds and insider deals.
This resulted in restored trust in financial reporting that even private companies follow to this day.
What does this have to do with governmental fraud?
Government and taxpayer-funded organizations are exempt from the rules set forth in Sarbanes-Oxley, leaving the door open to fraud, waste, and abuse.
Fight to pass these proven anti-fraud protections Hold officials accountable Stop fraud before it starts We already know this works.
It’s time to protect taxpayers.
02 💵 Lowering the Cost of Living & Protecting Homeowners Common-sense government to reduce everyday expenses and protect families.
Lowering the cost of living starts with common-sense government.
I support reducing unnecessary and duplicative regulations that drive up everyday expenses, keeping taxes reasonable and predictable for families and small businesses, and ensuring government spending is efficient, transparent, and delivers real, measurable value for Minnesotans.
Families across Minnesota are feeling the strain of rising housing costs, property taxes, energy prices, healthcare expenses, and basic necessities.
Addressing these pressures requires practical solutions, not more bureaucracy.
By reviewing regulations through the lens of affordability, supporting responsible economic growth, and demanding accountability for how taxpayer dollars are spent, we can help families keep more of what they earn while strengthening Minnesota's long-term economic stability.
Minnesota also needs tax policies that protect seniors and encourage growth.
Seniors on fixed incomes should not be taxed out of homes they've already paid for, and our tax system should make it easier for businesses to invest, grow, and create jobs here — not push them elsewhere.
03 🏛️ Responsible & Accountable Government Government exists to serve the people, not the other way around.
Government exists to serve the people, not the other way around.
Minnesotans deserve a system that uses taxpayer dollars responsibly, operates with transparency, and stays within clearly defined limits.
That starts with stronger financial oversight, regular performance reviews of state programs, and clear reporting so the public can see how funds are being used.
Waste, fraud, and abuse should be identified early and addressed quickly.
Agencies should be required to demonstrate measurable outcomes, not just increased spending.
Accountability also means respecting the proper role of government.
Policies should be carefully evaluated to ensure they are necessary, effective, and do not impose burdens on individuals, small businesses, or local communities.
Restoring trust in government requires clear rules, ethical leadership, and a commitment to stewardship of the public's resources.
A responsible government is transparent in its decisions, disciplined in its spending, and focused on delivering real results for the people it serves.
04 🌾 Midwestern Values, Accountability & Small Business Compassion paired with responsibility — fighting fraud and supporting small businesses.
Minnesota has a long tradition of caring for neighbors in need, and that compassion is something worth protecting.
But support programs only work when they are paired with accountability, integrity, and respect for the taxpayers and small businesses who fund them.
When fraud is allowed to take root, it undermines public trust and diverts resources away from the people and communities these programs are meant to serve.
My signature proposal is the Minnesota Integrity in Government Act.
It applies the same accountability standards public companies have used for twenty years, the Sarbanes-Oxley principles, to every state agency and every nonprofit that receives taxpayer dollars.
It works in three steps: PREVENT, every recipient must have a fraud-prevention plan on file before a dollar is spent; CERTIFY, agency heads personally sign off that controls are working, or face consequences; and EXPOSE, every audit failure is posted publicly within thirty days, with whistleblowers protected and rewarded.
The idea is simple: stop fraud before the check is cut, instead of investigating after the money is already gone.
Every dollar lost to fraud is a dollar that could have stayed with District 36 families in lower costs and property-tax relief.
Many Minnesotans are increasingly concerned that recent policy decisions coming out of state government are making it harder, not easier, for families and small businesses to succeed.
Hard-working business owners expect common-sense leadership: policies that are clear, predictable, and fair, not layers of unnecessary regulation, bureaucracy, or compliance costs that stifle growth and innovation.
Midwestern values mean compassion paired with responsibility.
They mean supporting small businesses as engines of opportunity, cracking down on fraud and abuse, and ensuring government programs are managed responsibly.
Our policies should reflect fairness, honesty, and respect for work, shaped by the realities of Minnesota communities, not political trends imported from elsewhere.
Minnesota can care for those who need help and create an environment where small businesses can grow, hire, and thrive.
That balance is essential to restoring trust and strengthening our state for the long term.
05 🗳️ Election Integrity & Trust Confidence in elections is essential to a healthy democracy.
Confidence in elections is essential to a healthy democracy.
Regardless of party affiliation, Minnesotans should know that their vote is secure, accurately counted, and handled through clear and consistent procedures.
Strengthening election integrity means maintaining transparent processes, strong chain-of-custody standards, and clear guidelines that are applied consistently across the state.
Bipartisan oversight at every stage of the process helps ensure accountability and builds public confidence.
Independent audits and regular reviews can further confirm that systems are working as intended.
It is also important that election laws are communicated clearly so voters understand how, when, and where to vote.
When procedures are transparent and consistently followed, trust grows.
The goal is not to make voting harder, but to make the system stronger, more secure, and worthy of the public's confidence.
Free and fair elections are the foundation of representative government, and protecting their integrity protects the voice of every voter.
06 📈 Growing the Economy & Keeping Jobs in Minnesota A strong economy is the foundation for opportunity and long-term prosperity.
A strong economy is the foundation for opportunity, thriving communities, and long-term prosperity.
Minnesota must be a place where businesses want to grow, invest, and create jobs, not a state they feel pressured to leave.
Tax and regulatory policies should be competitive with neighboring states so businesses are not driven across the river in search of a more predictable and affordable environment.
When companies relocate or expand elsewhere, Minnesota loses jobs, families lose opportunities, and communities lose part of their economic base.
Workforce development and education are important, but training alone is not enough if there are no good jobs available here at home.
Minnesota needs an economic climate that supports small businesses, encourages entrepreneurship, and attracts new employers in a range of industries.
That means stable tax policy, reasonable regulations, and a government that views businesses as partners in building strong communities.
When businesses grow in Minnesota, wages rise, families thrive, and young people are more likely to stay and build their futures here.
A healthy economy ensures that opportunity is not something people have to leave the state to find.
07 👴 Supporting Seniors Helping seniors age with dignity and security in the communities they built.
Minnesota should be a place where seniors can age with dignity, stability, and security in the communities they helped build.
For many older residents living on fixed incomes, rising property taxes and living costs are creating real financial strain and uncertainty.
Seniors should not be forced out of their homes because they cannot keep up with increasing property tax bills.
Expanding targeted property tax relief, strengthening homestead protections, and improving tax-deferral options can help ensure that longtime residents can remain in their homes and neighborhoods.
Clear communication about assistance programs is also essential so seniors know what support is available to them.
Supporting seniors also means looking beyond taxes.
Policies should promote access to affordable healthcare, reliable transportation, and community-based services that help older adults remain independent for as long as possible.
Protecting seniors is about preserving stability, respecting their contributions, and ensuring they are not left behind as costs rise.
A strong Minnesota honors the generation that built our communities by making it possible for them to stay in their homes and live with security and dignity.
08 🛡️ Public Safety Safe communities are the foundation of a strong Minnesota.
Safe communities are the foundation of a strong Minnesota.
Families should feel secure in their neighborhoods, businesses should be protected, and law enforcement should have the tools and support needed to do their jobs effectively.
Public safety policies should prioritize preventing crime, holding offenders accountable, and ensuring that victims are not forgotten.
At the same time, communities benefit when law enforcement, local leaders, and residents work together to build trust and long-term solutions.
Minnesota must support effective policing, strengthen local partnerships, and focus on practical strategies that reduce crime and improve safety for everyone.
09 📚 Education Trust teachers, reduce testing, and prepare students for real life.
The education of our children is one of the most important responsibilities of government.
Yet too often, schools are constrained by excessive standardized testing and rigid mandates that limit teachers' ability to do what they do best: teach.
Teachers should be trusted as professionals and given greater flexibility to assess what a child has learned and is capable of doing after a year in their classroom.
Reducing the emphasis on standardized testing would restore valuable instructional time and allow teachers to focus on meaningful learning rather than test preparation.
Education policy should also better reflect how children learn and develop.
Providing more opportunities for movement, physical activity, and hands-on learning, especially for younger students, can improve classroom behavior, support mental health, and help address rising rates of childhood obesity.
Less time sitting still and more active engagement leads to healthier, more focused students.
Classrooms should be places of learning, not political battlegrounds.
Schools should focus on teaching students how to think, through strong academics, critical thinking, and respectful discussion, rather than promoting specific political or ideological viewpoints.
Parents should be partners in education, and schools should remain focused on preparing students for success in life, not shaping their political beliefs.
Finally, Minnesota should actively promote apprenticeships, job shadowing, and career-pathway programs in partnership with local businesses and skilled trades.
Expanding these opportunities gives students real-world experience, creates multiple paths to success, and helps reduce reliance on student debt after graduation.
An education system that values teachers, respects how children grow and learn, keeps classrooms focused on academics over ideology, and prepares students for real life is essential to Minnesota's long-term success.
10 🔒 Online Safety Protecting our children in the digital age while preserving free expression.
As a parent and a member of this community, I understand the importance of ensuring our children's safety, especially in today's digital age.
Protecting children is one of my top priorities.
The internet offers countless opportunities for learning and growth but also presents risks, particularly regarding social media and adult content.
As your representative, I am committed to implementing measures to safeguard our children online.
Limiting Access to Social Media: I believe in implementing measures to restrict children's access to social media platforms that may expose them to harmful content or interactions.
By working with technology companies and stakeholders, we can ensure that age-appropriate safeguards are in place to protect our children while they navigate the online world.
Creating a Safe Online Environment: It's crucial to create a safe online environment where children can explore and learn without fear of encountering inappropriate material.
I will advocate for developing educational programs and resources to teach children about online safety and responsible internet usage.
Age Verification for Adult Content: To address the proliferation of adult content on the internet, I support the creation of a separate domain for adult-only content where age verification is required.
By implementing robust age verification measures, we can ensure that minors are protected from exposure to pornography and other explicit material.
11 🧠 Mental Health & Well-Being Long-term, practical solutions for mental health challenges facing all Minnesotans.
Mental health challenges affect Minnesotans of all ages.
In 2023, more than half of young adults reported experiencing depression, anxiety, or loneliness, troubling signal of broader pressures facing individuals, families, and communities.
The COVID-19 pandemic intensified mental health struggles across all age groups.
Social isolation, economic uncertainty, disrupted routines, and loss contributed to increased anxiety and depression.
At the same time, access to care became more difficult, with long wait times for therapy, limited provider availability, and uneven access across communities.
Children and adolescents face growing pressure from multiple directions: academic expectations, social dynamics, and constant exposure to digital media.
Social media often promotes unrealistic standards of appearance, success, and happiness, contributing to stress, anxiety, and low self-esteem at increasingly younger ages.
Schools play an important role in supporting student well-being.
Creating more opportunities for movement, physical activity, and outdoor time during the school day can improve focus, behavior, and mental health.
At the same time, we should be thoughtful about how much screen time children are exposed to in educational settings and encourage balanced approaches that support learning without overreliance on devices.
Addressing mental health requires long-term, practical solutions, not slogans.
That means improving access to care, reducing barriers for families, supporting early intervention, encouraging healthy routines, and ensuring policies reflect the realities people are facing today.
12 🏥 Healthcare Transparency Clear pricing information so families can make informed healthcare decisions.
While Minnesota boasts excellent healthcare options, there's still work to be done to ensure transparency in healthcare pricing.
It's essential that individuals have access to clear and comprehensive information about healthcare costs to make informed decisions about their care.
Costs should be clearly listed on health plan websites for greater transparency in healthcare pricing.
This empowers plan members to compare prices and make informed choices when seeking care.
By providing pricing, providers will have to justify costs and compete for business.
13 ⚡ Energy & Infrastructure Reliable, affordable energy for Minnesota's economy and families.
Reliable, affordable energy is critical to Minnesota's economy, families, and long-term environmental stewardship.
Our state needs an energy strategy rooted in both innovation and realism, one that keeps costs manageable while ensuring a stable power supply year-round.
Minnesota should pursue a balanced energy portfolio that includes renewables, conservation, and advanced nuclear energy.
Modern nuclear power is one of the most reliable, low-emission, and land-efficient sources of electricity available.
It produces consistent, carbon-free power regardless of weather conditions and can help stabilize the grid as more intermittent energy sources come online.
Investing in next-generation nuclear technology, grid modernization, and domestic energy infrastructure will strengthen energy independence, support high-skilled jobs, and reduce long-term environmental impact.
A responsible energy policy must prioritize reliability, affordability, and sustainability, ensuring Minnesota families and businesses have dependable power today while protecting natural resources for future generations.
