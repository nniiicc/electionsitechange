Georgia's General Assembly, also known as the "Gold Dome," has its own vocabulary to describe legislative processes.
Here's a guide tos ome of the common terms, along with some additional helpful information.
Mapmaking and Voting Rights Redistricting: The process of redrawing the boundaries of congressional and state legislative districts in Georgia.
This process determines which voters are grouped together to elect each representative.
Gerrymandering: The practice of drawing district lines specifically to give one political party an unfair advantage, enabling it to win more legislative seats relative to its overall share of the vote.
Voting Rights Act: The 1965 federal civil rights law enacted to protect minority voters from discrimination, including prohibiting electoral maps that dilute their voting power.
Louisiana v.
Callais : An April 2026 Supreme Court decision that gutted the remaining protections under the Voting Rights Act against discriminatory redistricting maps.
Legislative Sessions and Authority Special Session: A meeting of the Georgia legislature called by the governor outside the standard January-to-April regular session.
A special session is strictly limited to the topics specified by the governor on the agenda, known as "the call." Lame-Duck Session: A legislative session held after an election but before newly elected officials take office.
During this period, outgoing officials can act on legislation without facing immediate accountability from voters.
Veto Override: The constitutional mechanism by which the Georgia General Assembly passes a bill into law over a governor's veto.
To successfully override a veto, a two-thirds vote is required in both the Georgia House of Representatives and the State Senate.
