District 71 is growing fast, and growth keeps outrunning the roads, drainage, schools, and emergency services meant to support it.
Ocean's approach starts from one idea: the state should set a floor that protects every community, and local governments should be free to go further when their own conditions call for it.
Growth That Pays For Itself As Ocean sees it, Manatee County's real challenge with growth is sequencing.
New rooftops keep going up before the roads, bridges, drainage, water, and sewer capacity, and schools around them are ready to carry the load.
Ocean supports impact fees and other mechanisms that make new development pay its fair share of required infrastructure, instead of asking current residents to cover the difference later.
Ocean welcomes growth but wants to be smarter about where it goes.
He supports directing new development to areas with existing infrastructure, through infill, redevelopment, and mixed-use projects, rather than pushing pipes and pavement further out.
Transportation planning requires that same strategic foresight.
A wider bridge between Bradenton and Palmetto solves nothing if traffic simply bottlenecks in downtown Bradenton on the other side.
Ocean supports network-wide planning, expanding public transportation and regional rail as viable alternatives to driving, and treating safe sidewalks, crossings, and protected bike lanes as core transportation infrastructure.
Protecting Local Control Ocean opposes SB 180's restrictions on local land-use authority following natural disasters.
He believes statewide rules should establish a baseline standard every community can count on, while leaving cities and counties free to adopt stronger protections when local conditions demand it.
That same principle drives his opposition to the proposed cruise terminal near Terra Ceia and the Sunshine Skyway.
Ocean has no issue with the cruise industry itself.
His concern is the environmentally destructive dredging and filling required in and around the Terra Ceia Aquatic Preserve (one of our region's most vital natural buffers), and he supports keeping those protections strong.
More broadly, Ocean believes Tallahassee should establish necessary state standards and then step aside.
Local governments are closest to their communities' needs and must retain the authority to address them.
Ready Before The Storm Florida has a habit of paying for disaster recovery instead of investing in disaster prevention.
Ocean wants to flip that priority: stronger stormwater and flood-resilience investment, drainage improvements, and protection for the wetlands and natural systems that absorb storm impact before it reaches our neighborhoods.
On the ground, that means expanding home hardening programs, offering free or low-cost resilience inspections, providing need-based mitigation grants, making affordable financing accessible for everyone else, and guaranteeing insurance discounts that reflect the real risk a homeowner has reduced.
Protecting Natural Florida Ocean sees Florida's wetlands, aquifers, waterways, and wildlife habitats as vital infrastructure that protects water quality, absorbs stormwater, and keeps our state livable.
He supports giving the Department of Environmental Protection, water management districts, and wildlife regulators the staff and authority needed to enforce the protections already on the books.
Ocean also advocates for closer oversight of phosphate mining so extraction costs are not passed on to local communities, alongside decisive action against runoff pollution entering Florida's waterways.
Furthermore, Ocean opposes fracking and expanded oil and gas drilling in and around Florida, including offshore sites and areas near the Everglades.
He supports replacing pre-harvest sugar burning with cleaner harvesting methods, restoring the public's customary access to Florida's beaches, and rebuilding the independent statewide review process that once caught high-impact developments before they broke ground.
Energy And The Honest Answer On Data Centers Ocean supports clean energy and expanding solar power across Florida, from rooftops and parking lots to grid storage systems that protect against power outages during major storms.
On AI data centers, Ocean believes policy must be guided by facts, not slogans.
Before deciding how to regulate these facilities, he wants full transparency regarding water consumption, electricity demand, cooling methods, land usage, infrastructure expenses, and verified local economic benefits versus projections.
That is why he supports mandatory disclosure requirements for major data centers and remains open to tiered water-pricing structures for high-volume consumers, ensuring the financial weight of extraordinary demand does not fall on everyday water bills.
For Ocean, evidence comes first and policy follows.
Third Spaces And Schools That Serve The Whole Community Ocean speaks from experience on this issue: young people and seniors alike need places to spend time without having to spend money.
Community, connection, and simply having somewhere to gather are vital for mental health and belonging, but District 71 lacks sufficient community spaces.
One idea Ocean continues to advocate for is utilizing schools as public community infrastructure.
Keeping school facilities accessible beyond the standard day allows them to host tutoring, clubs, recreation, and local programs, giving these buildings a valuable second life for the surrounding neighborhood.
That principle connects directly to his overall vision for education.
Ocean supports strong, well-funded traditional public schools with qualified teachers, dedicated counselors, and manageable class sizes.
He views continuous access to education, from kindergarten through college or technical training, as core infrastructure that prevents social and economic problems before they begin.
Ocean opposes expanding private school vouchers with public tax dollars.
For charter schools, which operate as public institutions under a different model, he supports meaningful accountability standards.
Additionally, Ocean opposes book bans and political censorship in classrooms, opposes arming school personnel through the Guardian or Marshal programs in favor of trained professionals and evidence-based safety strategies, and believes education should empower capable, independent thinkers prepared to shape their own futures.
Accountability ⚖️ P ower has to be transparent, restrained, and answerable to the people it affects.
District 71 deserves a dedicated representative, accessible government, and a political system that serves all constituents rather than special interests.
A Full-Time Job For A Full-Time District Ocean is running to make representing House District 71 his full-time priority, with no private legal practice or side business competing for his time.
He views this as a commitment to accessibility rather than a critique of other candidates: constituents deserve a representative whose attention is fully devoted to them.
Florida's base legislative salary is $29,697 a year, a level unchanged since 2010.
Florida built its Legislature around the model of a part-time citizen assembly, but the state has outgrown that approach.
Florida is the third-largest state in the nation, featuring a complex economy, rapidly growing communities, pressing infrastructure needs, an insurance crisis, and a state budget exceeding $117 billion.
Governing a state of this scale should not be managed as a part-time job.
Getting Personal Wealth Out Of The Way Of Public Service Ordinary donors face strict contribution limits, while self-funded candidates generally do not.
Ocean Fitts views this disparity as a structural defect in political access rather than a reflection of individual motives, and he supports comprehensive campaign finance reform to curb the influence of personal wealth, mega-donors, and outside spending in Florida elections.
To back up this principle, Ocean has established strict fundraising standards for his own campaign.
He has pledged to refuse contributions from large insurance interests, utilities, fossil fuel interests, Big Sugar, Big Ag, Big Pharma, Big Banks, payday lenders, the NRA, AIPAC, and organizations advocating for the privatization of essential public services.
A Legislature That Works For Voters, Not Around Them Government shouldn't require insider knowledge to navigate.
Ocean Fitts supports making it easier to run for office, contact representatives, track legislation and state spending, and demand full disclosure of legislators' financial conflicts of interest.
He supports Fair Districts to end partisan gerrymandering, closing the write-in loophole in open primaries, and protecting citizen ballot initiatives.
Ocean defends home rule, believing Tallahassee should establish statewide standards while allowing local governments to manage local responsibilities.
He also supports repealing legislative term limits so voters decide who remains in office.
Above all, Ocean believes public authority must serve progress and remain accountable to the community.
His platform includes: Civil Rights and Personal Freedom: Ratifying the Equal Rights Amendment; protecting marriage equality and LGBTQ rights (including adoption access, healthcare, inclusive education, and ending conversion therapy); opposing restrictive abortion bans; legalizing recreational cannabis; and protecting workers from discipline for off-duty, legal cannabis use.
Privacy and Immigration: Safeguarding medical privacy; regulating government surveillance and AI technology; and separating civil immigration enforcement from local law enforcement so every resident can safely report crimes.
Public Safety and Justice: Establishing independent civilian oversight for law enforcement; reducing police militarization; deploying specialized responders for non-violent crises; enacting comprehensive gun safety measures (universal background checks, waiting periods, safe storage requirements, and limits on high-capacity magazines and assault weapons); repealing permitless carry and Stand Your Ground laws; opposing the death penalty; and restoring voting rights to individuals who have completed their sentences.
Affordability 💵 Judge a policy by the total cost of a stable life, not by whether one bill looks smaller in isolation.
Housing, insurance, healthcare, transportation, wages, and taxes add up in the same household budget.
Ocean’s affordability agenda treats them that way, because lowering one bill while quietly raising another does not create real affordability.
Fixing Florida's Property Insurance Crisis For many District 71 families, skyrocketing homeowners insurance bills are the defining element of the local affordability crisis.
Ocean Fitts supports enforceable claim timelines backed by adequate staffing and meaningful consequences, alongside an accessible mediation process so homeowners can resolve legitimate disputes without being forced into costly litigation.
Recognizing that accountability requires strong oversight, Ocean supports fully funding Florida's insurance regulation agencies.
To reward proactive risk reduction, he advocates for free or affordable resilience inspections, need-based home-hardening grants, accessible financing options, and required insurance discounts that accurately reflect a homeowner's mitigation efforts.
A Full-Time Job Should Be Enough Ocean Fitts operates on a simple principle: anyone working full time, or patching together multiple part-time jobs, should not be left without coverage or forced into financial ruin by medical bills.
He treats healthcare as an essential component of economic affordability rather than a secondary issue.
Believing that healthcare is a fundamental human right, Ocean supports expanding Medicaid and views universal coverage as Florida's ultimate goal.
While he is on record supporting Medicare for All as a long-term national direction, he remains focused on concrete, achievable steps at the state level.
Ocean advocates for reforming Florida's wrongful death statute, commonly known as the "Free Kill" law, so families are not barred from seeking justice in medical malpractice cases based on an adult victim's age or marital status.
An Economy That Actually Works For Working People Ocean supports wages that reflect the actual cost of living, alongside stronger protections for Florida workers.
He defends the right to organize and bargain collectively, while opposing state policies designed to weaken unions or restrict dues collection.
He also advocates for paid family, medical, and sick leave, paid vacation, and a stronger statewide floor for workplace protections.
Ocean wants to reform Florida's unemployment system so it functions effectively, supporting higher benefit amounts, longer benefit periods, streamlined work-search requirements, improved access to legal assistance, and a reliable application process.
Additionally, he supports protecting retirement security for public employees and expanding access to legal representation for workers pursuing legitimate workers' compensation claims.
Housing People Can Actually Afford Ocean supports expanding attainable housing, but opposes an "anything, anywhere" approach.
Location, development type, and true affordability are essential.
He advocates for mixed-use and infill housing near jobs, transit, and essential services to lower housing and transportation costs simultaneously.
He also supports stronger affordable and workforce housing mandates for major developments receiving public support or approvals.
Ocean is concerned about bulk corporate ownership crowding out individual buyers and large build-to-rent projects converting entire communities into long-term investment portfolios.
To address this, he supports evaluating limits on corporate home purchases and implementing policies that keep homeownership accessible for residents who intend to live in the community.
Regarding short-term rentals, Ocean distinguishes between residents occasionally renting their primary homes and investors operating commercial lodging businesses across multiple properties.
He supports restoring local authority so communities can effectively manage their own housing supply, tourism intensity, and infrastructure needs.
Fair costs, fairly shared Ocean views public transit and safe, connected streets as critical affordability issues.
Cheap housing far from jobs and services quickly loses its value once long commutes and extra vehicle costs are factored in.
He advocates for fairness in utility rates, supporting measures to ensure everyday customers are not quietly subsidizing the massive water, electricity, and infrastructure demands of major industrial users.
Additionally, Ocean opposes tax incentives and corporate subsidies that fail to deliver a clear, demonstrated benefit to the public.
