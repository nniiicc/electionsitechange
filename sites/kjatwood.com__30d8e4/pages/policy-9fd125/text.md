Home Policy About Volunteer Donate POLICY KJ Atwood for South Carolina House District 110 Every sunny day, parts of Charleston flood.
Every summer, the drive from James Island or Mount Pleasant onto the Peninsula gets a little longer.
Every year, another family gets priced out of the neighborhood they grew up in, and another surprise medical bill lands on the kitchen table, crushing a family who just wanted to see someone they love get better.
Meanwhile, Columbia hands blank checks to giant corporations and utility monopolies, and sends Lowcountry families the bill.
I'm KJ Atwood.
My wife Caitlyn and I run a small business here in Charleston.
We're raising our daughter here.
And I've spent my career in public health and technology, helping organizations solve problems instead of just talking about them.
I'm running for South Carolina House District 110 because our community deserves a representative who knows what it's like to make payroll, to lose family time to a commute that keeps getting longer, and to brace for the next rate hike our elected officials wave through.
We need representatives who go to Columbia and fight for real, specific fixes, not legacy politicians feeding us legacy talking points.
Here's what I'll fight for in the State House: safe roads and dry streets, relief from the cost-of-living crisis, an end to our politician’s corruption and secrecy, a protected coast, technology that serves our communities instead of exploiting them, and schools and families that get real support.
More details below.
Policy Points Infrastructure Fix what's broken, safely, and for everyone.
Drain Our Streets Charleston can flood even on a sunny day.
Downtown saw about 51 tidal flood events in 2025, nearly one every week (Post and Courier / NOAA).
In 1950 we flooded roughly 2 days a year.
We must act now: fund the drainage, pump, and check-valve upgrades our streets need, require new development to prove it won't flood the neighbors, and make sure resilience dollars reach our neighborhoods whose homes flood first and too often get help last.
Safer Roads, Stronger Bridges South Carolina has some of the most dangerous roads in America.
Too many people are losing their lives, too many bridges are in poor condition, and too many families are spending more of their day sitting in traffic.
Cheap roads that are unsafe are not a bargain, and doubling your commute with inaction is not a sign of success.
Our government has a responsibility to get the basics right.
We need to invest in safer roads, stronger bridges, and infrastructure that keeps people moving and gets them home safely.
South Carolinians deserve a transportation system that works as well as they do.
The Port of Charleston is one of the fastest-growing container ports in the country and a real engine for jobs in this district, but the roads and rail that connect it to the rest of the state haven't kept up with its growth.
We can invest in that freight infrastructure so the port keeps creating jobs without dumping more truck traffic onto neighborhood streets.
Your Commute Shouldn’t Keep Doubling Sitting gridlocked on Folly Road, or Highway 17, crawling along Coleman Boulevard, or fighting bottleneck traffic near the crosstown,costs you time, safety, and gas money.
Too often, Columbia decides where road dollars go based on political influence rather than public need.
We can change that: reform how the State Infrastructure Bank and gas-tax dollars are handed out so projects are ranked more appropriately.
Charleston is a donor county to the rest of the state, and people come here from all over the world to live and visit.
Our public infrastructure needs to keep pace with growth.
Our leaders need to look and plan ahead, and growth needs to pay for its own infrastructure.
Cost of Living Stop the squeeze on working families.
No More Surprise Bills No family should get hit with a $100,000 surprise bill because someone they love needed medical care.You'd never buy a car without knowing the price.
You shouldn't have to gamble on your health the same way.
Instead, South Carolina has one of the highest rates of medical debt in the country.
Too many families are forced to choose between getting the care they need and protecting their financial future.
We can start fixing this at the state level.
Homes for Families, Not Hedge Funds A home should be built for a family, not a hedge fund.
Across the Lowcountry, corporate landlords and out-of-state investors are buying up single-family homes in bulk, often paying cash, often outbidding local families who need a mortgage to compete, and pricing longtime residents out of the neighborhoods they grew up in.
We can do something about this: require large-scale buyers to register with the state and disclose their holdings, give individual homebuyers a fair first shot by putting a short waiting period on newly listed homes before institutional investors can bid.
Real Tax Relief for Families Do not be fooled by a "tax cut" targeted for people who need one the least.
Working South Carolinians already pay the highest overall state and local tax rate of anyone in SC, the bottom 20% of earners pay 10.1% of their income in taxes, while the top 1% pay just 6.5% (ITEP).
The income-tax plan Columbia just passed makes that worse: once fully phased in, the top 1% would get an average cut of about +$50,000 a year while 25% of earners in SC get no relief, or actually see a tax increase.
We shouldn’t raise taxes on everyday South Carolinians to fund tax cuts for the 1%.
Under the current plan, South Carolina will lose billions that could otherwise go to fund schools, roads, and healthcare (ITEP analysis of H.4216).
Tackle the Insurance Squeeze Here on the coast, families are paying more every year for property, auto, and health insurance while getting less coverage in return.
That's putting a real strain on household budgets.
We need more transparency and accountability.
When insurance companies ask for major rate increases, they should have to explain why in a process that's open to the public.
Homeowners who invest in making their homes more resilient should see meaningful savings on their premiums.
If you lower the risk, your insurance bill should reflect it.
We will not stop hurricanes, but we can build stronger homes, make insurance pricing more straightforward, and fight to make coverage more affordable for South Carolina families.
Stop Paying for Power You Don't Get Politicians in Columbia love writing blank checks to giant corporations and sending you the bill.
South Carolina families are still paying off a $9 billion nuclear plant that was never finished, the V.C.
Summer failure, while the same utilities fight for 30%+ rate hikes to fund the next project that's already doubled from $2.5 billion to $5 billion before a single brick was laid (SC Daily Gazette).
Look at Scout Motors: Columbia handed a foreign corporate giant $1.3 billion in taxpayer dollars.
It’s already $150 million over budget before producing a single vehicle, while state leaders skipped basic taxpayer protections.
We can bring jobs to SC without handing out blank checks with zero accountability.
Fighting Corruption & Holding Politicians Accountable SC is too corrupt.
Clean up our politics.
Make government answer to you.
We Need Dark Money Disclosures You have a right to know who's paying to influence your government.
South Carolina is one of just two states in the entire country that lets outside groups spend on our elections without disclosing a single donor (Coalition for Integrity).
It's not an accident: secret money is how special interests win while families lose.
We can shine a light.
Require every group spending money to influence SC elections to disclose its donors, in a public, searchable database.
Give the State Ethics Commission real teeth and real funding to enforce the rules.
And make sure every contribution, has a name attached.
Close the Lawmaker to Lobbyist Pipeline A lawmaker shouldn't cash out into a lobbying job the moment they leave office, trading their public service for a private payday.
South Carolina's current "cooling-off" period is just one year.
Politicians are there to serve the public, not to get rich off of our votes.
We can slam the revolving door in politics: extend the cooling-off period to 5 years, close the loophole that lets ex-lawmakers "consult" instead of "lobby," and stop the practice of legislators quietly steering contracts to businesses they profit from.
Implement Real Government Transparency South Carolina took a step in 2011 by publishing roll call votes, but lawmakers still hide behind voice votes on decisions that matter.
And the Legislature still largely polices its own ethics.
Sunlight is the best disinfectant.
We need our government opened to more transparency: require recorded, public votes on legislation and on judicial elections so every family can see how their representative voted, require bills to be released to the public ahead of voting to provide time for feedback, move ethics enforcement to a truly independent body instead of lawmakers judging themselves, and require legislators to fully disclose their assets, stocks, and all sources of income so voters know exactly who is profiting from the laws being passed.
Your Utility Shouldn’t Own Your Legislature Your monopoly power company should not also be one of the biggest donors to the people who regulate it.
While pushing to roll back consumer protections, utility companies spent hundreds of thousands of dollars between lobbying and donating to legislators (The Post and Courier).
A company gets a government-guaranteed monopoly over your electricity, and they’re buying more influence to exert control.
It’s a dirty deal, and it should be illegal.
We can end the conflict: bar regulated monopoly utilities and their executives from donating to legislators or dark-money groups.
If they want to influence our legislators, they can go on record in front of the public.
Voters Should Pick Politicians, Not the Other Way Around Voters should pick their politicians, not the other way around.
Today, South Carolina lawmakers draw their own districts.
When politicians draw the maps, they hoard power for themselves.
We can create maps that support your individual voice, and freedoms by creating an Independent Citizens Redistricting Commission to draw fair maps in the open, with public hearings and clear rules, so districts serve communities, not incumbents greedy for control.
Public Safety Keep families safe at home, and in public.
Tackle the Fentanyl Crisis This is one of the most urgent public safety and public health crises in the country right now.
I’ve lost loved ones to this crisis, and there’s more our state can be doing to protect its citizens.
We can equip all first responders and schools with Narcan, increase state penalties for fentanyl trafficking, and fund state-level task forces that disrupt distribution networks while expanding treatment options for addiction.
End the Silence on Domestic Violence Home should be the safest place in the world.
For far too many women in South Carolina, it is not.
South Carolina has ranked in the top 10 nationally for women killed by men in 23 of the last 25 years (Violence Policy Center).
This is a public safety emergency.
We must close the loophole that lets domestic abusers under a protective order keep their guns, and make sure every survivor in the Lowcountry has somewhere safe to go.
Protective orders should actually protect people.
Close Dangerous Loopholes, Keep Our Rights Law-abiding citizens have the constitutional right to bear arms, and every family deserves to feel safe in their community.
We need to close the background-check loopholes that let dangerous purchases slip through, keep guns out of the hands of domestic abusers, and give law enforcement more of the tools they are asking for to enforce the law.
Veterans Deserve Better The people who served our country shouldn't come home to a park bench.
Nearly 1 in 5 homeless residents in South Carolinians is a veteran (2024 SC State of Homelessness Report).
The Charleston area's own VA and local nonprofits work every day to close that gap, but the need keeps outrunning the resources.
We must do right by our veterans: fully fund the state's veteran homelessness services, cut the red tape between VA services and state housing assistance, and make it a state priority, not just a local nonprofit's mission, that no one who served this country sleeps outside in South Carolina.
Protecting Our Coast & Environment Our water and our way of life are worth defending.
Protect Our Harbor’s Natural Beauty Clean waterways and healthy salt marshes are worth more to this district than any single power project.
As utilities push for massive infrastructure expansions across the state, our focus must remain on protecting the Charleston Harbor environment that defines House District 110.
Once our fragile coastal wetlands are damaged, no utility rate hike can buy back the natural storm and flood protection they provide for us.
That same stewardship goes for what's already built here, our historic areas cannot be sacrificed to overdevelopment." Higher Ground for Every Neighborhood Flood protection should not be something only wealthy waterfront neighborhoods can afford.
We need to start with the communities that flood the most and have waited the longest for help.
We must invest in practical solutions, including restored marshes, living shorelines, better drainage, and stronger infrastructure.
State dollars need to be spent on funding the infrastructure dollars they’re responsible for.
Every neighborhood deserves protection.
No family should be left behind simply because they live on a different part of the same street.
Stop Paving Over Paradise Every year, more of the Lowcountry's marshes, farmland, and forests disappear.
They're being replaced by roads, parking lots, and new development faster than most of us realize.
Once it's gone, it's gone, and with it goes flood protection, local food, and our treasured coastal environment.
We need to halt the loss: strengthen and fund the South Carolina Conservation efforts so families and farmers can protect land voluntarily, reward conservation instead of only sprawl, and keep the green buffers that soak up floodwater before it reaches your street.
Technology, AI & Data Centers Don’t let Big Tech write its own rules.
Data Centers Must Pay Their Own Way Giant data centers are coming to South Carolina, and they use staggering amounts of power, and they can raise everyone’s power bills if we let them.
A single large facility can demand as much electricity as a small city.
Your family should not have to pay to power a tech company’s servers.
We can finish the job by requiring large data centers to cover the full cost of the new power plants, transmission lines, and water infrastructure they demand.
Long-term contracts should ensure families and small businesses never get stuck with the bill.
These data centers need to ensure their water usage doesn't hurt us.
We should never have a drinking well go dry so a tech billionaire can build another data center.
Your Face, Your Voice, Your Rules Scammers can now fake a loved one's voice or a candidate's face with a few clicks.
Technology is growing fast, and that's a threat to every family's wallet and to fair elections.
Nobody should be able to steal your likeness or con your grandmother with a deepfake.
We can push back: give South Carolinians the right to control their own voice and image against AI fakes, require clear labeling of AI-generated content, and toughen penalties for AI-powered scams and fraud.
Own Your Personal Data Your personal information, including where you go, what you buy, and your children’s data, is being bought and sold without your permission.
Even state agencies, like the SC DMV, have profited selling personal driver data to private third-party companies.
You should own and control your own information.
We can give families real control by establishing a clear consumer data privacy right.
You should be able to see what companies collect, say no to having it sold, and ask for it to be deleted.
Big Tech should have to earn your trust, not simply take your data or bury the truth in lengthy terms of service agreements.
No Black-Box Rule The government can use technology to serve people faster and cheaper, but only if it's transparent and fair.
Too often, families get denied a benefit or flagged by a computer system with no explanation and no way to appeal.
A machine should never be the black box that decides your family's fate without a reason.
We can require that if AI is used to make decisions about people's benefits, licenses, or services, it must be transparent, tested by the public, and open to a real human appeal, while using smart tools to cut wait times and government waste.
Strong Families & Great Schools Give every Lowcountry kid a fair shot.
Fund the School Down the Street The neighborhood public school is where most of our kids learn, and it deserves our full commitment.
South Carolina already underfunds its own public schools by roughly half a billion dollars a year (ACLU of SC), yet Columbia keeps steering public money to private schools.
Our state Supreme Court even struck the first voucher program down as unconstitutional (Eidson v.
SCDE, 2024).
Our state needs to work together with local school authorities to ensure our children have all of the resources they need.
Public tax dollars should not get used to fund private schools, who do not maintain the same financial audit requirements and accountability for our tax dollars.
Childcare You Can Afford For young families, childcare is often the biggest bill after the mortgage, sometimes it's even bigger.That price pushes parents out of the workforce, and squeezes family budgets to the breaking point.
We should move toward implementing affordable, quality pre-K for every four-year-old, expand childcare assistance for the infant and toddler years where costs are highest, give employers and communities incentives to grow the supply of quality care, and support the childcare workers we can't do without.
Healthy Moms, Healthy Babies South Carolina should be one of the safest places to have a baby, but today it isn't.
Our state has one of the highest maternal mortality rates in the country.
Every mom deserves to come home healthy.
We can save lives: protect and extend the full year of postpartum health coverage for moms, expand access to prenatal care and rural OB services, and invest in the maternal-health workforce so no mother in the Lowcountry gives birth without quality care nearby.
Value Our Teachers Every family knows a great teacher who has left for another opportunity.
Our kids can't learn in an empty classroom, and South Carolina keeps losing good teachers to burnout and better-paying states.
We can keep our teachers: we need to continue to raise starting teacher pay to stay competitive with our neighbors, cut the paperwork that pulls teachers away from teaching, and treat the people we trust with our children like the professionals they are.
