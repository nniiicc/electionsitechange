That’s what I will do as Attorney General – put Kansans first and ensure no politician or corporation is above the law.” – Chris Mann, candidate for Kansas Attorney General The Attorney General’s office shouldn’t be about Team Red or Team Blue; it should be about Team Kansas and making sure every Kansan feels safe.
The Attorney General of Kansas should have one priority – protecting Kansans.
Chris Mann knew from an early age that he wanted to serve and protect his community.
Following in his father’s footsteps, he chose a career in law enforcement.
But his life was forever changed when, on duty, Chris was struck by a drunk driver during a routine traffic stop in 2002.
The crash and resulting injuries ended his career in uniform.
But Chris knew he wanted to continue to help the people of Kansas, so he became an Assistant District Attorney in Wyandotte County.
Chris prosecuted everything from traffic violations to murders, always focused on protecting Kansans.
Then, as a Senior Staff Attorney with the Kansas Securities Commission, Chris fought to protect consumers from scams, fraud, and corporations that rip people off.
Now, with his own law firm, Chris represents victims of drunk driving crashes.
As a former police officer and prosecutor, Chris has stood toe-to-toe with violent criminals and fought to ensure victims and survivors receive the justice they deserve.
He will bring this same dedication to public safety to the Attorney General’s office.

The Attorney General should be a partner in public safety — stepping in to assist local law enforcement when needed so cases move forward, offenders are held accountable, and victims get the justice they deserve.
70% of law enforcement agencies in Kansas have 10 or fewer officers.
Yet, Chris hears time and again from local sheriffs and law enforcement that they’ve called Kris Kobach’s office for help in investigating a major crime, and they don’t even get a call back.
Or they’re told no.
The role of the Attorney General is to support local law enforcement, but Kobach is failing in that duty.
Law enforcement agencies need an Attorney General who is on their side — providing backup to local departments investigating and prosecuting complex crimes, communicating regularly with local leaders to get ahead of issues before they become crises, and picking up the phone when agencies call for help.
As Attorney General, Chris will hold quarterly meetings across the state with all local law enforcement leaders to increase communication and identify emerging crime trends that may require additional resources or support.
These meetings will be held in every corner of the state and online to reduce barriers to law enforcement getting the support they need.
As your Attorney General, Chris will say yes when local law enforcement calls his office for help investigating and prosecuting major crimes, including drug distribution and child sexual abuse.

Improving access to training The Attorney General’s office hosts training for local law enforcement throughout the year.
Most of these are held in Topeka, and many of the smaller departments across Kansas cannot afford to send their staff without sacrificing emergency response coverage in their communities.
As Attorney General, Chris will commit to hosting training sessions throughout the state and expanding online options to increase access for all of our law enforcement, not just those in the northeast corner.
Expanding mental health resources We must also ensure our law enforcement officers have the mental health support they need.
First responders are constantly heading toward danger, exposing them to countless physical and mental hazards.
First responders experience PTSD at a rate as much as three times the rate of civilians, with the rate of PTSD among first responders as high as 30%.
As a board member of First Responders Support Team, a non-profit dedicated to providing peer support services to first responders experiencing PTSD, Chris has firsthand experience in supporting first responders as they navigate various mental health challenges.
Chris will fight for increased access to mental health support for our officers.
Kansas does not recognize PTSD as an eligible workers’ compensation claim – we must change this.
Because officers are not able to receive job-related mental health benefits, many forgo care, leading to mental health issues that can be debilitating.
As Attorney General, Chris will advocate for legislation that adds PTSD as an eligible workers’ compensation claim, because we cannot let our first responders suffer in silence.
Providing high-quality, accessible training and supporting the mental health of our first responders is a key piece of retaining quality law enforcement that will keep our communities and state safe.

In the first six months of 2026, Kansans had more than $40 million stolen from them through fraud.
The vast majority of these scams are imposter scams – bad actors impersonating someone you know, the government, or another trusted entity.
And the development of artificial intelligence is making it harder to identify these scams.
As Chris travels across Kansas, he has heard time and again from local law enforcement and community members that Kansans are losing thousands of dollars to online scams.
Whether it’s a fake tractor for sale or someone pretending to be a family member who has been arrested, these scams are taking real money from hardworking Kansans.
Chris will bring his experience investigating and prosecuting for the Kansas Securities Commission to the Attorney General’s office to ensure Kansans, especially our elderly and veterans, are educated on and protected from scams.
Chris will partner with federal, state, and local law enforcement agencies to share intelligence and strengthen the state-level approach to fraud and scam prevention, particularly when it comes to the rise in cybercrimes.
Working in collaboration with other agencies will streamline the Office of the Attorney General’s ability to investigate when Kansans report scams and stop them before more money is lost.
When scams do happen, Chris will fight to deliver justice for victims, and he will work to secure sentences that send a strong message to other scammers that their schemes won’t be tolerated in Kansas.
Kansas must strengthen its defenses, and, as Attorney General, Chris will ensure that the Attorney General’s office says yes when Kansans call for help investigating and prosecuting their case.

In recent years, the deadliness of domestic violence has increased dramatically, for both victims and responding officers.

During Chris’s time as a police officer, he responded to countless domestic violence calls, working hard to ensure victims walked away safe and their attackers were brought to justice.
Chris will bring his firsthand experience in investigating and prosecuting domestic violence to his role as Attorney General.
The Attorney General’s office has a Victims Services division dedicated to supporting victims and survivors throughout Kansas through state and local programs aimed at reducing violence and providing aid.

As Attorney General, Chris will work diligently to ensure these resources are allocated as efficiently as possible so our local shelters receive the support they need to protect survivors.

Chris has heard from local law enforcement agencies across Kansas that elder abuse is one of their most reported crimes, with the majority of cases involving financial exploitation or scams.
In 2025, Kansas seniors reported losing nearly $56 million to scams and fraud.
The majority of losses were from investment fraud, government impersonation, and romance scams.
Between 2024 and 2025, there was a 1,143% increase in government impersonation scams, revealing a stark failure in educating Kansans about the warning signs of fraud.
The Attorney General’s office has an Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation division dedicated to working with state agencies, law enforcement, and prosecutors to prevent and prosecute these crimes.
Chris will work with the Elder and Dependent Adult Abuse Prevention Council, which is part of the Attorney General’s office, to develop recommendations for relevant state agencies and law enforcement to improve statewide fraud education and training on identifying and prosecuting these cases.
Our seniors and dependent adults deserve an Attorney General who will take decisive action to protect them from abuse, neglect, and exploitation.
Chris has prosecuted abuse and financial exploitation cases at the Wyandotte County District Attorney’s office and the Kansas Securities Commission.
He knows what it takes to hold bad actors accountable and deliver real results for Kansas seniors.
