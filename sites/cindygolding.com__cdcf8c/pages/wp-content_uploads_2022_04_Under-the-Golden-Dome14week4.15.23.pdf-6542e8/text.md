SF455 – Top Soil Bill – set aside • This bill would stop cities and counties from adopting regulations for top soil and storm water run-off at construction sites that are more restrictive than the DNR requirements.
I voted NO after visiting with several of our small towns and many constituents.
We have worked hard for years to improve water quality.
Top soil and storm water run-off are significant parts of that picture.
Each community has unique challenges so this should be left to local control.
Senate File 494 – Accountability in Iowa’s Welfare Programs - waiting Governor’s signature • This bill came from the Senate and passed the House today.
It has been significantly amended throughout the process as we sought feedback from Iowans.
Here are the details: o Requires applicants to complete an identity authentication process prior to receiving benefits. o Requires assets to be reviewed for eligibility for SNAP (formerly Food Stamps).  Sets liquid asset (cash & savings) limits - does NOT include 401K account  Home & Household items like clothes or TVs don’t count toward assets.  To be clear, this only applies to SNAP, not to Medicaid. o Requires the state to check all income, employment, and financial institutions to ensure applicants for welfare programs meet eligibility criteria for those programs. o Makes cooperation with child support services a condition of eligibility for Medicaid. • Opponents of this legislation say there is no fraud in the system.
They chose to ignore the fact that Iowa has been penalized $1.8 million by the federal government for having an error rate of 3.2% higher than the national average.
Additionally, in FY2022, the Iowa Department of Inspections and Appeals discovered 2,761 verified incidents of SNAP overpayment.
This is another example of the media misrepresenting the facts.

House Study Bill 245 – Protecting the Iowa Caucuses • This bill passed Ways & Means committee.
Now must pass the full House then go to the Senate. • There are 3 main pieces of the bill: o Requires participation in a precinct caucus to be in-person to protect Iowa’s first in the nation status.  The New Hampshire Secretary of State said that if Iowa allows mail-in voting, essentially replacing the caucus system with a primary, they will move to jump Iowa in the presidential primary calendar. o Requires party declaration at least 70 days before the caucuses.
This is to limit bad actors attempting to meddle in the opposing party’s caucus. o Removes the state from the Electronic Registration Information Center (ERIC).  Several key states have left ERIC, making it a less effective tool in maintaining our voter rolls.  Iowa’s Secretary of State has other tools available to maintain Iowa’s voter registration list.
As promised, I continue to push for real election integrity.
The Senate has stalled our bills for additional improvements to the voting process, just as they stalled our bills for land-owner protections.
House File 1 - Property Tax Bill – proposed • The biggest thing we hear about from constituents is the need for property tax reform. • House Republican plan provides real relief to Iowans and certainty to the taxpayer. • HF1 passed the House Ways and Means committee with an amendment. o Caps annual property tax increases to 3% for residential and agricultural properties and 8% for commercial and industrial properties. o Increases transparency by requiring more notice to the taxpayers and moving all bond referendums to the general election date. o Reduces the $5.40 levy by $1 and has the state fund the difference.
This will deliver more than $200 million in immediate real estate tax relief to Iowans.
Yes, this is complicated.
