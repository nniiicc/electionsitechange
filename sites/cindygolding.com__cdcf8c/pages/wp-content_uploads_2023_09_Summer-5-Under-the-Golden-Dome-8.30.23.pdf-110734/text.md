Reality check School Choice or Education Savings Accounts (ESA): I have received many questions about this topic and have read quite a bit of misinformation from many sources.
Let’s start with the law as it existed prior to 2023 - If a student open-enrolled to another public school, the new district received $7,635 state funding per student while the home district only received about $800 of other state funds.
For example, in Alburnett School District (my home district) nearly 40% of the student population is open enrolled from other public schools.
Alburnett has received the state funding for those students.
The home district retained the real estate taxes but lost most of the state dollars.
So now we have the School Choice (ESA) program: • • • • • • • • • • • ESA is NOT a voucher system!
Iowa schools receive an average of about $17,000 per student from all government sources.
The ESA dollars come from the State – not your real estate taxes marked for the school district (57.24% of my real estate tax goes to K-12 education; every district is different) 100% of Real Estate tax dollars for K-12 education stay with your local school.
Students First Act approved approx.
18,600 students for an Education Savings Account (ESA).
Of those children, approx.
60% were already attending private schools.
Last year, the public school received $0 state dollars for those students in private schools.
The new law provides the public school approx. $1,205 per student or nearly $13,450,000 additional funding to public schools that they did not receive last year.
The 1st year a public school student enrolls in a private school, the public school still receives $7,635 state dollars for that student!
The following year, the public school will receive $1,205 for that student.
This allows the public school time to adjust for the change of enrollment.
Public schools receive additional dollars for children with disabilities.
Private schools do not receive that increase.
Remember: public schools still receive ALL restate tax dollars earmarked for your school district regardless of attendance.
