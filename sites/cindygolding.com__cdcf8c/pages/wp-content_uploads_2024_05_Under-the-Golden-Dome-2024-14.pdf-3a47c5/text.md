Subscribe Past Issues Translate View this email in your browser Under the Golden Dome Week #14 & 15- 2024 Dear Friends, These last two weeks have been a scrambled mess!
As this was my first experience with the end of a two-year General Assembly, several veterans of this process warned me that my policy of reading every bill before voting would be very difficult.
That was an understatement!
As you may know, State Representatives are elected for 2-year terms and State Senators serve for 4 years.
Each two-year term is called a General Assembly.
My “freshman” term started in 2023 for the 90th General Assembly.
At the end of 2023, a bill could be put on the “Unfinished Business” calendar to stay viable for the second year of the 90th General Assembly.
In contrast, the end of session this year marked the end of the 90th General Assembly.
That means any bill not passed by both Chambers dies at the session.
Here’s where things get “tricky.” Something that didn’t pass the House or Senate might get put on as an amendment at the last minute.
Policy bills that failed in one committee might end up as an amendemnt on a bill in some other committee.
Some amendments showed up in the wee hours of the morning.
As I’ve noted in the past, “The Devil is in the Details!” I’ll give a summary of the accomplishments and disappointments next week - when the dust settles.
Some Highlights of Iowa House Week #14 & 15 HF 2681 - Regulation of Traffic Cameras This has been a hot topic throughout the state.
This bill allows the continuation of camera use but the process will be strictly regulated and permited by the Department of Transportation.
Local governments can only use the money generated by traffic cameras for transportation Past Issues Translate infrastructure improvement or for their police and fire departments.
Waiting Governor’s signature Subscribe SF 641 - Including Veterans in the Small Business Ivestment Program I was honored to floor manage this bill which gives veterans the opportunity to participate in the Small Business Investment Program.
The business must be at least 51% owned by a veteran.
