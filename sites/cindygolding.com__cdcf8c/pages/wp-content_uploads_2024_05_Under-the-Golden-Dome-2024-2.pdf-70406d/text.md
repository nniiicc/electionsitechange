While many of you were getting prepared for Caucus, the weather was not cooperating.

It was great to see so many friends, old & new.
Now that the candidates have moved on to other states, we can focus on the business at hand.
Governor Reynolds’ Condition of the State speech spelled out some very bold goals for this session.
The most controversial topic was the discussion the Area Education Agencies (AEA).
The Governor’s bill (HSB542) outlines an aggressive restructuring of the AEA system, focusing on students with special needs and removing many of the other services.
This caught many of us - legislators, teachers, parents. and AEA employees - by surprise!
It is important to know that the US Department of Education has called out Iowa as “needs assistance” in the implementation of the “Individuals with Disabilities Act” since 2018.
We have been told that if Iowa does not act, the Biden Administration could determine the path beginning in Sept 2024.
Additionally, while our students with disabilities score in the bottom 30%, our investment in their education is significantly higher than surrounding states.
The Governor believes we need better results from taxpayer dollars.
Linn County is served by Grant Wood AEA, an award winning organization.
Unfortunately, other Subscribe Past Issues areas of the state have not had the same experience.
Translate Since her speech, I have received hundreds of emails, phone calls and personal visits from constituents extremely concerned about the impact on students, especially in our smaller school districts.
Many have given solid suggestions for improvements and efficiencies that would enhance the AEA operations.
Groups of legislators have met with the Governor and her staff to share concerns and ideas.
As it currently stands, HSB542 will be amended before it comes to the floor for a vote.
I want to thank all who have taken time to contact me.
I haven’t been able to respond to everyone, but know that I have read every email and have communicated your concerns to the Governor.
Other news from the Capitol Veterans Affairs Committee We had a presentation by Todd Jacobus, Commandant of Iowa Department of Veterans Affairs.
He gave us an update on the Iowa Veterans Home, Iowa Veterans Cemetery, and Home Base Iowa.
HSB556, regarding National Guard scholarship programs is working its way through the committee.
Legislative Calendar Lots of folks have contacted me with great ideas for legislation.
Unfortunately, this year the General Assembly is shorter than 2023.
We are in the 90th General Assembly, second session.
The first session is 110 days, the second is 100 days.
As a result, the calendar for legislation is quite condensed.
The deadline for individual legislators to submit bills was Jan.
19.
