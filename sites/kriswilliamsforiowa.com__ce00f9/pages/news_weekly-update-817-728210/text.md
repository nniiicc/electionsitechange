The Issues - Economic Justice (The Wealth Gap)
We recently had a trillionaire – though it was short-lived.
But did you know the average CEO of an S&P 500 company now makes 312 times what their average worker makes?
It's even worse when you look at private companies.
One of the world's biggest, with multiple locations in Iowa – including one here in our district – pays most workers around $20/hour, while the CEO is estimated to make over $200 million a year from the company.
The Problem
Corporations bring jobs to our communities, but we have to look at their overall impact.
Too often, we see the same pattern: billion‑dollar companies move in to do one thing – extract.
We see names like Walmart, Cargill, and Koch slapped onto local causes, but we forget to ask why those causes need help in the first place.
In the 1900s, the wealthiest groups built libraries and schools to give back – to show why they were worth keeping around.
They also paid higher wages and taxes, often with a ratio closer to 20:1.
Over time, that gap grew.
Less revenue goes back into our communities.
In 1959, the poverty rate was 22.4%.
Today, the ALICE rate – which includes both poverty and working families who can't meet basic needs has climbed to 41%.
We see it in our schools too, as they once again qualify for free breakfast and lunch based on family income.
We should be asking why this is happening, not just celebrating the aid.
Every school should have this program anyway.
Hungry kids don't learn.
The Solution
Every worker deserves the freedom to live with dignity.
That means we need to regulate corporate greed.
If elected, I will fight to:
* Raise wages to a living wage
* Restore workers' rights
* Tax wealth
* Expand workplace democracy
We tax our homes, and the mortgages on them, this is whether we sell them or not.
It's past time we did the same to the oligarchs in our communities.
They hide their wealth in the stock market and take loans against it to dodge paying their fair share.
We also watch companies like Tyson close plants, just last week, one closed by Cedar Rapids, putting hundreds of Iowa families out of work.
These companies boast record profits, then turn around and put Iowans on the street.
We need to invest in ourselves.
Give workers a path to buy the company abandoning the communities and keep it running.
Programs that support co‑ops lead to longer retention, better products, and true corporate citizenship – because the owners aren't some out‑of‑state entity.
