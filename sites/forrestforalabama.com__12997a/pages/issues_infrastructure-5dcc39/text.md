Give communities local control.
The artery that connects Hazel Green and Meridianville to downtown Huntsville is doing more work every year, and the fixes keep arriving years after the traffic did.
How it works A growth problem the planning cycle hasn't caught up to.
US 231 is not a problem solved by adding lanes alone.
It's a population-growth problem that has outpaced the planning cycle that funds it.
Madison County is one of the fastest-growing counties in the state.
The transportation infrastructure that serves it operates on a state-level allocation rhythm that updates years after the demand has shifted.
That gap is where the cost lands.
Who pays Commuters , sitting in traffic on a corridor that wasn't designed for the volume it now carries Employers in Huntsville , competing for talent that has to make the drive Emergency services , responding through traffic on a corridor that runs through several growing communities Children , riding school buses on the same highway their schools sit on What we're building Direct funding allocation for US 231 corridor improvements, including intersection redesign at the highest-volume junctions, dedicated turn lanes where school zones intersect the highway, and shoulder widening for emergency response.
A local-control process for road and transit decisions that sit entirely within a single county.
Madison County should not have to wait on a statewide priority list to fund a turn lane in Meridianville.
Whether by passing legislation or organizing community pressure on ALDOT, progress will be made.
