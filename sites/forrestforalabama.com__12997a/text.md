Forrest started his first company at 19 while studying engineering at UAB.
In the makerspace, he learned to turn ideas into working systems, from 3D-printed prosthetics to N95 masks built during the pandemic.
He later founded Cicada, a software company that helps organizations turn complicated operations into clear, usable tools.
That is the method he brings to public service: understand the system, find where it fails, and build a fix people can measure.
Meet Forrest → A working plan for North Alabama.
From breaking the healthcare monopoly to protecting homes from Wall Street, fixing US 231, fully funding public schools, and making state government open its books.
Forrest has a plan for every family in our district.
Bring competition back to North Alabama healthcare.
One system now controls every general hospital in and around Huntsville.
Forrest supports reviewing the antitrust immunity that enabled consolidation, reforming Certificate of Need rules, requiring public financial disclosure, and applying public ethics and competitive-bid rules to health care authorities.
Read more → Protect new homebuyers and keep homes in family hands.
Alabama law lacks requirements for builder warranties and liability insurance.
Forrest proposes a statutory warranty, mandatory insurance, and protections for family homes and seniors.
Read more → Fix US 231 and give communities local control.
Thousands commute daily on US 231, a state road often neglected.
Forrest aims to give local control back to communities, reducing commute times and empowering residents.
Read more → Compete for teachers and prepare students for North Alabama jobs.
That means competitive starting salaries, safe and modern facilities, universal First Class Pre-K, and career and technical education tied to the industries already hiring here.
Read more → Fix a child-care market that is failing parents and workers.
Forrest supports a refundable child-care credit, a wage floor for child-care workers, universal Pre-K, and simpler licensing that protects children without blocking responsible providers.
Read more → Set the standard before asking voters to trust it.
Forrest will publish a quarterly report, maintain a public dashboard, hold regular town halls, defend open-records access, and release a pre-session scorecard showing what he intends to do and how voters can judge the result.
