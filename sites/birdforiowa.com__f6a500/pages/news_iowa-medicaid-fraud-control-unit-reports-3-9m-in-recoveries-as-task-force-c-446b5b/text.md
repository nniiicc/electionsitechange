Iowa Medicaid Fraud Control Unit reports $3.9M in recoveries as task force continues work
Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird said the Medicaid Fraud Elimination Task Force's recommendations are expected later next month.
The group has to send them to the governor by the end of October.
The group held its third meeting Thursday, providing updates on what the work groups are looking into.
"It's important that we are not just reactive but proactive when it comes to fighting fraud," Bird said.
"And so it is very important that we get those tips from people and that those are investigated and followed up on, but we also need to be more proactive looking at where the fraud may be occurring rather than waiting for someone to discover it and report it." The director of Iowa's Medicaid Fraud Control Unit said new federal evaluations ranked Iowa 10th in the country for convictions, despite the unit being among the smallest in terms of staffing and funding.
"For 2026, through the end of August, the unit has already reported recoveries over $3.9 million and total expenditures of nearly $1.2 million," Medicaid Fraud Control Unit Director Jeremy Ingrim said.
"This has resulted in a return on investment of 338%.
In other words, we've recovered $3.38 for every dollar spent to operate the unit."
After the meeting, Dean Lerner, who previously served as director of the Iowa Department of Inspections and Appeals, said the unit does not have adequate staffing or funding compared with units in other states with similar budgets.
Lerner said it would be a "mistake" for the task force not to recommend increases in both.
