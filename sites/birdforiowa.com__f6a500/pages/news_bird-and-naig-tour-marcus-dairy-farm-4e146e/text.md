Donate Connect with Brenna News Donate News Bird and Naig tour Marcus dairy farm September 21, 2026 Iowa Secretary of Agriculture Mike Naig and Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird visited Cornbelt Dairy in Marcus on Thursday, Sept.
17, where they made campaign speeches and toured the farm, taking a look at its new robotic milking system that has increased the farm's milk production by nearly 10%.
Naig said the implementation of the robotic milking system was part of the Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship Choose Iowa Dairy Innovation Grant Program, where grants are awarded to Iowa farms.
"They were able to secure these grant dollars and then put in two robotic milkers, which is a labor-saving piece of technology," Naig said.
"It's better for the cattle.
Their production is up, and this is a young family that has young children, and their time is precious to them.
Most importantly, it'll allow this farm family to continue to stay in the dairy business." Naig emphasized the importance of dairy farms in Iowa, saying Cornbelt Dairy's innovation is "value-added agriculture at its finest." "We take things that we grow in this state-corn and soybeans and grass and hay-and we turn it into something of incredibly high value, nutritious, safe food, and in this case, dairy products," Naig said.
"This is what we need to see, and we do see this all across the state.
What I love is that it's giving this smaller dairy an opportunity to continue." Bird said she had never seen a robotic milker before, describing the farm as "really neat." "I think it's important that we have more opportunities like this all over Iowa, where we can have dairy farms in different places, and there's certainly going to be a need for more dairy farmers because we see more folks coming to the state that want to process that milk, and that's a great value-added agriculture product.
It makes sense," Bird said.
Although the attorney general and secretary of agriculture offices are separate, they still work with each other, according to Bird.
Bird described Naig as a "great ag secretary." Both officeholders are Republicans.
"We work on a team on all kinds of ag, and it's had a real impact and a real impact for Iowa," Bird said.
Bird said she was raised on a farm, adding that many of her family members are farmers while she is a lawyer.
Bird said fighting for agriculture is one of the things she works on in the attorney general's office.
"I've got a farm and trade division.
We worked on a number of guidelines together, suing Biden's EPA a lot, year-round E15, suing California, all kinds of things," Bird said.
During media availability after the event, Bird said she is campaigning all over Iowa.
"As a prosecutor, I work very closely with law enforcement and county attorneys and also work to help crime victims and keep Iowa safe," Bird said.
"We're hearing good feedback, people appreciate our cold case unit, which focuses on the over 400 unsolved homicides in Iowa, making sure that nobody gets away with murder, and that we are always working to bring those cases to justice and get answers for survivors and families." In the 2022 Iowa Attorney General's election, Bird defeated long-time Democratic incumbent Tom Miller by 1.7%.
Miller had served as Iowa Attorney General since 1979.
Bird will face Nate Willems, a labor attorney of nearly 20 years, in the 2026 election.
Sabato's Crystal Ball has the race characterized as "lean Republican," meaning Bird is favored, but the election is expected to be competitive.
Bird said her and Willems are "very different people." "My focus is on supporting our law enforcement and prosecuting, upholding the Constitution, and on protecting people, so that's what I'm focused on, and that's the work that I do as attorney general," Bird said.
During Naig's media availability, he celebrated the passing of year-round E15 in the U.S.
Senate Agriculture Committee.
"That is great news.
It's good for consumers all across the country, but it's certainly good for our ethanol industry as well," Naig said.
When asked about water quality, Naig said the Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship is "making progress" on water quality.
"We're bringing an extra focus in places like Cherokee on converting cropland that's highly erosive ground or less productive ground into pasture for cattle, which is great for the environment and good for those farm families and those in the economy," Naig said.
"Cover crops, no-till, putting buffers along streams and nutrient management is always really important." Naig said Iowa is seeing more on farm dairy processing, citing his love for small farms and also Blue Bunny in Le Mars.
Naig said his favorite ice cream flavor is strawberry.
Bird said her favorite ice cream flavor is "butter brickle," noting it is hard to find nowadays, but it is a flavor she grew up eating with her grandparents.
"It's a little bit nutty, but it mostly has that brickle stuff in it, kind of like a toffee inside vanilla ice cream, and if I ever see it, I always get it because it's hard to find," Bird said.
