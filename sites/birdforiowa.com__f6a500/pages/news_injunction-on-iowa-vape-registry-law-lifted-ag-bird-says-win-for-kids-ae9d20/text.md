The 2024 law prohibiting the sale of unapproved vape devices in Iowa is no longer on hold.
AG Bird says it’s a win for Iowa kids.
The injunction placed on House File 2677 was lifted on July 31.
The law aimed to restrict the sale of electronic nicotine devices that weren’t approved by the Food and Drug Administration, putting these in a registry approved by the Department of Revenue.
Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird said the law, which was unenforced and has been on hold since 2025, opened the door for vape shops to market certain products to children.
“There are flavors like watermelon slushie, cotton candy, ice cream flavors, things that are really targeted at kids and we want to make sure that no child has access to vaping products.” Bird claims the industry of candy-flavored vapes generates over $11 billion in annual retail sales, and a law like this puts an end to those sales.
The Attorney General called it a step in the right direction for Iowa kids and said it will be helpful to adults.
“They will know that the products they’re buying that are on the approved registry that Iowa has are safe for them to use and have been looked at for that reason.” Despite the injunction’s lift, the law won’t take effect immediately.
“The Department of Revenue moves forward with their rulemaking process and in their registry.
So it’s a big win.
It does take a little bit of time for it to go into effect.”
