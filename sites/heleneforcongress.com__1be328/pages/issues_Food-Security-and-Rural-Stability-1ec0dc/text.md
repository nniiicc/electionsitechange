Food Security and Rural Stability
When family farms struggle, rural communities struggle.
And when rural communities struggle, hunger rises.
Family farms across North Dakota and the Great Plains are under serious pressure from rising input costs, volatile markets, and agricultural-sector consolidation.
Many farmers are working harder than ever while operating on increasingly slim margins.
At the same time, food insecurity is increasing in communities that rely on agriculture for their economic stability.
Food security is not just a social issue — it is also an agricultural and economic issue.
Programs like SNAP help families through difficult periods while supporting local grocery stores and rural economies.
When these programs are weakened, rural communities often feel the impact first.
North Dakota’s farmers and ranchers work every day to feed this country.
They deserve fair markets, stable policies, and support that allows family farms to remain viable for the next generation.
A strong agricultural economy and a strong food system should mean fewer families go hungry — not more.
Addressing hunger and strengthening family farms must go hand in hand if we want rural communities to remain strong.
