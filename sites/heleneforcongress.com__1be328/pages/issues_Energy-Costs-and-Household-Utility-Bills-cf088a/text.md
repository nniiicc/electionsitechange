Even families who do everything they can to conserve energy still face unavoidable fixed costs, such as delivery charges to transmit electricity from power plants to homes.
These rising costs hit low- and middle-income families hardest.
Many households now spend 6 to 10 percent of their income on energy, placing a significant strain on family budgets.
Reliable and affordable energy is not a luxury — it is a necessity for families, seniors, farmers, and small businesses.
Energy policies should prioritize affordability, reliability, and responsible development.
North Dakota Energy Across the country, families are noticing their monthly utility bills rising.
The average American household now pays around $265 per month for energy, an increase of about 12 percent in one year.
North Dakota is a major energy producer for the nation.
The families who live here should be able to afford the power that keeps their homes warm and their communities running.
North Dakota’s average residential electricity rate is approximately $0.11 per kilowatt-hour, about 31 percent below the national average.
The average monthly electric bill is about $89, though costs have steadily increased in recent years.
As demand grows — including potential data centers, artificial intelligence infrastructure, and new energy development — planning must be transparent and responsible.
Large-scale projects should include community input and careful consideration of agricultural land, water resources, and local infrastructure.
We should support a balanced energy strategy that: Maintains reliable and affordable power Encourages innovation and responsible development Protects agricultural land and rural communities Ensures transparency in large infrastructure decisions North Dakota has an opportunity to lead in responsible energy development while protecting families, farmers, and rural communities.
