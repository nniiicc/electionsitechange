Mental Health and Community Well-Being
When mental health care is unavailable, costs shift to communities.
People with untreated mental illness often end up in emergency departments, correctional facilities, or experience homelessness — instead of receiving the care they need.
This issue is personal to me.
As a travel nurse in Phoenix and Las Vegas, I worked in minimum-, medium-, maximum-, and forensic psychiatric units.
The work was demanding and difficult.
I remember being afraid at first.
But I reflected on my life’s work and realized that everyone deserves care, compassion, and dignity — without judgment.
It could be anyone in the jail suit.
Correctional facilities across the country are also facing shortages of nurses and mental health professionals, making it more difficult to address mental health needs effectively.
North Dakota Mental Health
Mental health challenges are significant in North Dakota.
Approximately 150,000 adults in the state live with a mental illness, and more than 15,000 adolescents aged 12–17 experience depression or anxiety.
Rural access to mental health care remains limited, and many communities face shortages of providers.
These challenges contribute to higher rates of suicide and untreated mental health conditions.
Medicaid plays a critical role in providing mental health and substance use disorder services.
Nearly 40 percent of non-elderly adults on Medicaid live with mental health or substance use condition.
Reductions in Medicaid funding could make it harder for North Dakotans to access therapy, medication, and supportive services.
When mental health care is unavailable, costs shift to emergency departments, law enforcement, and correctional facilities — creating more expensive and less effective outcomes.
Strengthening access to mental health care supports families, improves public safety, and builds stronger communities across North Dakota.
