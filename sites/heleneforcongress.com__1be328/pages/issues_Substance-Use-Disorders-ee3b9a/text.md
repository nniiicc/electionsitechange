Substance Use Disorders
This is a public health crisis that requires a public health response.
Addiction is not a moral failure — it is a medical condition.
Substance use disorder is a brain disorder that requires treatment, support, and pathways to recovery.
We must expand access to:
• Medication-assisted treatment
• Mental health services
• Prevention and early intervention programs
• Recovery and peer support services
• Community-based treatment options
Compassion, evidence-based care, and accountability must guide our response.
At the same time, pharmaceutical companies must be held accountable for their role in fueling the opioid epidemic.
Recovery is possible.
But access to treatment must be available when people are ready to seek help.
North Dakota Substance Use Disorder
North Dakota is experiencing a concerning rise in substance use disorder, particularly involving fentanyl and synthetic opioids.
Overdose deaths have increased significantly in recent years, with rising impacts among young adults, Native American communities, and rural populations.
Access to treatment remains a challenge, especially in rural areas where services are limited and transportation barriers exist.
North Dakota has taken steps to address this crisis through community-based behavioral health services, peer support programs, and prevention initiatives.
Programs like ND Cares provide important support for service members and their families.
But more work remains.
Addressing substance use disorder requires coordinated efforts across healthcare, public safety, education, and community organizations.
Families deserve hope.
Communities deserve solutions.
And those struggling with addiction deserve compassion, treatment, and a path forward.
