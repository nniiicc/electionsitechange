The government should not stand between a patient and their doctor.
During my time as a nurse at Planned Parenthood, I saw firsthand that reproductive healthcare is about far more than abortion.
Much of our work focused on: • Education for young mothers • Nutrition counseling • Contraception and family planning • WIC program access • Preventive care and well-woman exams These services support healthier families and stronger communities.
Protecting reproductive freedom also means protecting privacy, safeguarding patient-doctor relationships, and ensuring individuals can make healthcare decisions without political interference.
Wherever you are on your fertility journey, families should be embraced with hands-on support to turn possibilities into progress.
This issue is often framed as a political debate, but at its core, it is about healthcare, dignity, and personal freedom.
I advocate for protecting reproductive freedom and ensuring people have the right to make choices about their own bodies and healthcare.
At the same time, supporting families must extend beyond pregnancy.
Policies should promote maternal health, childcare, access to healthcare, and economic stability so that families and children can thrive.
North Dakota Reproductive Freedom North Dakota currently has one of the most restrictive abortion laws in the country.
The law limits abortion with narrow exceptions for rape or incest in early pregnancy and when a serious physical health risk exists for the mother.
These restrictions have created uncertainty for patients and healthcare providers and have limited access to reproductive healthcare services across the state.
This is not just a political issue — it is a healthcare issue.
Women, families, and healthcare providers deserve clarity, compassion, and respect when making difficult and deeply personal decisions.
