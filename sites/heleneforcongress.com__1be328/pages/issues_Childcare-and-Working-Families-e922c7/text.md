According to the Department of Labor, many families spend between 9% and 16% of their household income on childcare — sometimes more than they spend on groceries or housing.
We need policies that support working families while strengthening the early childhood workforce.
That includes expanding access to affordable childcare, supporting providers, and investing in early childhood education that gives children a strong start.
Children are the future.
Supporting families today helps build stronger communities tomorrow.
Strong childcare systems support parents, strengthen the workforce, and help communities grow.
North Dakota Childcare Childcare challenges are especially important in North Dakota, where workforce shortages and rural access issues make finding care even more difficult.
In North Dakota: Center-based infant care averages about $600 per month Family daycare averages about $508 per month North Dakota offers programs to help make childcare more affordable, including subsidies for low- and moderate-income families.
However, with a median household income of approximately $69,090 , childcare costs still exceed the federal affordability benchmark of 7% of household income .
Compared to the US as a whole, North Dakota had more households in the $50,000 to $99,999 income range and fewer in the $200,000 or more range.
When families cannot find affordable childcare, it affects workforce participation, economic growth, and community stability.
