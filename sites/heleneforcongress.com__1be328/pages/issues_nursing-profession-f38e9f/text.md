Why Nursing Matters in Public Office
"Leadership begins by showing up, listening, and serving." ~ Helene Neville
Before I became a congressional candidate, I was a nurse.
For more than 42 years, I cared for patients in hospitals, clinics, rural communities, Tribal health systems, correctional facilities, disaster response settings, and public health programs across eight states.
Nursing teaches something politics often forgets.
You don't begin by arguing.
You begin by listening.
You assess the situation.
You gather facts.
You work with a team.
You solve problems.
When someone is frightened, sick, or facing the worst day of their life, they don't ask whether their nurse is a Republican, Democrat, or Independent.
They simply hope someone competent, compassionate, and prepared walks through the door.
That philosophy has guided my entire career.
It also guides my campaign.
Leadership is service.
Leadership is showing up.
Leadership is helping people through difficult moments.
Only two nurses currently serve in the U.S.
House of Representatives, and none serve in the U.S.
Senate.
I believe America would benefit from more nurses helping shape public policy.
Nurses understand prevention.
We understand teamwork.
We understand public health.
We understand the challenges facing rural communities.
Most importantly, we understand people.
That perspective belongs in Congress.
