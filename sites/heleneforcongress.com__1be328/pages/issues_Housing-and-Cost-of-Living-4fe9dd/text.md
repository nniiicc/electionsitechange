Rental costs have also risen sharply, placing additional pressure on families, seniors on fixed incomes, and workers trying to build financial stability.
Several factors have contributed to rising housing costs.
Home construction slowed dramatically after the 2008 financial crisis, creating a long-term housing shortage.
Low interest rates during the pandemic also drove increased demand, pushing prices higher.
Even with some recent cooling, housing prices remain well above pre-pandemic levels.
Housing stability is one of the foundations of strong communities.
Policies should focus on expanding housing supply, encouraging responsible development, and ensuring that housing programs actually help families achieve stability.
North Dakota Housing North Dakota is experiencing real housing challenges, and finding solutions must be a priority.
While battling stage IV cancer in Bismarck and Fargo, I depended on rental assistance after my savings ran out.
That experience gave me direct insight into how housing policy impacts real people.
What I saw is this: when assistance increases, but housing supply does not, rents often rise alongside it — and the relief does not always reach the people it is meant to help.
For seniors living on fixed incomes — $1,200 to $1,400 a month — paying $600 or more in rent after assistance leaves almost nothing for food, medicine, or emergencies.
That is not sustainable.
Policy must stabilize families — not just circulate money.
We must increase housing supply, support responsible, affordable development, and ensure that housing programs improve stability for the people they are meant to serve.
The North Dakota housing market reflects these challenges.
The median home price is approximately $349,000, with recent increases putting homeownership further out of reach for many families.
While median rent averages around $1,100, rising costs still place pressure on working families and seniors.
Safe and stable housing strengthens families, supports economic growth, and helps communities thrive.
