It is a legal and constitutional reality rooted in treaties, federal law, and government-to-government relationships between Tribal nations and the United States.
These agreements recognize the authority of Tribal governments to govern their communities, manage their lands and natural resources, and preserve their cultures and traditions.
Treaties are not temporary agreements — they are binding commitments.
The federal government cannot pause its obligations when funding becomes difficult or when political priorities change.
Honoring treaty responsibilities is essential to maintaining trust and respecting Tribal sovereignty.
Despite these legal protections, Tribal communities often face challenges related to jurisdiction, resource management, healthcare access, infrastructure, and equitable funding.
Strengthening the government-to-government relationship between Tribal nations and the federal government is essential to addressing these challenges.
Tribal nations are also leading innovation and economic development.
For example, the Standing Rock Sioux Tribe has invested in renewable energy projects, including large-scale solar development, demonstrating leadership in energy independence and environmental stewardship.
Respecting Tribal sovereignty means recognizing Tribal governments as partners in building a stronger future for North Dakota.
My commitment is to listen, respect Tribal leadership, and support policies that: • Uphold treaty obligations • Strengthen Tribal health systems • Improve infrastructure and housing • Support education and workforce development • Promote economic opportunity within Tribal communities My experience working as a travel nurse at the Hopi Reservation in Arizona gave me firsthand insight into the strength, resilience, and cultural richness of Tribal communities, as well as the importance of culturally informed healthcare and governance.
Tribal sovereignty is not simply about protection.
It is about partnership, respect, and the right of Tribal nations to shape their own future.
North Dakota Tribal Nations North Dakota’s Tribal communities have shaped our state’s history and continue to play an essential role in its future.
Each Tribal nation has its own distinct origins, traditions, and languages, while sharing values rooted in respect for the land, community, and future generations.
Today, more than 31,000 American Indians live in North Dakota, representing approximately 5 percent of the state’s population.
Nearly 60 percent reside on reservations, and more than 40 percent of Native residents are under age 20.
These communities represent not only a rich cultural heritage but also an important part of North Dakota’s future growth and leadership.
Respecting Tribal sovereignty strengthens North Dakota — and strengthens our democracy.
