Before starting chemotherapy, I contracted COVID-19 and spent three weeks in a COVID unit.
I lost 15 pounds and temporarily lost my swallow reflex.
It was a frightening and uncertain time.
Frontline professionals were asked to do extraordinary work under extraordinary pressure.
In many facilities, nurses and doctors had to reuse masks and gowns for days because of limited supplies.
This should never happen in the wealthiest country in the world.
Pandemics are not hypothetical events.
Public health experts warned for years that a major outbreak was inevitable.
Our national preparedness must reflect that reality.
We must strengthen our response by ensuring: • Strategic stockpiles of protective equipment • Clear national emergency preparedness plans • Strong public health surveillance systems • Support and safeguards for frontline healthcare workers Healthcare workers will always show up for their communities.
The government must show the same commitment to preparedness, coordination, and protection.
During the pandemic, when supplies ran short, communities stepped forward.
My best friend Chris sewed masks for my colleagues.
A friend from high school made sixty masks for our staff.
Across the country, people used their time, talent, and compassion to support healthcare workers.
Those seamstress heroes represented the very best of America's fabric.
The masks may not have stopped the virus — but the love sewn into them gave us strength to keep going.
North Dakota Preparedness North Dakota healthcare workers demonstrated courage and resilience throughout the pandemic.
Between January 2020 and July 2023, North Dakota recorded more than 292,000 COVID-19 cases and over 2,200 deaths.
These numbers represent neighbors, families, and communities impacted by a crisis that tested our healthcare system and our preparedness.
We owe it to healthcare workers, families, and future generations to learn from this experience and strengthen our readiness for the next public health emergency.
Preparedness is not optional.
