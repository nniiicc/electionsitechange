For decades, politicians in both parties promised that letting businesses get bigger would lower prices and keep necessities affordable.
We now know that promise was false: big businesses took the tax breaks and did what was best for them – not the rest of us.
When businesses get too big, prices go up, quality goes down, and innovation suffers.
Now, prices keep climbing, hidden fees are everywhere, and groceries, gas, childcare, healthcare, and housing are unaffordable.
I have the expertise to fight for an affordable Washington by taking on big business and the status quo.
If elected, I’ll be the only trained economist in the legislature.
I’m also an attorney who spent 20 years fighting for low prices by stopping illegal monopolies at the Federal Trade Commission.
I took on grocery giants to stop them from joining forces to close stores, raise prices, and cut jobs.
I fought to keep healthcare costs low by stopping hospitals from merging to eliminate patient choices and raising prices.
I’m the mother of three school-aged children, so my husband and I know the high cost of childcare.
Being a parent can be hard enough without expensive daycare, gas that’s over $5 per gallon, and grocery budgets that don’t go as far as they did before.
Washington needs lawmakers with the perspective to bring new ideas and the knowledge to make them work.
In the legislature, I will work to make Washington affordable for working families: Strengthen the Insurance Commissioner’s office to reign in bogus denials of insurance coverage Strengthen the Attorney General’s ability to fight monopolies, stopping big businesses from secretly colluding to raise prices Lower the cost of childcare by cutting red tape and reducing insurance costs for responsible daycare centers Prohibit surveillance pricing, especially for necessities like groceries and clothes Speed up permitting to build more housing, especially starter homes Housing For decades, local governments made it difficult to build anything other than single-family homes and now housing shortages are statewide, driving up prices.
High housing prices are hurting our future by making it too hard for young adults to buy or rent a home and start a family.
High prices are also forcing out families who love their neighborhoods but are being priced out.
Our kids deserve the chance to build their lives here too, to afford a home or rent with stability in the communities where they grew up.
That opportunity is slipping away.
We need more housing period.
And we need it at every income level: starter homes, apartments near transit, and missing-middle choices that fit our neighborhoods.
We also need stronger protections for renters.
Corporate landlords are using AI to raise rents and tack on junk fees so renters have to spend every dollar just to keep their home.
That should be illegal.
Olympia needs AI expertise so we can get ahead of big corporations, stop playing catch- up, and put guardrails on AI to make it work for all of us.
I’ll fight for policies that speed up home building, protect renters from exploitation, and keep our communities stable and affordable for all of us.
Fund Strong Public Schools and Quality Affordable Child Care Every kid in Washington deserves a world-class education, no matter their ZIP code.
But years of underfunding have left schools facing impossible choices between classroom support, fair wages, and student services.
I’ll fight for stable, fair school funding that reflects our values and gives every student the tools to thrive.
Funding is only part of the picture.
We must ensure that every student and family has meaningful access to education.
That means expanding language access so that parents who speak languages other than English are never left out of decisions about their kids&#39; futures and students are not left out of learning in the classroom.
I’ll champion expanded language access and support for our increasingly diverse classrooms so every parent and student can meaningfully participate.
Now more than ever, our schools should provide safe and focused classrooms, and modern guardrails for technology that targets children.
I have been a fierce advocate for smartphone-free classrooms so teachers can teach, students can focus, and school can be places for learning.
I will keep fighting to ensure classrooms and educational technologies are used for focus not distraction.
Ensure Responsible Tech Technology should protect privacy, foster innovation, and keep children safe online.
I believe Washington can have both progress and responsibility where innovation fuels the economy and digital tools empower learning and connection without undermining trust, eliminating jobs, or exploiting kids.
I’ve built real expertise in consumer protection and AI policy.
With no ties to Big Tech, I’ll use my experience to make sure technology serves Washington families, not the other way around.
Healthcare The healthcare system is a mess, and we all know it.
Health insurance premiums are skyrocketing, yet we’re getting less coverage and more denials.
Health insurance companies and even Medicare are using AI to decide whether to deny coverage.
Clearly, Washington, D.C., isn’t going to help us.
We need creative, state-based solutions to protect patients.
Insurance companies should not be allowed to let AI decide whether a patient needs a certain cancer treatment or needs to stay in a skilled nursing facility.
Like AI, health insurance companies are heartless.
It’s up to us to empower state regulators to put patients and doctors – not insurance CEOs and shareholders – back in control of healthcare.
We’re fortunate in the 32nd District to have many hospitals and doctors nearby, but Washington has healthcare deserts that are growing.
Over 450,000 Washingtonians don’t have easy access to a pharmacy.
Washington ranks near the bottom of all states for serving people with mental health challenges.
Nearly half of Washington’s counties have zero working psychiatrists.
These healthcare deserts hit communities of color, low-income, and rural communities the hardest.
At the FTC, I fought and stopped healthcare mergers that would have made healthcare harder to find and driven up costs.
We need to invest in public hospitals and work with SEIU and other healthcare unions to train more primary care physicians, psychiatrists, pharmacists, nurses, and other essential healthcare workers.
Businesses caused our healthcare system to become an absolute mess.
Democracy, creativity, expertise, and hard work can provide solutions.
Support For Refugees When people flee war and persecution, we should treat them with compassion and charity.
The Trump Administration is trying to make America inhospitable to refugees through intentional cruelty, such as separating families and deporting refugees back to dangerous nations.
I am deeply opposed to these policies, which are not the values of any true Washingtonian – regardless of party.
Former Republican Governor Dan Evans once said, “There is no place in [the American] dream for a closed society, for a system which denies opportunity because of race, or the accident of birth, or geography or the misfortune of a family.” Governor Evans put his words into action and, in 1975, welcomed Vietnamese refugees, making Washington richer and more vibrant.
We can do so again today.
Over six million Ukrainians are refugees, including friends of mine.
I was in Ukraine just before Russia attacked, having served for years as a representative of the United States and technical advisor to the Ukrainian government on economics and anti-corruption.
I strongly support Ukraine’s fight against imperialism and barbarism.
I also believe Washington has another opportunity to show how our values are better than those of the other Washington.
If elected, I will support policies to provide protection and support to refugees, including classes to learn English and assistance obtaining housing and employment.
I will never back down from a fight to protect freedom and fairness.
I will fight the Trump Administration’s immoral actions and support policies that put Washington State’s values into action.
