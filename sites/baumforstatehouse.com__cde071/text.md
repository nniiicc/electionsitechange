About Charlie Baum Charlie was raised in Knoxville, attended college at Wake Forest University, and earned his Ph.D. from the University of North Carolina where he met his wife, Kelly.
Following graduation from U.N.C.
Chapel Hill in 1999, he and Kelly moved to Murfreesboro where he began teaching economics for the Department of Economics and Finance at Middle Tennessee State University.
He later served as department chair from 2008-2014.
Bloomberg Business Week, U.S.
News and World Report, and the New York Times have cited his research on employment trends, wages, labor force participation rates, and employment benefits.
Charlie was elected in 2018 as a Republican to represent District 37 in the Tennessee State House of Representatives.
He serves as Chair of the Higher Education Subcommittee, and he is a Member of the Finance, Ways, and Means Committee and Education Committee.
He served on the Rutherford County Commission from 2010 to 2018, chairing the commission’s Audit Committee and serving on the commission’s Budget, Finance, and Investment Committee.
Charlie is a past board member for Oaklands Mansion, the Community Foundation of Rutherford County, Murfreesboro Domestic Violence Center, the United Way of Rutherford and Cannon Counties, the Murfreesboro City Schools Foundation, the Linebaugh Library Foundation, and the American Heart Association.
Charlie was a scout with the Boy Scouts of America and achieved the rank of Eagle Scout.
Charlie and Kelly have three adult children, Elizabeth, Anna Jane, and Charles, who graduated from the Siegel schools in Murfreesboro, and they are active members of First United Methodist Church in Murfreesboro.

2024) The Hall Income Tax was eliminated in 2021 New laws for Tennessee · 2025-2026 Education The Tennessee General Assembly passed significant laws in 2025 and 2026 to benefit Tennesseans.
HB 2532 expands school choice by funding 35,000 scholarships in the 2026-27 school year, providing $7,530 per student to attend participating nonpublic schools.
This legislation also updates how the scholarships are distributed to better serve students across Tennessee, prioritizing students from low-income households.
New laws for Tennessee · 2025-2026 Healthcare HB 819 removes outdated certificate of need (CON) requirements to expand competition in Tennessee’s health care marketplace.
The law transitions to a licensure-based system, allowing providers to expand services without unnecessary regulatory barriers.
CON requirements will be phased out for most acute care hospitals by July 1, 2030.
HB 2503 Pending federal approval, this bill will authorize insurance companies to sell short-term, limited-duration health plans (and hospital indemnity insurance policies) to help Tennesseans maintain coverage while transitioning off TennCare, through Tennessee’s Affordable Care Act (ACA) marketplace.
New laws for Tennessee · 2025-2026 Protecting Your Rights HB 469 Second Amendment Rights.
Protects Second Amendment rights for renters by ensuring Tennesseans do not lose their constitutional rights simply because they rent their home.
Prevents landlords from prohibiting lawful firearm possession by tenants for leases entered into, amended, extended, or renewed after January 1, 2027.
HB 1476 Free Speech.
Protects free speech and ensures open debate at Tennessee’s public colleges and universities.
The law prohibits institutions from disinviting or refusing to host speakers based on their viewpoints, including in response to protests or political pressure.
HB 1194 Immigration.
Strengthens E-Verify requirements for businesses by expanding these requirements to all employers in Tennessee, ensuring no illegal immigrants employed in Tennessee can slip through the cracks.
The law lowers the threshold for E-Verify requirements from companies with six employees to one.
New laws for Tennessee · 2025-2026 Helping Families HB 354 Helps protect the elderly from financial abuse.
The legislation aims to strengthen coordination in investigating elder financial exploitation between the TBI and local prosecutors.
HB 571 Protects women-only spaces based on biological sex - protecting privacy, dignity, and safety in restrooms, changing areas, and sleeping quarters in public facilities, including public schools, correctional institutions, and domestic violence shelters.
HB 1457 Allows expectant mothers with high-risk pregnancies to access temporary accessible parking during their pregnancies.
Sponsored and passed by Charlie Charlie’s Legislation This session, Charlie sponsored and passed several bills that will benefit Tennesseans.
HB 494 Portable Benefits for Independent Workers Supports Tennessee’s independent workforce with voluntary portable benefits - promoting employment benefits for the growing number of independent workers in Tennessee, such as gig workers, freelancers, consultants, and musicians, without changing how they are classified for employment.
Tennessee is home to more than 510,000 independent contractors, many of whom do not have access to traditional employment benefits like health care, retirement savings, or paid time off.
This legislation removes barriers that previously prevented businesses from offering benefits to independent workers, while keeping participation completely voluntary for both companies and workers.
Companies can choose to contribute to benefit accounts owned and controlled by the worker, and workers can authorize voluntary contributions from their compensation to build savings for paid family or medical leave, health and dental coverage, retirement savings, and paid time off.
HB 2408 Cracking Down on Spam Calls Designed to limit the number of solicitation and spam phone calls we receive.
The legislation limits the number of calls an entity that makes telephone solicitations - using an automated telephone dialing system to deliver artificial or prerecorded voice messages to residential subscribers in this state - can make to Tennesseans per month.
It also requires these entities to maintain a record of the number of telephone solicitations made each month and report it to the state, and gives Tennessee’s Attorney General new authority to prosecute violators.
HB 1744 Practical Cash Transactions The U.S.
Mint struck its final pennies in November 2025.
This bill allows private businesses and financial institutions to round cash transactions up or down to the nearest five cents when exact change is unavailable.
HB 331 Promoting Home Ownership Promotes home ownership in Tennessee by authorizing an increase, from $4 billion to $5 billion, in the maximum aggregate principal amount the Tennessee Housing Development Agency (THDA) may issue in bonds and notes.
