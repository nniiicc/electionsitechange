It is not a privilege for the well-connected.
It is a human right.
Every family in this district and country deserves care they can easily access and care that won't bankrupt them to receive.
We are in the middle of a doctor shortage.
Nearly half our neighbors are on Medicaid or have no insurance at all.
With a median age of 39.9 in Louisiana’s 1st Congressional District, a significant portion of our population relies on Medicare, Social Security, and Medicaid to cover healthcare and basic needs.
It doesn't stop there.
Too many families have no dental coverage, no vision coverage, and no access to mental health care.
Our rural hospitals are disappearing.
More than 30 rural hospitals in Louisiana are at risk of closing their doors because Washington keeps cutting the funding that keeps them open.
When it comes to women's health, the crisis is even deeper.
Louisiana has some of the highest maternal mortality rates in the country.
We don't have enough OB/GYNs.
We have maternity care deserts.
And Black mothers in this state are dying at rates no family should ever have to accept.
Lauren believes that high quality healthcare includes reproductive justice and bodily autonomy.
Our veterans are caught in this crisis too.
Louisiana is home to tens of thousands of veterans, many in rural parishes where the nearest VA facility is hours away.
When rural hospitals close, veterans lose the community care options they depend on.
They served this country and they shouldn't have to fight another battle just to see a doctor.
For millions of seniors and people with disabilities, Medicaid's promise isn't just coverage.
It means the ability to stay in their own home or near their own family instead of being sent to a nursing home or institution.
That's what Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) makes possible.
However, while Medicaid is required to cover nursing home care, HCBS is still classified as an "optional" benefit and one that states can scale back whenever budgets get tight or the political winds shift.
That gap has always been a vulnerability.
Now it's being tested directly.
In June 2026, the Department of Justice issued new guidance signaling the administration may not enforce the community-integration mandate established by the Supreme Court's landmark Olmstead decision.
That decision comes as HCBS is already under strain nationwide, following the largest Medicaid cuts in the program's history.
The message this sends is unmistakable: the guardrails that kept disabled and aging Americans out of institutions are loosening, right as the safety net underneath them is being cut.
Lauren knows what's at stake here personally, through her own family's experience with disability advocacy, and as a special education teacher who has spent 17 years fighting for the dignity and independence of the students and families she has served.

This will help incentivize doctors to practice in Louisiana and other underserved states.
