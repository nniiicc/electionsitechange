Missourians are doing their part.
They’re working hard, paying taxes, raising families, caring for parents, keeping local businesses alive, and holding their communities together.
The problem is that the economy stopped working for the people who make it run.
When working people can afford to live, work, care for their families, and participate fully in their communities, Missouri’s economy gets stronger.

It’s time for the state to fully invest in public education to help build a healthy economy.
Strong public schools help communities attract businesses, workers, and families.
Employers expect a well-trained workforce.
As a former elected school board member and longtime educator, Suzanne will work to ensure our public schools and universities have the resources they need to provide quality education for their students.
She will work toward full funding for Lincoln University, including stable core funding and the land-grant match Missouri is obligated to provide.
Last session, Republican leaders made education funding less stable.
They advanced a plan to cut Lincoln University’s core funding by almost 40%, they left K-12 schools $190 million short, and they launched a task force to rewrite the funding formula to keep public education funding flat.
That kind of uncertainty is exactly why our community needs a representative who will fight for reliable, fair funding for our public schools and universities.
Public dollars should strengthen the schools that serve our communities.
Suzanne will advocate for a single set of rules and full accountability for any school receiving public funds, whether public or private.
Our state suffers when public schools are underfunded.
Strong schools are one of the best economic investments Missouri can make.

It’s time for lawmakers to address affordable and accessible healthcare that helps keep Missouri communities livable, workable, and attractive for growth.
Communities struggle if people have to drive an hour in a health crisis, if expectant mothers cannot find maternity care, or if people cannot get basic checkups before a manageable problem becomes an emergency.
If we want families to stay in Missouri, workers to move here, and businesses to invest here, we have to protect the basic healthcare services that make a community desirable.
Suzanne believes that every woman in Missouri deserves safe healthcare.
Missouri rates 44th in the country for maternal deaths.
Such poor maternal health is a warning sign that Missouri’s healthcare system is under serious strain.
When obstetric units close, doctors face legal confusion in emergencies, and healthcare workers move out-of-state, Missourians pay the price.
Young families, healthcare professionals, and employers pay attention to whether a community is a safe, stable, and reliable place to have children, raise a family, recruit workers, and invest for the long term.
The State Legislature has a responsibility to protect access to basic healthcare, keep hospitals and clinics open, and let medical decisions be guided by patients and their doctors.
