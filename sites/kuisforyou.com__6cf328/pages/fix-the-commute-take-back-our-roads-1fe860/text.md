Cut-through traffic, unpredictable commutes, and growing congestion are straining Newtown’s roads.
Michelle Embree Ku has a plan to push for I‑84 improvements, secure federal funding, and strengthen coordination on key state routes—delivering real relief for residents and businesses.
Since announcing my candidacy for the 106th State House seat, I’ve been engaging with Newtown residents to hear their concerns.
A recurring theme is the pressing issue of traffic—speeding, noise, congestion, and the time wasted navigating our increasingly busy roads.
Newtown, as one of the largest towns in Connecticut, finds its roads integral to daily life.
However, with growing congestion on I‑84, more frustrated commuters are choosing to cut through our town, crowding our local roads.
This issue isn’t just a local inconvenience, but a symptom of a broader problem that needs urgent attention.
When elected to represent Newtown in the House of Representatives, I’ll press for the expansion of I‑84 to three lanes from Waterbury to the New York state line.
This goal is not without precedent.
Since 2001, several studies, including the “I‑84 Corridor Deficiencies/Needs Study” and a Draft Environmental Impact Statement (DEIS) conducted from 2009 to 2011, have highlighted the critical need for improvements.
Despite these studies, the DEIS was never published, and subsequent action stalled due to a lack of funding.
The need, however, remains as pressing as ever.
While I understand that Hartford may not be ready to allocate billions of dollars based solely on one representative’s agenda, it’s incumbent on whoever represents Newtown — every town in this region, in fact — to push for progress on this issue.
Anyone who drives I-84 to or from Newtown during a weekday commute already knows how unpredictable the commute time is, and if you drive in Newtown when I-84 backs up, you are well aware of how a 20-minute ride across town can double.
Some of that congestion is avoidable with faster incident response.
California addressed this by keeping tow trucks on retainer along its most congested freeways specifically to pull disabled vehicles off the road within minutes instead of hours — a program that assists roughly 650,000 motorists a year and is built around a simple fact: every extra minute a stalled car sits in a lane multiplies the delay for everyone stacking up behind it.
Connecticut should look seriously at a dedicated incident-response fleet for I-84, so a flat tire or dead battery doesn’t turn into a two-hour backup through Newtown.
Just west of us, a reversible flex lane between Exits 3 and 7 is being built to ease rush-hour congestion, with construction targeted for 2027.
These are incremental fixes — shoulder-running lanes, ramp reconfigurations, incident response, interchange redesigns — that can deliver relief faster than a single, decades-long widening project ever could.
Ideally, a robust public rail option would take pressure off I-84 the way Metro-North already does for commuters along the I-95 corridor.
But Connecticut hasn’t built that alternative for the I-84 corridor, and until it does, we don’t have the luxury of waiting.
We have to invest in the largest east-west highway running through the middle of our state, because right now it’s the only option most of our residents have.
That’s exactly why I’m committed to building a coalition of legislators who’ll advocate for their communities along I-84, ensuring that our collective voice is heard — not just in Newtown, but from Danbury through Waterbury, and beyond.
Every town along this corridor has skin in the game: commuters losing hours a week to backups, small businesses that depend on reliable delivery windows, first responders who need clear routes, and families deciding whether Newtown’s commute times still make sense for their lives.
When one town lobbies alone, it’s easy for Hartford to defer.
When a dozen legislators show up together with a shared ask, it becomes much harder to ignore.
This is a matter of quality of life as well as economic development — and it’s a fight we win by building power together.
Additionally, I’ll do what I can to support Connecticut’s access to federal funding by supporting legislation that bans open containers of alcohol in motor vehicles.
This measure is crucial not only for public safety, but also to ensure Connecticut is eligible for federal transportation funding.
Currently, Connecticut is one of the few states that doesn’t comply with the federal Transportation Equity Act for the 21st Century, and this hinders our ability to secure crucial federal funds for road construction.
And finally, I’ll prioritize the work to secure improvements on the state roads.
Traffic has been a growing issue in town as new development is approved.
The infrastructure needs must be part of planning, and coordination between the municipality and state is vital to ensuring that routes 6, 302, 34 and 25 will safely accommodate the growing population.
Addressing these issues requires concerted effort and collaboration.
I’m eager to work with fellow legislators and local leaders to bring meaningful solutions to the table.
Together, we can make a real difference in improving our roads and easing traffic congestion for all Connecticut residents.
Better roads won’t happen by waiting—they’ll happen by fighting for Newtown.
Vote for Michelle Embree Ku.
She’s ready to lead with fresh urgency and is focused on practical results.
