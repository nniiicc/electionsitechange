Income tax is the primary source of revenue for federal, state and local governments.
With increasing demand for public services, the pressure to raise taxes is tremendous.

New Hampshire can deliver these services by growing the private sector, increasing the proportion of revenues from business rather than taxing individuals' incomes.

Why did you decide to run for re-election?
To do my best to reduce property taxes for Nashua.
Local casinos, such as the Nash, are required to pay about 10% of their slot profits to regional charities.
This is now nearly $30 million per year.
I believe some of this money should be given back to Nashua to reduce property taxes.
What have been some of your major accomplishments during your time in office?
I cosponsored a bi-partisan bill to establish a charitable gaming study commission to look at the overall situation.
A report is due out shortly.
Serving on the House tax committee (Ways and Means) for eight years, I have prevented attempts to create an income tax and a sales tax.
My committee also limits what NH can spend by providing revenue estimates.
NH has to have a balanced budget.
What are some of the major challenges facing Hillsborough District 10?
How would you address these challenges?
One of the biggest upcoming challenges in Ward 9 (Hillsborough 10) is the projected explosion in traffic on Spit Brook Road from the planned Tara Casino.
This expansion was just approved by the Nashua Planning Board and will bring an estimated 7,400 additional cars into Spit Brook Road every day.
We have no supermarkets and have to drive Spit Brook Road to cross the Everett Turnpike for groceries or going shopping.
There is also a safety concern with school buses.
I have just filed a lawsuit to stop this additional traffic.
I believe the casino should pay for direct access to the Everett Turnpike and not clog up Spit Brook road.
What are a few of your ideas to move District 10 forward?
Affordable housing remains a problem.
Folks who have lived here forever can't afford the high and increasing property taxes.
We need a cap on taxes for seniors.
Accessory dwelling units should be readily permitted to allow modest expansion of housing for those homeowners who desire them.
What are some of the differences between this election and the 2024 race?
The main difference from 2024 is the casino expansion in Nashua.
Smaller operators (Lucky Moose, River, Boston Billiards) have been sold to major out-of-state corporations (Delaware North, ECL Entertainment).
Nashua gets no license fee.
Massachusetts gets at least $25 million.
This is not fair.
