With membership having surged 62 percent, there was plenty to celebrate.
The gathering offered a warm blend of fellowship, fine dining, and insightful political discussion that underscored the group’s ongoing commitment to strengthening Republican values across the region.
Deborah Fischer, President of the PCCRW, and her executive board welcomed guests, including Matamoras Mayor Cory Homer and his wife Tiffany, as well as Blooming Grove Township Supervisor Tim Morey and his wife Danielle.
Other attendees included female elected officials such as the Recorder of Deeds, Prothonotary, Auditors, Judges of Elections, GOP Committee members, and more.
The evening’s highlight was guest speaker Danica Hartenfels, the Republican candidate for Pennsylvania State Representative in the 189th District.
Members had the opportunity to meet Hartenfels, hear her perspective on key issues facing the district, and engage directly with a candidate working to advance common-sense principles at the state level.
Her appearance provided both inspiration and practical insight for attendees focused on local and statewide advocacy.
Guests selected from a thoughtful menu of entrées—Fish &amp; Chips, Turkey Pot Pie, or Pappardelle Pasta Bolognese—accompanied by a salad with choice of dressing and a classic Crème Brulé for dessert.
The comfortable setting of the Apple Valley Restaurant created an ideal backdrop for conversation and connection among members.
Organizers, led by contacts such as Theresa Brown, carefully planned the successful event.
The PCCRW, established in 1949, continues its long tradition of fostering community and political engagement, as reflected in its motto: “Working to keep Pike County Republican Strong!” All are encouraged to become members, and current members are invited to stay connected through the group’s Facebook page, The Pike County Council of Republican Women, and via email at pikecountyrepublicanwomen@gmail.com.

Uplifting Women in Business in Monroe County March 25, 2026 March is Women’s History Month, a time to recognize the impact women have made throughout history and continue to make every day in our communities.
Here in Monroe County, that impact is especially clear through the growing number of women leading businesses, supporting one another, and helping shape the future of our local economy.
Recently, organizations like the Pocono Chamber of Commerce have highlighted the importance of supporting and empowering women in business across the Pocono region.
Through initiatives led by their Women in Business Council, they are creating opportunities for mentorship, networking, and professional growth.
These efforts help women succeed not only individually, but as part of a stronger, more connected business community.
Building Stronger Businesses and Stronger Communities Women-owned and women-led businesses are an essential part of our local economy.
They bring innovation, create jobs, and contribute to the vibrancy of our communities.
Just as important is the culture of collaboration and support that many of these leaders foster.
Programs and events focused on women in business create spaces where professionals can connect, share experiences, and build confidence.
Whether it is a young entrepreneur just starting out or an established leader mentoring others, these connections help strengthen the entire region.
Why This Matters for Our Region When we talk about economic growth, workforce development, and community investment, supporting women in business is part of that conversation.
Creating opportunities for leadership, education, and advancement benefits not just individuals, but families and communities as a whole.
As a candidate for state representative, I believe it is important to highlight and support initiatives like these.
While many of these programs take place at the local level, state leadership still plays a role in ensuring access to resources, encouraging small business growth, and supporting workforce development efforts that include everyone.
Looking Ahead Women’s History Month is not just about reflecting on the past.
It is also about continuing to move forward.
By supporting local organizations, uplifting women entrepreneurs, and encouraging the next generation of leaders, we can help ensure that Monroe County remains a place where opportunity is available to all.
The work being done right here in our community is a reminder that progress happens when people come together, support one another, and invest in a shared future.
