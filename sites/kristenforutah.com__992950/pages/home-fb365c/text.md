Trusted Leadership
- Issues That Matter Food Security: Food security begins with keeping Utah farms and ranches viable.
Utah’s family farms and ranches are disappearing at an alarming rate, and with them, an essential part of our food security.
Kristen is at the forefront of efforts to strengthen local agriculture by protecting water rights, supporting the next generation of farmers and ranchers, and making it possible for family farms to remain profitable.
By working directly with Utah’s agricultural communities, she is fighting to keep food production local, sustainable, and in the hands of the families who have built it.
Data Privacy: Convenience or security should not become a justification for unchecked surveillance.
A free society depends on the ability to live, work, learn, and communicate without being constantly tracked.
As technology becomes woven into everyday life, the ability to monitor and collect information about ordinary Utahns has expanded, from automatic license plate readers and school-issued devices to home networks and personal phones.
Kristen has sponsored legislation to establish clear limits on the government’s use, retention, sharing, and access to personal data, including repeated efforts to strengthen protections surrounding automatic license plate readers.
Kristen believes Utahns deserve both transparency about how their information is being used and meaningful protection from unnecessary or unchecked government surveillance.
Taxpayer Stewardship: Government should only take money from Utahns reluctantly, spend it deliberately, and be transparent about what it is doing with it.
Kristen believes Utah should keep taxes as low as responsibly possible while demanding transparency, accountability, and measurable value from government spending.
She has fought tax increases at the grassroots level, helped lead the successful Utah tax referendum effort, and continues to support policies that give taxpayers greater transparency and a stronger voice in how their money is collected and spent.
- Shared Principles Elections give the public the opportunity to choose someone to serve as their representative in government.
It is important to understand the principles through which a representative approaches the issues that affect our families, communities, and state.
And while words and campaign promises matter, an individual’s record of service is an even better measure of how they will represent you.
For more than 30 years, Kristen has been actively engaged in Utah’s political and legislative process, first as a citizen advocate and now as an elected representative.
Throughout that time, the consistent thread has been her commitment to the Constitution of the United States and to the principle that government exists to protect individual liberty, not to unnecessarily restrict it.
As a legislator, Kristen continues to bring those principles to the Capitol: advocating for limited and accountable government, individual and parental rights, election integrity, responsible taxation, privacy, and the freedom of Utahns to make decisions for themselves and their families.
The principles that guided Kristen as a citizen advocate continue to guide her as a legislator.
What has changed is her ability to put those principles into action.
- Experience Kristen Chevrier brings more than 30 years of experience in Utah’s political and legislative landscape, including more than two decades advocating for individual liberty in Utah and Washington, D.C.
Today, she brings that experience directly to the Utah Legislature, where she has worked to turn those principles into meaningful legislation and lasting policy.
As House District 54 Chair, Kristen pioneered the Legislative Collaboration Committees, creating a stronger connection between legislators and delegates and encouraging open discussion on the issues that matter most to their communities.
In the Legislature, she has sponsored and advanced legislation addressing data privacy, consumer protection, education, food security, and individual liberty.
Kristen’s legislative record includes HB 402, which became law in 2025 and established restrictions on certain food additives in public schools, and HB 357, which became law in 2026 and strengthened protections for motor vehicle data privacy.
She has also continued to pursue legislation establishing reasonable limits on government use of automatic license plate readers, demonstrating her commitment to protecting individual privacy as technology rapidly evolves.
Kristen’s leadership extends beyond the Capitol.
She has held numerous leadership roles in the Republican Party, co-founded and serves as executive director of a Utah advocacy organization focused on informed consent and consumer protection, and created “The Caucus Booklet” in both 2014 and 2024 to help Utahns understand and preserve Utah’s Caucus Convention Primary system.
Her experience has built a strong network of relationships across Utah and nationally.
She continues to put those relationships, experience, and principles to work for the people of House District 54.

Meet Kristen
Kristen received a Master’s Degree in English from BYU, where she met her husband, Brian.
Kristen and Brian have lived in Utah for over 30 years.
They have raised five children in Highland, and are grandparents of three little Utahns.
Kristen Chevrier has been a leader in the Utah political process for over 30 years and loves to involve her family, friends, and neighbors.
She has served in numerous leadership positions in the Republican Party, helped with many campaigns, written and edited Party training materials, platform planks, proposals, and, as a strong supporter of Utah’s Caucus Convention Primary system, edited and helped compile “The Caucus Booklet” –both the 2014 and the 2024 versions, in an effort to educate the public on the spectacular value of the Caucus Convention Primary system to our Republican form of government.
She has taught free parliamentary procedure classes to delegates, organized and hosted large scale meet the candidate events and debates, and spent decades recruiting and training people to effectively participate in the political process.
Kristen has been a citizen lobbyist in both the Utah legislature and in Washington, DC, advocating for various issues for over 20 years.
Kristen has led on protections for home schools; was a leader in efforts to overturn Common Core; has led and continues to lead efforts to protect and restore our caucus convention primary system; was a key driver of the highly successful tax referendum in 2019; and currently leads in protecting individual and parental rights in health care and education, restoring the doctor/patient relationship, reducing agency overreach and reigning in rule-making authority, prohibiting medical mandates, protecting our food supply, restoring election integrity, and enhancing data privacy.
As House District 54 Chair, Kristen is leading the pilot program for the Legislative Collaboration Committees, which bring our legislators and delegates together to discuss and work to resolve critical issues.
She is the co-founder and executive director of a Utah advocacy group focused on informed consent and consumer protection.
Her work is all volunteer and is inspired by her desire to heal chronic illness in her own children, her desire to protect the health of her grandchildren, and the desire to make America, as a whole, healthy again.
Throughout her advocacy she has built relationships locally and nationally that facilitate her efforts.
Kristen has shown her dedication to House District 54, Utah County, and the State of Utah with a lifetime of service.
Kristen is dependable, communicative, respectful, and knowledgeable and experienced on both issues and procedural processes.
