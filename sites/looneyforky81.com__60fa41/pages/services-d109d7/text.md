Protecting Waiver Access dozens of Kentuckians traveled to Frankfort to urge state leaders to protect Kentucky's 1915(c) Medicaid waiver programs.
These waivers allow children and adults with disabilities or complex medical needs to receive skilled care in their homes and communities rather than being forced into hospitals, nursing homes, or other institutional settings away from their families.
The need for these services continues to grow.
As of this year, nearly 19,000 Kentuckians are on waiting lists for Medicaid waiver services, and some families face waits of up to 10 years before receiving assistance.
Kentucky has the third-highest disability rate in the nation.
Approximately 394,000 Kentuckians have a developmental disability, yet there are only 34,566 funded slots across the state's three primary developmental disability waiver programs.
Despite these growing needs, Kentucky Republicans passed House Bill 2 this year, which includes significant changes to the state's Medicaid program.
Among them, my opponent Deanna Gordon voted in favor of HB 2, the bill that would land this great blow to the families already struggling.

Defending Kentuckians from mass surveillance Flock cameras are automated license plate readers (ALPRs) that capture a vehicle's license plate, make, model, color, and the time and location where it was seen.
They’re supposed to help recover stolen vehicles and solve crimes.
While the purpose may be noble, this technology enables the routine collection of location data on millions of drivers who are not suspected of any wrongdoing.
Civil liberties organizations have already raised concerns that this information can be stored in cloud databases, shared across thousands of law enforcement agencies, and used to reconstruct a person's travel patterns without a warrant.
These concerns have led to lawsuits, legislative proposals, and the termination or suspension of Flock camera programs in several states over questions of privacy, transparency, data sharing, and government oversight.
Make me your Representative and I’ll make sure Kentucky follows suit.
