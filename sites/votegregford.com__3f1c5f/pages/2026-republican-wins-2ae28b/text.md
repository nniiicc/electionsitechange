SCDOT Modernization Act Republicans passed a statewide overhaul of how South Carolina builds and manages roads.
The law cuts red tape, speeds up project delivery, and puts stronger accountability on SCDOT so taxpayers are not left waiting year after year.
Record infrastructure investment The 2026 state budget included a record commitment to roads and bridges — without raising taxes.
More money for the work people can see: pavement, bridges, drainage, and projects that shorten the commute.
The standard is simple: shorter drives, safer roads, and homes that stay dry.
Affordability Keep More of What You Earn Making South Carolina a place where families can build a future means cutting the tax burden, not growing government.
Historic income tax relief House Republicans delivered a major income-tax cut, lowered rates, and put the state on a path toward a flatter, simpler system — closer to zero income tax — so working families and small businesses keep more of their own money.
More than $1 billion in tax relief A responsible, balanced budget invested in roads, classrooms, public safety, and workforce development while returning more than $1 billion in tax relief.
No tax increase.
Relief that reaches households Republicans also cut the effective property tax rate on boats, delivered small-business tax relief, and increased the Homestead Exemption from $50,000 to $75,000 for qualifying seniors and disabled homeowners.
That means a larger share of a home’s value is shielded from property taxes, and more of a fixed income stays in the household.
Statewide What Else Republicans Delivered in 2026 The House advanced one of the strongest conservative agendas in state history: lower taxes, safer communities, stronger families, better roads, and a government that answers to the people.
Protecting children and parents New social media safeguards give parents more control and limit addictive design aimed at kids.
South Carolina also banned AI-generated child sexual abuse material, strengthened penalties for exploiting minors, expanded school choice, required school boards to livestream meetings, restored truth in grading, and advanced parental rights over education, health, and upbringing.
Public safety Republicans cracked down on organized retail theft, created a fentanyl-induced homicide offense, passed a hands-free driving law, gave law enforcement more tools against street takeovers, and backed officers, first responders, and K-9s.

Common sense in schools and communities The House stood up for girls’ sports and private spaces, classroom authority for teachers, and privacy in bathrooms, locker rooms, and overnight accommodations.
Jobs, farms, and consumers Republicans supported roadside markets, American-made materials in public projects, truth-in-labeling for shrimp, and protections for South Carolina agriculture.
Insurance and tort reforms were advanced to help lower costs and restore fairness.
Freedom and accountability The agenda included election integrity, limits on foreign ownership of sensitive land, cooperation with federal immigration enforcement, judicial selection reform, and a government that stays answerable to taxpayers.
