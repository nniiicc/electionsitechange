FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE January 20, 2026 Session Kicks Off The first week of the 101st Session of the South Dakota Legislature is in the books.
As is typical, it was largely ceremonial, informational, and organizational.
While it didn’t yet have the faster pace or heavier committee agendas that come later, the opening days play an important role in setting the tone and direction for the weeks ahead.
We began the session by hearing the State of the State from Governor Larry Rhoden, the State of the Judiciary from Chief Justice Steven Jensen, and the State of the Tribes from President Kathleen Wooden Knife of the Rosebud Sioux Tribe.
Governor Rhoden emphasized civility and outlined policy goals relating to property tax relief, public safety, and economic development.
Chief Justice Jensen highlighted the ongoing work of South Dakota’s Unified Judicial System, including strategic planning, efforts to strengthen indigent defense, expanded probation and treatment courts, and the importance of judicial security and workforce stability.
President Wooden Knife shared thoughtful remarks on cooperation and respectful relationships between tribal governments and the state.
An especially memorable moment came as part of our preparations to mark the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence.
We were treated to a historical portrayal of the document’s drafter, “Thomas Jefferson,” which provided both a bit of levity and an opportunity to reflect on the principles that form the foundation of our system of government.
It was also a good reminder of the responsibility we have as citizens to ensure the delicate balance of powers crafted by the Founders endures for future generations.
As is also typical during the first week of session, we had the opportunity to absorb all sorts of information.
For example, we received an update on the status of the Rural Health Transformation Funds, of which South Dakota was recently awarded $189 million.
These funds are intended to support innovative approaches to delivering care, strengthening local systems, and ensuring that South Dakotans don’t have to travel unreasonable distances to receive needed services.
Understanding how these dollars will be used, and what outcomes they intend to produce, will be an early-session priority.
We also received a presentation from the South Dakota National Guard.
Their service – both here at home and when called upon nationally – is exceptional, and it was good to receive an update on the status of current operations and future needs.
Beyond formal business, the first week provides the opportunity to reconnect.
It was good to catch up with colleagues, administration officials, and old friends around the Capitol.
With effective lawmaking often depending as much on trust and communication as it does on policy details, these relationships matter.
I also had a chance to connect with folks from the Watertown area who were in Pierre during the week, including leaders from Lake Area Tech, Codington-Clark Electric, and the Senior Health Information and Insurance Education (SHIINE) program.
As we move into the coming weeks, the pace will pick up quickly.
Committees will begin hearing substantive bills, amendments will be debated, and proposals will start moving to the House floor.
This is where the real work of the session takes shape.
