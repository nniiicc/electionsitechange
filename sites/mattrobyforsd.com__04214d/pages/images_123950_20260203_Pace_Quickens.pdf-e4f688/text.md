FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE February 3, 2026 Pace Quickens Weeks two and three of the 101st Session of the South Dakota Legislature brought a noticeable shift in pace.
Committees moved into more substantive work, bills began advancing to the House floor, and the focus turned from orientation to decision-making.
These weeks are where priorities start to take shape and policy discussions become more concrete.
The House has passed several bills I supported that are particularly relevant to District 5 and to communities across our state.
One of those was HB 1044, which approved the use of Rural Health Transformation Funds.
South Dakota was awarded these dollars to support innovative health care delivery models, strengthen local systems, and improve access to care—especially in rural areas.
Ensuring these funds are used responsibly and effectively will be an ongoing discussion as additional awards are made over the next five years We also passed HB 1049, a bill protecting our electric infrastructure from being owned, controlled, or operated by foreign countries of concern.
Reliable and secure energy systems are essential for public safety, economic stability, and national security, and this legislation helps safeguard infrastructure we all depend on.
Addressing workforce needs was another theme.
HB 1035 modified CPA licensure requirements to maintain strong consumer protections while allowing an alternative pathway to licensure in response to ongoing workforce shortages.
Similarly, HB 1029 revised provisions related to the practice of addiction counseling, helping ensure South Dakotans have access to qualified professionals while maintaining appropriate standards of care.
The House also passed HB 1101, which prohibits insurance companies from declining or limiting coverage solely because an individual is a living organ donor.
This commonsense measure ensures that those willing to make an extraordinary, lifesaving sacrifice are not penalized for doing so.
Several bills addressed education, natural resources, and local government needs.
HB 1047 authorized construction to expand the Blue Dog Lake Fish Hatchery, supporting fish stocking efforts and outdoor recreation opportunities that are important to our region.
SB 84 increased income thresholds for the Partners in Education tuition program, expanding access for more families.
HB 1130 allows school districts to pay for property insurance out of capital outlay funds, providing additional flexibility as districts manage rising insurance costs.
Beyond legislation, the past two weeks included several meaningful events and opportunities to learn.
The Legislature took time to memorialize legislators who passed away in the last year, honoring their service and contributions to our state.
I also received a briefing on the work of the Public Utilities Commission, which plays a critical role in overseeing utilities and infrastructure development.
Outside the Capitol, I had the chance to meet with South Dakota Community Support Providers, attend a reception with the South Dakota Catholic Conference and Bishop DeGrood, and participate in the Governor’s Prayer Breakfast featuring Will Graham (Billy Graham’s grandson).
It was also good to celebrate South Dakota Tourism alongside a group from Watertown and to spend time at the Mickelson Criminal Justice Center, where I had the opportunity to test my pistol skills in the annual Attorney General’s Shootout.
It's evident my aim needs a little work.
