FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE February 10, 2026 Getting Busy Week four of the 101st Session marked another busy stretch at the Capitol.
Committee agendas are overflowing, floor sessions are getting longer, and the issues discussed are getting more contentious.
The bill filing deadline has now come and gone.
In total, 570 bills were filed this session, not including resolutions.
That represents an increase of 81 bills compared to last year.
The increase from the first year of a term to the second year of a term is typical, though the overall volume is near a record.
With our time constraints, the large number of bills increases the likelihood that a bill will be defeated (rather than amended) because it isn’t “ready for prime time.” That’s also why you might see a bill defeated despite having an appealing title.
When that occurs, I’d encourage you to seek out the recording of the hearing or floor session to gain the full picture.
You can find them all by navigating to sdlegislature.gov.
I have introduced several bills of my own this session.
These include measures to make an appropriation for Lake Area Technical College; clarify the ability of nonprofits to fundraise through pull-tab machines; allow attorneys to issue subpoenas in administrative actions; require disclosure of third-party litigation funding; request onetime funding for a study on public transportation in South Dakota; and penalize “doxxing” of law enforcement and judges.
Bills of note that had my support and passed the House in Week 4 include: • • SB 4, which provides landlords an additional week to return security deposits.
This change is intended to reduce situations where landlords feel pressured to overpay for repairs or replace items that could instead be fixed, ultimately helping keep costs down for tenants while maintaining fairness for both parties.
HB 1107, which allows greater flexibility when donated land can no longer be used for the purpose it was originally intended.
This bill provides a court additional flexibility to repurpose donated land when conditions change, making the original intended use impossible or impractical. • • • SB 12, which provides a property tax refund from prior years for qualifying veterans and surviving spouses.
This is one of more than 25 bills this year aimed at providing property tax relief, with much more to come on this topic.
SB 46, 47, and 48, which were part of a package of bills that strengthened South Dakota’s open meeting laws.
These measures reinforce the public’s right to know and help ensure governmental decisions are made openly and with accountability.
HB 1056, which requires the state to submit a federal waiver request to exclude soda from the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP).
This bill reflects the belief that this important safety net program should be preserved to encourage healthier food choices.
Last week also included valuable conversations with District 5 residents who took time to be in Pierre.
I appreciated the opportunity to talk with municipal government and public power employees from the City of Watertown and Watertown Municipal Utilities, and to discuss healthcare and behavioral health priorities with Watertown healthcare leaders.
I also enjoyed learning about new initiatives from local homebuilders (check out www.beprobeproud.org), chatting with local Realtors about housing (and chili!), visiting with our school superintendents about education priorities, and supporting South Dakota Public Broadcasting alongside advocates from District 5.
And it was a great honor to have dinner with several students from Lake Area Tech’s Student Voice.
It is always encouraging to see familiar faces in Pierre – people who step away from their busy schedules to advocate for their professions and for causes that matter to them.
Those conversations help ensure the policies we debate are informed by realworld experience.
As always, hearing from constituents is invaluable.
