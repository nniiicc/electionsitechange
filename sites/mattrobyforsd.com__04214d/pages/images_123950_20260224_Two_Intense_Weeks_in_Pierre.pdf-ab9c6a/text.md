FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE February 24, 2026 Two Intense Weeks in Pierre The best word to describe the last two weeks in Pierre is intense.
With “crossover day” on February 24 (the deadline for bills to pass out of their chamber of origin) and the tendency for controversial bills to be dropped right at the filing deadline, high-emotion issues tend to pile up during this period.
We saw proposals targeting teachers, health care, economic development, and agriculture.
Thankfully, a governing majority of common-sense legislators were able to stop the most harmful proposals and keep our focus on practical solutions that strengthen South Dakota rather than divide it.
While many of the debates have required playing defense, several good bills moved out of the House during this stretch, including: • • • • • • • Expanding the Sturgis Readiness Center to support our National Guard.
Adding coaches to the list of mandatory reporters of child abuse or neglect.
Strengthening protections for seniors and vulnerable adults against financial exploitation.
Providing additional property tax relief for disabled veterans and surviving spouses.
Adjusting assessment methodologies for owner-occupied homes to bring greater stability and fairness.
Protecting patients from retroactive claim denials by health insurers.
Licensing non-medical home care agencies to improve accountability and standards of care.
We also passed bills authorizing counties and municipalities to issue a license for a cigar bar and enabling municipal capital improvement funding tools.
Not every bill will make headlines, but many will make a practical difference in communities across the state.
Two of the largest issues of the session – property taxes and data centers – remain unresolved.
On property taxes, there is broad agreement that some form of relief is warranted.
The debate centers on how significant that relief will be and how to structure it responsibly.
Several proposals remain alive and continue to move through the process.
I expect we will see bills become law that provide relief at the margins and increase transparency.
Achieving substantial structural reform, however, will be a heavy lift.
On data centers, the conversation has focused on balance.
If South Dakota hosts these facilities, we must ensure their negative externalities – such as upward pressure on energy prices – are minimized.
At the same time, we must determine whether we can remain competitive with other states.
The central question is whether to offer a form of sales and use tax exemption on equipment purchases.
That decision will likely determine whether large-scale data center projects move forward here.
Several of my own initiatives are still advancing – including legislation providing a criminal penalty for doxxing law enforcement officers and judges and a bill ensuring subpoena authority for attorneys in administrative proceedings.
A bill providing clarity for nonprofit fundraising through mechanical pull tabs is awaiting the Governor’s signature, along with municipal and building code updates I agreed to carry in the House.
Although there are plenty of policy fights left to be had, the discussions over the remaining few weeks of session tend to be largely budget related.
One of my primary focuses will be HB 1202 – a one-time funding proposal for Lake Area Technical College.
The bill would support construction of a facility to launch three high-demand programs at LATC: industrial refrigeration, HVAC, and water treatment.
Expanding this capacity strengthens our regional economy and addresses real workforce needs.
If you support workforce development and Lake Area Tech, I encourage you to contact legislators – both locally and statewide – and urge support for HB 1202.
Although the last couple weeks have been “intense,” I’m honored to be doing this work on behalf of District 5.
The long hours and emotional debates are balanced by the camaraderie of colleagues and the reminder that the bills we vote on are not academic exercises – they have real-world impact on our families, schools, businesses, and communities.
