FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE March 31, 2026 Recap of the 101st South Dakota Legislative Session With the conclusion of Veto Day, the 101st Legislative Session in South Dakota is now officially adjourned.
As with any session, the outcome is a mix of meaningful progress, missed opportunities, and lessons that will be carried forward.
My Bills and Outcomes This year, I was proud to sponsor and support several bills aimed at improving processes, strengthening communities, and updating outdated statutes.
Several measures successfully made it across the finish line and have now been signed into law.
These include allowing attorney-issued subpoenas in administrative proceedings, expanding nonprofit fundraising opportunities through mechanical pull-tabs, updating building codes, modernizing municipal statutes, and adjusting the timeline for landlords to return security deposits.
Each of these represents a practical improvement to how government and everyday systems function.
Legislation prohibiting the doxxing of judges and law enforcement officers has also been signed by the Governor – an important step in protecting those who serve in sensitive public safety roles.
Not every effort was successful.
A proposal to secure one-time funding for a new Trades Center at Lake Area Technical College fell short, despite strong local and regional advocacy.
While disappointing, I remain committed to advocating for technical education and workforce development in future sessions.
Another bill was withdrawn after productive conversations with the Department of Transportation, which agreed to pursue a community transit study using existing resources – an example of how good policy can still move forward, even without formal legislation.
Key Legislation I Supported This session also saw the passage of several significant measures aimed at addressing some of the biggest concerns facing South Dakotans.
Foremost among them was meaningful property tax relief.
Through a combination of one-time funding and capturing additional revenue from the sales tax sunset, homeowners can expect noticeable reductions in their property tax burden in the coming years.
Additional flexibility at the county level – through a county option sales tax – provides another tool to further reduce property taxes in counties that have been impacted most by recent property tax increases.
We also passed consumer protections for virtual currency kiosks, strengthened safeguards for vulnerable individuals against financial exploitation, increased penalties for those who interfere with the free exercise of religion, and authorized local governments to issue licenses for cigar bars.
These measures reflect a continued focus on public safety, individual rights, and local control.
Data Centers: Growth with Guardrails As I’ve discussed in prior columns, large data centers are increasingly considering South Dakota as a location.
These projects can bring significant investment and tangible benefits – but they also have the potential to place real demands on resources.
This year, we took important steps to ensure that growth in this sector happens responsibly.
Legislation passed requiring data centers to pay their own way when it comes to the electric generation they require, rather than shifting those costs onto existing ratepayers.
We also reinforced the authority of local governments to have a meaningful say in where these facilities are sited and put safeguards in place to protect our water resources.
However, one key piece remains unresolved.
We could not reach agreement addressing the tax treatment of data centers.
As a result, South Dakota remains at a competitive disadvantage compared to other states that have adopted more targeted tax policies for this industry.
That is an issue I expect will continue to be part of the conversation going forward.
Investing in South Dakota’s Future In addition to policy changes, the Legislature approved targeted investments across the state.
These included funding for rural healthcare, infrastructure improvements, water and environmental projects, and support for victims of crime.
From riparian buffer initiatives to youth career training and communitybased programs, these investments are designed to strengthen both our economy and quality of life.
And, of course, the Legislature fulfilled its core constitutional responsibility by passing a balanced state budget.
This process involves months of detailed work by appropriators from across the political spectrum, carefully reviewing each request to ensure taxpayer dollars are spent responsibly and in alignment with the state’s priorities.
Conduct and Culture While I am proud of much of the work accomplished this session, I would be remiss if I did not acknowledge some troubling trends in how that work was carried out.
We saw legislators deliberately absent from contentious votes.
We saw disputes over matters as trivial as seating arrangements distract from the business of the people.
We saw unsubstantiated accusations of impropriety or corruption.
And, perhaps most concerning, we saw the questioning of members’ religious convictions and sincerity.
Disagreement is part of the legislative process, and strong opinions are to be expected.
But respect for one another – and a willingness to engage in good faith – are essential to maintaining the integrity of the institution.
South Dakotans deserve a Legislature that debates issues vigorously while still treating one another with basic civility.
Looking Ahead No legislative session is perfect.
Compromise is inherent in the process, and according to the Rolling Stones, “you can’t always get what you want.” But taken as a whole, this year’s work reflects a continued commitment to fiscal responsibility, local control, and common-sense policy.
As always, I appreciate the input, feedback, and engagement from constituents throughout the session.
Your perspective helps shape the decisions made in Pierre, and I look forward to continuing that conversation in the months ahead.
