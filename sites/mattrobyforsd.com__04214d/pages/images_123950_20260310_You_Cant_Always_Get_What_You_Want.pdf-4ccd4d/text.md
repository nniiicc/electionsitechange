FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE March 10, 2026 You Can’t Always Get What You Want When I think about the legislative process, a song from the Rolling Stones comes to mind: “You Can’t Always Get What You Want.” That certainly rang true for me and others last week as the House considered a one-time appropriation for Lake Area Technical College to build a new Trades Center.
The project would have allowed the addition of three new programs – HVAC, refrigeration, and water treatment – all areas where South Dakota employers are facing workforce shortages.
Because the proposal involved one-time spending, it required a two-thirds majority to pass.
That meant we needed 47 votes.
In the end, we were only able to secure 43.
A great deal of work went into the effort, especially from LATC President Tiffany Sanderson and others who helped make the case for the project.
While the vote was disappointing, Lake Area Tech will continue pursuing other avenues to move the project forward.
Still, it was frustrating to see some legislators unwilling to make this type of investment in our state’s future.
While the LATC proposal did not pass, the House did approve a number of bills last week that I supported.
Several measures focused on public safety and consumer protection.
These included bills that combat virtual currency kiosk fraud, enhance penalties for drug crimes occurring in our state prisons, and protect the privacy and security of genetic data.
We also passed a bill prohibiting the creation and distribution of non-consensual, pornographic “AI deepfakes” of real people.
The bill was introduced at the request of the Attorney General, and I volunteered to carry it on the House floor.
Learning more about the issue opened my eyes to the very real harm these activities can cause.
The House also approved safeguards against foreign influence in our elections, protections for individuals exercising their constitutional free speech rights, and provisions for wildfire mitigation planning for utilities.
Infrastructure and economic development were also part of last week’s agenda.
The House approved funding for public improvements related to the expansion at Ellsworth Air Force Base, funding to support rural infrastructure in counties across the state, and authorization of loans to support expansion projects at the Rapid City and Sioux Falls airports.
Finally, the House approved requirements for county notifications to townships, expanding community safety zones to include licensed child-care programs, and modifying provisions related to tax increment financing (TIF) districts.
The bill revising TIF district laws was the product of interested parties coming together to agree on reasonable updates.
This was a great example of the type of collaboration that leads to good law.
With only one week remaining, the Legislature is beginning to put a bow on the main run of the 2026 session.
While a few policy issues still need to be resolved, the final days will largely focus on financial matters, including property tax proposals, one-time spending decisions, and final passage of the General Appropriations Act – the only mustpass bill every year.
I intend to run through the finish line advocating for issues important to District 5 and working to ensure South Dakota remains a great place to live, work, and raise a family – both today and for the next generation of South Dakotans.
