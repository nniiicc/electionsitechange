FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE March 3, 2026 Crossover Day Drama We reached an important milestone in Pierre last week: Crossover Day – the deadline for bills to pass out of their chamber of origin.
Unlike past sessions where crossover day went late into the evening, the House concluded its work this year relatively early and dramafree.
The Senate, however, experienced a more turbulent day.
A couple of senators were intentionally absent from the floor to avoid contentious votes and potential tie-breaking action by the Lieutenant Governor.
That kind of maneuvering reflects poorly on the Legislature and is a disservice to constituents who expect their elected officials to show up and cast votes on tough issues.
While the turmoil in the Senate received most of the news this week, the House passed several important measures that I was proud to support.
Among them was House Bill 1140, which allows courts to require treatment at certain nonprofit entities as a condition of probation or parole.
House Bill 1300 protects victims of human trafficking and domestic abuse by exempting them from certain clemency notice requirements.
We also passed House Bill 1082, which establishes parameters for reimbursing school districts that provide free or reduced-price meals to students.
Water infrastructure remains a priority across our state.
Senate Concurrent Resolutions 606 and 607 encourage review and approval of Missouri River water rights applications and support congressional authorization and federal participation in major rural water projects identified by the South Dakota Association of Rural Water Systems.
These measures are good steps in the ongoing efforts to ensure long-term water security for our communities.
Other bills passed this week include Senate Bill 111, requiring social media companies to provide users access to their collected personal data and increasing transparency; Senate Bills 81 and 82, which clarify protections for service animals and establish penalties for their misrepresentation; House Bill 1262, appropriating funds for a non-residential, school-based therapeutic services facility in Brown County; Senate Bill 89, creating a task force to study emergency medical services funding; Senate Bill 113, increasing penalties for preventing the practice of religion; and House Bill 88, clarifying procedures related to examining real property in condemnation cases.
I’m especially pleased to report progress on a few of the bills I’ve sponsored.
House Bill 1298, prohibiting the electronic publication of personal information about judges and law enforcement, passed the House by a strong margin and now heads to the Senate.
House Bill 1201, clarifying the ability of certain non-profits to fundraise through mechanical pull-tab machines, was signed into law last week.
House Bill 1234, revising provisions related to serving subpoenas in contested cases, is now headed to the Governor’s desk.
The data center debate continues.
Efforts to position South Dakota competitively from a tax and regulatory standpoint were sidetracked amid the Senate’s above-noted procedural tactics.
However, legislation ensuring sensible guardrails on data center impacts remains active and is likely to become law.
Property tax relief discussions also continue in earnest.
Several proposals have been set aside due to a lack of broad consensus, but a new approach – Senate Bill 245 – appears to be gaining meaningful support in the House.
Additionally, the Governor’s proposal, Senate Bill 96, would allow counties to adopt a half-cent sales tax to directly offset property taxes.
I am more hopeful than I was last week that we have an opportunity this year to deliver meaningful relief.
