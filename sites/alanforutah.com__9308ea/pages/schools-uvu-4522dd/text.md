Happening now What changed for child care Utah calls itself family-first, but our child care assistance runs entirely on federal money.
When that money fell by about $20 million, 900 working families started losing help on October 1.
The Legislature can fill that gap in January.
I’ll push for it.
The Legislature didn’t make this cut.
Federal funding dropped, and the program had no state money to soften the blow.
The state Office of Child Care’s own advisory committee voted to urge state leaders to fill the gap.
It fits a pattern.
In 2024, the Legislature passed a law to help child care workers afford care for their own kids.
It has never been funded.
Bills to fund it, at about $# million a year, did not pass in 2025 or 2026.
01 Fill the gap in January so working families can keep the care they count on.
02 Fund the child care worker support the Legislature already passed.
03 Build steady state support so families aren’t one federal formula change away from losing care.
The district connection What changed at UVU Following the Legislature’s HB 265 directive, UVU developed a plan to shift $8.9 million within its 2025–26 budget.
Some programs and administrative budgets were reduced, and staffing changed as money moved into other programs and student support.
That was a reallocation, rather than a simple $8.9 million loss to the university’s overall budget.
It still meant changes for departments, employees, and students.
01 Track the changes and push for help when cuts clearly hurt students or programs our community needs.
02 Protect and expand programs tied to in-demand jobs in Utah County.
03 Require clear reporting on class access, graduation, staffing, and tuition.
