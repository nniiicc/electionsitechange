Why affordability is personal
My family came here for opportunity.
My mother came to Utah from Peru and my father from Spain.
They met in Provo and chose to build our family here because they believed hard work and education could open the door to a better life.
That belief shaped my siblings and me.
To me, affordability and education are part of the same promise: people who work hard should be able to build a life here and prepare for a good career without being priced out.
Why this race, why now
Competition makes representation better.
All three of my siblings moved out of state because Utah became too expensive for them to stay.
I don't want more families to face the same choice between staying close to home and finding a place they can afford.
I'm running because District 61 voters deserve a real choice.
Every representative should have to earn the job.
No party deserves a blank check.
A competitive election gives residents the chance to ask who will lower costs, protect local opportunity, explain major votes, and keep listening after Election Day.
How I solve problems
Find the risk early.
Protect people.
Show the result.
My work in cybersecurity taught me to look for problems early, protect important information, and take responsibility for the result.
Those habits matter at the Capitol too.
Before the state approves a major subsidy, development, or technology purchase, residents should know what it costs, what could go wrong, who benefits, and who could be left with the bill.
I will explain the choices and check whether the decision actually worked.
I will work with Republicans, Democrats, and independents whenever it helps West Provo and Southwest Orem.
My test will be straightforward: Who pays?
Who benefits?
Was the decision made in public?
Does it help District 61 families afford to stay here?
