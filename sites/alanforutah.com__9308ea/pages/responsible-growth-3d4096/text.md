What this means for your household
Before a major project moves forward, residents should know how it could affect utility rates, reliability, water, roads, and noise.
They should also know who will pay for new capacity.
Invest in accurate water tracking so conservation plans are based on real use and clear results.
Require firm plans for efficient cooling, water reuse, less noise, and clean air before approval.
Plan housing and business growth alongside roads, transit, utilities, schools, and emergency services.
A local example
In February 2026, planning documents described a proposed 66,000-square-foot data center at 1507 S. 180 E. in Provo.
The applicant projected electrical demand of 5–50 megawatts, with expansion depending on available Provo Power capacity.
Its first-phase water estimate was 7,000–12,000 gallons a day for cooling and domestic use.
These were estimates for the proposal at that time.
They are not measurements from an operating facility, and later plans may differ.
