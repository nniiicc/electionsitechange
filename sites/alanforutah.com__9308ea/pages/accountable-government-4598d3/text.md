Service promises You should know what your representative is doing.
Here are five promises voters can check for themselves.
01 · LOCAL Regular district meetings Hold public conversations in District 61 and report honestly on what is happening at the Capitol.
02 · CLEAR Plain-language votes Give short explanations of major votes, the choices involved, and what they mean for HD61.
03 · OPEN Publish the numbers Show the costs, risks, and promised public benefit behind major subsidies and uses of public money.
04 · RESPONSIVE A clear way to get help Make it easy for residents to ask questions, request help, and know what happens next.
05 · RESULTS Report back to the district Report back on results, unfinished work, and upcoming priorities.
One example Politicians shouldn't rewrite the rules for themselves.
In 2018, Utah voters passed Proposition 4 to stop politicians from drawing district lines to favor themselves or their party.
Lawmakers later changed parts of that voter-led reform.
The Utah Supreme Court also reviewed whether Amendment D's ballot materials accurately told voters what the measure would do.
The principle is simple: voters deserve the full story before they cast a ballot.
