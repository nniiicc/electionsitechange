The tax exemptions used to support data centers have cost taxpayers $655 million.
Our state is getting robbed—while the evidence suggests that there is minimal long-term job creation.
Instead, data centers bring noise pollution, water contamination, and environmental destruction.
Still, there is a legitimate need to bring investment into our state.
I will push for legitimate research on if regulated data centers can benefit our communities, only then will I vote to end a moratorium.
Reward hard work: Raise the Minimum Wage to $20 an hour If you worked 60 hours a week for 365 days at $7.25 an hour you would make…less than $23,000 a year.
Are you kidding me?
That isn’t a livable wage.
Our state needs to start rewarding hard work.
My plan would raise the minimum wage to $20 an hour over the next five years and provide subsidies for small businesses to offset the costs.
Wages are not keeping up with skyrocketing costs.
Poverty wages in our state are unacceptable.
Expand healthcare access: Everybody Needs a Doctor.
The recent cuts to Medicaid have hurt Hoosiers badly.
But even before that, rising premiums, costly deductibles, and a 7.5% uninsured rate—show a system that isn’t working.
Many people with healthcare still can’t afford to go to the doctor.
Indiana needs comprehensive reform.
I will work across the aisle to support recent efforts to cap hospital prices.
I will also introduce legislation to expand transparency regulations so that we have publicly available data on hospital ownership and acquisitions —and can better keep prices low.
Finally, I will vote against Medicaid eligibility cuts as Indiana leads the nation in disenrollment.
We need to cap lifesaving medication such as insulin.
I also stand with healthcare professionals and nurses.
I want to make working in healthcare safer for nurses and medical staff.
I would like to see safer patient-nurse ratios in the hospital.
In rural Indiana, we are dealing with maternity care deserts.
I want to expand maternal healthcare options in rural areas so that expecting moms don’t have to drive an hour or more to a different county to get the care they need.
Fund public schools: Eliminate School Vouchers Indiana spends around $500 million a year on school vouchers.
Yet, only 34% of rural students have access to a private school near them.
A family of four that makes over $230,000 a year can get a voucher.
The program is subsidizing students that were already going to private schools.
I would introduce legislation that phases out the voucher program and redirects the funds back into our public schools.
75% of the money would go towards at-risk schools primarily in rural or inner city areas.
The other 25% would go towards piloting alternative forms of teaching and learning that could eventually be expanded across the state.
My goal is to fix the education system—for all of our kids.
Data has shown that this past year, the majority of school vouchers went to wealthy families making $100k to over $200k a year.
This isn’t the most fiscally responsible way to spend our tax dollars.
Empower our workers: Repeal Right to Work and Allow Public Sector Bargaining Strong unions are the backbone of the working class.
They are key to negotiating collective bargaining agreements.
Indiana has an 8.3% union membership rate—it was 29% in 1989.
We must repeal the disastrous 2012 Right to Work decision.
Research finds that among 30 factors that would make an employer choose a location for business development, Right to Work has never been in the top 10.
That means we are sacrificing better working conditions for a policy that doesn’t even attract jobs.
I will introduce legislation that allows complete public sector bargaining.
Right now, teachers are only allowed to bargain over their salary, this should be expanded to allow working conditions, class size, benefits, evaluation, curriculum.
Because we must leave education in the hands of our educators.
Likewise, police and fire departments are only allowed to bargain informally, this is a disservice to our first responders and must be changed.
Other public sector employees are no different and deserve to be able to bargain.
I will introduce concurrent legislation that establishes annual audits to ensure unions are functioning democratically and avoiding corruption.
Indiana must grow alongside its workers.
Fund our state: Legalize Cannabis Ohio, Illinois, and Michigan have legalized recreational cannabis.
Every year we lose over $200 million dollars in potential tax revenue.
Indiana residentsspend a whopping $1.8 billion a year on cannabis and none of that money can go back into the state.
Are you serious?
Imagine what $1.8 billion could do to our local economy.
It’s beyond time that we legalize cannabis and use it to fund our public services and boost growth.
Legalization means more money for schools, safety departments, and infrastructure.
It would also help the numerous people in need of pain management or PTSD treatment.
It is a fiscally beneficial policy that could help break our state’s dependence on opiods.
Family matters: Guarantee Paid Family Leave Family is an integral part of Indiana.
And it’s time we start taking that seriously.
Only 21% of Indiana workers receive paid family leave from their employers.
This gap jeopardizes the health of our communities.
Most of us have no safety-net in place if something happens to a kid, spouse, or parents.
We are forced to decide, stay home and take care of our family or work to pay the bills and put food on the table.
That’s an impossible choice.
It is past due that our state guarantees paid family leave.
My plan would create a social insurance program.
This program would cover 8 weeks of paid leave per qualifying life event.
It would replace up to the Indiana average weekly income, currently $1278 a week.
It would be financed through an automatic weekly payroll deduction of around $2.
This is how we come together to take care of our community.
Because we need each other the most when times are tough.
Headstart for Hoosiers: Support Affordable Childcare Let me take a step back from policy for a second.
I have a third year old daughter.
I know that affordable childcare really matters.
Indiana is in the midst of a crisis.
There is a 46% gap in childcare coverage.
Finding childcare for a single infant consumes 24.7% of a family’s income on average.
Toddler care is about $16,802.
Preschool is $13,158.
These numbers are entirely unsustainable, Indiana has some of the worst childcare affordability rates in the country.
Early education matters, and our politicians are failing.
Let me be direct—I support universal childcare.
But getting there requires creative policymaking.
It is imperative that both price and supply are considered.
I would increase funding for CCDF subsidies and raise the income threshold for eligibility.
Likewise, I would invest in grants and tax credits to address the “childcare deserts” that plague rural Indiana.
I will mandate the collection of county-level pricing data, so that families know what options are available.
Indiana is one the four states that doesn’t collect this data—and it is a common sense fix.
With each step we can become more ambitious, and get closer to developing a framework for universal childcare.
Property tax reform: Help People Keep their Homes Taxes should never be the reason someone loses their home.
I would introduce legislation that prohibits those on retirement or disability income from having their property reassessed unless it is being sold or inherited (only if the value is worth more than $450,000).
I will also work to establish a lower tax rate for those who are elderly, disabled, low, or middle income.
I also want to look into options to lower property taxes for farmers.
But this must not come at the cost of necessary funding for our public services such as our public schools.
Balance is important to sustain our communities without crushing homeowners.
End price gouging: Investigate Utility Companies I will support an amendment that requires Indiana Utility Regulatory Commision approval for the sale of any major utility company in the state to ensure that it won’t raise prices on consumers.
I will also push for further investigation into the state’s utility companies and demand transparency.
If rates remain unchanged, I will support eliminating the state’s 7% sales tax on utility bills.
Respect our rights: Affirm Indiana’s Constitutional Right to Hunt and Fish We must affirm Indiana’s constitutional right to hunt and fish.
That requires a genuine commitment to our land and our environment.
I will defend the National Resource Commission and ensure the department does not lose funding.
I will also push for the creation of a statewide task force on water contamination, while establishing clear standards on PFAS chemicals and mandating water tests and cleanups when water levels get too high.
Alongside, collaboration with Purdue University to deploy Mesonet sites across the state to make it easier for farmers to manage pesticide runoff.
But we must also consider the future generation.
I will push to pilot an “Outdoor Education” class in our public schools with a curriculum designed by hunters, anglers, farmers, and scientists.
Alongside a student run “Conservation Conference” where they can present real projects related to the outdoors.
Our Mission This campaign is about putting Hoosiers first .
That means fighting to make daily life easier.
Politicians on both sides have forgotten the working class.
They focus on cultural issues; while bills have gone up, schools have gotten worse, and our public lands are under threat.
Our communities lack affordable childcare.
Many of us are skipping visits to the doctor altogether, because the cost of healthcare is just too expensive.
The roads are filled with potholes and the drinking water is contaminated.
And in spite of everything—the only thing our politicians can agree on is taking corporate donations.
That’s why my campaign is entirely grassroots funded and only accepts individual donations.
I’ve done my best to knock on as many doors as possible, and will continue to do so until Election Day.
I believe that politics is about community—not corporations.
My focus is on the bread-and-butter issues.
As a working mom with a three year old daughter, this is personal for me.
I want my daughter to be able to grow up in Southern Indiana and believe that it’s the best place on earth.
And I want the same for everyone in our community.
You can find my plan below, but first, thank you.
Indiana raised me.
