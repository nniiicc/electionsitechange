PILLAR II
Fix the Broken System
I've spent my life around high-stakes systems.
When a system fails, you don't scream at it.
You find the broken components and you fix them.
Congress is a broken system — and both parties built it that way.
A Congress That Legislates
The current Congress has passed just 95 public laws.
It will add some more in the coming months, but the last five Congresses averaged 355, and the 118th already set the record for fewest laws since the Civil War at 274.
This Congress is on track to set the wrong kind of record.
That is not legislating.
That is theater with a payroll.
Budgets Done On Time, Honestly
In the fifty years the modern budget process has existed, Congress has passed all its spending bills on time exactly four times.
The last time was 1997. $41 trillion in national debt by 2028 is not conservative — it is reckless.
Voting to add more than $4 trillion to the debt while calling yourself a fiscal conservative is not a position; it is a performance.
I will not perform fiscal conservatism.
I will practice it: honest budgets, on time, with tradeoffs stated out loud.
Courts You Can Trust
Both parties dismantled the 60-vote consensus requirement for judicial confirmations — step by step, each convinced the other started it, each escalating when it held the majority.
The result: judges confirmed by bare majorities, courts presumed partisan.
I will support restoring consensus requirements for lifetime appointments.
And the districts themselves are rigged.
I will support independent redistricting nationwide: no party draws its own map, period.
Money You Can Trace
You can believe in me.
You can donate to me.
You cannot control me.
This campaign takes no PAC money — not corporate, not party, not billionaire super PACs — and publishes its funding with 100% transparency.
Every dollar this campaign raises comes from a person, is reported in the open, and buys exactly one thing: nothing.
I invite every candidate in this race to do the same.
PILLAR III
Build the Future
I'm an engineer.
Engineers don't fear the future — we build it, and we build the guardrails that keep it from hurting people.
New technology should make ordinary people stronger, not just make powerful people richer.
Energy & the Environment
I have spent a decade modernizing power systems — microgrids, battery storage, grid resilience for utilities and the Department of Defense.
The grid that powers your home, your base, and your shipyard is aging while demand is exploding.
That is a national security issue, an economic development issue, and an environmental issue all at once.
I don't need a party to tell me what to think about energy.
I've built it.
This district already paid the price for letting politics override engineering.
The federal government ordered a halt to the offshore wind project off Virginia Beach — the largest in the nation, supporting roughly 1,100 Hampton Roads jobs — citing security concerns it never specified about a project the military itself had already reviewed and approved.
The 26-day stoppage added $228 million to the project's cost.
That wasn't security.
It was politics, and Hampton Roads got the bill.
AI & Technology
America must lead in AI — falling behind is not an option.
But leading doesn't mean letting it happen to people.
It means guardrails that protect workers, families, and kids — and access that lifts the shipyard worker and the small business owner, not just the shareholders of the companies that build it.
Neither party knows what to do.
We have seen this movie before.
We don't have to repeat it.
Guardrails and access, from day one.
That's what an engineer means by built right.

Deliver for Hampton Roads
Everything above is about fixing the country.
This is about your street.

Veterans & Military Families
This district has more veterans and military families per square mile than almost anywhere in America — and we are failing them in the one area that matters most: readiness.
Ships going to sea in poor material condition.
Aircraft grounded for repair.
Vehicles that aren't ready.
Service members who aren't trained.
No amount of token improvements will fix a problem that is now decades in the making.
I will fight for service members and their families, show up at the facilities that serve them, and measure my own performance by whether readiness improves.
I intend to bring all my service and engineering experience to bear on this.
We can — we must — do better.
Jobs, Costs & the Kitchen Table
The shipyards, the port, the bases, and the businesses that serve them are this district's economy — and every one of them is squeezed by the same things squeezing your family: costs rising faster than paychecks, housing that outruns wages, health care bills nobody can predict.
I won't insult you with a slogan.
Kitchen-table math is still math.
I'll show my work.
A Coast That Stays Above Water
Hampton Roads floods on sunny days.
Norfolk has the highest rate of sea-level rise on the East Coast - rising water and sinking land at once - it threatens homes, roads, and the naval infrastructure the entire country depends on.
This is not a debate topic in this district.
It's a drainage problem, an insurance problem, and a national security problem.
Flood resilience funding for this region is a first-term priority.
Educators and Our Kids
Teachers are one of the most important groups in our society if not the most important.
Parents deserve to be kept informed on decisions that materially affect their child’s wellbeing.
Somehow education has become more of political cage fight with book bans on one side, wholesale mandates on the other, than a focus on the job that matters: teaching kids to read, think, and add.
School boards together with educators should be developing curriculum, not individuals with an agenda, and not Washington.
Congress’s role is funding and civil rights protections, not running a classroom.
It is clear that some things must change, but I must be honest, education is not in my background the way national security or energy are.
I will do the work and spend time with parents, teachers, and school boards to better understand what is needed.
