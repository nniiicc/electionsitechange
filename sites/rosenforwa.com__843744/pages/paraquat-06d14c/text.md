One of the world's most dangerous herbicides Paraquat is one of the most acutely toxic herbicides still in use.
The EPA's own warning campaign is called "One Sip Can Kill." A small accidental swallow can be fatal, and there is no antidote.
But the concern goes beyond acute poisoning.
The Parkinson's connection Paraquat has been linked to Parkinson's disease.
An NIH-supported study found that people who reported using paraquat were about 2.5 times as likely to develop Parkinson's disease as those who hadn't.
Parkinson's is exactly the kind of chronic disease where definitive proof of causation in humans is extraordinarily difficult.
We can't ethically expose people to paraquat for decades to run the experiment, and observational research has to untangle a lifetime of different exposures and other factors.
That uncertainty is the point.
We shouldn't demand proof we may never get while people continue to be exposed.
Much of the world has already acted Paraquat is already banned in more than 70 countries, including the European Union, United Kingdom, and China.
And in 2026, two U.S. states acted.
Vermont became the first state to ban paraquat, followed by California, which is phasing it out statewide.
Washington still allows it.
Washington's approach isn't enough Paraquat is currently a restricted-use pesticide in Washington.
Applicators must be certified and complete special paraquat training.
But training someone to handle a dangerous chemical more carefully doesn't answer the underlying question: should we still be using this chemical at all?
Responsible handling doesn't resolve the long-term health concern.
What I'd do Phase paraquat out of Washington.
And do it without abandoning farmers.
Washington should provide technical assistance, safer alternatives, reasonable transition time, and real financial support — with the pesticide industry helping bear the cost.
No permanent loopholes because a chemical is profitable, familiar, or difficult to replace.
Protect people.
Help farmers transition.
Find a safer way.
More than 70 countries have already moved beyond paraquat.
Vermont acted.
California acted.
Washington can too.
