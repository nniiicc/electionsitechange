ON THE ISSUES...
EDUCATION
Most of my recent legislative attention has gone to schools.
I backed a statewide "bell-to-bell" ban on classroom cellphones and helped lead a study of whether folding School Administrative Units into county-level bodies would improve efficiency.
I served on the Commission to Study the Costs of Special Education, which recently turned in its report.
Twelve education bills I sponsored or co-sponsored were signed into law this term.
One size does not necessarily fit all when it comes to education.
I support a balanced blend of education options: public, private, charter, & home schools.
Alternative Education
We should encourage hands-on, real-world learning like Career and Technical Education, the Learn Everywhere programs, apprenticeships, and school/business collaborations and internships.
Higher Education
We need to examine and support ways to expand access to higher education.
One example is dual enrollment programs for high schoolers working with community colleges.
Community Colleges
Our community colleges are hidden jewels.
They teach skills for jobs that communities need and, working with the university system, offer a more affordable way to get college credits.
Let's make it easier to transfer credits from community colleges.
CHILDCARE
We need to make it easier to provide childcare and make it easier to get into childcare.
To that end, I have sponsored 3 bills that have made it into law:
HB1584 (2024) increased home daycare capacity from 3 children to 4
HB1771 (2026) protected a waiver for child care staffing ratios
HB1433 (2026) created a childcare tax credit for qualifying businesses.
BUSINESS
Small Businesses
Our current state budget lowered business taxes and our business revenue increased.
I want to keep business taxes low to help the small businesses in our state.
Grow Businesses
We need to make it easier to start and/or grow a business in New Hampshire, including recognizing out-of-state professional licensing.
