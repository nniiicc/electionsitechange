West Virginia’s Temporary Assistance for Needy Families Crisis
The current issues with Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) in West Virginia are almost comical, if not for the fact that thousands of needy children and their families are being harmed by it.
The TANF funds in question come from the federal government, and it appears West Virginia is not being a good steward for those funds.
The Legislature’s job is to “appropriate,” or approve, the use of funds for various purposes.
In this case, that was done.
Then the Governor, in conjunction with his appointed department Secretaries, the Treasurer, and Auditor, is to make sure the funds are used as approved and to keep good records of what is spent, for what, and where.
That apparently had NOT been done.
When questions arose about why the back-to-school clothing allowance was not being handled as it had in the past, the Governor cited a sudden lack of funds.
When follow-up investigations were done, that assertion proved false.
The Governor then changed his story, claiming that poor families were abusing the system by taking cash from ATM’s and using it for “beer and cigarettes.”
TANF money is usually added to EBT cards, and federal rules mandate that ATM withdrawals be allowed (with some logical restrictions) to enable those families to make the most judicious use of the funds.
The Governor’s office issued a statement on the TANF crisis on August 3rd.
State Senator Amy Nichole Grady (R-Mason) told MetroNews Television that after testimony from the Secretary of the Department of Human Services, the legislature still does not understand what happened to the funds.
She stated, “We still left not really knowing exactly what the problem was.
You know, we funded what the governor asked for.
The reason given for the vouchers not being rolled out on July 1 wasn’t sufficient, and there was still no evidence or data to be used to provide clear evidence on that comment that was made about spending it on beer and cigarettes.
And so, we’re still left with, you know, nothing.
We still don’t know exactly what the problem was.”
