The association notified the campaign in a letter from OEA President Jeff Wensing, who chairs OEA's Fund for Children and Public Education.
The endorsement followed a screening committee review of Jones's written responses on public education issues.
OEA represents approximately 120,000 teachers, education support professionals, and higher education faculty across Ohio, including educators working in the public school districts that serve Seneca and Sandusky counties.
The endorsement makes Jones the choice of both of Ohio's major teachers' organizations.
The Ohio Federation of Teachers endorsed his campaign earlier this year.
"I'm a Clyde High School graduate, and the people who taught me set me up for everything that came after," Jones said.
"The teachers and support staff in this district are folks I know by name.
When they tell me what's happening in their buildings, I listen.
Having them behind this campaign matters to me." Jones has made public school funding a central issue of his campaign, pointing to the gap between where school funding decisions are made and where the consequences land.
"Those decisions get made in Columbus, but they show up here," Jones said.
"They show up in classrooms, and they show up on property tax bills.
I want the people making them to hear from the folks doing the work." Jones has also been endorsed by the UAW Ohio State CAP Council.
Aaron Jones is a production supervisor at Toledo Molding & Die in Tiffin, where he has worked for more than 20 years, and represents the 1st Ward on Tiffin City Council.
He served four years as an Airborne Infantryman with the 3rd U.S.
Infantry Regiment, The Old Guard, in Washington, D.C.
He and his wife Tracy live in Tiffin and have four grandchildren.
The campaign does not accept corporate PAC money.
