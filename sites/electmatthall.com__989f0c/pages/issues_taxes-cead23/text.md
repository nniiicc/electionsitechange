Matt Hall has led efforts to cut more than $3.5 billion in waste, fraud and abuse over the last two years while bringing Republicans and Democrats together to make state government more effective and efficient.
What Matt Hall Has Accomplished
Made Government More Efficient
Matt Hall led bipartisan efforts to pass two consecutive balanced budgets that decreased state spending by $800 million in 2025 and another $800 million in 2026.
These budgets are the first time in decades that state spending has actually decreased.
By cutting waste, fraud and abuse and draining unnecessary government slush funds, Matt Hall made state government live within its means and prevented a proposed $800 million tax increase in 2026.
Eliminated Ghost Employees
Matt Hall led the effort to eliminate 2,250 “ghost employee” positions that were costing taxpayers more than $400 million.
These unnecessary positions padded government budgets instead of providing value to taxpayers.
Matt Hall brought Republicans and Democrats together to eliminate these positions and stop taxpayers from funding government jobs that weren’t needed.
Drained Bureaucrat Slush Funds
Matt Hall has led efforts to drain state bureaucrat slush funds and return unused taxpayer money to the state’s general fund.
Michigan has more than $12 billion sitting in these funds, earning roughly $600 million in interest each year.
That is money that could be put to work for taxpayers instead of sitting in government accounts.
You should be earning interest on your money—not the state government.
Cracked Down on Fraud
Matt Hall has made stopping fraud a priority by putting stronger safeguards in place to protect taxpayer-funded benefits.
He required child-care providers to track attendance so the state can stop paying for care that was never provided, strengthened oversight of autism service providers through independent audits, and is upgrading Bridge Cards with chip technology to help prevent benefit theft.
Matt Hall strengthened fraud prevention in food stamps and Medicaid by requiring the state to stop relying on the honor system to pay out welfare benefits.
Now Michigan officials must regularly verify every welfare applicant’s eligibility; immigration or legal status; and reported income using state and federal records.
State officials must also regularly check death records and monitor out of state transactions to identify non-Michigan residents who are improperly receiving food stamps.
These reforms help stop fraud and ensure taxpayer-funded benefits go only to people who are legally eligible.
Ended Corporate Welfare
Matt Hall has worked to end corporate welfare—billions of taxpayer dollars handed out to the most connected and profitable corporations in Michigan.
He eliminated major corporate subsidy programs, including the Strategic Outreach and Attraction Reserve (SOAR) Fund and the 21st Century Jobs Fund, after they failed to deliver the promised results.
Hall ended the $500 million annual funding for SOAR and drained the remaining money from the fund, while also stopping the diversion of tobacco settlement dollars into corporate giveaways.
Every taxpayer dollar given away to a corporation is a dollar that cannot be invested in priorities that provide a broader public benefit—fixing roads, keeping communities safe, educating our children, supporting local communities, and improving public health.
What Matt Hall is Working to Do
Matt Hall will keep working to make state government more effective and efficient and deliver better value for your tax dollars.
By cutting waste, fraud and abuse, Michigan can put more money into roads, public safety, education, public health and essential services in local communities instead of wasting it on programs that don’t deliver results.
