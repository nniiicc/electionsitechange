Matt Hall’s Hall Ethics, Accountability and Transparency Plan (HEAT) ends secret pork spending and brings real accountability to the state budget.
This historic reform was needed after Michigan saw secret pork spending projects become the subject of criminal investigations and felony charges.
Matt Hall believes if a politician wants to spend taxpayer money on a pet project, he or she should have to put his or her name on it, explain why it benefits the public, and stand behind that decision.
What Matt Hall Has Accomplished Ended Secret Pork Spending Under HEAT, lawmakers must put their name on special spending requests, explain the public benefit and show how the project benefits their district.
Requests must be made public at least # days before lawmakers can vote, giving taxpayers time to see where their money is going and who will receive it.
The process also requires a public Appropriations Committee hearing on proposed spending requests.
This is a major change from the old system, when hundreds of millions of dollars in special projects could be buried in massive budget bills and approved with little public scrutiny.
Protecting Taxpayer Dollars HEAT puts guardrails around who can receive taxpayer-funded projects.
For-profit corporations cannot receive these special spending projects, and nonprofits must be established organizations with a proven track record in Michigan.
These safeguards help ensure taxpayer dollars go toward legitimate public purposes —not organizations created simply to receive a special spending project.
Stronger Legislative Oversight HEAT also created stronger legislative oversight , giving the Michigan House of Representatives greater ability to investigate state government and hold agencies accountable.
Closed the Revolving Door Matt Hall also took on the revolving door between public service and lobbying.
In 2020, Matt Hall raised questions about whether a sitting Michigan legislator could simultaneously work as a lobbyist outside Michigan.
The issue exposed a gap in Michigan’s ethics laws, and Matt Hall continued working to close that loophole.
After years of work, he successfully changed the law so legislators can no longer lobby while serving in office.
HEAT also prevents politicians and senior state officials from immediately going from public service to lobbying.
The new law creates a two-year cooling-off period, preventing public officials from walking directly from government service into lobbying.
Matt Hall believes public officials should not be able to walk out the door of government on Friday and start selling their access to government on Monday.
Public service should be about serving the people—not preparing for your next lobbying job.
What Matt Hall is Working to Do End Secret Deals with Taxpayer Money Matt Hall’s Hall Ethics, Accountability and Transparency Plan (HEAT) is not finished.
Nondisclosure agreements have been used in negotiations over electric vehicle battery plants and major data center projects, keeping details of economic development deals secret while taxpayer-funded incentives are being considered.
Matt Hall passed bipartisan legislation through the Michigan House prohibiting legislators and legislative staff from signing nondisclosure agreements related to their legislative work.
The legislation still needs to become law, and Matt Hall is working to get it across the finish line.
Taxpayers deserve transparency when their money is on the line.
Public officials should be able to examine, question and explain major deals—not be forced to keep the public’s business secret.
