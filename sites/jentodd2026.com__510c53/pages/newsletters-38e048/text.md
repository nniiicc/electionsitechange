Outrage.
I’m generally a positive and happy person.
But as we head into the November Election, outrage is the only word I can think of to describe how I feel about what’s going on in IL-15.
Every day, the people I talk to give me another reason to work harder and do better to win this election in November for everyone.
Not just those who agree with me.
For example: This weekend in Henderson County, I talked to Gary , a farmer who isn’t sure he can afford to plant next year.
Fuel costs are high, crop prices are low, and the operating expenses are too much.
I talked to Scott , a multi-generational farmer who has spent thousands more than in past years on fuel for his combine, tractors, and trucks.
He’s worried there won’t be a farm left to pass down to his son and grandchildren.
And last week, I spoke with Molly , who lives with an autoimmune disease .
She turns 26 in November and will age out of her parents’ health insurance and lose her coverage.
Her employer doesn’t offer insurance, she makes too much for Medicaid, and she can’t afford the alternatives.
These are real people.
Real families.
Real problems.
And they are exactly why I’m running for Congress.
A Choice In November!
I’m outraged that working people are being asked to do more with less while Mary Miller makes decisions that leave them behind.
I’m outraged that farmers are worried about losing the land their families have worked for generations.
I’m outraged that people are being forced to choose between healthcare and putting food on the table.
And I’m outraged that every voter I speak with who has attempted to reach out to their elected member of Congress, Mary Miller, gets dismissive responses .
“We are aware of the issue.” “Congresswoman Miller has no comment at this time.” “That is not an issue we can address at this time.” And then there are the times when no one answers the phone, and constituents never hear a word back.
But outrage alone isn’t enough.
We have to turn that outrage into action.
That means making a contribution #ago .
It means picking up the phone and phone banking.
It means knocking on doors, canvassing our communities, and talking to our neighbors.
It means every single one of us doing everything we can to say enough .
We can win this in November, but it will take all of us.
I’m ready to keep showing up, listening, and fighting for the people of Illinois’ 15th District.
Are you ready to turn your outrage into action?

Jennifer P.S.

We’re in the final stretch - that moment after Labor Day when more and more eyes turn to the November election.
So, let me be direct about what's at stake.
Can families afford to live here?
Can they get healthcare when they need it?
And will our farms, small businesses, hospitals, and communities have the resources they need to thrive?
There are two Very Different Visions for Illinois 15 on the ballot in November A Choice In November!
AFFORDABILITY My position: Families should not have to choose between groceries, medicine, utilities, and other basic expenses.
Federal programs should strengthen the communities that need them.
Her record: H.R.
1 also reduced projected federal SNAP spending by $211 billion through 2035.
This law will reduce the number of people receiving SNAP, reduce average benefit amounts, and shift more of the program’s costs to states.
HEALTHCARE My position: Rural families are under attack.
Every one of Mary Miller’s constituents should be able to get quality healthcare close to home, and our rural hospitals, nursing homes, and healthcare providers need the support to remain open and viable.
That’s what I’ll fight for.
Her record: Enthusiastically voted for H.R.
1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act which The Congressional Budget Office estimates will reduce federal Medicaid spending by about $1.2 trillion over 2026–2035 and reduce Medicaid enrollment by 12.9 million people by 2035.

FARMS, JOBS & RURAL COMMUNITIES My position: Farmers should be able to make a living, keep their farms in the family, and pass them on to the next generation.
Farmers need more than promises.
They need policies that actually help them keep their farms and make a living.
That’s what I’ll fight for.
Her record: She voted for farm policies that increased crop insurance and commodity payments, while farmers continue to face rising costs and uncertainty in their markets.

Meet Jennifer where you are!
Mary Miller raises cattle.
Specifically, Angus cattle.
She’s done it for more than 40 years.
She’s also a member of Congress on the House Agriculture Committee , including the Livestock and Nutrition Subcommittees.
So my question is: Is Mary Miller using that experience and position to fight for the people she represents?
Because right now, the people she represents are caught between two very real crises.
Cattle Producers They are rebuilding herds that are at their lowest level in roughly 75 years.
They’re already facing high costs and a pressured market.
Now the administration is moving to allow an additional 661 million pounds of imported beef trimmings into the country.
When more outside beef enters an already tight market, American producers feel that pressure.
They can’t simply raise their prices to make up the difference.
Affordability At the same time, families are paying an average of $7 a pound for ground beef.
Ground beef is one of the staples families rely on when they’re trying to stretch a grocery budget: Burgers.
Tacos.
Meatloaf.
When ground beef gets more expensive, families have fewer affordable options.
And that’s the challenge: We have to protect American cattle producers without making beef even harder for families to afford.
My position is simple: Protect American cattle producers while keeping beef affordable for families.
Strengthening domestic cattle production Keeping beef affordable Demand country-of-origin labeling so consumers know where their beef comes from Making sure imported beef meets U.S. food-safety standards I won’t trade farmers for families, or families for farmers.
We need both.
We Need a Seat at the Table!
And here’s what’s telling: This isn’t even a partisan fight.
Republicans in Congress and the Senate have spoken out against the import plan.
Mary Miller sits on the committee that oversees this.
I haven’t seen her say a word.
I’ve been talking with Democratic, Independent, and Republican cattle producers right here in this district.
They’re telling me the market is in turmoil, and they want a seat at the table.
So do I.
But asking these questions from the outside isn’t enough.
I need to get into that room.
Which means we need to compete across 35 counties and 17,000 square miles.
That’s ONE-THIRD of the entire state.
Mary Miller already has those resources.
We have to earn them, one grassroots contribution at a time.
Farmers and families both deserve a fighter.
Jennifer P.S.
Farmers shouldn’t have to choose between a fair market and affordable groceries.
Families shouldn’t have to choose between feeding their families and buying American.
We need both.

Last week, the Illinois Republican introduced legislation that would end federal recognition of Pride Month and replace it with “Family Month.” The proposal, which aligns with the views of a Republican Party increasingly skeptical of gay marriage, landed as Pride Month celebrations were getting underway across Illinois and the country.
Her Democratic challenger, Jennifer Todd, argues Miller is focused on the wrong things.
“If Mary Miller really wants to honor families she should be fighting to lower the prices of gas, and groceries, ensuring we have access to health care, making sure our kids can get a good education, and help family farmers survive.
That’s what honoring families really means,” Todd said in a statement to Playbook.
Equality Illinois echoed that.
“The diversity of our nation, including LGBTQ+ people and families, is one of our greatest strengths.
We are strongest when we recognize the richness of our communities and affirm that everyone belongs.
We can celebrate the many kinds of families that exist while also honoring the resilience of LGBTQ+ people,” the advocacy group said in a statement to Playbook.
She’s not the only Archie Bunker.
Miller is following the lead of Indiana and Tennessee governors who have issued proclamations recognizing “Nuclear Family Month.” And there’s Tennessee Congressman Andy Ogles, who recently drew criticism — including from fellow Republicans — after his social media post declared that “Homosexuality has no place in America.” The post was later deleted, with Ogles saying the “stupid” and “harmful” post was made by a member of his team who was reprimanded.
True colors : Miller’s legislation is just the latest example of her courting controversy.
Days after taking office in 2021, Miller drew criticism for quoting Adolf Hitler.
In 2022, while celebrating the Supreme Court’s decision overruling Roe v.
Wade, she called it a “historic victory for white life,” which her office later said was a slip of the tongue, with the wording of the prepared remarks purportedly terming the ruling a victory for “the right to life.” Last year, she came under fire for a xenophobic post identifying a guest House chaplain as Muslim when he was Sikh.
Miller said at the time the man “should never have been allowed” to lead the prayer.
She’s MAGA: Miller also objected to certifying the results of the 2020 election and has referred to a trans colleague, Sarah McBride, as “the gentleman from Delaware” on the House floor.
Those positions and others have made her a close ally of President Donald Trump, who endorsed her reelection bid.
Back home, though, constituents have complained that she’s difficult to reach and rarely holds public town halls.
(She’s also known to dodge reporters’ calls, including your Playbook host’s.) Losing patience: Miller has won reelection comfortably, but she also drew challengers in the March primary — a sign that criticism of her combative style isn’t limited to Democrats.
