MAIN ISSUES
ECONOMY FOR WORKING VERMONTERS
In our communities and across the entire country it is harder and harder for households to make ends meet with 61 percent of workers living paycheck to paycheck.
With the rising cost of housing, gas prices, and groceries, families are making difficult choices in their budgets.
I believe that a full-time job should pay enough to support a family.
State policies should ensure that everyone has their basic needs met.
We should not live in a state where childhood poverty rises, life expectancy decreases, and where financial security is out of grasp.
Our economy should center on Vermonters doing well and having their needs met.
To start, we need to align our systems to be best serving those who need it.
For instance, the solution to homelessness is affordable housing, yet we are not proportionally building housing that meets the budgets of families in extreme poverty.
It is imperative that multifaceted challenges are connected to the solutions we need.
Together, we need to champion an economy that works for all of us.
COMMUNITIES WITH CARE
A thriving community has access to health care, mental health services, child care, substance use support, and independent & senior living services.
Vermonters deserve affordable and accessible care.
No one should ever have to choose between their well-being and financial stability.
This requires keeping local facilities and services open in our rural areas.
Health care remains one of the greatest costs faced by Vermonters.
Last year, one in four Americans skipped needed medical care because they could not afford it.
Vermont residents already face long travel times for appointments and to travel to child care drop off and pick up - further closures will only worsen the gap in care.
In the state house, I will prioritize decreasing the cost and increase access to care.
HOUSING FIRST
Vermont faces a severe affordable housing shortage and one of the highest rates of homelessness in the nation.
In 2025, 4,588 Vermonters were unhoused, with many facing additional personal challenges in need of systemic solutions, such as mental illness, physical disabilities, raising children, or seniors over 65.
As a legislator, I support a Housing First approach and look forward to exploring community-centered solutions.
We need affordable housing tailored to both our downtown centers and rural settings.
Representing Bolton, Buels Gore, Huntington, and Waterbury, I am committed to housing projects that fit the scale and character of our towns.
I will work to modernize zoning and permitting, invest in municipal infrastructure, expand financing options, increase affordable housing opportunities, support both renters and homeowners, and improve services for Vermonters experiencing homelessness.
SUPPORTING EDUCATION
Education is embedded with the history of our state and schools are the hubs of our communities.
Access to education is important from child care to K-12 to post-secondary opportunities.
In Vermont over 90% of high schoolers express interest in education after graduation, yet only 70% pursue any training.
We need to continue addressing barriers to pursuing postsecondary - college, technical training, and certificate programs - to support student aspirations and have the skills we need for our workforce.
