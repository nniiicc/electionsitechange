What is Brad Christ's most significant legislative achievement?
House Bill 495, signed into law in 2025, is Brad’s landmark achievement — restoring state oversight of the St.
Louis Police Department and delivering the most comprehensive public safety reform in a generation.
Brad introduced the concept behind HB 495 on his first day in the legislature and fought for nearly three years to get it passed.
Majority Floor Leader Alex Riley credited Brad as ‘the driving force that got it done.’ Governor Kehoe praised the bill as ‘a major step forward’ and a central piece of Missouri’s Safer Missouri crime plan.
What this means for your family: St.
Louis neighborhoods are safer because Brad refused to give up on reforming a broken system.
What does HB 495 actually do?
HB 495 restores state oversight of the St.
Louis Metropolitan Police Department, increases penalties for violent crimes, and strengthens protections for children and communities.
The law establishes a new Board of Police Commissioners — four citizen commissioners and the Mayor — to oversee the SLMPD.
It imposes felony charges for repeat stunt driving and street takeovers, classifies child endangerment involving fentanyl as a ‘dangerous felony,’ increases penalties for burglary and organized retail theft, and creates a new Committee on School Safety.
What this means for your family: Families in and around St.
Louis benefit from tougher penalties, smarter oversight, and a renewed commitment to safe neighborhoods and safe schools.

What has Brad Christ done to lower taxes?
Brad Christ supported every major tax cut to come through the Missouri House since he took office —more than $1 billion in tax cuts by eliminating capital gains taxes and the tax on Social Security benefits, and reducing Missouri’s income tax from 6% to 4.7%, plus freezing property taxes for seniors.
The results speak for themselves.
As the tax rates have fallen state revenue went up, not down.
For every $1 Missouri cut in income taxes, the state has taken in roughly $2.50 more in revenue — from $8.9 billion before the cuts began to $13.6 billion today — a 53% increase.
Lower taxes mean more people working, more businesses hiring, and more money moving through Missouri’s economy.
What this means for your family: Families in District 96 are seeing real savings — more take-home pay, lower investment taxes, and protection for seniors facing runaway property tax assessments.
Does Brad support freezing property taxes for seniors?
Yes.
Brad Christ chaired the House Special Committee on Property Tax Reform as a freshman legislator — an almost unheard-of honor — and advanced legislation to freeze property tax assessments for homeowners over 62.
Senate Bill 756, which Brad’s committee moved forward, protects seniors from the 200-300% property tax increases that were hitting St.
Louis County homeowners hard.
Brad understood that seniors on fixed incomes should not be taxed out of the homes they spent a lifetime paying for.
What this means for your family: Seniors in District 96 can age in place and stay in their homes without fear of confiscatory property tax increases.

What has Brad done on workers' compensation reform?
Brad sponsored House Bill 497 to reform Missouri’s workers’ compensation system, making it fairer for both employees and employers.
The bill ensures workers’ compensation covers genuine work-related injuries, prevents double recovery from both workers’ comp and private insurance, and streamlines the claims process to reduce abuse and backlogs.
This protects injured workers while preventing the system from being exploited in ways that drive up costs for every employer.
What this means for your family: Honest workers get fair treatment faster, and businesses are protected from fraudulent claims that drive up costs for everyone.
What is Brad's position on education?
Brad Christ supports quality public schools, expanded educational choice, and the largest education budget in Missouri history.
Brad voted for the largest education budgets in Missouri history, delivering record funding for public schools while expanding educational opportunities for Missouri families.
Since FY2020, total foundation formula funding has increased from $3.34 billion to $4.27 billion — a 27.7% increase.
Today, 31% of Missouri’s General Revenue goes toward funding public schools, six percentage points above the 25% required by the Missouri Constitution demonstrating the state’s substantial commitment to K–12 education.
Those investments are making a difference right here at home.
State revenue going to Lindbergh Schools has increased 86%, from approximately $10.4 million in FY2022 to $19.5 million in FY2026.
After increasing to $11.17 million in FY2023 and $11.62 million in FY2024, funding jumped to $16.35 million in FY2025 and $19.47 million in FY2026.
Missouri has also increased its commitment to school transportation.
Transportation funding was approximately $93.2 million in FY2020, $93.9 million in FY2021 and $113.9 million in FY2022, with additional increases in subsequent budgets.
Brad believes strong public schools and greater educational choice can go hand in hand.
Every child deserves access to a quality education, and every family should have options to find the school that best meets their child’s needs.
What this means for your family: District 96 families gain more educational options and better-funded schools without sacrificing quality or choice.
What has Brad done to fight higher electric rates?
Brad formally opposed Ameren’s proposed 10% rate increase and voted for new protections designed to limit how quickly electric rates can rise.
Ameren’s proposal would add about $13 a month, or $156 a year, to the average residential bill, following a 12% increase in 2025.
Brad filed his opposition with the Public Service Commission and voted for SB 4, which lowered the statutory rate-growth cap to 2.25% annually and strengthened other consumer protections.
What this means for your family: Brad is working to protect household budgets from higher electric rates and make sure families aren’t stuck paying costs created by massive new power users.

What is Brad's position on Missouri's emissions testing program?
Brad Christ is leading the fight to end Missouri’s outdated and costly emissions testing program, which fails just 2% of vehicles and costs St.
Louis County residents millions of hours and dollars each year.
With 98% of vehicles passing the test, the program no longer serves its original purpose given dramatically improved vehicle manufacturing standards over the past 40 years.
Brad is leading a citizen petition drive to collect signatures from across the region and deliver them directly to the Governor, state lawmakers, and the Missouri Air Conservation Commission before the next legislative session.
The typical emissions test costs a resident 1 to 3 hours and $24 in fees, creating a burden that falls hardest on working families and lower-income residents who can’t easily take time off work.
What this means for your family: Families in St.
Louis County get back their time and money — and relief from a bureaucratic mandate that government agencies themselves admit no longer makes sense.
Why does Brad Christ believe the emissions testing program should end?
Ground-level ozone levels in the St.
Louis region have dropped steadily over 20 years, making a strong case that the program has outlived its usefulness.
In FY2023, Missouri conducted 765,799 emissions tests and found just 15,433 initial failures — a 98% pass rate.
Missourians are spending approximately $5,015 for each failed vehicle found, an economically irrational outcome.
The number of testing stations has been declining for years as shops lose money on each test, creating longer waits and greater inconvenience for everyone.
Brad’s position is backed by data, not ideology.
What this means for your family: An end to the program means no more mandatory trips, no more $24 fees, and no more lost hours from work or family time for a test that almost always passes.

What has Brad done to reform property taxes?
Brad has pushed for major property tax reforms to protect homeowners and seniors from unfair assessments and unexpected tax increases.
