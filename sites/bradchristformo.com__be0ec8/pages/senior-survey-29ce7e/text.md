In 2023, Missouri gave counties the power to freeze property taxes for seniors, and St.
Louis County adopted it.
We want to make sure everyone who qualifies is receiving it.
Until now, seniors had to reapply every single year to keep their freeze — and one missed form could cost hundreds of dollars.
The County Council just ended that requirement here at home.
This year, property tax reform passed both chambers by huge bipartisan margins but the clock ran out before it became law.
Because the freeze is run county by county, no two counties handle it the same way.
A statewide fix
would set one clear standard for every senior in Missouri
Across St.
Louis County, families have opened assessment notices to find their home values jumped
overnight — with no inspection and no explanation.
Every year, seniors had to gather paperwork and reapply — a real hardship for anyone dealing with an
illness, a move, or trouble with technology
Today, if a computer-generated valuation is too high, the homeowner must hire an appraiser and prove
the government wrong.
Reform would flip that around.
Right now, an assessment can jump 30 or 40 percent based on a computer model, without anyone ever
setting foot on the property.
Property tax measures are usually written in confusing millage rates that most voters cannot translate
into real dollars.
Some tax increases are placed on low-turnout April or special elections, where a small, motivated group
can decide a tax that everyone pays.
When used-car prices spiked, many Missourians watched the personal property tax on a vehicle they had
owned for years go up — even as the car aged
For seniors on a fixed income, rising property taxes can be the difference between staying in a longtime
home and being forced out.
