PRESS RELEASE FOR IMMEDIATE DISTRIBUTION OCT 2nd, 2026 JENN MACK CALLS FOR NEW ENFORCEMENT DIVISION TO PROTECT TEXAS AGRICULTURAL INFRASTRUCTURE, FARMERS, AND RANCHERS Campaign outlines statutory tools already available to address the Texas data center crisis — and the transparency Texans deserve before deals are signed SPRING, TX — There are statutory and constitutional authorities available to the Texas Comptroller’s Office regarding municipal non-compliance, environmental protection, and public transparency that could help with the current Texas data center crisis; Jenn Mack says tools that should be put to work now.
1.
Adding businesses to the Divestment Statute List 2.
Immediate suspension or clawing back grant disbursements of Businesses found in violation and still receiving grants through the Comptroller’s Office 3.
Referring the Issue to the Office of the Attorney General for Fraud and antitrust violations investigation To better tailor this analysis or move forward with a specific inquiry, they may request from developers and cities relevant details regarding: - The specific county, aquifer, or floodplain involved. - The specific developer, local agency, or entities executing the agreements. - Any structural evidence of immediate damage or specific contractual clauses of concern.
If elected, I will direct every state agency under the Governor's authority to make public the terms of any statelevel agreement, incentive, or tax arrangement involving data center developers — and I will publicly call on the Comptroller's Office, the General Land Office, and TCEQ to do the same with records already in their control.
Texans have a right to know which aquifers, floodplains, and counties are being targeted before the deal is signed, not after the wells run dry.
