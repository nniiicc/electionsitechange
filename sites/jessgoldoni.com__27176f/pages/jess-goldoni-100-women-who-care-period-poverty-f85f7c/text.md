Connect with us P Become a Volunteer Support Priorities Endorsements Request Yard Sign Contact Donate Home / News & Updates / 100 Women Who Care Back to News & Updates In the Community Packed With Dignity: Jess Joins 100 Women Who Care to Fight Period Poverty By the Goldoni Campaign · June 29, 2026 · 3 min read Community Leadership On the evening of June 18, the tables at St.
John’s Lutheran Church in Bennington turned into an assembly line for good.
Members of the Bennington chapter of 100 Women Who Care — Jess Goldoni among them — worked shoulder to shoulder to pack care kits for women and girls in need.
In just 45 minutes, the volunteers assembled 350 period supply kits in partnership with Access Period, an Omaha-based nonprofit.
By the organization’s own measure, those kits add up to roughly 1,750 worry-free days for women and girls across the community.
“You’re not just giving supplies.
You’re taking one less worry off of the plate of that child or that woman.” Access Period works to end period poverty by providing free menstrual products to anyone in Nebraska who needs them, partnering with schools and nonprofits across the state while also chipping away at the stigma around menstruation.
The need is bigger than many people realize: roughly one in four people who menstruate experience period poverty at some point in their lives, and going without can become a barrier to school, work, and daily life.
For Jess, the evening was an eye-opener.
“I had no idea,” she said.
“I knew that there were organizations like this that existed, but I didn’t realize that there was this large of a need.” To her, the math is simple: menstrual products are “just a basic need.” She was struck by how the work was done — hands-on, together.
“The way that they’ve done this, to have the volunteers actually packaging these together, was really smart,” she said.
“It’s a way for us to physically give back and actually have a sense of giving back yourself.” Steering committee member Kelsay Brohimer said the chapter hoped the night would do two things: shine a light on period poverty, and remind every woman receiving a kit that it was “packed with love and dignity.” She pointed to the power of many people giving a little time.
“You can always make a difference individually, but when we come together the difference made is so much larger,” she said.
Jess agreed that big change can start small.
“Sometimes one person can make the biggest difference, because one person impacts another person on a one-to-one level,” she said.
She also valued the simple gift of community: “How often can you just hang out and do something good with other women?
It was really nice to be able to slow down and just spend an hour to give back.” That spirit — neighbors showing up for neighbors — is exactly the kind of District 18 Jess is fighting for: one where everyone has what they need to show up to school, to work, and to life with dignity.
