I was born and raised in Billings, and I have paid the same bills you have.
I dropped out.
I got knocked down.
I spent a stretch of 2018 living in a trailer without a paying job while I helped liquidate the family business and buried my grandfather.
I know what a bad year looks like from the inside because I have had one, and it wasn’t that long ago.
I installed guardrail for most of my twenties.
I’ve run heavy equipment for William Civil out of Bozeman.
Now I consult on projects for a construction company in Billings, and I work with guys who can tell you the price of a bundle of shingles this week and what it was in the spring.
My opponent runs his own firm here in town.
His name is on the door.
He defends murder and homicide cases and he’s good at it, and the life he’s built is the kind of life a career like that builds.
It’s a long way from where most of this district actually lives.
None of that makes me better than my opponent.
What it makes me is closer to the problem.
When the people writing the affordability laws haven’t felt any of it in twenty years, you end up with a $161 tax cut sold as help and a paycheck that still doesn’t cover the month.
HD 47 doesn’t need another polished résumé in that room.
It needs someone who’s actually lived on this side of the ledger.
Hard work should still mean you can provide, get ahead, and build a life worth passing on.
