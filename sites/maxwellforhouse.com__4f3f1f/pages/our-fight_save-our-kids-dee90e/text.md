That is about 4.4 times the 798 reported in 2021.

Protect children.
Reach families earlier.
Child safety does not begin at the courthouse.
It begins with responding to addiction, family violence and instability before children are left carrying the consequences.
In Montana’s 2023 prevention cases, chemical dependence was the most commonly reported reason for Child Protective Services involvement.
Domestic abuse was another major factor.
These are statewide prevention-case figures, not Yellowstone County removal reasons.
Treatment and family support must work alongside immediate protection whenever a child is unsafe.

Protection needs more than promises.
Our fight is for help that reaches children, clear responsibility when systems fail, and public investment that can show what it accomplished.
Give investigations the capacity to act Establish current staffing, forensic capacity and case delays before making funding decisions.
Target resources to the documented gaps, with survivor support and due process built into the response.
Make stable homes the goal Support safe reunification when possible, timely permanency when it is not, foster families and independent child advocates.
Track how long children wait and whether they remain safe after leaving care.
Make it easier for kids to reach help Strengthen access to trusted adults, trauma-informed care and youth mental-health support.
Prevention should include sexual-violence education, online safety and a clear path to reporting harm.
Support families before crisis takes over Connect treatment, domestic-violence services, housing stability and practical family support.
Protecting children and helping parents recover should not be competing goals.
Close the gaps in the public record Seek county-level reporting on substantiated harm, investigation outcomes, time to services and permanency.
Publish useful totals without exposing children, families or sensitive investigations.
Invest where it makes a difference Pair targeted spending with clear responsibilities and measurable outcomes.
New laws matter, but so do the people, training and services needed to make protection real.
These are campaign priorities, not programs already delivered or promises that one state legislator controls every agency.
State and local government, schools, courts and community partners all have a role.
