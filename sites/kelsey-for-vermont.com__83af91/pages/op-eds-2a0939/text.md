Endorsements Contact Donate What makes someone an effective elected official?
At its core, public service requires listening, problem-solving, ethical responsibility, and the willingness to adapt when something isn’t working.
These are not abstract ideals – they are the daily practice of nursing.
That’s why nurses will excel in elected office.
At the foundation of nursing practice is a framework known as ADPIE: assessment, diagnosis, planning, implementation, and evaluation.
It’s not enough to act – we must measure whether our actions are working.
And if they’re not, we adjust.
We try again.
We improve.
Imagine what governance could look like if that same mindset guided public policy.
Too often, legislation is passed and left untouched, even when it no longer serves the people it was meant to help.
Nurses are trained to ask: Is this working?
If not, why?
And what can we do differently?
Evaluation is not optional in nursing – it is essential.
It should be essential in government, too.
Nursing is also grounded in ethics.
We treat every patient, regardless of background, belief, or circumstance.
We meet people where they are, and we advocate for their needs – even when it’s difficult.
As an elected official, that same ethical obligation translates into representing every voice in the community.
In Hinesburg, that means listening to neighbors, showing up in conversations, and bringing those perspectives into every discussion – whether in committee rooms, in hallway conversations, or even over lunch with colleagues.
Representation isn’t just about casting votes; it’s about carrying your community with you into every space where decisions are made.
Communication is another cornerstone of nursing.
In healthcare, jargon can create barriers – but clear, honest communication builds trust.
(See article: Nurses Ranked Most Trusted Profession for 24th Consecutive Year ) As nurses, we learn how to explain complex issues in ways that people can actually understand.
More importantly, we learn to listen.

Housing availability and affordability are a huge problem today in Hinesburg and all of Vermont.
Here are actions I have taken to become a fully informed and effective representative for all of Hinesburg: Met with experts in the field who live and breathe housing assistance, housing finance and home builders such as Champlain Housing Trust, Evernorth, and Let's Build Homes, Hinesburg Affordable Housing Committee Engaged with Hinesburg residents about their housing experiences -- at work as a nurse, on my bus commute, as I am campaigning, with my friends, literally anywhere I go!
Learned everything I can through things like Ezra Klein's "Abundance", listening to authoritative sources, and seeking out conversation and education through other community and Vermont leaders Participated in education and discussion events such as a showing of "Fault Lines: Inside America's Housing", a documentary followed by a discussion by Let's Build Homes in Burlington For more thoughts on what we can do at the state level for our housing situation: Read Full Op Ed on The Housing Problem Thoughts?
