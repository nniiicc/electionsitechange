What is the current political landscape in Frankfort?
There are 100 members in the House, 80 Republicans (the marjority party) and 20 Democrats (the minority party).
In the Senate, there are 32 Republicans (majority party) and 6 Democrats (minority party).
With a super-majority in both chambers, republicans control the flow of what bills are heard and passed.
As a Democrat in the super-minority, our vote is rarely needed to pass a bill out of a committee or on the House floor.
Our current Democrat governor, Andy Beshear, can and often vetoes bills that are dangerous for Kentuckians.
However, the Republican majority will override any veto he signs, which is in their power and right to do so as the majority.
The reality is incredibly frustrating.

When are new districts drawn?
Every 10 years, the U.S.
Census Bureau conducts a head count of everyone in the United States .
Based on this count, new legislative and congressional district maps are drawn, reflecting changes in the population and its demographics, to ensure equal representation.
The Census is directly tied to our representation in elected office and is a critical foundation of our democracy.
Kentucky does not have an independent redistricting commission.
This means legislators can draw their own maps, which introduces political bias like making districts lean toward a favorable outcome for one party.
Voters should choose their representatives, not a political party choosing their voters.
Our Constitution requires ensuring districts are contiguous and compact.
In addition, district maps cannot violate the Voting Rights Act of 1965, which prohibits racial gerrymandering that would in any way disenfranchise minority voters.

View the MAP When is the General Assembly in Session?
The Kentucky General Assembly’s (GA) session always convenes on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in January.
In odd-numbered years, the session is 30 legislative days and must end by March 30th.
In even-numbered years, the session is 60 legislative days and must end by April 15th.
