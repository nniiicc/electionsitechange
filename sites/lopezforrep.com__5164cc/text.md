Lisa Lopez Candidate · 6th Norfolk District The Issues That Matter What I'll fight for on Beacon Hill "Massachusetts generally has a high quality of life.
But affordability remains a critical issue.
Day to day expenses like gas, rent, child care, groceries and taxes — and bigger ticket items like college tuition, healthcare, housing and saving for retirement.
We can't save our way to long term financial stability.
We have to make thoughtful choices about what short term investments deliver long term benefits, and rethink traditional approaches to how government services are delivered and funded." 01 Housing & Affordability Seniors, veterans, young families — everyone deserves stability.
As chair of both the Canton Housing Authority and the Affordable Housing Trust, I've been working this complex problem for years and know how much it matters.
02 Healthcare Access & Affordability Massachusetts was a leader in healthcare access and affordability when "Romney Care" was first implemented in 2007.
Working families and retirees count on Beacon Hill's commitment to MassHealth and Medicaid as bedrock protections for our quality of life.
03 Education & Opportunity As a long-term trustee of Boston's Cathedral High School — serving mostly first-generation, college-bound urban youth — I know Massachusetts has a well-deserved reputation for educational excellence.
Students from less affluent districts deserve the support to experience it too.
04 Climate & Clean Energy I championed Canton's municipal energy aggregation program (known as Canton Electrical Choice) to deliver cleaner, cheaper energy to residents.
On Beacon Hill I'll push for policies that take our climate obligations seriously.
05 Civil Rights & Immigration After President Trump's first election I became a pro bono immigration attorney because I believe in standing up for the most vulnerable.
I'll bring that same conviction to the State House.

I grew up the oldest of five in a middle-class New Jersey family.
My father — born in the US to a Puerto Rican mother and Mexican father — and my mother were my role models for community service and love of country, even though I never shared their politics.
After law school I came to Massachusetts, raised my three children here, and never left.
For nearly 25 years I was the only woman on the executive team at Haemonetics, helping build a global business that improved the lives of surgical and cancer patients.
It was hard.
It was thrilling.
And it taught me that you get more done through persistence, collaboration, and showing up every single day.
That's the same approach I've brought to every role in this community — and the same approach I'll bring to Beacon Hill.
