We Can't Let That Disappear.
April 30, 2026 Share: Last month, my daughter performed in Seussical the Musical at Hidden Hollow Elementary.
Before that, it was The Lion King.
She lights up on that stage in a way I don't see anywhere else.
But this post isn't really about my daughter.
It's about my son.
My son is autistic.
Making friends doesn't come easily to him.
Reading social cues is hard.
Walking into a room full of kids can be overwhelming.
Then he was cast in Aladdin at Hidden Hollow.
The teachers gave him a speaking role.
I don't think they'll ever fully understand what that meant to our family.
He learned lines.
He rehearsed with other kids.
He made friends in a way that felt natural for the first time.
He had a place where he belonged.
That didn't happen because of a textbook or a standardized test.
It happened because someone at his school believed that arts education matters enough to fund it, staff it, and make space for every kid, including mine.
That program is the Beverley Taylor Sorenson Arts Learning Program, and it almost lost its funding this year.
What Is the BTS Arts Program?
The Beverley Taylor Sorenson Arts Learning Program is a state-funded program that places trained arts specialists in elementary schools across Utah.
It's been around since 2008 and currently operates in over 400 schools.
The specialists work alongside classroom teachers to integrate arts like drama, music, dance, and visual art into core subjects.
The program is funded by the Utah State Legislature and supported by Art Works for Kids, a nonprofit foundation, along with seven Utah universities including BYU and the University of Utah that provide professional development for the specialists.
At Hidden Hollow Elementary right here in Eagle Mountain, this program is what makes school plays, choir, and performing arts opportunities possible for kids like mine.
The Funding Threat Earlier this year, the Utah State Board of Education discussed reducing funding for the BTS Arts program ahead of the 2026 legislative session.
Lawmakers told K-12 schools to prepare for budget cuts, and the BTS program was on the list.
Parents and educators across the state pushed back.
The program was spared for now.
But "for now" isn't permanent.
The program's future depends on legislators who understand what it does for real kids in real classrooms.
And here's the thing: the funding doesn't even reach all Utah elementary schools yet.
There are kids in our state who never get this opportunity.
Instead of cutting the program, we should be expanding it.
Why This Matters for Eagle Mountain Eagle Mountain has a median age of 23.
Nearly half our population is under 18.
We are a community defined by young families raising kids.
The quality of our schools isn't an abstract policy issue here.
It's the reason most of us moved here.
Programs like BTS Arts aren't extras.
They're the reason some kids show up to school excited.
They're the reason a shy kid finds their voice.
They're the reason my autistic son made friends for the first time through a school play.
When we talk about education funding, this is what we're talking about.
Not line items.
Kids.
What I'll Do If elected, I will fight to protect and expand the Beverley Taylor Sorenson Arts Learning Program.
Specifically: I will advocate for stable, ongoing legislative funding for BTS Arts so schools like Hidden Hollow don't have to worry every budget cycle about whether their program survives.
I will push to expand the program to reach more elementary schools across the state, because every kid in Utah deserves access to arts education, not just the ones who happen to attend a school that received a grant.
I will support adequate funding so that programs like these aren't the first thing on the chopping block when budgets get tight.
Arts education isn't a luxury.
For some kids, it's the thing that makes school worth showing up to.
I know because I watched it happen at my own kitchen table, when my son came home from rehearsal smiling and said he had a friend.
That's worth fighting for. — Kristin Meyer, Candidate for Utah House District 50 Share: Stay in the loop Get campaign updates and new posts delivered to your inbox.
