Kristin Meyer for HD 50 About Issues Compare Candidates NEW Blog Get Involved Donate Back to Home What I'll Fight For Real solutions for Eagle Mountain, Cedar Fort & Fairfield Eagle Mountain is one of the fastest-growing communities in America.
Our population has grown from 3,000 to over 60,000 in just two decades.
That growth is a testament to how much people love this community, but it also means our roads, water, schools, and services have to keep pace.
I'm running because I believe our district deserves a representative who is laser-focused on the issues that affect our daily lives.
Not culture wars.
Not national politics.
Our community.
Roads & Infrastructure That Keep Up With Our Growth Eagle Mountain families know the reality: our roads weren't built for the number of people now using them.
Commutes to Salt Lake Valley and Utah Valley are getting longer every year, and new housing developments continue to be approved while road projects lag behind.
We need a representative who fights for state transportation funding and holds developers accountable for the infrastructure their projects require.
Fight for Eagle Mountain's fair share of state transportation funding.
UDOT has committed $2.4 billion to Utah County, and our district needs to see real results, not just promises.
Require that road infrastructure is planned and funded before new high-density developments are approved, not after.
Advocate for expanded public transit options connecting Eagle Mountain to employment centers along I-15 so families have alternatives to sitting in traffic.
Support completion of critical east-west road connections like Mid Valley Road and the Cedar Valley Freeway corridor to reduce bottlenecks.
Protecting Our Water, Planning Our Growth Water is the defining issue of Eagle Mountain's future.
Our city's water planning depends on a lot of things going right at the same time, and our population keeps growing.
At the same time, large-scale developments that use significant amounts of water have been approved without enough public transparency about the long-term impact on our supply.
We cannot keep approving growth on paper while our water margins shrink.
Families need to know their taps will keep running for generations.
Require independent water impact assessments before approving major new developments to ensure our water supply isn't being overcommitted.
Support long-term water conservation planning that protects residential users first, not industrial interests.
Push for transparency in water supply reporting so residents can see real data on capacity, demand, and risk, not just optimistic projections.
Advocate for state investment in water reuse infrastructure and regional water planning to protect fast-growing communities like ours.
Great Schools for Every Eagle Mountain Kid With a median age of just 23 and nearly half our population under 18, Eagle Mountain is full of kids who deserve excellent schools.
Our families chose this community to raise their children, and they expect classrooms that aren't overcrowded, teachers who are supported and want to stay, and schools that have the resources to help every student succeed, including kids with disabilities who need specialized support.
Advocate for state education funding that accounts for rapidly growing districts like Lake Mountain School District, so new schools and classroom capacity keep pace with enrollment.
Support competitive teacher pay and working conditions to attract and retain great educators in our community.
Teacher turnover hurts our kids.
Strengthen coordination between schools and community disability service providers so families raising children with special needs aren't navigating the system alone.
Protect school mental health resources and ensure that licensed professionals can do their jobs effectively while keeping parents informed and involved.
Real Support for Families, Not Just Promises As a mom raising a child with autism, I know firsthand how overwhelming and exhausting the system can be.
Families in our district wait months or years for critical services.
Providers are underfunded.
Coordination between schools, state agencies, and community programs is fragmented.
I'm running because I've lived this, and because every family raising a child with a disability deserves a representative who understands the fight and will show up for them.
Reduce waitlist times for critical disability services by pushing for adequate state funding of providers and direct support professionals.
Ensure providers are properly reimbursed so families can actually access the care their children are entitled to, not just on paper.
Strengthen coordination between schools, Medicaid waiver programs, and community providers so families aren't stuck making dozens of phone calls to piece together services.
Advocate for respite care funding and caregiver support programs because parents can't fight for their kids if they're running on empty.
