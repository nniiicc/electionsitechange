Getting help shouldn’t take months of phone calls or money a family doesn’t have.
I’m proud to be a Mental Health Now candidate.
I’ve committed to making mental health a priority if elected to the Utah House, because access to care affects everything from our children’s ability to learn to our families’ ability to stay healthy and financially secure.
We tell people to reach out.
We need to make sure there is someone available when they do.
Here are four priorities I would work toward: 1.
Make care easier to get and afford.
Push for insurance coverage that connects people with providers who are actually available, and fewer unnecessary barriers that delay treatment.
2.
Help children before they reach a crisis.
Support more school counselors, psychologists, and social workers, and stronger connections between schools, families, and community providers.
3.
Strengthen our mental health workforce.
Support scholarships and incentives to help more people enter mental health professions and serve communities where care is difficult to find.
4.
Improve crisis response.
Support mobile crisis teams and places where people can receive timely care from trained mental health professionals, with follow up support after the immediate crisis has passed.
Mental healthcare is healthcare.
Our budgets and policies should reflect that.
People deserve to know that help is within reach, that their struggles matter, and that their elected leaders are willing to do something about it.
