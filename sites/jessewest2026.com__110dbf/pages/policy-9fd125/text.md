THE PHILOSOPHY The philosophy is simple: government should stay out of your life, your wallet, and your choices.
That means no forever wars, no death penalty, no drug prohibition, and no government picking economic winners and losers.
That's the whole platform.
War & Foreign Policy · Death Penalty · Immigration · Healthcare · Economy · Cannabis · Public Lands · Voting Reform War & Foreign Policy The best way to support the troops is to bring them home.
Defense should mean defending — not endless nation-building campaigns that drain resources, lives, and our national focus.
We've spent trillions abroad while infrastructure crumbles at home.
Enough.
A strong military doesn't require permanent global occupation.
Real strength comes from strategic readiness, not exhausted deployments in forever wars with no clear mission or exit strategy.
We can defend America without policing the world.
When veterans come home, we owe them real support — healthcare, mental health services, housing, and job training.
Too many servicemembers return to bureaucratic nightmares and broken promises.
That's unacceptable.
Unwinding the military industrial complex overnight isn't realistic.
It would cost jobs and cause real economic disruption.
The pragmatic move is to redirect that spending toward space exploration and airspace defense.
Not ideal, but better than another forever war.
Specific Proposals: • End U.S. involvement in undeclared conflicts and forever wars • Bring troops home from non-strategic deployments • Redirect military spending toward veteran care and domestic infrastructure • Require Congressional authorization for any new military engagement • Expand mental health and transition support for returning servicemembers Death Penalty & Criminal Justice The government has executed innocent people.
That's not justice — that's a mistake you can't take back.
Since 1973, over 190 people on death row have been exonerated.
How many didn't get that chance?
We'll never know.
The death penalty doesn't deter crime, doesn't bring closure to families, and costs more than life imprisonment.
What it does do is give the state irreversible power over human life — a power no government should have, especially one with a documented history of getting it wrong.
Criminal justice should be about accountability, rehabilitation, and public safety — not vengeance.
Life imprisonment holds the guilty accountable without gambling innocent lives on a broken system.
Specific Proposals: • Abolish the death penalty at the federal level • Expand access to DNA testing and post-conviction review • Reform prosecutorial misconduct protections • End mandatory minimums and restore judicial discretion • Invest in rehabilitation and reentry programs that actually reduce recidivism Immigration & Freedom of Movement Freedom of Movement is a core libertarian philosophy and is a critical right you enjoy as an American.
Immigration reform is not about handouts or giving away resources.
That's socialism and we already have a social support system in place that does not work.
People and communities can care for themselves if the government will get out of their way.
Otto T.
Mallery said it best, "When goods do not cross borders, soldiers will".
There is no need, ever, for any purpose, for the United States to have secret police, that is masked and given the authority to kill citizens.
ICE must go and the DHS needs to be reduced significantly.
We need to fix the system and instead of incentivizing people to come and take advantage of it, we need to incentivize people to come and participate in a system that works.
Just like drug and alcohol prohibition, criminalizing immigration does not work.
Let's bring the whole system into the light, it will be safer and more efficient.
Specific Proposals: • Open the borders to a freer exchange of labor, ideas, and goods that grows American business. • Cut the billions wasted on detention, walls, and deportation, and redirect it to real priorities. • Give businesses access to the workers they need without the bureaucratic red tape. • Focus law enforcement on real threats like organized crime and trafficking, with screening at points of entry. • Build one fair tax system that everyone pays into, native-born and newcomer alike. • Create clear, straightforward pathways to citizenship and integration. • Manage local resources so both residents and new arrivals get fair access without overwhelming services. • Fix our foreign policy.
If we keep damaging other nations, open borders won't work.
That's a core issue.
Healthcare The American healthcare system isn't broken.
It's working exactly as designed.
Insurance companies, hospital systems, and drug giants have spent decades buying laws that shield them from competition, lock in their margins, and send you the bill.
That isn't a free market.
It's a cartel with a lobbying budget.
The fix isn't more government.
It's less.
Strip out the middlemen.
Kill the rules that exist only to protect the incumbents.
Let doctors and hospitals compete for your business on price and quality.
Your health savings should be yours.
Money you own and control, that follows you from job to job and state to state, with the same tax break your employer already gets for buying your coverage.
Not an account the government fills and runs for you.
Your money.
Your doctor.
Your choices.
No bureaucrat, public or private, deciding what you deserve.
Specific Proposals: • Expand tax-free Health Savings Accounts and give individuals the same tax break employers get, so your coverage isn't chained to your job • Break the government-protected middlemen (PBMs, insurers, and the pharmacies they own) that skim the spread between you and your pharmacist • Repeal certificate-of-need laws that block new clinics and providers from competing • Cut the FDA red tape that keeps cheaper generics and biosimilars off the shelf • Require up-front pricing from every hospital and provider, so you never get a bill for a number nobody quoted you • Let people buy health insurance across state lines Economy & Workers The economy doesn't belong to politicians, lobbyists, or billionaire donors.
It belongs to the people who actually build things, serve customers, and create value.
Government's job is to stay out of the way — not pick winners and losers through subsidies, bailouts, and tax carve-outs.
Workers don't need government permission to organize, negotiate, or start their own businesses.
They need freedom from occupational licensing schemes, zoning laws that kill home-based businesses, and regulations written by big corporations to crush small competitors.
The tax code is a weapon used by politicians to reward their friends and punish their enemies.
Simplify it.
Flatten it.
Let people keep what they earn instead of navigating a system designed to confuse and extract.
Specific Proposals: • Eliminate occupational licensing for non-safety-critical professions • End corporate subsidies and industry-specific tax breaks • Simplify the tax code and lower rates across the board • Repeal zoning laws that restrict home businesses and housing supply • Protect the right to organize without government interference Cannabis Legalization What you do with your own body is your business.
The government has no place deciding which plants you can grow or consume — especially when alcohol and tobacco, both more dangerous, are perfectly legal.
The War on Drugs is a failure, and cannabis prohibition is its most glaring absurdity.
Legalization isn't just about freedom — it's about ending decades of pointless arrests, incarceration, and ruined lives over a plant.
It's about letting farmers grow a cash crop, entrepreneurs build businesses, and states collect tax revenue instead of spending billions on enforcement.
Deschedule it.
Expunge the convictions.
Let states regulate as they see fit.
This isn't complicated — it's just common sense and respect for individual liberty.
Specific Proposals: • Remove cannabis from the federal controlled substances list • Expunge all federal cannabis convictions and release non-violent offenders • Allow interstate cannabis commerce and banking access • Permit veterans to use medical cannabis without losing VA benefits • Let states regulate cannabis like alcohol without federal interference Public Lands & Environment Libertarians, committed to protecting individual rights and minimizing government overreach, should support environmental policies that safeguard clean air and water as fundamental extensions of the right to life, liberty, and property.
This is a hierarchy of rights, and while private property rights are important, clean air, clean water, and clean soil in our country is a more important right.
The pollution of shared natural resources directly violates the rights of individuals to live free from harm imposed by others.
When a factory dumps waste into a river or releases harmful pollutants into the air, it initiates a form of physical force against those downstream or downwind, depriving them of clean water and air essential for their survival and well-being.
Supporting policies that prevent such harm aligns with the Libertarian principle of opposing the initiation of physical force.
Moreover, ensuring clean water and air is critical to upholding the Libertarian commitment to property rights.
Pollution that contaminates private wells, destroys crops, or reduces property values constitutes a trespass on private property.
This interference undermines the individual's right to use and enjoy their property without outside interference.
Libertarians should champion environmental policies that enforce accountability for pollution, using the free market to incentivize responsible behavior while ensuring that polluters bear the costs of their actions rather than externalizing them to others.
Finally, protecting air and water quality promotes voluntary and contractual relations among individuals.
A healthy environment supports a thriving free market by reducing conflicts and fostering trust between parties.
Conversely, unchecked pollution leads to increased costs for individuals and businesses, from healthcare expenses to cleanup efforts, ultimately hindering economic freedom.
Libertarians should advocate for policies that empower individuals and communities to protect these vital resources while limiting government interference to its proper role: enforcing contracts, protecting property rights, and prohibiting the initiation of harm.
Supporting clean air and water is not just compatible with Libertarian principles; it is essential to their fullest realization.
Specific Proposals: • Oppose federal land transfers that restrict public access • Expand protections for climbing, hiking, and camping access • Shift environmental management authority to states and local communities • End taxpayer subsidies for fossil fuel extraction on public lands • Streamline permitting for renewable energy projects Voting Rights & Electoral Reform Democracy dies when politicians get to choose their voters instead of the other way around.
Gerrymandering, ballot access restrictions, and winner-take-all systems protect incumbents and lock out competition.
If you're afraid of voters having more choices, you don't trust democracy.
Ranked-choice voting, open primaries, and automatic voter registration aren't radical ideas — they're basic reforms that give voters more power and break the two-party stranglehold on American politics.
Competition is good for business and it's good for democracy.
Voting should be easy, accessible, and secure.
End arbitrary restrictions that make registration difficult, expand early voting and vote-by-mail, and ensure every eligible citizen can participate without jumping through hoops designed to suppress turnout.
Specific Proposals: • Implement ranked-choice voting for federal elections • End partisan gerrymandering through independent redistricting • Establish automatic voter registration at 18 • Expand early voting and no-excuse mail voting nationwide • Lower ballot access barriers for third-party and independent candidates Agree?
