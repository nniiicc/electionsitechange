Red Tape "Modernizing the Navy shouldn't mean more middle management in D.C.; it should mean more tools and higher pay for the pipefitters and welders in Bremerton." The Problem The Navy's Shipyard Infrastructure Optimization Program (SIOP) is a multi-billion dollar, 20-year project.
While billions are allocated, the money often gets swallowed by "consultant fees" and "administrative oversight" in DC before it ever hits the dry docks.
The federal government is more interested in "process" than "production." The Solution Move the money from the office buildings in Virginia to the dry docks in Bremerton.
Fund actual shipyard workers, not DC consultants Invest in tools and equipment, not bureaucratic overhead Prioritize readiness over red tape Blue-Collar Environmentalism vs.
Green Experiments Federal mandates that prioritize unproven "Green" maritime technologies over the immediate maintenance needs of the existing fleet put both national security and local jobs at risk.
Rapid electrification of support vessels threatens job stability.
Forcing "experimental" tech onto the shipyard before the infrastructure is ready is dangerous.
"We need ships that can fight today, not social experiments that might float tomorrow." Police Staffing & Rural Safety "It's unacceptable that Washington ranks last in police staffing." The Problem In places like Mason and Jefferson Counties, response times are a matter of life and death.
Washington ranks last in police staffing nationwide, leaving rural communities dangerously underserved.
When you call 911, help should come fast—no matter where you live.
The Solution I'm advocating for a 'Federal Public Safety Floor'—direct federal incentives for local agencies to hire and retain officers.
A 911 call should be answered just as fast in Port Angeles as it is in Seattle.
