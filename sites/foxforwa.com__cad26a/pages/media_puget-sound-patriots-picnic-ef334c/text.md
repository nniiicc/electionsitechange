Home About Teresa Mission Media DONATE Back to Media & News Campaign Update Teresa Fox at the Puget Sound Patriots Picnic: We Have Not Yet Begun to Fight At the Puget Sound Patriots Picnic, Teresa Fox delivered a powerful message on patriotism, sacrifice, liberty, and the responsibility every generation bears to preserve the Republic.
August 15, 2026 Campaign Staff Teresa Fox was honored to speak this afternoon at the Puget Sound Patriots Picnic , joining community members, constitutional conservatives, and longtime supporters connected through the Bremerton Kitsap Rifle and Revolver Club.
In her remarks, Teresa spoke about the ordinary Americans who built this country, defended it, and handed its freedoms forward through courage and sacrifice.
She described herself as a strict constitutionalist, a strong Second Amendment supporter, a lifelong supporter of veterans, and a candidate focused on public safety, lowering government waste, and providing adequate representation across all six counties of Washington’s 6th Congressional District.
Teresa also made clear that she sees a stark choice before voters this November.
“I am the contrast.” For Teresa, that contrast begins with public safety and constitutional rights.
“Public safety, both foreign and domestic, is the number one priority of government under the U.S.
Constitution, period.
Protection of individual rights is number one in the Washington Constitution.” She pledged to listen to the people she seeks to represent and told the crowd: “I will listen to you and will do everything I can to restore our right to keep and bear arms.” But Teresa’s message reached beyond any single issue.
She spoke about the generations of Americans who came before us, ordinary people who answered extraordinary calls, sacrificed for liberty, and left future generations a Republic worth preserving.
Referencing John Paul Jones, Teresa recalled: “When John Paul Jones was in the heat of battle in close quarters and asked to surrender, he said, ‘We have not yet begun to fight.’” She continued: “We owe our very lives and freedoms to those ordinary men.
They shed blood so that we may be free today.” The fight facing Americans today, Teresa said, is different.
“Today we must fight, not a revolution of musket and cannon, but of ideas and renewed effort.
Today we must do our part to keep the Republic.” That message presents voters in Washington’s 6th Congressional District with a clear contrast.
While Congresswoman Emily Randall has publicly celebrated support from activist organizations, including endorsements from Indivisible groups, Teresa Fox spent the afternoon standing with Puget Sound Patriots and speaking directly to citizens committed to constitutional liberty, individual rights, limited government, public safety, and preserving the freedoms generations of Americans sacrificed to secure.
Teresa summarized that choice in some of the strongest words of her speech: “This election is not a contract with America, this election is a contrast FOR America.
And the contrast could not be more clear.
Law and order or chaos on the streets.
It’s our choice in November.” Her campaign, she told the crowd, is a campaign for the people, with People over Politics as a cornerstone.
Teresa also spoke about the energy she sees beyond political models and conventional predictions.
“A lot of great patriots in this room, a lot in Oly.
I want to do great things in Washington, D.C.” And as she looked toward the future, Teresa returned to the responsibility inherited from those who founded and defended this nation.
“Together we can give our dear America a 500th birthday.
We are ordinary men, as our founding fathers were ordinary.
We here today come from that stock.” Her closing words captured the spirit of the afternoon: “We are blessed to live in the greatest country ever born.
A beacon of shining light and hope to the rest of the world.” The contrast Teresa presented is one voters will decide for themselves in November: Law and order or chaos on the streets.
People over Politics.
A campaign for the people.
A Republic worth preserving.
“May we be forever free.
