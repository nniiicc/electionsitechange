Emily Randall’s dismissal of a constituent’s concerns, arguing that representation in Washington’s 6th Congressional District must be based on service to all constituents, not race, sex, party, or demographic group.
September 9, 2026 Campaign Staff Statement from Teresa Fox, Candidate for Congress, Washington’s 6th Congressional District There are moments in public life when a short exchange tells us something much larger about how our elected officials view the people they represent.
This week, Emily Randall’s verified Facebook account posted about her effort to secure $1.25 million in federal Community Project Funding to relocate and rebuild a Jamestown S’Klallam Tribe natural-resources building away from flooding and tsunami hazards.
A constituent responded: “It’s always special groups with you, how about working with all our citizens!
Get real” The response from Randall was: “do you mean ‘why don’t I see more posts from you about white men?’” That response should concern every resident of Washington’s Sixth Congressional District, regardless of race, sex, political party, or whether they agree with the original commenter.
Representation should not be based on skin color.
Yesterday I met with that citizen of Washington’s 6th Congressional District — Jeff — whose valid economic concern was dismissed by my opponent because she treated him as a “white man.” Jeff and I had a great conversation about the actual job of a representative.
We talked about prioritizing our tax dollars and not wasting them.
That is what my opponent should have done.
We did not need to talk about race, sexual orientation, or whether we believe in little green men.
Because that is NOT what this was about.
Let me be equally clear about something else.
This is not an argument against the Jamestown S’Klallam Tribe.
Tribal members are our neighbors and constituents.
Protecting infrastructure from flooding and tsunami hazards, safeguarding natural resources, and maintaining the ability to monitor shellfish and salmon are legitimate issues for public discussion.
A serious debate can and should be had about whether a particular project warrants federal funding and whether $1.25 million is the appropriate amount.
But that isn’t what happened here.
A citizen questioned his representative’s priorities.
Rather than answer the substance of his concern, the response from her reduced that constituent’s criticism to his presumed race and sex.
Those facts become even more troubling when we remember what the job of a United States representative actually is.
When you are elected to Congress, you do not represent only the people who voted for you.
You don’t represent only Democrats or Republicans.
You don’t represent only Tribal communities, veterans, union members, business owners, women, men, racial minorities, LGBTQ residents, rural communities, or urban neighborhoods.
You represent ALL of them.
As I have campaigned through our six counties, one message has been resoundingly clear: the majority of our constituents feel they do not feel they have a voice.
Adequate representation has been a cornerstone of this campaign.
They are getting a voice.
Washington’s Sixth District is extraordinarily diverse in geography, economics, and experience.
It stretches across communities with profoundly different needs.
Randall herself has described WA-06 as six counties, each with its own opportunities and challenges.
That diversity should be something a representative brings together, not something used to sort constituents into competing demographic categories.
What this exchange also shows is that Congresswoman Randall is out of touch with the reality of what is actually happening in our district.
Families here are living through a public safety crisis and an economic disaster.
People are literally dying on our streets, citizens are paying exorbitant taxes with little to show for it, their businesses and families are no longer safe.
They can see she has done nothing meaningful about either.
Instead of addressing those realities, she dismissed a constituent’s concern by invoking her tired “white man” race baiting.
She is working on out of state issues of no concern to us – abortion rights while abortion is legal here; supporting criminal illegal aliens instead of victims and citizens; and working her political activist communist agenda.
She should be here, in District 6, working on issues important to the majority of us.
To be sure, this is not an isolated comment from my opponent.
This is the latest in a long string of bigoted and racist remarks and a race-baiting pattern of behavior.
She also frequently declares there is a “war on brown bodies” and calls our citizens in Forks, who wear flamingo and AK47 shirts, a “mixed bag.” Let’s stop the division.
Constituents can be blunt, frustrated, and sometimes downright angry.
Holding public office requires the ability to answer difficult questions without treating someone’s race or sex as a rebuttal.
We have spent far too many years being encouraged to see one another first as categories.
I want something different for Washington’s Sixth.
I want the fisherman in Westport and the shipyard worker in Bremerton to know they matter.
I want the Tribal family on the Olympic Peninsula and the military family near Tacoma to know they matter.
I want farmers, small-business owners, seniors, young families, and people struggling to pay their grocery and utility bills to know they matter.
And yes, that includes white men.
It also includes everyone else.
Nobody should have to belong to the right demographic group to deserve the attention and respect of their member of Congress.
When I am entrusted to represent Washington’s Sixth Congressional District, I will first and foremost uphold the Constitution.
And importantly, I will represent you.
No matter your skin color.
Because that IS what this is about.
Teresa Fox Candidate for U.S.
