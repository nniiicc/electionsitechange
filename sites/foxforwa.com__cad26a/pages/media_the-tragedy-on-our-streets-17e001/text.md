Home About Teresa Mission Media DONATE Back to Media & News Opinion The Tragedy on Our Streets Why It’s Time to End the 'Homeless Industrial Complex' and Restore Public Safety June 9, 2026 Campaign Staff The Tragedy on Our Streets: Why It’s Time to End the 'Homeless Industrial Complex' and Restore Public Safety I am running to represent Washington’s 6th Congressional District because, like many of you, I look at our communities today and barely recognize them.
Everywhere I go, from Bremerton to the furthest corners of our six-county district, the questions I hear most are centered on a singular, urgent crisis: public safety and the catastrophic state of homelessness on our streets.
For me, this crisis isn't abstract or merely political.
It is deeply personal.
I have friends whose children are dead—stolen away by an unchecked drug epidemic.
We are on the verge of losing an entire generation to narcotics and the crime that follows in their wake.
What we are witnessing is a human tragedy on a scale previously unknown in the United States.
While there are many pathways to homelessness, the horrific reality for those trapped on our streets is universal.
They are subjected to repeated rapes, robberies, and murders.
They are preyed upon by human traffickers, plagued by devastating health conditions from severe drug abuse, and shuffled from corner to corner with no stability.
It is clear to anyone paying attention: the current approach is not just failing—it is actively enabling a humanitarian disaster.
The Cost of a Broken Status Quo For years, our state's failed laws and lenient policies have directly fueled this crisis.
We have asked our law enforcement officers to do more with less, while tying their hands.
Meanwhile, our homeowners, families, and local businesses pay the price through damaged property, lost livelihoods, and a pervasive sense of insecurity.
We can no longer throw billions of taxpayer dollars at this problem without demanding quantifiable results.
For too long, we have funded a "homeless industrial complex"—a network of unaccountable programs and NGOs that profit off the continuation of the crisis rather than its cure.
We must stop enabling dependency and stop encouraging individuals to migrate to our district for limited resources.
It is time to keep more hard-earned money in your pockets and expect real solutions, not endless lip service from career politicians.
A Comprehensive, Accountable Way Forward As your representative in Congress, I will leverage federal avenues to address this public safety and humanitarian emergency.
However, federal intervention cannot be top-down or disconnected from local reality.
Since launching my campaign in February, I have spent my time on the ground, listening to citizens, business owners, and law enforcement personnel across all six counties.
My approach to legislation is built on three cornerstone principles: Inclusive Stakeholder Engagement: Effective policy cannot be written in a vacuum.
It must include the voices of all constituents—including law enforcement, medical professionals, and local business leaders.
Quantifiable Metrics: We must tie every federal dollar to measurable outcomes.
If a program isn't reducing homelessness, curbing addiction, or improving safety, it will lose its funding.
Restoring Law and Order: We must support our police and enforce the law to protect both the public and the vulnerable populations currently being victimized on our streets.
Finding Common Ground This is a complex crisis with no overnight fix, but the first step toward a solution is admitting that the status quo is broken for everyone.
We must approach this challenge with mutual respect for our varying perspectives, look past partisan divides, and find the common ground required to heal our communities.
Public safety is not a partisan issue; it is a fundamental human right.
I ask for your vote as your 6th Congressional Representative so that we can finally prioritize safety, accountability, and compassion for all of us.
