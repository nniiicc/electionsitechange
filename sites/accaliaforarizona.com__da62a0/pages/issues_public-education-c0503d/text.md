Home Meet Jayme Endorsements Make Endorsement Issues Events Photos Volunteer Voter Information Contribute Yard Signs Contact Home ❭ Issues ❭ Public Education Public Education Arizona students deserve fully funded public schools that families are proud to send their children to.
For too long, Arizona has underfunded education while expecting schools and teachers to do more with less.
When classrooms are overcrowded, teachers are underpaid, facilities are outdated, and extracurricular programs disappear, families naturally begin looking elsewhere.
I understand why parents want options — but I believe the long-term solution is rebuilding strong public schools and strong neighborhood communities.
That starts with investing directly into classrooms and students.
I support increasing teacher pay, reducing class sizes, modernizing facilities, expanding Career & Technical Education (CTE) and dual enrollment opportunities, protecting special education funding, and increasing access to school counselors and mental health services.
I also support universal free school meals and stronger support for rural schools because every child deserves the opportunity to succeed regardless of where they live.
Our neighborhood schools should once again become the heart of our communities.
Schools used to be places where families gathered for little league games, clubs, performances, playgrounds, and community events.
As communities become more isolated, we’re seeing the effects in youth mental health, community identity, and social connection.
I want to see schools become family resource hubs again by supporting extracurriculars, reopening public access to fields and playgrounds outside school hours, and investing in welcoming campuses that bring people together while still maintaining modern safety standards.
Strong schools create strong communities, and strong communities create better outcomes for our kids.
REGARDING AZ ESA VOUCHERS Arizona’s ESA program has moved far beyond its original purpose.
It was initially designed to help students with specific needs, including children with disabilities, access educational support.
Today, much of the funding is going to families who already had access to private education, while public schools, which still educate the overwhelming majority of Arizona students, struggle with underfunding.
