Emerson Levy understands what it means to balance a family budget, because she does it too.
She is a working mom who knows the value of a dollar and the pressure many Oregon families feel when trying to pay bills, manage student loans, save for the future, and provide opportunities for their children.
Life is getting more expensive, and too many families feel like they are working harder just to keep up.
Emerson believes government should focus on practical solutions that help families get ahead, lower unnecessary costs, and make Oregon a place where working people can build a future.
Emerson has worked to protect consumers and help families keep more of what they earn.
She supported stronger utility rate protections to ensure families have a voice when costs increase, including greater review of rate impacts and protections against unnecessary winter rate increases.
She also helped ban hidden online fees so Oregonians know the true cost of what they are buying before they check out.
Emerson has fought to protect families from financial hardship by supporting protections that keep medical debt from damaging credit reports.
She also helped expand broadband assistance and increase support for utility bill payment programs, ensuring more families can stay connected and afford essential services.
Emerson will continue working on practical solutions that lower costs, protect consumers, and help Oregon families build a stronger financial future.

Everyone deserves access to quality healthcare, no matter where they live.
Emerson Levy believes healthcare should focus on delivering better care, expanding access, and making services more affordable for Oregon families.
Emerson has worked to strengthen healthcare access right here in Central Oregon by expanding access to primary care, telehealth, dental care, and mental health services, especially for families who face barriers to getting the care they need.
Emerson has helped protect access to care by supporting local clinics and safety net providers, including efforts that helped keep 13 Oregon clinics operating after federal Medicaid cuts.
She has also worked to protect patients from rising costs by supporting protections against surprise ambulance bills and ensuring community health centers can continue accessing critical prescription drug discounts.
Prescription costs are another place she’s seen families get squeezed unfairly.
She championed and passed the Co-Pay Fairness Bill, changing how prescription drug costs are calculated so patients aren’t penalized by insurance loopholes
When Emerson learned that young people experiencing mental health crises were spending weeks in emergency rooms because there were not enough safe places for them to receive care, she acted.
She secured $3.1 million for youth mental health beds in Central Oregon, a project expected to break ground in 2026.
This investment will create one of the first dedicated youth emergency mental health care options on the east side of the Cascades.
Emerson has also secured more than $4 million to expand healthcare access through local clinics, helping more Central Oregon residents receive care closer to home.
Beyond expanding access, Emerson has worked to protect patients and improve how healthcare is delivered.
She supported efforts to prevent corporate takeovers of medical practices that can interfere with patient care, reduce unnecessary barriers for behavioral health providers, and strengthen protections for Oregonians seeking healthcare.
School safety is one of my top priorities.
I have a young daughter and I deeply understand the fear many parents feel sending their kids to school.
My school safety strategy includes improving emergency response times in schools, increasing school-based health and mental health centers for students, and risk assessments for school entrances and exits.
I brought forward a Silent Panic Alarm Technology bill that was one of the most bipartisan pieces of legislation of the entire 2023 legislative session, sponsored by 32 of the 35 House Democrats and all 25 House Republicans.
This legislation would provide wireless panic alert devices for schools to use in case of a wide number of emergencies, reducing the precious time it takes to reach first responders.
I was able to secure $2.5 million of funding for this technology through the budget process.
This important technology is in the process of coming to our schools.

Protecting the natural resources and beauty that make Central Oregon special is a responsibility Emerson Levy takes seriously.
Our forests, rivers, public lands, and open spaces are part of what make this region unique, and we must protect them for future generations.
As Chair of the Natural Resources Subcommittee for Ways and Means and a member of the Climate, Energy, and Environment Committee, Emerson has worked to balance responsible growth with protecting Oregon’s natural resources.
She voted for the POWER Act, a first-in-the-nation effort to ensure utility costs are fairly shared as Oregon’s energy needs grow, including with new technologies like data centers.
Since the POWER Act was passed, Oregonians utility costs have gone down, while data center’s power costs have risen.
Emerson Levy will always make sure Central Oregon is focused on lowering rates for people, and making sure data centers pay their fair share
She has also helped secure long-term investments in Oregon’s natural resources, including support for the 1.25 Percent for Wildlife Act, which will provide approximately $38 million annually for fish, wildlife, and habitat conservation across the state.
This effort brings together hunters, anglers, landowners, conservation organizations, and community leaders to protect Oregon’s natural heritage.
Emerson is also a champion for Oregon’s state parks and is working to create a public private endowment, inspired by the conservation legacy of Theodore Roosevelt, to provide sustainable funding to preserve these treasured places for generations to come.
Emerson will continue working to protect Central Oregon’s natural beauty, support responsible economic growth, and ensure Oregon remains a leader in conservation and innovation.

Wildfires are changing the way we live in Central Oregon and across our state.
Our communities have experienced the fear and disruption that comes when fires threaten our homes, forests, and way of life.
Preparing for wildfires is not just about responding to fires, it is about reducing risks before they start.
Emerson believes Oregon needs a stronger, year-round wildland firefighting workforce.
Each fire season, communities face challenges finding enough trained personnel to respond, and building a reliable workforce is essential to protecting our homes, communities, and natural resources.
Emerson will continue supporting programs like the Oregon Youth Conservation Corps, which trains young people in wildfire response and prevention while creating opportunities for the next generation of conservation leaders.
Working with Senator Anthony Broadman, Emerson helped pass House Bill 3940, creating Oregon’s first dedicated funding source for wildfire mitigation projects.
This investment supports proactive efforts like prescribed burns, forest thinning, and fuel reduction to help lower wildfire risks and protect communities before disaster strikes.
Emerson will continue working on practical solutions that improve wildfire preparedness, protect Central Oregon communities, and ensure Oregon is ready for the challenges of a changing climate.
Water is not an abstract issue in Central Oregon.
It’s the difference between a working ranch and a dry field, a growing community and one that’s outgrown its supply.
Emerson has been part of nearly every major water conversation in the Legislature because she understands how much is riding on getting this right for both agriculture and the families moving into the region.
She championed the Deschutes Basin Water Banking Bill, a market-based approach designed to let water move to where it’s needed most without trampling the rights of the farmers and ranchers who depend on it.
She also backed stronger groundwater accountability measures to make sure Oregon is actually tracking and managing what’s being used before it becomes a crisis.
Emerson will keep working on practical water solutions that protect agricultural livelihoods, support smart growth, and make sure Central Oregon isn’t caught flat-footed by the water challenges ahead.
