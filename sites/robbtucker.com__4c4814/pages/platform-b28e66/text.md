Federal agencies write regulations for Los Angeles and San Francisco, then hand them down to communities that operate nothing like a major metro area.
Out here, policy isn't an abstract debate.
It decides whether a sheriff's radio actually works during a wildfire evacuation, whether a national forest gets thinned before it burns, and whether a small business owner can keep the doors open under a mountain of federal paperwork.
Robb Tucker's approach to federal policy comes straight out of his years on the Nevada County Board of Supervisors.
County government is where you see, up close, whether a federal program actually works or just adds another layer of red tape.
Robb has had to stitch together mismatched state and federal funding streams just to keep a shelter open or fund basic mental health care, and he's watched permitting delays and excessive fees stall housing and forest health projects for years, leaving county officials to explain to neighbors why nothing is getting built.
That experience shapes a simple governing philosophy: keep decisions close to the people whenever possible, and hold Washington strictly accountable for the things only the federal government can do: securing the border, managing public forest lands, enforcing interstate commerce law, protecting taxpayers.
Those core duties deserve urgency and precision.
What Washington should stop doing is burying rural counties in unfunded mandates that drain local budgets and choke off regional economies.
This is meant to be a working plan for Congress, not a list of slogans.
Each section lays out the federal failure, how Robb's local experience shapes his view of it, and the specific legislation he'll push for.
He supports a government that lives within its means, respects private property, and honors the constitutional rights of law-abiding citizens, and he believes public service starts with taking responsibility for the place you live.
Above all, this agenda is about restoring fiscal sanity, ending the double standard for political insiders, and making federal agencies answer to the people who have to live with their decisions.
Learn more about Robb's position on specific issues below: Local Control and Federal Accountability Federal agencies keep writing rules as if rural Northern California were Los Angeles or San Francisco.
Those one-size-fits-all mandates look fine on paper, but on the ground they mean years of delay on forest work, water projects, housing, broadband, roads, bridges, and emergency access, while local communities wait for something, anything, to move.
County government is where federal ideas either work or fall apart, and Robb has watched that play out firsthand as a County Supervisor.
Washington needs to listen earlier, move faster, and stop treating rural communities as an afterthought.
That means keeping decisions close to the people, cutting unfunded mandates, and holding federal land and money managers to the same urgency as the residents who live with the consequences.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On In county government, Robb's record is defined by trying to make road projects, treatment contracts, emergency plans, housing grants, and public safety purchases actually happen despite regulatory hurdles.
He has consistently voted to approve: Inter-Agency Contracts and Emergency Plans: Streamlining local operational agreements to bypass state and federal bureaucratic gridlock.
Local Grant Administration: Aligning county budget allocations to ensure federal dollars are directed toward immediate, tangible local infrastructure needs rather than administrative red tape.
Federal Actions Supported Early County Consultation Mandates: Requiring federal agencies to consult counties early before making major land, water, habitat, forest, or infrastructure decisions.
Rural-Impact Reviews: Creating a formal rural-impact review process for major federal rules affecting housing, roads, water, fire risk, public lands, energy, or emergency services.
Streamlined Funding Compliance: Giving small and rural jurisdictions simpler compliance rules and transforming rigid federal grant programs into flexible local block grants.
Public Safety, Fentanyl, and Federal Law Enforcement Public safety takes more than slogans.
It takes the federal government actually doing its job on border security, federal prosecution, interstate crime, drug networks, and human trafficking.
When it doesn't, Northern California absorbs the fallout: a fentanyl crisis, organized retail theft, and rural sheriff's offices stretched thin trying to cover huge stretches of territory with too few deputies.
The federal government can't replace local law enforcement, but it does have a duty to back it up instead of pushing costs down to the counties.
Every time Washington fails to secure the border or coordinate offender releases, local deputies absorb the burden.
Robb wants federal dollars going to the tools deputies actually use, like radios, vehicles, and forensic equipment, instead of sitting in Washington bureaucracy.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On On the Nevada County Board of Supervisors, Robb has voted to secure immediate field tools for first responders, including approving funds for: Sheriff Communications and Mobile Technology: Upgrading localized radio networks so deputies have reliable coverage during rural patrols and wildfire evacuations.
District Attorney and Probation Grants: Supporting local prosecutorial and supervision tools to track offenders and process cases efficiently.
The Family Justice Center: Funding dedicated victim-support resources to combat domestic violence and human trafficking locally.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported The Agent Raul Gonzalez Officer Safety Act (H.R.
35): Robb supports this bill to establish strict penalties for intentionally fleeing a pursuing federal officer in a vehicle, directly protecting law enforcement and enhancing border safety.
The Subterranean Border Defense Act (H.R.
495): He backs this legislation to require annual reporting on illicit cross-border tunnel operations, forcing a clearer understanding of how criminal networks move drugs, weapons, and human cargo.
Rural Deputy Recruitment and Retention Grants: Robb will fight to expand flexible federal grants to help rural counties fund deputy hiring, retention, vehicles, radios, body cameras, and local crime lab backlogs.
Homelessness, Treatment, and Public Order Unsafe encampments, trash, and untreated mental illness on California's sidewalks and in its parks and flood zones aren't just an eyesore.
They're proof the current approach has failed.
Business owners clean up the mess, and police and outreach workers cycle through the same individuals again and again, because federal dollars keep funding the same failing programs without asking for results.
Robb calls his approach “compassion with standards.” Letting people live and die outside in squalor isn't compassionate, and pretending untreated addiction and mental illness aren't damaging public spaces isn't honest either.
Every federal homelessness dollar should be tied to results: fewer unsheltered encampments, more treatment beds, and real pathways to long-term stability.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On At the county level, Robb deals directly with the concrete reality of these challenges through localized contracts.
He has voted to approve: Homeless Outreach and Shelter Support Contracts: Funding immediate, structured shelter services paired with active, hands-on community outreach.
Permanent Supportive Housing and Treatment Services: Linking local housing assistance directly to behavioral health and substance-use recovery programs.
Federal Actions Supported Compassion with Standards Grant Rules: Amending federal funding streams to allow local governments to use housing dollars for safe, orderly shelters that enforce rules, case management, and mandatory treatment paths.
Public Land and Waterway Cleanup: Authorizing federal funds to directly support the cleanup and restoration of federally connected waterways, transportation corridors, and public lands impacted by encampments.
Strict Non-Profit Accountability: Legislation to hold agencies and non-profits strictly accountable, cutting off federal funding if they receive public money but fail to deliver reductions in unsheltered homelessness.
Wildfire, Forests, and Disaster Resilience Wildfire shapes daily life in Northern California: defensible space, evacuation routes, insurance premiums that keep climbing, public safety power shutoffs.
The federal government owns and manages most of the forest land in the region, and its permitting rules routinely stall the fuel reduction, timber work, prescribed fire, and biomass projects that would actually keep communities safer.
Robb's principle is simple: manage the forest before it burns.
The federal government should be an urgent partner in fire safety, not an obstacle to it.
That means letting counties, tribes, and local partners clear hazardous fuels instead of waiting for an unmanaged federal forest to go up.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On On the Nevada County Board of Supervisors, Robb has consistently voted to fund immediate, physical readiness projects.
His votes include: Shaded Fuel Breaks and Hazardous Fuels Reduction: Clearing dense undergrowth and removing hazardous vegetation along critical community borders and evacuation routes.
Advanced Emergency Alert Systems: Funding automated notification networks to ensure residents can evacuate safely and efficiently during an active fire.
Critical Facility Backup Generators: Approving emergency power backup systems so essential medical, public safety, and communication facilities stay online during emergencies.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported The Fix Our Forests Act (H.R.
471 / S.

The Federal Disaster Assistance Coordination Act (H.R.

The Post-Disaster Assistance Online Accountability Act (H.R.

Good Neighbor Authority Expansion: Fighting to expand stewardship contracting so counties and local agencies can clear overgrown federal land directly.
Water, Agriculture, and Working Lands Water is the lifeblood of California, and federal agencies keep mismanaging it: burdensome wetlands rules, endangered species decisions made with no local input, restrictive water contracts.
It all threatens local reservoirs, canals, flood control systems, and the family farms, ranches, and timber operations that keep the rural economy running.
Real environmental stewardship doesn't mean locking people off the land.
Robb wants to expand infrastructure, storing more water in wet years and planning ahead for dry ones, and to protect local water rights and area-of-origin principles from federal overreach.
Local irrigation districts, farms, and water agencies should have the dominant voice in decisions about land they actually manage.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On As a County Supervisor, Robb has consistently used his vote to back working landscapes and agricultural resilience, including supporting: Local Infrastructure and Flood Mitigation Contracts: Approving localized road, bridge, and culvert maintenance to protect working lands from severe storm runoff and erosion.
Rural Resource Protection: Voting to support regional water reliability and agricultural health through streamlined county-level land element updates.
Federal Actions Supported Permitting Reform for Water Storage: Streamlining federal environmental permitting for new water storage, groundwater recharge, recycled water, stormwater capture, and conveyance improvements.
Sound-Science Mandates for Habitat Decisions: Requiring federal agencies to use rigorous sound science and mandatory local consultation before making species or habitat designations that impact local water supplies.
Rural Water System Reliability Grants: Funding targeted upgrades for small, rural water systems struggling to meet strict public health and reliability standards.
Housing That Fits Communities Housing affordability is a genuine crisis, made worse by federal taxes, complex financing rules, and environmental reviews that drag projects out for years.
State and federal mandates often try to force high-density urban housing models onto rural communities without funding the infrastructure to support them, ignoring wildfire risk and evacuation routes that just don't work at that density.
Robb's family developed and operated the Forest Springs Mobilehome Community, so he knows firsthand that manufactured housing, mobile home communities, modular units, and starter homes are a real part of the affordability answer, not an afterthought.
Federal policy needs to respect local control and fire risk.
It doesn't make sense to mandate housing in places without the roads, water, sewer, or fire protection capacity to keep new residents safe.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On Through his county work, Robb has voted on the concrete mechanics of housing development.
His record includes votes to approve: Down Payment Assistance Programs: Providing direct financial paths to homeownership for local working families.
Senior and Supportive Housing Financing: Utilizing targeted funding to help finance housing options for seniors and permanent supportive housing for vulnerable residents.
County Housing Element Updates: Directing the local planning process to ensure housing development aligns with actual community capacity and wildfire safety.
Federal Actions Supported Infrastructure-Linked Housing Funds: Directing federal infrastructure dollars explicitly to communities that approve housing but completely lack the utility, broadband, or fire safety infrastructure to support it.
Financing Reform for Lower-Cost Housing: Reforms to federal financing and mortgage guidelines that artificially raise costs or make it harder to build and preserve manufactured or modular homes.
Fast-Track Environmental Reviews: Speeding up federal environmental reviews for locally approved infill, rehabilitation, and workforce housing projects.
Jobs, Small Business, and American Industry Small businesses don't have compliance departments or legal teams on staff, yet they're the ones buried under federal paperwork, sudden regulatory changes, and permitting delays.
When Washington raises taxes or changes the rules without warning, it's the independent business owner, their employees, and their customers who pay for it.
Robb ran a family business, so he thinks about economic policy in terms of real-world cash flow, not theory.
An economy only works if it lets people work hard, build something, raise their kids, and stay rooted in the community they love.
That means the economy should serve main street families over massive institutions, which requires cutting red tape and rebuilding American self-reliance.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On At the county level, Robb focuses on minimizing government interference while keeping the economic foundation stable.
His votes include: Workforce and Employment Services: Allocating local funds to job training and employment assistance programs aimed at getting out-of-work residents back into the workforce.
Routine Infrastructure Maintenance: Approving regular commercial corridor, road, and bridge repairs so local commerce can move safely and efficiently.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported The Protect Small Businesses from Excessive Paperwork Act (H.R.

The Rural Small Business Resilience Act (H.R.

Mandatory Small-Business Impact Assessments: Forcing federal agencies to explicitly account for and publish plain-language estimates of how any proposed rule will impact small businesses before it can take effect.
Education, Skills, and Parents Education is a local and parental responsibility first, but the federal government keeps using student aid and funding strings to push top-down, one-size-fits-all rules onto local schools.
Federal programs have also long overemphasized expensive four-year degrees while shortchanging the technical trades and vocational paths that lead to stable, high-paying jobs.
This one is personal.
Robb went through local public schools, his mother served for years on the school board, he's coached local youth, and he and his wife are raising their own kids in the community.
He wants the federal role limited, practical, and pro-parent, and he believes honest work deserves equal respect whether a student ends up in college or decides to wire, weld, fly, farm, or go into public safety.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On As a County Supervisor, Robb has voted to support youth and workforce preparedness by integrating county behavioral and support services with local school districts, including: Youth Mental Health and Counseling Support: Funding mental health and counseling tools to support students and families early.
Community-Based Youth Support: Approving structural grants for youth counseling, recreational safety, and localized career training pathways.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported The Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act (H.R.

Expanded Trade and Technical Aid: Legislation to allow federal student aid to follow students into high-quality, short-term credential programs, trade schools, and technical apprenticeships.
Parental Rights and Transparency Mandates: Protecting parents' rights and demanding absolute transparency regarding school curricula and costs wherever federal education dollars are involved.
Rural Health Care, Mental Health, and Recovery Rural health care is broken by geography: long travel distances, chronic provider shortages, and emergency call volumes too low to keep an ambulance district solvent.
Despite all that, the federal government still forces small rural hospitals and clinics to operate under reimbursement rules written for massive urban medical systems, pushing local facilities toward closure.
Robb's guiding principle: don't wait until someone is living on a sidewalk, sitting in an emergency room, or cycling through county jail before treatment becomes available.
Rural health care needs flexible federal rules, reimbursement rates that account for low patient volumes, permanent telehealth access, and real capacity for mental health and substance-use recovery.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On On the Nevada County Board of Supervisors, Robb has used his vote to build an immediate, practical health safety net.
His votes include approving: Recovery Residences and Substance-Use Services: Funding dedicated local treatment facilities to directly combat fentanyl and opioid addiction.
Telehealth Expansions: Deploying remote medical and behavioral health resources to bypass geographic and transportation barriers.
Jail-Related Mental Health Care: Funding behavioral healthcare inside county correctional facilities to treat underlying conditions and stop repeat offenses.
Veterans Psychotherapy Support: Funding targeted, localized mental health outreach and psychotherapy for area veterans.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported The No Wrong Door for Veterans Act (H.R.
1969): Robb supports this bill to reauthorize and improve VA suicide-prevention grant programs, ensuring veterans can access care without getting lost in a dense bureaucracy.
Permanent Telehealth Flexibilities: Fighting to make temporary emergency telehealth flexibilities permanent under Medicare and Medicaid for rural areas.
EMS Distance-Funding Reform: Rewriting federal emergency medical funding guidelines to recognize rural distances and low call volumes, keeping local ambulance districts fully funded.
Broadband, Energy, Roads, and Emergency Infrastructure Infrastructure isn't glamorous until it fails.
A rural road washes out, a power shutoff closes a small business, a fire station loses its communication feed.
Federal broadband funding, meanwhile, keeps getting mismanaged, sending taxpayer dollars to already-overbuilt suburban areas while rural families deal with dropped connections, kids can't finish homework, and patients can't get to a telehealth appointment.
For Robb, infrastructure is about survival and function: these are the basic systems that keep a rural county running.
Energy and infrastructure policy needs to stay realistic: power that's affordable, cleaner where it makes sense, and above all reliable when families and first responders need it most.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On Robb has actively managed the local implementation of infrastructure grants on the Board of Supervisors.
His record includes votes to approve: Road Rehabilitation and Bridge Repair Contracts: Funding the continuous clearing, paving, and repair of critical transit and evacuation corridors.
Emergency Alert Systems and Backup Generators: Installing physical backup power systems and automated emergency communications at critical facilities.
Communications Facility Upgrades: Financing local tower and facility projects to expand broadband and public safety radio networks.
Federal Actions Supported Rural-First Broadband Mapping: Forcing accurate mapping and requiring federal broadband funds to be directed to unserved and underserved rural communities first, rather than overbuilt areas.
Dig-Once and Permitting Speedups: Implementing strict federal "dig-once" policies to coordinate broadband installation with road work, while slashing permitting timelines for transmission, culverts, and local generation.
Grid Hardening and Local Energy Recognition: Providing federal backing to harden energy infrastructure against wildfire ignition, while formally recognizing reliable local energy sources like biomass, small hydro, and geothermal.
Border Security, National Security, and Veterans National security isn't an abstract concept out here.
A weak, unenforced southern border means fentanyl on rural streets and cartels embedding in local communities.
A hollowed-out domestic industrial base leaves the country dependent on hostile foreign supply chains, and a bloated VA bureaucracy routinely bounces veterans around when they try to access the benefits and mental health care they earned.
Robb grew up around military service: both his grandfather and father flew for the Air Force.
He sees keeping faith with veterans as a direct local and national responsibility, and a government that can't secure its border or its strategic manufacturing supply chains is failing one of its core constitutional duties.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On As a County Supervisor, Robb has voted to ensure that local veterans never have to fight a baseline bureaucracy to get immediate support.
His votes include: Veterans Psychotherapy and Mental Health Funding: Approving dedicated county health contracts to provide direct counseling and psychiatric care to local veterans.
Veterans Service Officers (VSOs): Funding and expanding localized administrative support to help veterans navigate and secure their federal benefits directly.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported Absolute Border Enforcement and Barrier Funding: Fighting for direct funding for physical border barriers where appropriate, advanced surveillance technology, increased staffing, and an immediate end to sanctuary policies that block cooperation on serious criminal offenders.
Strategic Supply Chain Reshoring: Pushing federal mandates and purchasing power to rebuild domestic production of critical defense materials, medicines, energy equipment, and strategic minerals to end dependence on the Chinese Communist Party.
Fiscal Sanity, Ethics, and Honest Government Washington runs on a financial and ethical double standard.
The federal government borrows and spends trillions with no consequences, buries infrastructure projects in litigation for years, and lets billions go to improper payments and fraud.
Worst of all, members of Congress can still use confidential briefings and committee access to trade individual stocks and build their own portfolios while in office.
Robb ran an independent business, and he knows the numbers are real: families and employers have to live within their means, and the government should too.
Public service should be public service, not a platform for insider financial advantage.
If everyday Americans have to play by the rules and balance their books, so should the people writing those rules.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On Across his entire tenure on the Board of Supervisors, Robb’s record focuses on strict fiscal accountability and transparency, voting to ensure that: Contracts and Grants are Performance-Based: Forcing county contracts and grant allocations to be tied strictly to measurable community results rather than simply funding ongoing government activity.
Balanced County Budgets: Voting to pass strictly balanced annual county operating budgets that respect local taxpayers and avoid structural debt.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported The Stop Insider Trading Act (H.R.
7008): Robb strongly supports this measure to bar Members of Congress, their spouses, and dependent children from buying individual stocks while in office.
It's a bill that passed the House even though Nancy Pelosi and Ami Bera both voted no.
Balanced Budget Amendment: Pushing for a federal balanced budget amendment or enforceable, long-term fiscal spending rules to curb rampant inflation and national debt.
Congressional Regulation Approval: Requiring formal congressional approval for major federal regulations with large economic impacts, alongside mandatory plain-English public reporting of regulatory costs.
Constitutional Rights and Civic Trust Civic trust is eroding because people see a two-tiered system of justice, where constitutional rights get treated as optional obstacles instead of absolute boundaries.
Federal agencies keep overreaching into people's private lives, misusing personal data, threatening religious liberty, and using funding strings to pressure local institutions into cutting parents out of decisions about their own kids.
Constitutional rights are the non-negotiable boundaries that make self-government possible, and people lose trust fast when they see one rule for political insiders and another for everyone else.
A government that actually respects free speech, the Second Amendment, religious freedom, and due process is the only kind of government people will ever trust again.
The Local Record: What He Has Voted On As a County Supervisor, Robb is directly accountable across a physical dais to his neighbors and has voted to ensure local government operations strictly respect individual liberties and parental transparency.
His record includes: Protecting Private Property and Land Use: Voting against automotive or structural zoning overreaches and ensuring county compliance updates respect private property rights.
Parental and Medical Transparency: Ensuring that county-administered health and behavioral grants operating in coordination with local schools maintain absolute parental notification and consent protocols.
Federal Legislation and Actions Supported The Constitutional Concealed Carry Reciprocity Act (H.R.

The Safeguard American Voter Eligibility (SAVE) Act (H.R.

Defending Due Process and Data Privacy: Fighting for legislation to strengthen due process in administrative proceedings, curb civil asset forfeiture abuse, and completely protect Americans from warrantless surveillance and federal data misuse.
