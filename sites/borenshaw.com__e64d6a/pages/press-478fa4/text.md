July 1, 2026 Renshaw-Backed Law Cuts Healthcare Costs for Arkansas Families: Medicaid Patients Gain Access to Local Physical Therapy on July 1 NORTH LITTLE ROCK, Ark. – On July 1, a new Arkansas law takes effect that will save taxpayer money and put better healthcare within reach for thousands of Medicaid patients.
Bo Renshaw spent three years fighting to make it happen.
Under the old system, adult Medicaid patients needing physical therapy had to go to a hospital — often driving past a perfectly qualified local clinic to do it.
That meant delayed care, higher costs, and in many cases, people simply gave up and didn’t go.
Act 103 of 2025 fixes that by requiring Medicaid to reimburse physical therapy at community clinics starting July 1.
Getting physical therapy early saves money — for patients and for taxpayers.
Studies show early PT reduces the need for surgery by up to 47%, cuts long-term opioid use, and lowers overall healthcare costs by an average of $2,700 per patient.
Arkansas’ own Department of Human Services reviewed the data and confirmed it expects the change to save money.
Renshaw, a practicing physical therapist and small business owner, pushed for this change because he sees it every day in his clinic.
When patients can’t afford to get there, they skip care — and end up needing surgery or medication that costs far more.
This law puts the emphasis back on the patient where it should be.

May 7, 2026 Arkansas Legislature Puts More Money Back in Your Pocket NORTH LITTLE ROCK, Ark. — The 2026 Arkansas fiscal session wrapped up this week, and the headline is simple: Arkansas is giving more money back to the people who earned it.
The legislature passed a budget that increases the homestead property tax credit from $600 to $675 — immediate relief for every homeowner in North Little Rock and Sherwood starting this year.
They also structured the Revenue Stabilization Act to make room for the income tax rate cut that Arkansas families have been promised and deserve.
That budget framework puts Arkansas on a path to keep more money in your wallet — not in Little Rock.
In a special session this week, the legislature also passed a cut to both the top personal income tax rate and the corporate tax rate — putting an additional $180 million back into the hands of Arkansas taxpayers.
Bo supported the cut.
