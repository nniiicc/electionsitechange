Charlotte, NC — Early Saturday morning, on their way to greet voters at the polls, volunteers with Rev.
Rodney Sadler’s campaign witnessed masked agents detaining two neighbors.
“We were just driving in for the last day of early voting when we saw masked agents in the middle of the road pushing two men against their car in handcuffs,” said Rae LeGrone, a high school art teacher and volunteer with Rev.
Rodney Sadler’s campaign.
“We remembered our training, started filming right away, asked for their names, called the ICE watch hotline, and notified neighbors.”
“Trump’s ICE and CBP are trying to instill fear in our communities, and we will not let them,” said Rev.
Rodney Sadler.
“The movements that came before us faced down intimidation and violence to win the right to vote, and now our Charlotte community will continue to stand up for the safety and dignity of all our neighbors.
My faith is clear: Love God, Love Your Neighbor, Love The Stranger.
This Tuesday, I have deep faith that the voters of House District 106 will choose love over fear.”
The past week – the final week before Election Day – has seen significantly increased ICE activity in Charlotte.
Siembra NC has confirmed at least three separate ICE operations in the last three days, and on Tuesday, ICE detained four people at the Mecklenburg County Courthouse.
Saturday is the last day of Early Voting in the highly contested primary between fourteen-year incumbent Rep.
Carla Cunningham and Baptist minister Rev.
Rodney Sadler.
Last year, Rep.
Cunningham cast the deciding vote for HB318, forcing local law enforcement to collaborate with ICE, and declared “All cultures are not equal.”
Three months later, Operation Charlotte’s Web shut down small businesses across Charlotte and created such fear that more than 30,000 students stayed home from school.
Former Trump advisor Steve Bannon claimed earlier this month, “We’re going to have ICE surround the polls come November,” and Donald Trump has repeatedly threatened to “nationalize” elections.
The U.S.
Constitution explicitly gives states jurisdiction over election administration.
Rev.
Dr.
Sadler is running for office on a platform of strong public schools, homes we can afford, wages we can live on, community safety, accessible healthcare, voting rights, and a state government that fights for working families instead of pointing fingers and blaming scapegoats.
