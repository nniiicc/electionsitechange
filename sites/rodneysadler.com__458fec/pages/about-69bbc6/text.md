It was 2013, and Republicans in the State Legislature were trying to deprive working families of desperately needed healthcare; to starve our public schools of necessary and equitable funding; and to strip away the voting rights that previous generations bled and died for.
I remember hearing our footsteps on the hard floor as we crossed the threshold, arm in arm.
As we sang and prayed together, kneeling on the cold floor, an officer stepped forward.
At 45 years old, I had never before been arrested, and I must admit that I felt afraid.
I came home to Charlotte and realized that something had changed in my life.
I knew that we needed a moral movement so that we could say: we should never have a government that lifts up the wealthy on the backs of the poorest among us.
My Family Love God, Love Your Neighbor, Love the Stranger.
These were lessons that I learned early, in church and in my own family.
My father, Rodney Sadler Sr., and my mother, Audrey Powell, both came from families who fled the South at the height of racist terror in the 1920’s and 30’s.
My mother’s family were from Virginia, my father’s from Edenton, North Carolina.
They were high school sweethearts, they married young, and had me when they were just 18 and 19.
They separated a few years later – but they stayed close: literally, within two doors.
My parents both remarried – my mother to Stuart Hollis and my father to Annie Ryan.
I soon had family – parents and siblings – from both the United States and Bermuda; a family that was Black and white, immigrant and native born.
Our families remained close throughout the years, often visiting each other in our homes, and taught me that love can extend beyond all boundaries.
You Have to Do Something About It I still remember the words of my 10th grade teacher, Tom Smith: “If you believe in something, you have to do something about it.” He wouldn’t let us get away with passive belief.
He insisted that, if we thought something was wrong, we had to act on it.
I believe that every child deserves a teacher like that.
One who challenges us, teaches us to think and to love learning.
That will only be possible when we fully fund and support strong public schools where all of our children can thrive.
Believing in strong public schools isn’t enough.
It is that commitment, among others, that brought me to the steps of the NC General Assembly in May 2013 – and it is that commitment that calls me to run for NC House District 106 today.
My daughter constantly reminds me that the work I do today has meaning.
That it shapes the world for tomorrow.
Because that’s her world.
That’s where she’ll live.
She deserves a just and equitable world, where opportunities abound for all of God’s children.
