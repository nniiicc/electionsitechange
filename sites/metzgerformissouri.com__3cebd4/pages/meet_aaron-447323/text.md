Meet Aaron
A Joplin neighbor, a healthcare worker, a husband and dad — running for the people he lives next to.
I’m Aaron Joseph Metzger.
I live in Joplin with my wife and our daughter, and I’m running for Missouri State Representative in District 161 because the people I love — my family, my coworkers, my neighbors — are working harder than ever and getting less for it.
They deserve a state government that works for them.
Right now, they don’t have one.
I’m not a career politician.
I’m a CT technologist.
For the last 13 years, since May 2013, I’ve worked the night shift at a hospital right here in Joplin — scanning patients in the middle of car wrecks, heart attacks, strokes, and every other emergency that doesn’t wait for daylight.
You learn a lot about a community on the night shift.
You learn who has insurance and who doesn’t.
You learn which families drove an hour because their local hospital cut services.
You learn which neighbors put off care for so long that by the time we saw them, there wasn’t much we could do.
I’m running because I’m tired of watching it happen — and because Jefferson City keeps making it worse.
Healthcare you can actually use.
Whether you’re a farmer, a teacher, a small-business owner, or a single parent working two jobs, you should be able to take your kid to the doctor without it wrecking the family budget.
I’ll fight to protect Medicaid, push back on policies that drive up costs, and do everything I can to keep our regional hospitals open and staffed.
A heart attack in Jasper or Newton County shouldn’t be a death sentence because the closest open ER is too far away.
A paycheck that goes far enough.
Working families in District 161 are squeezed from every direction — rent, groceries, childcare, utilities, gas.
The basics.
People are doing everything right and still falling behind.
We need an economy where a hard week of work pays the bills and leaves a little something at the end of the month.
That means real action on cost of living — childcare, housing, wages — not slogans.
Serve all Missourians.
Respect the voters.
I’m tired of politicians who go to Jefferson City to fight on TV instead of solve problems — and who think their job is to argue with the voters who put them there.
The people I scan at 3 a.m. don’t care which party gets credit.
They care whether their kid’s school is good, whether their grandmother can afford her medication, whether the road home is safe.
And when Missourians make a decision at the ballot box, my job is to respect it — not find ways around it.
That’s the deal.
I’ve spent more than a decade taking care of my neighbors one patient at a time.
Now I want to take care of them in Jefferson City — by showing up, answering the phone, and voting like the people back home are watching.
Because they are.
I’d be honored to earn your vote.
— Aaron
