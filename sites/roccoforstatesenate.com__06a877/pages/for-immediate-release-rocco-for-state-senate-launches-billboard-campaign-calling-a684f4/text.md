The billboards carry a clear message: “The moment you are silent on injustice is the moment you are complicit in injustice.” “I believe Derek Chauvin did not receive a fair trial,” Rocco said,” “and neither did officers J.
Alexander Kueng, Thomas Lane, and Tou Thao.
The proceedings took place under intense public pressure, with national Democratic leaders and activists openly warning of violence if the verdict did not match their demands.
That atmosphere tainted the neutrality and fairness essential to any legitimate judicial process.” Rocco noted that George Floyd’s extensive criminal history was a relevant factor, while Chauvin was acting in accordance with his training as a law enforcement officer.
“These fundamental facts were overshadowed by the socio-political climate.
A fair trial requires an impartial jury free from external threats and political intimidation.
That did not happen.” Rocco agrees with Chauvin’s attorney Gregory Joseph that the case should be dismissed on technical grounds.
Barring that, he calls for a federal pardon and a state retrial in a neutral venue — proposing Warroad in Roseau County, Minnesota.
“If Tim Walz can pardon a pedophile, he can push for a dismissal, fair retrial, or even just pardon Chauvin.
