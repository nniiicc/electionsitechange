Criminal Justice Reform
Our criminal justice system should protect everyone equally, but unfortunately, it never truly has.
That is why our system needs reform to right the wrongs of the past, foster public safety, and help put people on the path toward a brighter future.
In the State Senate, I supported meaningful criminal justice reforms aimed at reducing recidivism, expanding expungements, and ensuring reintegration after incarceration.
I also supported expanding the use of police body cameras, pressed for reform in police training, and advocated for increased transparency in policing.
I brought this experience to Congress and am helping make our criminal justice system work for everyone.
I’ll continue to push for an end to mass incarceration, legalize marijuana and expunge convictions, and help to reimagine a system that prioritizes reentry and prevents recidivism.
In Congress, I’ve:
- Secured Community Project Funding for a project to provide healthcare to individuals reentering the community from incarceration and for a project to improve police training.
- Cosponsored the George Floyd Justice in Policing Act, comprehensive legislation to hold police accountable and build trust between law enforcement and our communities.
The bill makes it easier for the federal government to successfully prosecute police misconduct cases, ends racial and religious profiling, eliminates qualified immunity for law enforcement, and bans the use of chokeholds and no-knock warrants.
- Cosponsored the Clean Slate Act, to help people who have paid their debt to society get a true second chance by automatically sealing eligible federal records for nonviolent offenses and creating a pathway for others to petition the courts to have their records sealed.
