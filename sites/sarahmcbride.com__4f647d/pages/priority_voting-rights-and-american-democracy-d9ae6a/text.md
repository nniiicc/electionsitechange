Voting Rights and American Democracy
Democracy is strongest when every citizen can make their voice heard.
We can make our democracy healthier and more inclusive by removing barriers to voting, enacting strong protections against voter disenfranchisement in all its forms, and by removing big money from politics.
In the State Senate, I joined many of my colleagues in passing vote-by-mail, early voting, and same-day voter registration protections.
In Congress, when congressional Republicans brought the largest voter suppression bill to the House floor, I voted against it.
I strongly oppose the so-called SAVE America Act, which would create unnecessary barriers to voter registration and make it harder for millions of eligible Americans—including married women, military families, and naturalized citizens—to exercise their fundamental right to vote.
This bill would amount to a poll tax on American citizens.
I also reject election denialism in all its forms.
Free and fair elections are the foundation of our democracy, and we must protect the integrity of our elections by defending both the right to vote and the peaceful transfer of power.
In Congress, I’ve:
- I cosponsored the John R.
Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act to restore and strengthen the protections of the Voting Rights Act, safeguard every American’s right to vote, and ensure voters—not politicians—decide our elections.
- I cosponsored the DISCLOSE Act to shine a light on dark money in our elections by requiring organizations spending money to influence campaigns to disclose their major donors, and I support a constitutional amendment to overturn Citizens United, so our democracy is accountable to voters—not billionaires and special interests.
