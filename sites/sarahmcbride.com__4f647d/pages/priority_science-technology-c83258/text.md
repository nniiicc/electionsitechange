The administration’s attacks on basic science and its firing of career scientists threaten our ability to compete and undermine the breakthroughs that drive economic growth and improve lives.
I support strengthening research at our public universities and national laboratories, protecting the independence of federal scientists, and investing in Delaware’s growing biotechnology and life sciences sector, alongside emerging industries like artificial intelligence and advanced manufacturing.
By strengthening partnerships among universities, researchers, entrepreneurs, and businesses, we can create good-paying jobs, grow Delaware’s innovation economy, and ensure America remains the global leader in science and technology.
Artificial intelligence presents potentially one of the biggest changes for humanity in our history.
No concentration of such power—whether in an individual, a company, a nation, or a technology—can be allowed to go unchecked, unrestrained, or unregulated.
We need human-centric laws that protect our economy, our finances, our privacy, our health, our democracy, and our national security.
For AI to bring good, we must protect against its harm, and Congress must act with the urgency this moment demands.
In Congress, I’ve: Introduced the bipartisan READ AI Models Act, to create a standardized “nutrition label” for artificial intelligence models that gives people clear, easy-to-understand information about an AI model.
The bill passed the House Science, Space, and Technology Committee with unanimous bipartisan support.
Successfully led an effort to force the Trump Administration to reverse harmful cuts to the Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) here in Delaware and other MEP programs across the country, preserving federal funding for a program that helps small and mid-sized manufacturers grow, innovate, and create jobs.
Secured House passage of my amendment providing $5 million to advance cutting-edge liquid cooling technology developed in Delaware , supporting next-generation systems that can make data centers more energy efficient, reduce strain on the electric grid, and strengthen military technology.
The investment would support Delaware innovation and good-paying jobs while advancing American competitiveness and national security.
