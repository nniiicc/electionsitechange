Congresswoman Sarah McBride, a Wilmington native and decades-long public servant, proudly represents Delaware as its sole member of Congress.
Before being elected to Congress, Sarah represented Delaware’s First Senate District from 2020 to 2025.
As a state senator, Sarah passed legislation expanding access to health care, strengthening public education, promoting green technologies, preventing childhood lead poisoning, and protecting workers and families.
During her time in the State Senate, Sarah passed the landmark Healthy Delaware Families Act, creating Delaware’s statewide paid family and medical leave program and marking the largest expansion of the state’s social safety net in decades.
She also advanced legislation expanding health care access, strengthening mental health and media literacy education in public schools, and protecting workers and families.
In her final term, she passed the bipartisan Protect Medicaid Act of 2024, securing the largest ongoing investment in Delaware’s Medicaid program since the Affordable Care Act and unlocking more than $175 million annually in new funding.
Sarah has brought that same focus on delivering results to Congress.
She serves on the House Committees on Foreign Affairs and Science, Space, and Technology and as a House Democratic Deputy Whip for Policy.
She also co-leads the House Democratic Caucus Working Group on Caregiving, helping develop policies to support caregivers and working families.
In her first term, Sarah has introduced bills supporting Delaware farmers, protecting consumers, and expanding family leave protections for working parents – with more of her bills being bipartisan than any first term Representative in this Congress.
Her office has returned more than $8 million in backlogged federal benefits to Delawareans and secured more than $164 million in Congressionally directed spending for projects across the First State.
In 2026, her office received the Democracy Award for Best Constituent Service in Congress from the nonpartisan Congressional Management Foundation.
Sarah’s commitment to public service began long before she held elected office.
She worked for former Governor Jack Markell, the late Attorney General Beau Biden and served in the Obama-Biden White House.
She later served as the national spokesperson for the Human Rights Campaign, the nation’s largest LGBTQ+ civil rights organization.
Prior to serving in the State Senate, Sarah also led the successful effort to pass Delaware’s landmark statewide nondiscrimination law.
In recognition of her advocacy, Governor Markell awarded her the Order of the First State, making her one of the youngest recipients of Delaware’s highest civilian honor.
Sarah married her late husband, Andrew Cray, in 2014 and is the proud aunt of seven.
She is a graduate of Cab Calloway School of the Arts and American University, has taught public policy at the University of Delaware, and is the author of the 2018 memoir Tomorrow Will Be Different , which includes a foreword by President Joe Biden.
