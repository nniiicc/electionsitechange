Sarah McBride’s record of delivering for and defending Delaware during her first term is on full display WILMINGTON, Del. – In case you missed it , with 20 days until election day, the Delaware News Journal released its 2026 voter guide for the race for Delaware’s sole seat in the U.S.
House of Representatives.
Rep.
Sarah McBride (D-Del.), who made history when elected in 2024, has represented Delaware in the House since 2025.
Rep.
McBride’s first term in Congress has been focused on lowering costs for Delawareans, showing up every day to defend Delaware, cutting through partisan gridlock and government red tape, and ensuring government treats her neighbors with respect.
Key excerpts from the article, including her priorities on caregiving, affordability, foreign affairs, and AI, are below.
On Affordability: “McBride has highlighted issues including the cost of living, the nation’s care infrastructure, foreign policy and regulation of artificial intelligence , among other priorities, as she seeks a second term in Congress.
“On affordability, a major issue throughout the campaign, McBride has argued that foreign policy and trade decisions can affect consumer costs.
She pointed to ending the war in Iran, while also criticizing the Trump administration’s approach to trade policy.” On Rep.
McBride’s Priorities: “‘But the reality is, we can’t just go back to how things were two, three, four years ago,’ she said in an interview with Delaware Online/The News Journal. ‘While we have to undo the harm of this administration when it comes to tariffs and gas prices and health care cuts – we’ve also got to lay the foundation for the kind of change that the public is demanding .’ “For McBride, that largely comes in care infrastructure.
The co-lead of the House Democratic Caregiving Working Group in Washington said she hopes to push for nationwide paid family and medical leave, universal child care, as well as greater investment in home care.
Such core issues may sound familiar to her Delaware Senate days.
“Hopeful to return to a Democratic majority, McBride described a ‘day one’ aim in restoring the enhanced premium child tax credit established during the pandemic.
She also described plans for a housing bill to help more states encourage building both new homes and a trade workforce.” On Rep.
McBride’s First Term: “As she campaigns for reelection, McBride has highlighted her first-year record in Congress , including introducing five bipartisan bills and co-sponsoring 439 pieces of legislation, as well as helping secure more than $164 million in federal funding for Delaware since 2025, according to her office.
“Since taking the reins in 2025, McBride’s team said her office has returned more than $8 million to Delawareans in tax refunds, Social Security, veterans benefits and more …” On Foreign Policy: “ A standout moment on that chamber floor came over the summer, when McBride fell among more than 100 House Democrats breaking ranks in a vote to eliminate aid to Israel.
That amendment failed, but it still marked an evolution for the Delawarean. “‘I think many people in this country have gone on a journey on this topic, specifically as it relates to the actions of the Israeli government,’ McBride said Sept.
30. ‘People were right to denounce the terrorist attack by Hamas on Oct.
7 [2023].
In the aftermath … they were right and are right that Israel has a right to exist and defend itself…And, as the actions of the Israeli government have unfolded since Oct.
7,’ she continued, ‘I and many Americans have come to the conclusion that those actions go far beyond self-defense.’ “Broadly on foreign policy, McBride said the U.S. should be leading a ‘commitment to international law’ and human rights that ‘holds ourselves and our allies to that standard.’ Over the last several decades, she argued, ‘too often’ that has fallen short.” On Addressing AI: “ McBride described AI as one of the ‘most significant transformations of society’ and has been discussing the technology since her first run.
The 36-year-old didn’t shy away from a call for an ‘arms control approach’ to AI globally, while pursuing domestic policy that could reflect the U.S.
Food and Drug Administration. “‘It is my hope that a Democratic majority takes this moment on with the seriousness of purpose and the urgency that it requires to establish, essentially, the equivalent of an FDA for frontier AI models to ensure true and clear liability.’” Rep.
McBride’s full list of priorities can be found on her campaign website: sarahmcbride.com .
