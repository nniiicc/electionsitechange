Democratic state Senate candidate David Sanders said Indiana’s higher education policies are “placing a muzzle on academic free speech,” making protections for Purdue faculty and local decision-making central to his campaign for District 23.
His sharpest criticism centered on Senate Bill 202, authored by Republican state Sen.
Spencer Deery.
The law requires Indiana’s public universities to adopt policies promoting free inquiry, free expression and intellectual diversity while subjecting tenured faculty to periodic reviews.
“It’s eliminated, in essence, tenure protections,” Sanders said.
“And it is placing a muzzle on academic free speech.”
Sanders also connected Purdue’s academic reputation to its international faculty and students.
He said reducing students’ exposure to people from different backgrounds would “impoverish the educational experience.”
“We have students and faculty from around the world who look toward Purdue as a superior place to get higher education,” Sanders said.
He said he founded Stop the Water Steal to oppose a proposed transfer of as much as 100 million gallons of water per day from an aquifer connected to the Wabash River to the LEAP Lebanon project.
This project aimed to use water from the Wabash watershed to manufacturing facilities for corporations like Eli Lilly.
Sanders credited the organization and its allies across the political spectrum with helping halt the proposal.
