Alina's Law Revised legislative proposal A BILL Amending Title 23 (Domestic Relations) of the Pennsylvania Consolidated Statutes, adding provisions to permit tiered electronic monitoring of respondents in Protection From Abuse cases under judicial order with due process protections, mandatory risk assessments, and victim-controlled alert systems.
The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania hereby enacts as follows: SECTION 1.
Title 23 of the Pennsylvania Consolidated Statutes is amended by adding a section to read: Section 6108.4.
Electronic Monitoring and Proximity Alert Orders. (a) Authorization; Request; Notice; Hearing.
(1) Request and Timing.
A plaintiff may request electronic monitoring by: (i) indicating the request in the initial petition for a temporary order under this chapter, which shall provide the defendant with general notice that electronic monitoring may be at issue at the final hearing; or (ii) motion at or after the final protection from abuse hearing.
If the plaintiff indicates the request in the initial petition and it is not adjudicated at the final hearing, the final order shall include a notation that electronic monitoring has been requested and shall set a prompt monitoring hearing date.
(2) Notice; Hearing.
Electronic monitoring may be ordered only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, and the defendant shall be advised that electronic monitoring may be imposed.
(3) Consent.
The defendant may consent to electronic monitoring at any time, including by stipulation without a contested hearing, provided the court conducts a brief colloquy to confirm knowing and voluntary consent and makes the findings required by this section.
The Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts shall develop a standardized mitigation and consent checklist to memorialize consent, technology capabilities and limitations, and the specific terms ordered.
(4) Failure to appear.
If a separate monitoring hearing is scheduled and the defendant, after proper notice, fails to appear, the court may proceed in the defendant's absence and issue appropriate orders.

Prior to entering any electronic monitoring order, the court shall receive and consider the results of a validated lethality or intimate partner violence risk assessment, as well as relevant criminal justice information reasonably available to the court.
The court shall consider the following factors: - whether the defendant has previously violated a Protection From Abuse order involving the same plaintiff; - whether the defendant has a history of domestic violence or stalking; - whether there is a demonstrated, continuing risk to the plaintiff's physical safety; - whether the defendant has access to firearms; - whether the defendant has violated bail or court supervision in the past; - defendant is found to present a substantial risk of violating the final protection from abuse order or committing a crime against the victim punishable by imprisonment; - any other relevant factors suggesting elevated risk.
The court must consider all factors listed above and make findings of fact on the record.
If the court finds by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant poses a continued and substantial risk to the plaintiff's safety, and that less restrictive conditions are inadequate, the court may order electronic proximity monitoring.
In determining whether to order monitoring, the court shall consider whether monitoring will deter the defendant from seeking to harm, stalk, threaten or otherwise endanger the plaintiff.
(6) Notwithstanding the discretionary language above, if the defendant has been charged with or is found to have committed aggravated assault against the plaintiff or a domestic partner, the court shall order electronic monitoring unless it enters written findings stating the reasons why such monitoring is not necessary to protect the plaintiff.
In the absence of a charge or conviction, credible testimony or other reliable evidence of aggravated assault may support electronic monitoring under paragraph (5).
(7) The plaintiff must provide informed consent to participate in the alert system and may withdraw consent at any time.
The court shall not sanction a plaintiff for declining, or later withdrawing from, participation in the alert system.
If the plaintiff withdraws consent, the court shall promptly review whether any form of monitoring without plaintiff alerts remains necessary to protect the plaintiff and whether less restrictive conditions suffice.
(8) The defendant shall be given notice, and a hearing before the imposition of electronic monitoring.
(9) Petition contents for electronic monitoring.
When electronic monitoring is requested, the petition or motion shall include, to the extent known and available, a description of conduct that would lead a reasonable person to believe the plaintiff's health, welfare or safety is at risk and an allegation that the defendant presents a continuing danger.
The absence of criminal charges shall not bar issuance of an order upon the necessary evidentiary showing.

(1) Exclusion zones.
The plaintiff may submit a list of specific locations from which the plaintiff requests the defendant be excluded, hereinafter the "exclusion zones." The court shall determine the exclusion zones and distances based on victim safety, reasonableness, and technological capability.
The order shall specify distances and parameters for each exclusion zone.
The electronic monitoring shall enforce geographic exclusion zones designated by the court, including but not limited to the plaintiff's residence, workplace, school, or other locations the plaintiff regularly visits.
(2) Moving proximity protections; neutral exchanges.
The court may specify moving exclusion or proximity protection for the plaintiff through a mobile application or comparable technology that securely maps a protective radius around the plaintiff while maintaining plaintiff privacy consistent with subsection (e).
The order may also include neutral exchange locations and any necessary, narrow exceptions for custody exchanges or court-ordered appointments, with advance coordination through supervising authorities.
(3) The monitoring device and system shall generate an automated, contemporaneous alert to the plaintiff and to the supervising authority designated by the court, which may include probation, pretrial services, sheriff, or other agency, and to local law enforcement if the defendant enters within a specified radius of any exclusion zone.
Alerts shall be staffed by trained personnel twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, who will attempt to contact the plaintiff and defendant as appropriate and coordinate with law enforcement for immediate safety.
Supervising authorities shall pre-notify local law enforcement in the jurisdictions where the parties reside or work, providing a basic information sheet to support timely response.
(4) Upon ordering monitoring, the court shall instruct supervising authorities to enter order terms into applicable statewide protection order or law enforcement systems for enforcement, and to maintain a 24/7 escalation protocol for imminent threats and verified breaches.
(5) Supervising authorities shall maintain graduated response protocols for low-battery and charging violations, starting with automated notices and escalating to live outreach and field checks where necessary, and shall immediately escalate tampering or removal alerts to law enforcement and the plaintiff.
(6) If electronic monitoring is ordered, the court shall also impose a condition that the defendant shall not purchase or possess firearms or ammunition for the duration of the monitoring and shall advise the defendant on the record of the consequences of violating this condition.
(7) Device standards.
Devices shall be non-invasive wearables (e.g., ankle or wrist) that cannot be removed except with specialized tools, use the least intrusive means necessary to enforce exclusion zones, and support encrypted, secure transmission and storage of data, with auditable logs of alerts and responses.

(1) Unless otherwise specified by the court, electronic monitoring may be ordered for any period up to the duration of the final protection from abuse order.
The court may set earlier review dates or time-limited terms as needed.
Either party may petition for modification or termination upon a material change of circumstances.
(2) Monitoring is paused while the defendant is incarcerated and shall be reinstated upon release if still in effect under the order, subject to any court modification.
(3) If the court removes monitoring before the final order expires, the court shall notify the plaintiff contemporaneously. (d) Financial Conditions; Funding.
(1) No person shall be denied the opportunity for due process or subjected to a monitoring order solely due to financial resources.
(2) The court shall assess the defendant's financial means and may: (i) waive monitoring costs; (ii) assign costs to state or county resources; or (iii) impose partial cost-sharing based on ability to pay.
The Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts, in coordination with the Pennsylvania Commission on Crime and Delinquency and counties, shall establish a funding framework for installation and monitoring, including state allocations to support indigent respondents and 24/7 coverage.
Counties may supplement with local funds.
The court's order shall specify the responsible supervising authority and cost allocation consistent with this framework.
The court shall appoint counsel for indigent defendants for electronic monitoring hearings; such appointment does not extend to the underlying civil PFA proceedings unless otherwise required by law. (e) Technology Standards and Privacy Protections; Data Privacy; Access Retention.
(1) The monitoring equipment shall use the least intrusive means necessary to provide victim alerts and location data.
(2) Data collected shall be: (i) limited to location information relevant to exclusion zone enforcement; (ii) encrypted in transit and at rest and maintained under strict confidentiality; (iii) inaccessible to third parties except via valid subpoena or court order or as otherwise authorized by court order for enforcement and investigative purposes.
Access shall be limited to supervising authorities, law enforcement, and the court.
Upon motion and court order, data necessary to prosecute or defend alleged violations may be disclosed to the plaintiff's or defendant's counsel subject to protective orders.

Data shall be retained only for the duration of the monitoring order and any ensuing investigation, prosecution, or contempt proceeding, and then securely deleted consistent with statewide standards.
The Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts shall promulgate retention schedules and minimum cybersecurity controls.
(4) Vendor requirements.
The Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts shall establish minimum certification standards for vendors providing monitoring hardware, software, call center, and data services, including 24/7 staffed alert handling and auditable incident logs. (f) Enforcement, Documentation and Contempt.
(1) Supervising authorities shall maintain contemporaneous logs of alerts, contacts, directives, and responses.
Verified breaches and tampering events shall be promptly documented and transmitted to law enforcement and the court as appropriate.
(2) A breach of an exclusion zone or device tampering may constitute indirect criminal contempt of the protection order when the conduct violates no-contact or stay-away terms; the court shall specify in the order that verified zone breaches and tampering are enforceable as contempt and may also constitute separate criminal offenses under applicable law.
The order shall advise the defendant that violations may result in arrest without a warrant. (g) Reporting and Review.
(1) The Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts shall collect and publish anonymized annual statistics on the issuance, duration, effectiveness, and outcomes of electronic monitoring orders, including alerts, violations, enforcement outcomes, and victim safety indicators.
(2) The Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts shall evaluate program efficacy every three years with recommendations to the General Assembly. (h) Judicial Education.
The Pennsylvania Supreme Court shall ensure that judges and court personnel receive training on the standards, risks, and procedures associated with electronic monitoring orders, including trauma-informed approaches and civil rights protections.
Training shall include risk assessment, due process standards, technology capabilities and limitations, and data privacy.
The Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts shall coordinate with counties to ensure 24/7 live alert handling, designate supervising authorities, and promulgate model orders, mitigation and consent checklists, and standard operating procedures.

Section 6108(a) of Title 23 is amended by adding a paragraph to read: § 6108.
Relief. (a) General rule.
Subject to subsection (a.1), the court may grant any protection order or approve any consent agreement to bring about a cessation of abuse of the plaintiff or minor children.
The order or agreement may include: (6.1) Electronic monitoring and proximity alert orders.
If the court finds by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant presents a continued and substantial risk of violating the final order or committing a crime against the plaintiff, and that less restrictive measures are inadequate, the court may order that the defendant wear an electronic monitoring device and comply with geographic exclusion zones and proximity alert conditions as set forth in section 6108.4.
If the court orders electronic monitoring, the court shall also order the defendant not to purchase or possess firearms or ammunition for the duration of monitoring.
The following shall apply: (i) The court shall determine the distance at which the defendant is to remain from the protected persons and specific locations, including exclusion zones and any moving proximity protections implemented through approved technologies. (ii) The court may order monitoring for any period up to the expiration of the final protection from abuse order and may review, modify, or terminate monitoring upon motion or on its own motion based on changed circumstances. (iii) If the device is removed or monitoring is terminated before the expiration of the final protection from abuse order, the court shall notify the plaintiff contemporaneously. (iv) The order shall advise the defendant on the record that violations may result in arrest without a warrant, contempt sanctions, bail consequences in any criminal matter, and additional criminal charges where applicable.
SECTION 3.
This act shall take effect in 60 days.
