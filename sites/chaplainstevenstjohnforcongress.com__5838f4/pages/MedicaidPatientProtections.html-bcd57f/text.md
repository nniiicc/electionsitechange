Steven St John for Congress Home Solutions Healthcare Contact Proposed Medicaid Patient Protections A practical federal framework to prevent eligible patients from losing healthcare because a medical or personal crisis made it impossible to meet an administrative deadline.
Proposed legislative framework The problem A person can remain legally eligible for Medicaid yet lose coverage because a notice went to an outdated address, paperwork was misunderstood, work hours were not documented, or a patient was hospitalized, intubated, incapacitated, homeless or too seriously ill to respond.
When coverage is interrupted for an administrative reason, the consequences may include delayed treatment, denied prescriptions, medical debt and uncompensated hospital care.
Patients and families should not face those consequences solely because a crisis prevented them from completing paperwork on time.
An eligible person should not lose healthcare solely because hospitalization, serious illness, incapacity, homelessness, a failed notice or an administrative error made timely compliance impossible.
Protections I intend to propose Automatic protection during hospitalization or incapacity Pause termination and reporting deadlines when reliable health-system or government information shows that a person is hospitalized, seriously ill or incapacitated.
A meaningful grace and correction period Provide additional time to submit missing information and correct an error before coverage is terminated.
Coverage while eligibility is being resolved Continue coverage during a timely review or appeal when available records indicate that the person may remain eligible.
Rapid and retroactive reinstatement Restore coverage promptly—and without a harmful gap—when a person proves continued eligibility or shows good cause for missing a requirement.
Reliable notice before termination Require more than one reasonable contact attempt, using available mail, telephone, text or electronic channels, before ending coverage for failure to respond.
Protection for homelessness and unstable housing Do not terminate an otherwise eligible person merely because that person lacks a permanent mailing address or never received a notice.
Help for families and authorized representatives Give legally authorized family members, caregivers and representatives a workable way to protect coverage when a patient cannot act personally.
Use existing information before demanding more paperwork Require agencies to check reliable government and healthcare data already available to them before asking a patient to prove the same facts again.
Clear explanations and accessible assistance Notices must explain, in plain language, what is required, the deadline, the consequences, available exemptions and how to obtain immediate help.
Accountability and public reporting Require reporting on administrative terminations, reversals, processing delays and coverage restored after appeal so Congress and the public can see where eligible people are falling through the cracks.
Why I am proposing these protections For nearly ten years as a Las Vegas hospital chaplain, I have stood with seriously ill patients and overwhelmed families at the bedside.
I have seen how difficult it can be for a family to understand insurance, locate documents and make urgent decisions while someone they love is intubated, incapacitated or near the end of life.
Public policy must account for what actually happens inside a hospital room.
Administrative safeguards are not loopholes.
They are protections against eligible people losing healthcare at the moment they are least able to defend themselves.
Status: This page describes a proposed legislative framework and is not the text of an introduced bill.
Specific statutory language would be developed through consultation with patients, families, healthcare professionals, disability and homelessness advocates, state Medicaid agencies and legislative counsel.
