Who faces new Nevada Medicaid requirements?
Nevada says some members will need to work, volunteer, or attend school beginning January 1, 2027.
Exemptions include certain parents, pregnant people, people with disabilities or serious health conditions, and people age 65 or older.
Update your contact information and check your renewal date.
Ask the state whether an exemption applies; do not assume every member faces these rules.
Source: Nevada Division of Social Services Available now · enrollment ahead 2.
What can families do now about health insurance for next year?
Nevada Health Link says next-year plans can be previewed starting October 1; enrollment begins November 1.
Compare premiums, deductibles, prescriptions and provider networks.
Update income and household information.
Free enrollment assistance is available at 1-800-547-2927.
Confirm the closing date directly: retrieved official pages contain differing deadlines for the upcoming enrollment period.
Source: Nevada Health Link Announced · effective October 16, 2026 3.
What immigration change takes effect this week?
USCIS announced inflation increases for certain immigration-related fees, effective October 16.
For example, the specified initial asylum, parole and TPS employment authorization fee rises from $560 to $570; the TPS application fee rises from $510 to $520.
These are specified fees, not necessarily the full cost of a filing.
Check the current fee schedule and instructions for your exact application before submitting it.
Source: USCIS bulletin · September 30, 2026 Upcoming · 2027 drug costs 4.
What Medicare drug costs are changing?
The Part D out-of-pocket limit for covered drugs increases from $2,100 in 2026 to $2,400 in 2027.
Premiums and noncovered drugs are outside this limit.
The maximum deductible rises from $615 to $700; some plans charge less or no deductible.
Compare your own plan.
The prescription payment option spreads costs across the year but does not reduce them.
Source: Medicare.gov Current rules · wages 5.
What is Nevada’s minimum wage, and where can workers ask for help?
Nevada’s minimum wage is $12 per hour, effective since July 1, 2024.
The state Labor Commissioner accepts wage complaints and provides information about overtime and other workplace rules.
Keep pay statements and records of hours worked.
Rules and exemptions vary; contact the Las Vegas office at 702-486-2650 about your situation.
Source: Nevada Office of the Labor Commissioner Program operating in 2026 6.
Is help available for a renter facing eviction?
Legal Aid describes an eviction diversion program beginning February 2, 2026, for eligible renters age 62 or older and people receiving SSI because of disability.
It covers specified nonpayment cases in Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas.
A timely tenant answer is required.
Rental assistance depends on eligibility and availability; it is not guaranteed.
Other tenants can ask Legal Aid about available help.
Source: Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada Existing resource · small businesses 7.
Where can small business owners get practical help?
The Small Business Administration and its resource partners offer free or low-cost counseling and training.
Use the local assistance directory to find help with business planning and financing questions.
Counseling does not guarantee approval for a loan or grant.
Source: U.S.
Small Business Administration What could lawmakers consider?
Possible legislative approaches include simpler benefit notices and appeals, funding for affordable housing, enforcement of wage laws, and changes to immigration processing capacity or fees.
These are policy options for public discussion, not claims that a bill has been introduced or enacted.
Each would need a defined scope, funding, and evaluation of costs and benefits.
Federal lawmakers, state lawmakers and local governments have different responsibilities.

El límite de gastos de bolsillo de la Parte D para medicamentos cubiertos sube de $2,100 en 2026 a $2,400 en 2027.
Las primas y medicamentos no cubiertos quedan fuera del límite.
El deducible máximo sube de $615 a $700; algunos planes cobran menos o ninguno.
Compare su propio plan.
La opción de pagos de medicamentos distribuye los gastos durante el año, pero no los reduce.
