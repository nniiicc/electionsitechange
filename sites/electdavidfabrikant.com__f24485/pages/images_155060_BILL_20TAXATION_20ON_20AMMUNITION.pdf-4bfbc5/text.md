TAXATION ON AMMUNITION All ammunition shall be assessed a 5,000% tax, (a .10 cent munition would cost $5.00).
The tax revenue shall fund the creation and management of “Weapons Information and Education Centers”.
Each “Center” shall maintain a variety of weapons, which can be utilized “only” at the facility.
Ammunition purchased at the “Center” and used at the “center” shall be without an assessment of the “Ammunition Tax”.
The ammunition purchased at the “Center” shall not be permitted to be removed from the “Center’.
Individuals shall not be permitted to bring ammunition into the “Center”.The “Center” shall conduct sales of ammunition for use outside of the “Center”, subject to an evaluation of the purchaser’s authority to possess the requested ammunition.
A purchaser or transferee is authorized to purchase ammunition if their information matches an entry in the Automated Firearm System and is not prohibited from purchasing a firearm.
Limitations on purchasing a firearm shall be based on background checks which review the applicant’s: criminal history, mental competency, and psychological competency.
Any determination by a judge, medical doctor, or licensed therapist that the applicant is not physically, or mentally, or psychologically authorized to possess a firearm, shall also prevent the application from purchasing ammunition at the “Center” whether for use at the “Center” or for private use outside of the “center”.
If an applicant’s request for approval is denied, they shall be provided with a written explanation regarding the basis for denial.
The denied applicant may request the governing entity which deemed the applicant “unfit” to review the applicant under an appeal.
Authorized users shall be permitted to bring their weapons to the “Center” for purposes of education, however all weapons must not be loaded when entering the facility and must not be loaded when leaving the facility.
The “Center” shall inspect all weapons upon entering and exiting the facility.
People will be permitted to store their weapons at the “Center”.
Weapons stored at the “Center” shall not be utilized by any other individuals.
A storage fee will be assessed.
Safety gear shall be required by all individuals located within the firing section of the facility.
The weapons provided by the “center” for public use shall include weapons not permitted to be in the possession of citizens, categorized as “Non-Civilian Weapons”.
Such weapons shall include: (large capacity weapons, weapons with a caliber larger than .45, multifire weapons (gatling gun, “machine guns”, “tommy guns”), experimental weapons, military weapons (assault rifles, grenade launchers), tanks, amphibious vehicles.
The weapons listed above shall not be permitted to be possessed, sold, transferred, gifted, inherited, traded or moved from one party to another in any capacity.
Non-authorized individuals shall not be permitted to manufacture any weapon categorized as “Non-Civilian Weapons”.
Any person, without authority from a regulating agency, engaged in the manufacturing of “Non-Civilian Weapons” shall be guilty of a Class C Felony, and sentenced accordingly.
Any person in possession of a “Non-Civilian Weapon” without authorization from an authorized regulating agency, shall be guilty of a Class D Felony, and sentenced accordingly.
Any Non-Authorized individual, manufacturing, distributing, trading, selling, transferring, marketing, negotiating, or gifting ammunition shall be guilty of a Class C Felony, and sentenced accordingly.
All “Non-Civilian Weapons” that are currently owned, or in the care, custody or control of civilians shall be turned over to a “Weapons Information and Education Center”.
All “Non-Civilian Weapons” can either be sold to the “Center” for the current appraised value as determined by appraisers on site, or the “Non-Civilian Weapon” can be kept at the “Center” in a storage locket, and used at the “Center” by the owner of the weapon, or anyone designated by the owner as an authorized user, if said person has been approved by the facility.
The “Center” shall provide education on SAFETY, including the proper methods to caring for all weapons, shall provide a history of the uses, the dangers of using each weapon, cleaning the weapons, storing weapons, proper transfer of weapons, the laws and penalties for having and using the above weapons, assembling and disassembling various commonly used weapons, laws (State, Federal, and International) that pertain to weapons or ammunition.
The “Center” shall be maintained by the “State” in which the “Center” is located.
Oversight shall be maintained by the Federal Government.
The “Center” shall also act as a “forgiveness center” where people may turn in weapons that are not permitted to be in their possession, either because it violates this regulation or because it violates another regulation, whether State or Federal.
Explanation/Notes The purpose of the legislation is to remove weapons located within this country, that are deemed hazardous to the public, and potentially causing mass casualties.
While the US Constitution 2nd Amendment guarantees the right to bear arms, it also calls for the “militia” to be well regulated.
Militia is vaguely used in various circumstances, and often refers to people who are not within any organized government entity, who engage in the defense of our nation, which under particular circumstances, could refer to anyone legally permitted to be in this country.
The “Center” is intended to provide a safe location where people can learn about gun safety, while also permitting individuals who hold an interest in weapons that are deemed hazardous, to utilize those weapons without those weapons being available in public.
The generally accepted uses for guns: A) protection of yourself or others, B) Hunting, whether for fowl, or game.
Weapons which fire large caliber ammunition are not practicable for either protection or hunting.
Weapons categories as “rapid fire” are equally not useful or practicable for defense or for hunting.
Weapons that hold a large supply of ammunition serve no legitimate purpose.
Weapons that do not have legitimate purposes serve as enjoyment, and shall not be held, stored, used or maintained by civilians.
Making ammunition more expensive is designed to discourage those who seek to engage in mass casualties.
The tax would make school shootings too expensive for many potential perpetrators to engage in, and those that would seek to take such action would be forced to place a real value on people’s lives.
At the moment, ammunition costs as little as 10-15 cents for most types of ammunition, and as much as $6.00 for nearly specialized munitions.
Without regulation, our current system allows a potential perpetrator to purchase enough bullets to eliminate an entire high school for less than the cost of filling up most vehicles with a tank of gasoline.
A handgun used for the defense of oneself typically holds up to 18 bullets.
Large capacity guns can hold significantly more.
A handgun obtained for the defense of oneself, is statistically not likely to ever be used, which means that the purchase would be a one time investment.
Hunters typically take 1-3 shots when hunting a large animal.
Duck/Fowl hunters typically take 5-10 shots.
Therefore, this tax would make these legitimate uses affordable to those who want a gun for protection purposes.
This tax would also be affordable to hunters, as the ammunition would still cost less than the cost of gas to get to and from the hunting location.
