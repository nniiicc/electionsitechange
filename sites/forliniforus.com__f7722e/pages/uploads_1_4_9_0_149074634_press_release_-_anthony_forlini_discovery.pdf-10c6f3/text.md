CONTACT INFORMATION ​ Contact: Amy Hawkins​ ​ Email: info@anthonyforlini.com ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ Release Date January 13, 2026 Candidate for Secretary of State Sounds the Alarm on ​ Potential Massive Voter Record Issues Statewide Macomb County Reviewing Jury, Voter Records After Citizenship Issues Found MACOMB COUNTY, Mich. - Macomb County officials are reviewing state data systems after discovering that non-citizens were included in recent jury pools and, in some cases, appeared in voter registration records.
Anthony G.
Forlini, candidate for Michigan’s Secretary of State commented, “Macomb County is Michigan’s third largest county.
The fact we have discovered this egregious flaw, indicates to me there may be a much larger issue throughout the State that needs investigation.” This error was first discovered in the process of selecting jury pools for the 16th Judicial Circuit Court.
They are generated through random selection from Michigan’s driver’s license database, which does not consistently verify citizenship status.
County officials said that has resulted in individuals who are legally ineligible for jury service receiving summonses.
This discovery moved them to a deeper internal review, comparing jury pool records with the state’s Qualified Voter File identified 239 non-citizens in jury pools over a four-month period.
Fourteen of those individuals appeared to have been registered to vote at some point, with at least one record indicating possible participation in multiple elections.
Forlini noted, “The Secretary of State’s office under Jocelyn Benson should have caught this years ago.
This reeks of malfeasance and mis-management.
As soon as I am elected to Secretary of State, I will investigate to the fullest degree possible.
This simply cannot be allowed.” Michigan relies on self-reported citizenship during the driver’s license and voter registration process and does not use systematic federal verification, officials said.
Errors may occur because of misunderstanding, language barriers or inaccurate self-reporting, allowing incorrect information to be carried across databases.
County officials said the issue raises concerns for both jury eligibility and election administration and are urging improvements to verification tools and data-sharing systems to prevent future errors.
