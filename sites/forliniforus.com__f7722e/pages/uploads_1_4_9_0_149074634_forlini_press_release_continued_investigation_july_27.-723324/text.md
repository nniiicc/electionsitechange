CONTACT INFORMATION ​ ​ Contact: Amy Hawkins​ ​ Email: info@anthonyforlini.com ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ Release Date July 27, 2026 Michigan Must Close Gaps that Could Allow ​ Non-Citizens onto State Voter Rolls Macomb County findings demonstrate the need for stronger statewide verification, transparency and accountability MACOMB COUNTY, Mich. - Anthony Forlini, Republican candidate for Michigan Secretary of State, is calling for stronger statewide safeguards after work conducted under his leadership as Macomb County Clerk identified individuals who reported that they were not U.S. citizens but also appeared in Michigan’s Qualified Voter File.
Between January 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026, more than 1,000 prospective Macomb County jurors reported that they were not U.S. citizens.
Therefore, they were not eligible to serve on a jury.
A cross-check conducted by the Macomb County Clerk’s Office found that 40 of those individuals appeared in the Qualified Voter File.
Five also appeared to have an associated voting history.
The findings were referred to the Michigan Bureau of Elections on January 13, April 28 and July 10, 2026, for further review and appropriate action if required.
“These findings do not justify assumptions about any individual - but they absolutely justify a thorough investigation,” Forlini said.
“When someone reports being a noncitizen for jury service but appears in the state’s voter file, Michigan officials have a responsibility to determine why, correct the record and tell the public what safeguards are being put in place.” Under Michigan law, only United States citizens are eligible to vote or serve on a jury.
Yet non-U.S. citizens continue to receive jury summonses and must self-report their ineligibility to be excused from service.
Forlini said the situation they have uncovered in Macomb demonstrates a larger weakness in Michigan’s voter-registration system worth investigating: information contained in one government database may not be consistently reviewed against information maintained in another.
“Michigan has access to information that can help identify registrations requiring further review,” Forlini said.
