Maryland Supreme Court Rules on Congressional Redistricting Fight – WBAL Interview Last week, the Maryland Supreme Court ruled that, although the language “is misleading and therefore violates due process requirements,” Question 3— the proposed ballot question that could set new standards for the way congressional maps are drawn in Maryland—can remain on ballots with slight language changes.
This ruling by the Maryland Supreme Court is disappointing and very concerning.
How can a General Assembly action that violates an established law surrounding ballot initiatives along with putting what the court acknowledges was a deceptive wording be something that the Court simply makes an adjustment to and allows to go forward?
This Court is aiding and abetting the deceptive behavior of our state’s one-party supermajority.
The General Assembly failed to follow the very election law passed by a unanimous bipartisan vote – the court has cast this violation aside.
Even with the Court’s changes to the question language, the ballot initiative does not explain the practical consequences of this amendment passing, that it removes protections under the Maryland Declaration of rights for citizens in regards to redistricting, giving Annapolis politicians sole power.
The only way to stop this now is for voters to reject this power-and-rights grab by voting down Question 3 at the ballot box in November and I look forward to campaigning actively to defeat it.
If you would like to engage with an organization that will be fighting AGAINST Question 3 – go to www.ProtectMDVotes.org .
