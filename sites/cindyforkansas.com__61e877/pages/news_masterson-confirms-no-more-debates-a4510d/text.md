Skip to main content About Issues Supporters News Store Get Involved Donate Donate Press Release Masterson Confirms: No More Debates For Immediate Release · September 18, 2026 TOPEKA, KS – Republican nominee for governor Ty Masterson is refusing to debate Senator Holscher again.
His spokesperson confirmed to the Kansas Reflector that Masterson has “no plans to accept any further debates.” Masterson’s retreat comes less than a week after the two faced off at the Kansas State Fair, where Holscher hammered him over his 20-year record in the legislature and six years running the Senate.
Again and again, she pressed him on skyrocketing property taxes, underfunded public schools, and the failed Brownback tax experiment he helped design, and now his campaign is ensuring he never has to face those questions on a stage again.
“It is completely unacceptable for Ty Masterson to think he should be the next governor of Kansas while refusing to stand in front of everyday Kansans and defend his record,” said Senator Cindy Holscher.
“He is betting that big money corporations and special interests can buy this election, but I believe in earning it.
I will debate him anytime and anywhere, because Kansans deserve a governor who will show up and answer the tough questions.” Holscher remains committed to talking to Kansas voters in every corner of the state to make her case for bringing down costs for families, lowering property taxes, and fighting back against tax giveaways to the rich that leave ordinary Kansans holding the bag.
### State Senator Cindy Holscher understands the struggles everyday Kansans face.
Holscher was raised on a farm by parents who worked hard for a living in construction and public schools.
She worked her way through college and is one of the first in her family with a college degree.
She is a mom who sent her three kids to public schools.
Holscher will make sure government serves everyday Kansans, not the wealthy and powerful.
She will fight to fully fund our public schools, lower property taxes, and make things more affordable in Kansas.
