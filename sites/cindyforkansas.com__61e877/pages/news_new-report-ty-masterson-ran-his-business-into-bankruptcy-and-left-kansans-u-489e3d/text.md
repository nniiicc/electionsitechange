TOPEKA, KS – Ty Masterson wants Kansans to trust him with the state’s finances, but new reporting out today from American Journal News shows how the last operation under his leadership turned out.
MasterBuilt Homes, the Andover homebuilder Masterson co-founded and led as CEO, collapsed into Chapter 7 bankruptcy carrying $1.13 million in debts it could not pay.
Masterson’s response was to blame an employee instead of taking accountability, yet Statehouse Republicans responded by handing him the keys to the state budget as chair of the Senate Ways and Means Committee, where he helped ram through the Brownback tax experiment that gutted Kansas schools and drove the state’s finances into the ditch – similarly to his failed business.
Ty Masterson bankrupted his business, then bankrupted the state, and everyday Kansans were left holding the bag both times.
More from the report:
- Masterson was the CEO of MasterBuilt Homes, a residential construction company based in Andover, KS.
- The company went under in 2006 and officially filed for Chapter 7 bankruptcy at the end of 2010.
Chapter 7… MasterBuilt reported owning roughly $300,000 in assets while owing $1.13 million in debt.
Some of the outstanding debts included $209,000 to Corner Bank, $53,890 to Emprise Bank, and $85,000 to Fisher Lumber of Garden Plain.
The company also reported more than $160,000 in credit card debt
- Masterson was elected to the Kansas Senate in 2009.
Despite his business record, Republicans in the legislature chose Masterson to lead the Senate Ways and Means Committee in 2012.
The committee is responsible for reviewing state agency budgets and proposed expenditures.
- Masterson blamed the bankruptcy on an employee and claimed he had little oversight of the company in this period because he was “getting engaged and excited about politics.”
