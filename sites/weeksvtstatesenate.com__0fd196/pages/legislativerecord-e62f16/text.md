A Record of Getting Things Done for Vermont
During the 2025–2026 legislative biennium, Senator Dave Weeks wrote or co-sponsored 77 bills and resolutions — and cast 109 recorded floor votes.
That's a working senator.
Highlighted Legislation
- S.17 & S.308 — Military Retirement Tax Exemption.
Exempting military retirement pay and survivors' benefits from Vermont state income tax — keeping the veterans who defended us from being taxed out of the state they call home.
- S.39 — State Aid for School Construction.
Delivering state funding to rebuild and modernize Vermont's aging school buildings.
- S.69 — Protecting Kids Online.
Requiring age-appropriate design standards for social media platforms used by Vermont children.
- S.313 — Career and Technical Education Reform.
Transforming Vermont's CTE programs to train the skilled workforce our economy needs.
Bills Drafted by Senator Weeks
- S.287 — Self-Funding Limited Access Highways.
A feasibility pathway for modern limited-access highways in Vermont — tackling both our economic stagnation and the deadliest roads in the nation.
- S.281 — Zero-Carbon Nuclear Feasibility Study.
Developing a realistic Vermont energy strategy that includes carbon-free nuclear power, studying the option aligned with a comprehensive state energy plan.
- S.275 — Historic Cemetery Restoration Fund.
Partnering with the Vermont Old Cemetery Association (VOCA) to fund materials for volunteers repairing and preserving Vermont's historic cemeteries.
- S.73 — Adverse Possession Notification.
Protecting Vermont property owners' rights with proper legal notice.
- Education Supervisory Union Consolidation Study — examining ways to reduce administrative overhead and focus education dollars on students.

First Term, Real Results — The 2023–2024 Record
Elected in 2022, Senator Dave Weeks hit the ground running: in his first term he wrote or co-sponsored 58 bills and resolutions and cast 144 recorded roll-call votes.
Highlighted Legislation
- S.38 — Downtown Permitting Relief.
Updating Act 250 to spare our downtowns from duplicative state permitting bureaucracy when a local zoning board has already spoken — cutting red tape for Vermont's main streets.
- S.118 — Military Retirement Tax Exemption.
Exempting military retirement pay and survivors' benefits from state income tax.
First fought for in this session, carried on into the next biennium — a multi-year fight to keep veterans from being taxed out of Vermont.
- S.297 — Felony Theft Threshold Reform.
Using the cumulative value of stolen property when determining felony versus misdemeanor charges — closing the loophole that lets repeat thieves rack up misdemeanor convictions for serious losses.
