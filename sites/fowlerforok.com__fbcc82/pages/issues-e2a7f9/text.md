From day one, this campaign has been driven by a simple belief: that government should work for the people who make this state strong, and people should know the people in office who represent them.
These priorities were driven by thousands of conversations I and my volunteers have had on doorsteps and in the community.
Oklahoma's schools need leadership in the State House that listens to parents, supports teachers, and keeps politics from getting in the way of learning.
I'll work to strengthen teacher pay, invest in classrooms, and make sure every student has a path to a successful future—whether through college, CareerTech, the trades, or military service.
Oklahoma homeowners need meaningful property-tax relief, but we also need to be honest about how we pay for it.
While some politicians promise to simply eliminate property taxes, I won’t support cuts that leave schools and essential local services scrambling to replace the revenue.
I support increasing the homestead exemption and providing responsible state funding to offset the lost local revenue—giving homeowners real relief without defunding the communities they live in.
Oklahomans are paying more and more to insure their homes and cars, and families deserve both fair rates and fair treatment when they file a claim.
I’ll push for greater transparency when insurers raise rates, stronger reporting on cancellations and non-renewals, faster deadlines for handling claims, and meaningful penalties when companies fail to meet their obligations.
I also support expanding incentives and insurance discounts for proven storm-resistant improvements that can reduce losses.
If you work full time, you should be able to afford the basics.
I support raising Oklahoma’s minimum wage so it better reflects today’s cost of living, while also investing in workforce training, supporting local businesses, and creating pathways to higher-paying careers.
As housing, groceries, insurance, and other everyday costs rise, working families deserve paychecks that rise with them.
Technology can make life safer and easier, but not at your expense.
Technology like Flock license-plate cameras needs clear rules: transparency, limits on how long data is kept, and protections against abuse.
Data centers should cover their own power and water costs, not pass them on to families, and should need local approval before they build.
And people should determine who gets hired or insured, not AI.
Be part of the conversation to build a stronger future for all Oklahomans.
