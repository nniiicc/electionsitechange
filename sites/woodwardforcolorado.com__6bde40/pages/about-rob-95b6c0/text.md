Home About Rob Issues Endorsements Contact Donate Get to know Rob Woodward ​ ​​ ​ ABOUT Rob’s upbringing taught him the value of hard work, grit, and economic opportunity.
Rob's great-grandfather moved to Riverton, Wyoming, in 1906 to serve as the surveyor for the town, canal, and road projects throughout Wyoming.
His grandfather George was born in that same town in 1910 and drove trucks for his entire adult life.
Rob’s father Bob was born in Riverton in 1947.
When Rob was born, his family lived in a camper while his dad moved around the state on a road construction crew.
When Rob was two, his dad quit in order to seek more stable opportunities for his young family.
They moved into the trailer court with a single-wide trailer.
Rob’s dad went to work as a stocker at a local grocery store.
Promotions came quickly, and he became manager.
His boss, a Wyoming state senator, sold him a small share of Mr.
D's.
Rob’s dad bought the store a few years later.
By the time Rob was in middle school, his father had changed the store’s name to Woodward's IGA and began opening stores in Gillette and Casper.
This growth was a testament to the grit that comes with owning and growing small businesses in communities, something that had a profound effect on Rob’s outlook on life.
In the 1980s, the oil industry crashed, so the Gillette and Casper stores closed, but the original store lasted until 2003, closing soon after Walmart came to town.
EARLY OPPORTUNITIES At six years old, Rob took on his first job - sacking ice for his dad at the grocery store - for a penny per bag.
He developed the ice business, bringing in additional production to stockpile in spring before significant demands every summer.
Rob learned early the value of investing in young entrepreneurs; his growing business and intelligent business practices allowed him to negotiate a raise with his dad to 10 cents per bag.
By middle school, he began using a Radio Shack TRS-80 computer and Lotus 123 spreadsheets to track payroll, wages, and vacations for the grocery store.
He helped implement scanning equipment into the store and managed the dairy department through high school.
COLLEGE AND CAREER ​ In 1987, Rob enrolled at Colorado State University.
So close to graduating in three years, he found himself just one credit short.
This event sparked an opportunity that would change the course of his life.
The department chair allowed him to write a business plan as a capstone project.
He chose to write it on Subway because his girlfriend, Paula, liked the sandwich chain, and he wanted to impress her.
He earned an A on the business plan, and the numbers showed it was a good investment.
In the fall of 1990, at age 21, Rob took the $4000 savings from his summer job to open his first store.
He graduated from CSU a month later with Paula, and they got engaged the next day.
There would be a few stops along the way before Rob and Paula would land back in northern Colorado.
A move to Chicago allowed Rob to work in corporate finance while earning his MBA at night.
In 1993, he moved to New York to help his boss launch an investment banking company.
1995 brought the Woodwards back to Fort Collins.
Paula was eight months pregnant, and they wanted to raise their children near family.
The move meant being closer to family and a return to the values they held dear as they entered parenthood.
FAST FORWARD ​ The Woodwards now have three grown children, all making their impact on the world.
Their oldest child Andrew graduated from Loveland High School, then Pepperdine University, and earned a Master's from the University of Denver.
Daughter Allie graduated from Loveland High School before heading off to Pepperdine.
After working in DC, she married an Air Force pilot, brought Rob and Paula’s first grandchild into the world, and is now stationed in Spokane.
Kate graduated from Resurrection Christian School before choosing to study Industrial Engineering at Cal Poly, and is now settled in Timnath with her husband.
BORN FOR BUSINESS Rob has been a small business entrepreneur since he was four, running lemonade stands, Kool-Aid stands, hot chocolate stands, and even farm stands to sell his mom's garden yields in the driveway.
He built a freezer on a bicycle and drove around selling ice cream at age eight.
He made plans to open a frozen yogurt shop in high school and even developed plans for a credit union at CSU.
Rob is a dreamer.
He loves analyzing an idea, casting a vision, and executing a plan to bring the concept to life.
He and Paula grew their dream by opening restaurants including Subway, Bagelmakers, IntaJuice, and more.
Investing in small businesses is a passion for Rob and Paula, and they know firsthand the dedication and sacrifices that come with making a small business work.
After 35 years in the sandwich business, Rob retired in 2025.
PAYING IT FORWARD Rob actively serves his community on multiple levels.
