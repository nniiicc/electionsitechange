As a real estate attorney, Lou sees Rhode Island's rising tax burden up close — not as abstract policy, but as numbers on a settlement statement.
Three changes in particular are worth every homeowner's attention: Real Estate Conveyance Tax ("Tax Stamps") Rhode Island's real estate conveyance tax rose 63%, from $2.30 to $3.75 per $500 of consideration, effective October 1, 2025, under R.I.
Gen.
Laws § 44-25-1(a) .
That applies to the entire price on every property, residential and commercial.
On top of it, § 44-25-1(b) adds a second $3.75 per $500 on residential consideration above $800,000 — so above that line the tax stamps double.
The threshold indexes to inflation every January.
It's a direct hit on every Rhode Islander who buys or sells a home, and a much larger one than most people realize.

Gen.

Family farms, businesses, and multi-generational property in Scituate can be swept into this tax more easily than families expect.
Non-Owner-Occupied Property Tax A new surcharge applies to non-owner-occupied residential property assessed over $1 million, enacted via R.I.
Gen.
Laws § 44-72-1 et seq. and effective July 1, 2026.
It exempts properties rented 183 or more days per year.
The concern for Scituate is the assessment trend: a threshold that is not indexed catches more property every year that values rise, including family land and inherited homes that were never the intended target.
Small Business & Cost of Living Rhode Island's tax and regulatory climate makes it harder than it should be for Scituate's shop owners, contractors, and family businesses to grow.
Lou will push for fiscal policy that lets small businesses breathe rather than piling on new taxes and red tape.
