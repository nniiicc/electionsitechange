Wes Moore Launches Statewide Ad Blitz Highlighting Real Results In Maryland Moore-Miller Campaign Continues Multi-Seven-Figure Ad Buy With New TV Ads And Focus On Black Media The Moore-Miller for Maryland campaign has launched a statewide multi-seven-figure ad buy that includes two new tv ads focused on the real results he’s delivered for Maryland and direct outreach to the Black community through targeted digital, radio, and print advertisements.
“What Marylanders will see over the next month are the real results Governor Moore has delivered to grow our economy, drop violent crime to record lows, deliver relief from skyrocketing energy bills, and build the best public schools possible,” said Senior Communications Adviser Carter Elliott, IV.
“As the campaign looks to November, Gov.
Moore is keeping his foot on the gas, not taking a single vote for granted and communicating directly with Marylanders on the real results he’s delivered since taking office.” The Moore-Miller campaign just launched two new tv ads highlighting the real results Governor Moore has delivered for Maryland.
In “What I’m About” Gov.
Moore highlights the work he’s done to drop violent crime to record lows, deliver relief from skyrocketing energy bills, and build the best public schools possible.
And, in “Here We Are” , Krystal Gonzalez, a mother who tragically lost her daughter to gun violence, discusses how Gov.
Moore has worked with everyone to put record funding in local law enforcement and bring homicides in Maryland to a 40-year low.
In a new radio advertisement, “Barbershop,” Gov.
Moore discusses his commitment to public safety with a local Marylander who experienced the effects of gun violence.
He ends the ad discussing his record investments in local law enforcement and touting Maryland’s 40-year low in violent crime.
The Moore-Miller campaign is currently utilizing Black radio in the DC, Baltimore, and Salisbury media markets.
Approximately 78% of all radio dollars are being invested with Black-owned radio broadcasters, driven primarily by the Urban One (Radio One) and WHUR-FM (Howard University) in the Washington market.
The Baltimore radio investment is approximately 89% Black-owned, and Washington radio investment is approximately 80% Black-owned.
