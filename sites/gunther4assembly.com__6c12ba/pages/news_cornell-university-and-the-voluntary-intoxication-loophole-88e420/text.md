This week, a case out of Cornell University has drawn national attention to a real gap in New York law.
Under the state's current statute, a sexual assault survivor can be denied justice if they had voluntarily consumed alcohol or drugs before being attacked - even if that left them unable to consent.
It's commonly called the "voluntary intoxication loophole," and it has let real cases go unprosecuted, not because anyone disputes what happened, but because of how the law is written.
Governor Hochul and Attorney General James have both said this needs to change.
I agree with them.
Here's what I think is also worth knowing: legislation to close this loophole has been introduced in the Assembly every year since at least 2020.
It has never once reached the floor for a vote - not because of Republican obstruction, but because Assembly leadership in my own party has kept it from coming up.
Survivors, advocates, and lawmakers from both parties have pushed for this for years, and it has gone nowhere.
I'm telling you because I think it matters more that something this important gets done than who gets credit for doing it, or who's standing in the way.
If elected, I will support closing this loophole - and I won't stay quiet if leadership, in either party, keeps a bill like this from getting a vote it deserves.
Survivors in this state have waited long enough.
