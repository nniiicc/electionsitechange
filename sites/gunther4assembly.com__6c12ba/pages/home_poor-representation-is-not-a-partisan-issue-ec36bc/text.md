Meet John News Endorsements Volunteer Voter Information Donate by Mail Contribute Home / Poor Representation Is Not a Partisan Issue 16 Oct Friday, 10:49 AM · 2026 Poor Representation Is Not a Partisan Issue Poor Representation Is Not a Partisan Issue By John Gunther, Candidate for NYS Assembly, District 114 Whatever your party affiliation, you probably want the same thing from your Assemblyman: someone who gets things done for this district.
Matt Simpson attends the barbecues, the firehouse dinners, and the parades.
People across this district know him, and many of them, whatever their party, like him.
But when we vote, we're not choosing a neighbor.
We're choosing who goes to Albany for the next two years to fight for the North Country.
So here is a fair question for every voter, Republican, Democrat, or neither: after three terms, what has changed because he was there?
His Record By my count, Matt Simpson is the lead sponsor of 37 Assembly bills this term.
None has been signed into law.
Two have passed both houses and may still reach the governor: a transfer of state land in Queensbury to Warren County, and a bill removing residency requirements for Washington County deputy county attorneys.
The other 35 died.
That includes his bills on road salt reduction, on giving towns the final say on where renewable energy plants go, and on battery storage setbacks.
While I credit him for raising those issues, none of them became law.
Why the poor record?
"He's in the Minority".
That's the usual answer, and it's true that the majority in Albany decides what moves.
But if the same good ideas die year after year, the question is what to do differently.
A representative who is serious about results finds partners across the aisle, builds coalitions, and trades support to get things moved.
If that hasn't happened in three terms, it’s a pattern.
The fights that matter most here aren't partisan.
Lake George, local control of battery plants, and the pressure on property and sales taxes will only be solved by someone who can work with people they disagree with.
Where Was He?
Representing a district means showing up when people ask for help.
Here's what I've seen: The Lake George Association meets on road salt runoff.
Simpson doesn't show.
Ticonderoga fights Brookfield's massive lithium-ion battery project.
Simpson doesn't show.
Northampton fights another battery project.
Simpson doesn't show.
A Queensbury redemption center and local nonprofits plead for help.
Simpson doesn't show.
John Gunther invites him for a beer to talk North Country needs.
Simpson doesn't answer.
When the people you represent are asking for you, being there is the job.
What I'm Putting in Writing I'm running as a Democrat, and we won't all agree on everything.
I'd rather tell you where I stand than ask you to trust me.
Here's what I'm committing to: I'll push for a road salt limit to protect Lake George and the Adirondack Park.
I'll fight for local control, so a town's own vote on a project like a battery plant carries real weight.
I'll sponsor the New York Health Act, to relieve pressure on county budgets and property taxpayers.
I'll report back.
Each season, I'll publish what I promised, what I did, and what got in the way.
Check Both of Us Look up my opponent's record.
When you've done that, look up mine, and keep checking it if I win.
If I deliver, tell your neighbors.
If I don't, tell me.
Questions?
Write to me at gunther4assembly@gmail.com.
I'll answer.
