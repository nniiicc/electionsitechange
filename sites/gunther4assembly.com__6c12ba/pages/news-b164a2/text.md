Cornell University and the Voluntary Intoxication Loophole
This week, a case out of Cornell University has drawn national attention to a real gap in New York law.
Under the state's current statute, a sexual assault survivor can be denied justice if they had voluntarily consumed alcohol or drugs before being attacked - even if that left them unable to consent.
It's commonly called the "voluntary intoxication loophole," and it has let real cases go unprosecuted, not because anyone disputes what happened, but because of how the law is written.

John Gunther Announces his Candidacy for New York State Assembly District 114
“For the past six years, Matt Simpson has been a do-nothing Assemblyman in the do-nothing State Assembly,” Gunther charged.
“We can’t afford health care.
We can’t afford to pay our utility bills.
The way New YorkState treats its corrections officers, civilian staff and the incarcerated is a disgrace.
What have Matt Simpson and the State Assembly done?
Nothing.”
Gunther called the 114 th Assembly District “the Forgotten 114 th ”.
“The people of the North Country are invisible because our Assembly representative is silent.
Matt Simpson has been in the Assembly since 2021.
Yet, on the issues that matter most, he’s never said a word on the Assembly floor,” Gunther stated.
Gunther cited three immediate priorities.
- Universal health care: “I’ll sponsor and vote for the New York Health Act providing health care for all.”
- High utility rates: “Politicians from both parties protect the Public Service Commission which, in turn, guarantees oppressive and excessive utility profits.
I will work for impactful public service reform by reforming the Public Service Commission.”
- New York State’s Prisons: “I join with Senator Dan Stec in calling for the resignation or firing of Corrections Commissioner Daniel Martuscello.
By no objective standard is he capable of leading a department in crisis.
Nothing changes until he’s gone.”
John Gunther has spent nearly a half-century building several North Country businesses employing hundreds.
He founded The Works, Incorporated in Greenwich.
“I made my way the way most do.
Borrowed smart money.
Paid it back on time.
Never stiffed a vendor or contractor.
Created every job I ever promised,” Gunther said.
“I’ll campaign on my record by running against Matt Simpson’s.”
Gunther announced from his home in Shushan, Washington County.
The 114 th Assembly District encompasses most of Essex, Warren and Washington Counties and a few towns in Saratoga and Fulton Counties.
He will be on the Democratic and Working Families Party lines in the June Primary unopposed.
