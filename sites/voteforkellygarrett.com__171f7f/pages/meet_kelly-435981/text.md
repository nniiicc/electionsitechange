Meet Kelly
Kelly Garrett is a community driven leader who has spent more than a decade fighting for the people she serves.
In Lathrup Village, she created the first infrastructure committee in the city’s history and led the charge to rebuild what residents had gone far too long without.
Under her leadership, the city replaced several miles of water mains, cleaned and repaired many miles of sewer, removed lead service lines, upgraded fire hydrants, and completed the entire project $1.5 million dollars under budget.
She also oversaw the reconstruction of 8.3 miles of neighborhood roads.
Kelly secured $7.2 million dollars in state and federal funding and fought to bring new businesses into the community while ensuring that the police force remained strong and well supported.
Her work has always been grounded in one belief.
People deserve safety, stability, and a fair chance to build a good life.
As someone who understands the real pressures working families face, Kelly knows that far too many people are struggling to make ends meet.
Families are stretching every dollar to put food on the table, keep their lights on, stay warm in the winter, and remain secure in their homes.
She has seen the weight that Michigan households carry, and she refuses to look away.
Kelly is committed to fighting for a future where families can breathe again and where basic needs do not feel like luxuries.
Her service extends deep into community and public leadership.
She has served as a Councilwoman, Mayor, Planning Commissioner, DDA Commissioner, Chair of the Southfield Lathrup Village Democratic Club, Vice Chair of the Oakland County Democratic Party, President and Secretary of Mothering Justice, creator of the first Michigan Legislative Care Caucus, and Vice Chair of the Southfield Lathrup Village Optimist Club.
Kelly was appointed by Governor Whitmer to the Michigan Commission on Community Action and Economic Opportunity.
She is also a proud member of Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority Incorporated.
Kelly’s story is rooted in resilience, service, and results.
Her vision is focused on stronger neighborhoods, safer communities, and a Michigan where families do not have to struggle just to survive.
