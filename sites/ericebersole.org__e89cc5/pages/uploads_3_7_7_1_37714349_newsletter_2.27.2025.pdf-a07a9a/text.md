I am the lead sponsor of two bills that will help protect those who engage in sports wagering.
This is a relatively new and legal activity in Maryland and we already have numerous personal protections – from self-exclusion to free hotlines & counseling for those who find they have difficulty resisting excessive betting.
These bills go even further: HB 922 requires - instead of merely authorizes as specified in current law - a sports wagering licensee or sports wagering operator, that advertises in the State, to contract with a licensed independent evaluator to evaluate and rate the sports wagering licensee’s sports wagering content, sports wagering experts, sports wagering influencers, and content partners.
In this way, we can be sure that bettors are not being moved by advice encouraging betting a certain way, by media posts, for the benefit of the sports book and not the consumer.
Also, it will protect against these touts (people who give betting advice) offering access to unlicensed, and therefore illegal, sports books HB 1140 Prohibits an entity from operating, conducting, or promoting online sweepstakes games or supporting the operation, conducting, or promotion of online sweepstakes games.
These games are often cleverly disguised casino gambling sites using “tokens” instead of money, but which largely amount to gambling chips.
If people use these sites to gamble, not only are they breaking the law along with the gambling entity, but more importantly, they do not enjoy the protections against fraud that state legalization and regulation provide.
In addition, the revenue we expect from wagering activity goes to illegal operators, rather than the state.
This bill requires the State Lottery and Gaming Control Agency to deny license applications and revoke licenses and prohibits the State Lottery and Gaming Control Commission from issuing a license or allowing commencement or continuing operations when they have been deemed to be more than online entertainment and are actually online wagering.
On Thursday, February 27th, I had the opportunity to meet with CASA representatives.
During this meeting, we spoke about important bills related to protecting immigrants in Maryland.
I am a strong supporter of immigrant families and children, and I believe in protecting these communities from the destructive power of ICE.
Recently, I introduced the bill HB1513: Public Schools– School Security Personnel– Prohibition of Immigration Investigation and Enforcement Functions.
If passed, this bill would prohibit School Resource Officers (SROs) from working with ICE in counties that allow collaboration between county police officers and ICE through 287(g) programs.
In doing so, this bill would allow Maryland to take an important step towards ensuring that immigrant children feel safe and protected while at school.
One of the key elements to the success an SRO can have is to gain the trust of the students in the school.
In this way they can have students see law enforcement in a different, non-threatening light – and the SRO can offer real help and advice to them.
This trust building must not be eroded.
