The arguments against fracking are clear.
The dangers to drinking water and the surface environment are well documented.
The mail I receive, both from our district and from the potentially impacted areas of Western Maryland, are uniformly against allowing this practice here in our state.
The long term value of beauty and tourism far outweigh the perceived short term benefits of this gas extraction.
The only mail I receive in favor comes from fossil fuel business interests.
There is some debate as to whether we should pass another moratorium or an outright ban.
In the short term, they are the same, but in the long run, a moratorium requires action for continuance while a ban would take much more deliberate and supported action to overturn.
I support the ban.
We need to make our future safe from this environmental danger.
This legislation is moving along nicely.

I fully support the bill to limit testing hours to 2% of instructional time.
It is also important to note that measuring the impact of tests merely by the number of hours a particular student might test each year is not a fully sufficient measure.
To that end, I have suggested an amendment to the bill that makes the Social Studies assessment a performance based assessment, allowing for less recall and short answer and encouraging in-class evaluation.
I will support this and, down the road, other necessary legislation to make sure we have as much instructional time as possible to do the job of educating our students.
I continue to advocate against the BOOST program, which amounts to a voucher system.
Scholarships, using public taxpayer dollars, were awarded to students last year to attend private schools.
In the 2016 session, the Maryland budget included $5 million for these vouchers.
This appropriation was slipped into the budget at the eleventh hour in a manner that made it nearly impossible to stop and was done in the face of the failure of such legislation in my committee, Ways and Means.
Any program that pulls children out of their community will leave many more disenfranchised students behind.
This bill was created to “boost” students away from underperforming schools - but 78% of the funds went to students who had already been enrolled in private schools in the previous year.
And now, emboldened by the new presidential administration, the governor wants to double this commitment of public tax dollars to send students to private schools.
I have advocated with the leadership in the House of Delegates to not only stop any increase on this misuse of taxpayer money, but to reverse this program and use this money where is should be used-- to improve our public schools.
Environmental Concerns – There has been an effort by the governor to claw back on some of the protections for oysters.
Oysters are essential to the work of cleaning and preserving the Chesapeake Bay and thus, we have taken serious measures to protect this population.
A Department of Natural Resources survey, conducted recently, revealed higher levels of oyster reproduction and a lower mortality rate.
Given that each adult oyster filters and cleans up to 50 gallons of water per day—gobbling up algae, and removing dirt and nitrogen pollution—that's good news for the health of the Chesapeake Bay and for us.
We had seen the population decrease to single digit percentages of the historic population and our programs are responsible for beginning a fledgling recovery.
It is too early to dial back our efforts.
House Bill 924 would prohibit the Department of Natural Resources from reducing or altering specified oyster sanctuaries until the Department develops a fisheries management plan for the scientific management of the oyster stock following completion of a specified study and reports.
