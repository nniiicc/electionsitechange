There is a link to the full report below.
There is a lot there, but if you jump to page 44, you will find a more bare bones list of the recommendations.
Among the things I am pleased we were able to agree on was the recommendation of the elimination of the middle school Government Test, and the restructuring of the Government High School Assessment to a more learning interactive setting and a classroom structure.
The PARCC test is federally mandated so we couldn’t sweep it out the door, but we made recommendations to reduce its impact on instruction including shorter total time, shorter units of testing that would fit into single periods, and lifting the requirement that only a certified teacher may administer and proctor the test.
I was hoping for even more, like the outright reduction of duplicative benchmark testing, but for now each school system will be asked to set up a District Committee on Assessment which will take that and other relevant issues up with local needs and initiatives in mind.
The next step is for MSDE and the local school systems to weigh in on whether or not they will follow the recommendations and, if not, their reasoning.
Then it is up to us in the legislature to act accordingly as we see fit.

Recently, I have received a number of communications concerning noise pollution that residents are having in their homes.
Two contributors to this are BWI Thurgood Marshall Airport and The Merriweather Post Pavilion.
While these two institutions provide very different services to our area, ones that we value, it is important that they be good players in consideration of those who reside nearby.
Merriweather – Since the Sweet Life Festival in 2015, there has been a consistent concern in Town Center, Wilde Lake, Harpers Choice, and Beaverbrook with the level of sound and disruption in these communities.
The members of your District 12 legislative team have been taking a number of steps to understand the history and science of Merriweather and its sound systems.
First, we have an open dialog with Merriweather.
They have taken some steps to ameliorate sound proliferation in the area and there are more to come as the renovations to the facility continue.
Among these are redirecting the speakers and taking more direct control of performers’ volumes.
But there may be more investigation and work to be done.
First, we are taking a hard look at the law passed a few years ago to regulate sound and evaluating it.
We are getting independent sound experts to educate us and will be holding a town meeting soon to drill down on the true nature of the problem and what we can do about it, all the while keeping the asset of Merriweather viable and attractive.
BWI Thurgood Marshall - In addition to some runway repairs, NextGen flight patterns at the airport have added significantly to the noise folks in Elkridge, Hanover, Long Reach, and Catonsville are experiencing.
I recently attended a meeting with the FAA at their DC office with a contingent of local, state, and federal representatives.
We had a constructive meeting discussing the increased noise due to air traffic over the affected areas.
I was there with Howard County Council Chair Calvin Ball, Howard County Council Vice Chair Jon Weinstein, [pictured with me below] and representatives for US Senator Ben Cardin, House of Representatives members Sarbanes, Cummings, and Ruppersberger, County Executive Kittleman, and Delegate Clarence Lam.
A few key outcomes: - The FAA committed to reviewing the near-term improvements recommended by the community and the Maryland Aviation Administration (MAA) and report back to us about their disposition and progress, including interim altitude changes. - FAA will work with MAA to initiate the formal process for a procedural review with community input. - FAA will respond to the MAA's April 25 letter, which included recommendations (near- and longterm) to mitigate noise. - FAA committed to improve their communications with the community through the local representatives, community organizations, and the County Government.
We will conduct a meeting by mid-September with MAA, FAA, and our local representatives to coordinate efforts and actions.
