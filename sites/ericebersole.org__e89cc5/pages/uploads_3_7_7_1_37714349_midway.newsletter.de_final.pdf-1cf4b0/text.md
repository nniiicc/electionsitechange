2 The Freedom to Read Act (HB 785) Earlier this session, I helped shut down a bill looking to implement the type of “book banning” that we are seeing in many states throughout the country.
There must be a diverse, inclusive pool of books in our libraries; not a broad censorship standard.
In order to ensure that Maryland’s libraries will always provide library materials and services for all individuals without exclusion based on origin, views, or partisan disapproval, I am proud to co-sponsor HB 785 – The Freedom to Read Act.
By setting standards that promote an inclusive environment and open access to information, this bill will preserve the diverse perspectives in our libraries while also challenging the attempts at censorship that are too common today.
The challenges faced by our libraries are not limited to censorship, however.
Many states have passed laws that threaten penalties – often as harsh as prison – upon librarians for refusing to remove the “banned book of the day” from circulation.
The Freedom to Read Act ensures that this will never happen in the State of Maryland by providing librarians protection against retaliation for acting in accordance with the standards outlined above The Freedom to Read Act is legislation that puts Maryland in the driver’s seat in the fight against book banning and censorship.
I am excited to support this bill as a co-sponsor.
Tax Assistance for Low Income Marylanders (HB451) As tax season is upon us, I understand the stress and hardships that can come with navigating and filing your taxes.
With that, I am proud to support HB451, The Tax Assistance for Low Income Marylanders Funding Bill, introduced by the Speaker of the House.
This bill will restore funding to the Creating Assets, Savings, and Hope (CASH) in Maryland Campaign for low-income tax clinics and services.
The CASH Campaign offers free tax preparation services for low-income Marylanders, and this bill would launch a grant program dedicated to funding mobile and on-demand tax clinics for rural, senior, and underserved communities.
Furthermore, the bill would reinstate the distribution of abandoned property funds to low-income tax services, increasing the funding from $250,000 to $500,000.
Far too many Marylanders are unable to afford tax services and there are far too few tax clinics to benefit communities.
Filing taxes can often be arduous for marginalized communities to navigate, whether they are not 3 within reach or inaccessible by public transportation.
This bill would allow the CASH campaign to direct $150,000 directly to mobile and on-demand tax clinics so that they may reach all communities and ensure every Marylander has the ability to file their taxes without stress.
I am proud to support this bill and help the CASH Campaign continue their hard work, assisting low-income Marylanders with their taxes this year.
