Best, Eric Ebersole Session's Legislative Priorities Human Trafficking (HB270): One of the biggest priorities for me this session is to pass HB0270, which requires innkeepers to provide training to their staff to identify signs of human trafficking.
Human trafficking is a horrific issue that is more prevalent in our communities than most people think.
Requiring people who work in hotels, motels, and similar businesses to be trained in identification of human trafficking is crucial in stopping these crimes.
Human traffickers often use inns to fly under the radar and to support their illegal operations, so having employees be able to recognize and report suspected traffickers is key in the fight against trafficking.
This bill requires that inns keep electronic records of their guests and that they provide this training to employees, greatly aiding law enforcement in identifying this illegal activity.
It will no doubt make our community a safer place and save countless individuals from exploitation and violence.
Mobility (HB726) To better promote accessibility for elderly and disabled individuals in the district, I will be proposing HB0726 to provide the opportunity for MTA Mobility to work with outside contractors, like Uber and Lyft.
Better enabling elderly and disabled individuals to access transportation is a must for me, as I know it can be challenging for these groups to rely on family and friends to transport them all of the time.
Passing this bill will expand the opportunities of people in our district and across Maryland to successfully use transportation services, no matter where they live or need to go.
Early Childhood Mental Health Expansion (HB0513) School Psychologist Retention (HB1231) An exciting initiative that I am working on is aimed at expanding efforts to promote better early childhood mental health in HB0513.
I’m also working on HB1231 this session, which promotes better retention of psychologists in schools to support the mental health of our district’s children.
This expansion will create the Infant and Early Childhood Mental Health Consultation Program, which increases governmental support given to young children, their families, teachers, and caregivers.
This program will increase access to mental health services for those groups and require the Governor to include $6,000,000 in funding for the program in his annual budget.
We will be improving the lives of young children and their families by providing the proper supportive resources at a key stage of development in their lives.
Still feeling the effects of the pandemic on mental health, young students have had to make major adjustments to their lives in the past few years.
Now more than ever they need a proper support system in school to help them deal with the stressful experiences in their lives.
This bill will do just that by working with the State Department of Education to help formulate a plan to incentivize our mental health professionals to stay in their positions in schools longer and give them the tools to make a real difference for children.
