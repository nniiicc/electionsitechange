As the chair of the Education Subcommittee in the Ways and Means Committee, I deeply hold my dedication to our youth, our teachers, the support personnel, and administrators that make up the Maryland Public School System.
Education and the children we teach are our future.

We considered several initiatives on revising the Blueprint for Maryland’s Future I want to assure you that the Blueprint for Maryland’s Future is not causing the general fund deficit and I remain committed to fully funding education so that every student may succeed, no matter their zip code.
Accordingly, changes to the blueprint were brought forth in HB 504: The Excellence in Maryland Schools Act.
The Blueprint for Maryland’s Future is a transformative and generational piece of legislation, crafted using years of research to ensure the best possible education for every child in Maryland.
When the Governor brought HB504: Excellence in Maryland Schools Act, a bill proposing major changes to the Blueprint, we saw an opportunity to create better solutions for Maryland’s education system.
After collaborating to make several amendments, the bill now ensures an efficacious implementation of the Blueprint as intended.
We have ensured that none of the at-promise students (our special needs learners, multi-language learners, and those in concentrations of poverty) will see a reduction in the support they need to be successful.
In response to persisting teacher shortages, this bill delays establishing more collaborative time for teachers for three years, however it innovates substitute solutions and sets up grants right from the beginning to establish collaborative time.
It establishes the Maryland Teacher Relocation Incentive Grant, improves the Grow Your Own Educators Grant Program, and protects teacher candidates from pay reductions.
These measures will improve teacher recruitment and retention across the state and attract teachers from out of state as well.
Despite the difficult fiscal climate, this bill still prioritizes maintaining the quality of our schools.
This bill maintains the target per-pupil spending amount, increases staffing for community school administration, and provides more flexibility for where counties can spend Concentration of Poverty Grants.
It also establishes and funds the Academic Excellence Program, which will deploy instructional coaches and coordinators of professional learning (CPL) to low-performing schools.
By refining the Blueprint’s implementation, this bill upholds our promise to invest in our schools, support our educators, and provide every student with the opportunities they deserve.
In addition to HB 504, several notable pieces of legislation that focus on education passed the House of Delegates including: HB147: County Boards of Education - Elementary and Middle Schools - Student Technology Use Policy: Student phone and technology use in school has been increasingly problematic in recent years and is proving to be detrimental to student achievement, mental health, and social development.
Certain Maryland counties have acted by establishing policies that prohibit elementary and middle school students from using a personal electronic device during school hours.
HB147 Student Technology Use Policy requires all county boards to develop such policies, ensuring that all students across the state can be held accountable to focus on their learning.
This bill stalled in the Senate, but 19 of our 24 school districts have a recently updated policy indicating good local response.
We will work on the bill again next year to bring everyone on board.
HB0161: Primary and Secondary Education - Comprehensive Health Education Framework – Established: We owe it to each Maryland student to provide a high-quality and comprehensive health education, but some county curricula have been deviating from this standard.
HB161 Comprehensive Health Education Framework - Established requires the State Department of Education to collaborate with the Maryland Department of Health to develop an updated comprehensive health education framework to include important topics like mental health, substance abuse prevention, family life, human sexuality, violence prevention, and safe internet use.
With this framework, counties can create age-appropriate curricula and establish alternative plans for students whose parents opt them out of family life and human sexuality topics.
This ensures all students a quality health education while providing for unique circumstances.
HB197: Public Schools - Restorative Practices Schools - Comprehensive Plan: Restorative practices are a proactive, research-supported, and culturally responsive student discipline model that still keeps students accountable for any harm done and provides methods to repair affected relationships.
HB197 Public Schools - Restorative Practices Schools - Comprehensive Plan provides support for schools to use more restorative practices.
It also requires MSDE to establish Restorative Practices Schools to recognize schools that integrate restorative practices into daily practices and activities of the whole school.
Through conflict resolution, mediation, and trauma-informed care, this model ensures that teachers and students feel safer and are more engaged in learning.
HB325 Adult Education - High School Diploma by Examination - Requirements and Study This legislation requires the Maryland Department of Labor to allow individuals to take the GED in English or Spanish, and requires the Department to study the feasibility and cost of offering additional languages as well.
This will remove significant professional barriers to adult immigrants and K-12 youth, making Maryland more equitable for everyone.
HB493 Public Schools - Commemorative Day - Ruby Bridges Walk to School Day: Designating November 14 as Ruby Bridges Walk to School Day to commemorate Ruby Bridges, who, on November 14, 1960, became the first African American student to integrate an all-while school in New Orleans, Louisiana; and requiring the State Department of Education to develop model content for a program of education for Ruby Bridges Walk to School Day.
Public transportation is always an important topic in Annapolis, and this year a number of notable bills were brought forward for the Assembly to consider.
Among these bills, some of note are: HB375: Bicycles, Play Vehicles, and Unicycles - Authorizations and Requirements (Jay's Law) : Former Maryland law forbade bicycles on sidewalks as the default statewide.
However, certain localities have overridden this law for safety reasons, such as concerns about children riding bikes to school.
This inconsistency has created confusion for cyclists, especially since roads often switch between state, county, and city designations.
HB375 Bicycles, Play Vehicles, and Unicycles - Authorizations and Requirements (Jay's Law) amends state law to read that, unless prohibited by local ordinance, a person may ride a bicycle, play vehicle, or unicycle on sidewalks as long as they yield to pedestrians and ride safely.
This bill was a great step towards Maryland’s Zero Deaths goals by aligning Maryland code with safe and equitable biking.
HB707: Vehicle Laws - Licenses, Identification Cards, and Moped Operator's Permits - Notation of Nonapparent Disability (Eric's ID Law): HB707 Eric’s ID law is named after Eric Carpenter-Grantham, who is an African American 20-year-old with autism.
After George Floyd was murdered, his mother feared for his safety if he were stopped by law enforcement due to his invisible disability.
HB707 requires that individuals have the option of indicating their non apparent disability and including notation of it on their driver’s license, identification card, or moped operator’s permit.
This bill also requires law enforcement to be trained concerning interactions with people with non apparent disabilities.
With this legislation, we are taking a critical step toward preventing unnecessary escalation and ensuring equitable treatment in law enforcement.
Energy legislation has received a great deal of attention this session.
Everyone has experienced an increase in their gas and electric bills.
As a result, the legislature brought forward and considered a number of meaningful pieces of legislation for consideration.
HB1035: The Next Generation Energy Act: The bill protects ratepayers by prohibiting the use of multiyear rate plans.
Additionally, gas companies will need to justify any new gas infrastructure investments and are further prohibited from recovering through rates any trade association memberships, or purchases and uses of private planes.
The bill also holds data centers accountable for their energy uses by ensuring that they pay their own costs for interconnecting to the electrical system.
HB1036: Renewable Energy Certainty Act: This legislation creates statewide standards for solar and battery storage projects, it prohibits excessive restrictions that prevent responsible solar and battery storage development, and it ensures that Marylanders are protected against predatory practices HB1037: Energy Resource Adequacy and Planning Act : This bill establishes the Strategic Energy Planning Office.
Obtaining information from PJM continues to be a tricky and difficult endeavor and they don’t always have the best interests of Marylanders and its citizens at heart.
The establishment of this office will ensure that Maryland has an accurate assessment of current and future energy needs and will create solutions of ways to address those needs with minimal cost to ratepayers in the state.
Given the recent immigration enforcement executive orders from the Trump Administration, the members of the Maryland General Assembly and myself worked to pass legislation to protect Maryland immigrants.
I co-sponsored HB1006: Immigration Enforcement- Sensitive Locations- Guidelines and Policies (Protecting Sensitive Locations Act).
This bill requires the Attorney General to develop guidelines for entities at sensitive locations to limit immigration enforcement from issuing arrests, searches, inspections, seizures, and subpoenas at sensitive locations, such as schools, medical centers, places of worship, among other locations.
In doing so, HB1006 intends to re-instill safety for immigrants in Maryland.
Through this, we are ensuring that Maryland children feel secure to attend school and that all Marylanders can worship and attend events such as funerals, weddings, protests, etc. without fear of ICE detainment.
I also co-sponsored HB1222: Public Safety– Immigration Enforcement (Maryland Values Act), which will prohibit state and local enforcement agencies, including county sheriffs, from entering into immigration enforcement agreements.
This will prohibit counties in Maryland from engaging in 287(g) programs with ICE, which currently allows local police officers to act as ICE agents in order to detain, arrest, and question individuals in Maryland.
By prohibiting this agreement, Maryland will work to ensure that we remain a safe place for immigrants.
House Bill 1431: State and Local Agencies– Enforcement of Federal Immigration Law– Restrictions on Access to Immigration (Maryland Data Privacy Act).
This bill requires law enforcement agencies to deny access to privacy databases to any individual who appears to be or is seeking information access for the purpose of enforcing federal immigration law.
In doing so, HB1431 ensures privacy for all Marylanders, helping to restrict federal ICE raids and seizes.
Overall, during this session, my fellow members and I jumped into action in response to President Trump’s executive orders, working to better protect and support Maryland immigrants.
Improving healthcare affordability and quality were top priorities in Annapolis this legislative session.
My fellow legislators and I passed HB424: Lowering Prescription Drug Costs for all Marylanders Now Act.
This bill requires the Prescription Drug Affordability Board to establish a process for setting upper payment limits for purchases and payer reimbursements of prescription drug products, along with authorizing the board to reconsider an upper payment limit for a drug that becomes a shortage and requiring the board to confer with the Maryland Medical assistance program before establishing an upper payment limit.
In doing so, this bill takes a crucial step towards lowering prescription costs in Maryland.
The General Assembly also passed HB158: Department of Aging– Senior Call-Check and Social Connections Program.
This bill will improve and rename the Senior Call-Check Service and Notification Program to be the Senior Call-Check and Social Connections Program in order to foster more social engagement, wellness, and service integration for senior citizens.
We also passed a series of bills aimed towards improving Maternal Health.
HB1538: Maryland Medical Assistance Program - Maternal Health Monitoring Pilot Program established a program dedicated to support pregnant and postpartum persons who have higher risk of pregnancy complications.
HB170 : Health - Maternal and Child Health Population Health Improvement Fund - Use extends the deadline for the Maternal and Child Health Population Health Improvement fund from 2025 to 2027.
HB553: Maryland Medical Assistance Program - Maternal Health Self-Measured Blood Pressure Monitoring requires the Maryland Medical Assistance Program to provide coverage for self-measured blood pressure monitoring.
Overall, this legislation aims to make healthcare more accessible and affordable, making critical efforts to improve the health of all Marylanders.
Within the district, there are a number of abandoned and neglected cemeteries that have drawn my attention.
Caring for the final resting places of our loved ones is important because we should honor the memory and dignity of those who have passed.
Additionally, cemeteries often contain valuable historical information which provides insight into our local history.
Neglected cemeteries are also an issue of public safety.
Abandoned cemeteries can attract vandalism and could possibly pose safety risks as well.
I was happy to support HB535: Abandoned and Neglected Cemeteries Fund - Establishment .
This bill will provide funding to groups who wish to provide care and upkeep of cemeteries that have long gone without care.
I will be happy to work with groups within my district who wish to support and repair the abandoned and neglected cemeteries within the district and ensure that they obtain the funding required to meet this community need.
Personal Legislation This year I sponsored several pieces of legislation.
I am proud to report that HB1, the See Someone, Save Someone Act, passed the House and Senate unanimously.
The See Someone, Save Someone Act requires the Maryland Department of Transportation to train its employees how to recognize and report the signs of human trafficking.
Roughly 40% of human trafficking victims reported that they were trafficked using public transportation.
I believe that this type of training is an important step towards combating human trafficking.
HB 986: Primary and Secondary Education -New Teachers- Retention Program: – Last session I passed legislation that would create new and equally rigorous pathways to teacher certification.
That effort was to allow choices that would be alternatives to the Praxis test that tends to weed out people of color.
This legislation goes further – creating policy that will keep teachers once we encourage them into the profession.
All first, second-, and third-year teachers will be assigned a mentor in their building and the school must create a cohort of these teachers – overseen by a veteran educator – that will meet to share solutions to the challenges encountered in the first years of teaching.
I have a personal stake here.
I had a tough first year all those years ago and a few veteran teachers organically decided to step up and help me navigate that year.
Had they not been willing to give their free time, I may not have survived that year and gone on to teach for 35 years.
This bill adds structure, as well as compensation for participating mentors and cohort guides.
Teachers were generous with me then and generally teachers now would gladly volunteer, but we ask them to do so much and they need to be valued and given professional development time or money for their good advice and help.
