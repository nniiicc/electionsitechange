Contact Select Page 🚨 The Academy of Natural Sciences Closure: Coincidence, Crisis — or Political Opportunity for Shapiro?
FOLLOW THE 2026 TIMELINE.
I'm not claiming that Governor Josh Shapiro caused the Academy of Natural Sciences' financial problems.
I'm not claiming the museum's closure was fabricated.
And I'm not claiming we currently have proof that the Governor's office staged the announcement for political purposes.
But the chronology raises legitimate questions about timing, communications, political involvement, and what happened behind the scenes.
So let's follow the timeline.
APRIL 2026 Public reporting reveals that Drexel University and the Academy of Natural Sciences are considering a major restructuring of their relationship.
And this is important: Governor Josh Shapiro and Philadelphia Mayor Cherelle Parker are already involved in the discussions.
Shapiro didn't suddenly discover this on September 1.
His involvement predates the public closure announcement by months.
THEN COMES DAVID COHEN David L.
Cohen—former U.S. ambassador to Canada, former Comcast executive, and one of Philadelphia's most prominent civic figures—becomes involved in trying to find a solution.
According to Cohen, Governor Shapiro personally asked him to get involved.
Drexel's own September statement confirms that Shapiro designated Cohen to serve as a third-party facilitator.
So we aren't talking about a governor who simply issued a supportive statement after the fact.
The Governor had a designated intermediary working directly with the parties to the dispute.
Cohen has described holding dozens of meetings over the course of months.
And the stated objective was to preserve the Academy's research, education, and public-engagement missions.
So far, that sounds like a legitimate attempt by a governor to help save an important Philadelphia institution.
Then the political landscape gets more complicated.
THE DATA-CENTER CONTROVERSY Shapiro had previously embraced Amazon's enormous Pennsylvania data-center investment as a major economic-development victory.
But by 2026, opposition to data centers had become a significant public and political issue.
His administration had also been developing its policy framework for data centers.
Then came August 18 .
Shapiro issued an executive order dramatically changing Pennsylvania's approach to data centers.
The order removed AI data centers from the Fast Track permitting process, imposed new environmental and community requirements, increased local involvement, and prohibited nondisclosure agreements involving data-center projects.
The Governor said his position had evolved after listening to Pennsylvania residents concerned about energy costs, environmental impacts, and their communities.
That was a major public policy shift.
THEN CAME THE MEASLES CONTROVERSY On August 25 , Pennsylvania health officials announced two measles-associated deaths as the state was dealing with a measles outbreak.
The announcement quickly became the subject of a public dispute involving Governor Shapiro and U.S.
Health Secretary Robert F.
Kennedy Jr.
Kennedy questioned the classification of the deaths and criticized Pennsylvania's handling of the information.
Shapiro defended the state's reporting and accused Kennedy of politicizing a public-health matter.
The circumstances surrounding at least one of the deaths were subsequently disputed by the county coroner, and the federal reporting was later revised.
The important point here is not to decide that dispute in this post.
The important point is that, in the final week of August, Governor Shapiro was involved in another highly public controversy involving government information, public health, and competing accounts of what had actually happened.
And then came September.
AND THEN — SEPTEMBER 1 Drexel announces that the Academy's historic Parkway museum will close September 30.
Thirty days.
The public is shocked.
But there's another remarkable element to the announcement.
At the same time the public is learning that the museum is closing, we're learning that Shapiro has been working behind the scenes for months to preserve the Academy's larger mission.
And the Governor's designated intermediary is already in position.
Cohen.
The Mayor is involved.
Drexel is involved.
The Academy is involved.
And on September 2, Philadelphia government formally enters the transition effort, with Deputy Mayor Patricia "Trish" Wellenbach becoming involved as a senior adviser.
Drexel has subsequently described the Governor, Mayor Parker, and Cohen as continuing partners in the effort to preserve the Academy's future.
Now stop and look at that sequence.
A genuine institutional crisis. ↓ Governor Shapiro becomes directly involved months before the public closure. ↓ Shapiro designates a prominent Philadelphia civic figure to facilitate a solution. ↓ Shapiro faces increasing public and political pressure over the data-center industry. ↓ August 18: Shapiro makes a major policy pivot on data centers. ↓ August 25: Pennsylvania announces two measles-associated deaths, followed by a public dispute over the reporting and classification of those deaths. ↓ September 1: The Academy announces that its museum will close. ↓ At the same time, the public learns about Shapiro's months-long effort to preserve the Academy's larger mission. ↓ September 2: Philadelphia government formally enters the transition effort.
Is all of that connected?
We don't know.
And that's exactly the point.
HERE'S THE QUESTION I WANT ANSWERED Was the September 1 announcement simply the unavoidable culmination of a months-long financial and institutional crisis?
Or did the political circumstances surrounding Governor Shapiro influence the timing, messaging, or presentation of the announcement?
Because there is a potentially very different political narrative available to the Governor.
Only weeks earlier, Shapiro was dealing with major controversy surrounding data centers and a public dispute over the state's handling of measles-associated deaths.
Now the story becomes: Governor Shapiro is fighting to preserve science.
Governor Shapiro is protecting Philadelphia's history.
Governor Shapiro is working with the Mayor.
Governor Shapiro has brought in David Cohen to broker a solution.
Governor Shapiro is standing up for a beloved scientific institution.
That is a favorable political narrative.
Again, that does not prove the Academy announcement was politically staged.
But it makes the timing and communications worth examining.
AND THERE IS ANOTHER QUESTION Why was the public given only 30 days between the announcement and the museum closure?
When was September 1 actually selected?
Who selected it?
When did the Governor's office know?
When did the Mayor's office know?
When did Cohen know?
When did Drexel know?
When did Academy employees know?
And most importantly: What did the Governor's communications staff know—and when did they know it?
Did anyone discuss the political implications of the announcement?
Did anyone discuss how Shapiro's involvement should be presented?
Did anyone discuss the timing in relation to the data-center controversy?
Did anyone discuss whether the Academy could become a positive story for the Governor?
Or was September 1 simply the date dictated by operational and financial circumstances?
THAT IS THE INVESTIGATION We're not asking people to believe a conspiracy theory.
We're asking for the records.
Emails.
Calendars.
Meeting invitations.
Text messages.
Draft press releases.
Communications plans.
Internal talking points.
Financial analyses.
Funding proposals.
And especially the first record in which someone identifies September 1 as the date for the public announcement.
If the records show that the date was selected for completely legitimate operational reasons, excellent.
That would answer an important question.
If the records show that the Governor's political or communications operation became involved in shaping the timing or narrative, that would tell us something very different.
And if there is a document connecting the Academy announcement to a political communications strategy?
Then we're no longer talking about coincidence.
We're talking about evidence.
Until then, I'm not asking anyone to reach a conclusion.
I'm asking everyone to follow the timeline.
Follow the records.
Follow the communications.
Follow the money.
And let the evidence—not politics, speculation, or anybody's preferred narrative—tell us what really happened.
