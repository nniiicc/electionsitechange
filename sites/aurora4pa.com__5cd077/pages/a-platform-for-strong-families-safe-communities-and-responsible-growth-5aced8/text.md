Contact Select Page A Platform for Strong Families, Safe Communities, and Responsible Growth I believe government works best when it protects vulnerable people, removes barriers to opportunity, and spends taxpayer dollars preventing problems instead of paying for them after they become crises.
I support economic growth, private enterprise, and technological innovation, but I don't believe growth should come at the expense of families, neighborhoods, or the people who built our communities.
Markets create opportunity, but they also need guardrails when they leave seniors, children, veterans, or working families behind.
Strong Families Housing is one example.
We need more homes, but we also need to protect the people already living in them.
Seniors shouldn't spend a lifetime paying off a manufactured home only to face displacement when large investment firms buy the land beneath them and dramatically raise lot rents.
Residents deserve a fair opportunity to purchase their communities, and affordable housing should remain affordable for the people who depend on it.
I believe our veterans deserve more than our gratitude.
They deserve access to housing, healthcare, and the opportunity to continue working without unnecessary financial penalties.
Seniors who choose to remain active in the workforce shouldn't be punished for doing so, and Social Security should reflect the economic realities facing today's retirees.
One of my strongest priorities is children.
Too often, government doesn't intervene until a child is already in foster care, the juvenile justice system, or a psychiatric emergency.
I believe we can do better by identifying struggling children and families earlier and connecting them with schools, healthcare providers, behavioral health professionals, and community organizations before problems become lifelong crises.
That is the vision behind the Youth Support and Opportunity Network (YSON): a coordinated, prevention-focused system that strengthens families rather than simply reacting after they fall apart.
Safe Communities Public safety is also strongest when prevention and accountability work together.
Law enforcement, behavioral health professionals, educators, and community organizations each have a role.
Supporting one should never mean ignoring the value of the others.
Responsible Growth I also believe our communities deserve a real voice in how they grow.
I am not opposed to artificial intelligence or new technology.
Innovation has created tremendous opportunities and will continue to shape our future.
But the rapid expansion of enormous data centers into established communities raises legitimate concerns about land use, energy demand, water resources, infrastructure, and quality of life.
Local residents shouldn't feel that decisions affecting their neighborhoods are being made without them.
If corporations benefit from these projects, they should also bear the costs of the infrastructure and public services they require.
Growth should happen with communities, not to them.
Throughout all of these issues, one principle remains consistent: government should be transparent, accountable, and measured by outcomes—not by how much money it spends or how many programs it creates.
Success should be measured by whether more children are safe, more seniors remain in their homes, more veterans have opportunities, housing becomes more attainable, and communities retain a meaningful voice over their future.
At its core, this isn't a platform built around ideology.
It's built around stewardship.
I believe in protecting what is worth preserving, improving what isn't working, and making decisions that leave our communities stronger for the next generation than they are today.
Here's a specific outline: Guiding Principle Government should protect the vulnerable, expand opportunity, respect local communities, and invest in prevention rather than pay for avoidable crises, all while protecting limited government, liberty, and property rights.
1.
Protect Seniors and Veterans Modernize Social Security so seniors are not penalized for continuing to work after reaching retirement age.
Expand property tax and housing relief for seniors living on fixed incomes.
Increase affordable housing options for veterans.
Help seniors age in place with dignity and independence.
Support caregivers and strengthen access to healthcare and community services.
Goal: Honor those who built and defended our country by ensuring they can live with security and dignity.
2.
Preserve Affordable Manufactured Housing Give residents the first opportunity to purchase manufactured home communities when they are sold.
Protect residents from excessive or predatory lot rent increases.
Encourage resident-owned communities, nonprofit ownership, and community land trusts.
Support construction of new manufactured housing while protecting existing communities.
Increase transparency when investment firms acquire manufactured home parks.
Goal: Preserve one of America's largest sources of naturally affordable homeownership.
3.
Increase Housing Supply Responsibly Reduce unnecessary permitting delays.
Encourage mixed-income housing near existing infrastructure.
Support adaptive reuse of vacant commercial properties.
Incentivize affordable housing without sacrificing local character.
Balance responsible development with community planning.
Goal: Make housing more attainable while respecting the communities where people already live.
4.
Youth Support and Opportunity Network (YSON) Develop a statewide early intervention network focused on children and families before crises occur.
The YSON model would include: Early identification of at-risk youth.
School-based behavioral health partnerships.
Mobile crisis response teams.
Family stabilization services.
Housing, food, transportation, and employment supports.
Coordination between schools, healthcare, behavioral health providers, law enforcement, and community organizations.
Strong privacy protections and outcome-based accountability.
Goals: Reduce foster care placements.
Reduce youth homelessness.
Reduce juvenile justice involvement.
Reduce human trafficking risk.
Improve school attendance and graduation.
Strengthen families before intervention becomes necessary.
5.
Fiscal Responsibility Through Prevention Invest in solutions that reduce long-term public costs.
Examples include: Early behavioral health treatment instead of repeated hospitalization.
Family support instead of foster care whenever safely possible.
Stable housing instead of chronic homelessness.
Prevention programs instead of repeated crisis intervention.
Goal: Spend taxpayer dollars earlier and smarter, instead of later and more expensively.
6.
Public Safety Through Prevention and Partnership Support law enforcement while recognizing that lasting public safety requires multiple partners.
Invest in: Community policing.
Behavioral health crisis teams.
Victim services.
Human trafficking prevention.
School safety partnerships.
Evidence-based violence prevention.
Goal: Build safer communities through accountability, prevention, and collaboration.
7.
Accountable Government Measure programs by outcomes, rather than dollars spent.
Increase transparency and public reporting.
Reduce duplication between agencies.
Encourage bipartisan problem-solving.
Make government easier for citizens to navigate.
Goal: Restore trust by making government more effective and accountable.
8.
Responsible Technology and Community Protection Support innovation while ensuring that communities have a meaningful voice in how large-scale technology projects are developed.
Encourage artificial intelligence and technological innovation that benefits society.
Support responsible economic development without sacrificing neighborhoods, farmland, or natural resources.
Require meaningful public input before approving large-scale data centers.
Prioritize data centers in appropriate industrial or commercial locations rather than established residential communities whenever feasible.
Require developers to address impacts on electric infrastructure, water resources, transportation, emergency services, and the environment.
Ensure large corporations pay a fair share of infrastructure costs their projects create, rather than shifting those costs to taxpayers.
Increase transparency in local approval processes and long-term community impact assessments.
Goal: Embrace innovation while ensuring that growth happens with communities—not to them.
The Common Thread Every proposal in this platform follows the same philosophy: Protect seniors from displacement.
Honor veterans with opportunity.
Keep children out of crisis through early intervention.
Preserve affordable homeownership.
Encourage responsible economic growth.
Hold government accountable.
Ensure that large corporations—including investment firms and technology companies—operate as responsible partners in our communities.
Core Philosophy Government should solve problems before they become crises, protect the people who built our communities, and ensure that economic progress strengthens—not weakens—the places we call home.
My community stewardship platform promotes the idea that people, families, and communities should remain at the center of public policy , with economic growth, innovation, and government all serving that broader purpose rather than becoming ends in themselves.
Smart policy can achieve these goals while protecting limited government, liberty, and property rights.
