Nick Morris believes our district can be a great place for all to live, work and raise a family setting a standard in stability for others.
Nick's priority issues:
Cost of Living
A job should provide the opportunity to build a good life.
But when housing, utilities, childcare, groceries, healthcare, and other necessities keep getting more expensive, even families doing everything right can struggle to get ahead.
We are working harder the rich are getting richer our towns are suffering ...And the other 98% of us cannot save for the future.
Nick believes Ohio’s economy should work for the people who work for it.
- Make it easier to afford a place to live.
Nick will support efforts to increase Ohio’s housing supply and give communities more tools to encourage the construction of housing that working families, young adults, and seniors can afford.
- Stand up for consumers.
Nick will fight hidden fees, predatory practices, and the growing “annoyance economy” that nickel-and-dimes families for necessities and everyday services.
- Invest in opportunity here at home.
Nick will support small businesses, workforce education, skilled trades, and pathways that allow Ohioans to learn here, work here, live here, and build their futures here.
Education
Schools are the heart of our towns and our family lives.
Their benefit goes far beyond students sitting in classrooms today.
They strengthen neighborhoods, prepare our future workforce, attract employers, support property values, and create opportunities that can change a person’s life trajectory.
Nick believes public dollars are for public education ...
- Create stability for local schools and taxpayers.
Nick will fight for the state to meet its constitutional responsibility to fund public education, so districts aren’t forced to continually return to local property taxpayers to maintain essential programs and services.
- Fair investment in public schools.
Big cities have charter and private schools, which are pulling our state’s money away from public schools.
- Demand accountability for public dollars.
If the state spends taxpayer money on education, taxpayers deserve to know where that money goes and whether it is delivering results.
That includes private and charter schools.
- Create more pathways to a successful future.
College isn’t the only route to success.
Nick will support affordable pathways to college, skilled trades, workforce credentials, apprenticeships, and entrepreneurship so students can pursue the future that’s right for them.
Healthcare
Where you live, what you earn, or what kind of job you have shouldn’t determine whether you can see a doctor or afford the medicine you need.
Healthcare costs affect everything from a family’s monthly budget to whether someone can remain healthy enough to work and care for the people they love.
Nick believes every Ohioan deserves access to affordable, quality healthcare, and that no family should face financial hardship simply because someone gets sick.
- Protect access to care.
Nick will oppose efforts that make it harder for Ohioans to access healthcare and support policies that strengthen access to doctors, hospitals, mental health services, and other providers in our communities.
- Lower the costs families actually pay.
Nick will support greater transparency and accountability around healthcare costs and look for ways Ohio can reduce unnecessary fees and out-of-pocket expenses.
- Strengthen our healthcare workforce.
Ohio needs enough nurses, physicians, behavioral health professionals, and other providers to serve our communities.
Nick will support education and workforce pathways that help Ohio train — and keep — healthcare professionals.
- Take steps towards ending unethical practices.
Nick will support legislation that requires insurance companies to be transparent about costs up front, puts the decision-making back in the hands of service providers, and streamlines care.
Small Towns
Ohio’s small towns and townships should be able to welcome new investment without putting their taxpayers or their future at risk.
But when a small community is negotiating with a massive corporation or developer, the playing field isn’t always level.
A part-time township trustee shouldn’t be expected to have the same resources, expertise, and negotiating power as a billion-dollar company.
Nick believes the state’s job isn’t to make decisions for local communities, it’s to make sure they have the expertise, information, and protections they need to make good decisions for themselves.
- Keep local decisions local.
The state should provide expertise and establish reasonable protections, not dictate what development looks like in every community.
The people who live in a community should have a meaningful voice in decisions that affect their land, infrastructure, utilities, schools, and quality of life.
- Give small communities a seat at an informed negotiating table.
Large cities often have experienced economic development staff, attorneys, planners, and consultants to evaluate major projects.
Small towns and townships deserve access to that same level of expertise.
Nick supports a state role that connects local officials with independent experts who can help them understand proposals, negotiate agreements, and protect their taxpayers.
- Protect taxpayers when developers speculate.
Companies have the right to take risks on new projects, including data centers, but they shouldn’t take those risks with a community’s money.
For large developments that require significant public investment or incentives, Nick supports financial safeguards, including performance bonds when appropriate, so taxpayers aren’t left paying for infrastructure or other costs if a project stalls or is abandoned halfway through.
- Make sure development delivers for the community.
Economic development incentives should come with transparency, accountability, and enforceable commitments.
If a company receives public support, communities deserve to know what they’re getting in return, whether that’s jobs, infrastructure investment, tax revenue, or other measurable benefits.
Property Taxes
For too many Ohioans, rising property taxes are making it harder to afford the homes and communities they’ve invested in for years.
Homeowners shouldn’t have to worry that the next property reappraisal will make staying in their home unaffordable.
Property taxes are more than a bill; they are an investment in the common good.
They support the schools, police and fire departments, libraries, roads, and services that make our communities places where people want to live.
The challenge is making sure that responsibility is shared fairly and that taxpayers can see a real public benefit from the dollars they contribute.
Nick believes Ohio needs a fairer approach to property taxes — one that keeps people in their homes, asks the state to carry its fair share, and protects the community investments our tax dollars make possible.
- Provide meaningful relief for homeowners.
Nick supports expanding and strengthening the Homestead Exemption to provide targeted relief for seniors, working families, and longtime homeowners who are being squeezed by rising property values and taxes.
- Make the state pay its fair share.
Ohio cannot keep shifting the cost of essential services — especially public education — onto local property taxpayers.
Nick will fight for a stronger state investment so communities aren’t forced to choose between raising local taxes and cutting essential services.
- Demand accountability for tax breaks.
Tax abatements shouldn’t amount to giveaways for special interests while homeowners and small businesses are left to pick up the tab.
When public dollars are used to incentivize private development, Nick believes there should be transparency, measurable community benefit, and accountability for delivering what was promised.
- Protect the common good.
Property-tax reform shouldn’t come at the expense of schools, police, fire departments, libraries, or other services residents depend on.
Nick will pursue reforms that lower the burden on homeowners while preserving the investments that make our communities strong.
