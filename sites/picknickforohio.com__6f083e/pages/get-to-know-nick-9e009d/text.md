Biography
About Nick Morris
Born and raised in Stark County, Nick has served the community as a high school and college educator as well as a county employee for over a decade leading education, health and environmental partnerships.
He is the Director of Community Learning and Civic Leadership at Walsh University.
Nick volunteers with Louisville HS Swim Boosters and the Ohio Foundation Quarter Horse Association.
Nick lives on a small farm near Louisville with his wife Sarah, two children Lauren and Luke, two dogs and a variety of 4-H animals.
He spends most of his free time supporting his kids’ activities.
Current occupation
Walsh University: Director of Community Learning and Leadership
Nick directs the Brother Francis Blouin Scholars Program, a mission-based service and scholarship program based in Catholic Social Teaching.
The program focuses on intersections of poverty and quality of life Issues such as education, health and housing.
Outcomes of this work focus on the delivering capacity-building service to community partners while preparing students for civic leadership upon graduation.
Additionally, he facilitates community-engaged learning and civic leadership programming across the campus building programs on campus that support the development of civil discourse among diverse student perspectives.
He has a dream of creating a pipeline for students into nonprofit board leadership opportunities.

Values statement:
Work hard.
Be real.

Hard Work
Show up.
Do the work.
Get something done.
Nick believes public service is exactly that: service.
He was raised to work hard, come prepared, listen carefully, and follow through.
Whether he’s preparing students to become community leaders, working alongside local organizations, or serving his community, Nick believes progress comes from people who are willing to check their ego at the door and do the work.
The people of the 48th District deserve a representative who works as hard as they do; someone focused less on political games and more on delivering results.
Authenticity
Be who you are.
Say what you mean.
Listen when you disagree.
Nick isn’t running to become a politician; this isn’t a stepping stone to another office.
He’s stepping up to run because he believes that every community in the district has so much to offer.
He’s a husband, father, son, educator, neighbor, and community volunteer.
All of those experiences have shaped how he sees the world.
He believes you can stand firm in what you believe while listening to and learning from those with a different perspective.
Nick will tell you where he stands, listen when you tell him where you stand, and look for the common ground that allows us to move forward.
He will find those whose voices aren’t being heard and let them know that there is a seat at the table with their name on it.
No performance.
No political theater.
Just a reasonable, rational approach to getting things done.
Faith in People
People are capable of more when we give them the opportunity to succeed.
Nick has spent his career helping people recognize their potential and preparing students to become leaders in their communities.
That experience has reinforced something he deeply believes: our greatest resource isn’t a political party, a government program, or an institution.
It’s people.
Government doesn’t need to have all the answers.
Elected officials can lead by bringing subject matter experts and the people closest to the challenges to the table, listening to them, and working together toward solutions.
Nick believes our future should be driven by hope, not fear.
By the belief that we can make a difference and build something better.
That means having the courage to reimagine what’s possible while honoring the traditions, hard work, and legacy that made our communities what they are today.
That’s the kind of government Nick believes in, and the kind of representative he intends to be.
