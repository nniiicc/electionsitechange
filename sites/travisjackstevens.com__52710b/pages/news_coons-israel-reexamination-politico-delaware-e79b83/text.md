T Travis Jack Stevens • Thursday, October 8, 2026 • 6 min read News · Thursday, October 8, 2026 · Travis Jack Stevens, write-in candidate for U.S.
Senate, Delaware · 26 days to November 3 On Tuesday, POLITICO reported that Delaware’s senior senator, Chris Coons, wants a “thorough reexamination” of the U.S.-Israeli relationship and is open to sanctioning Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu if his government stays on its current path.
POLITICO describes Coons as a longtime supporter of Israel who could chair the Senate Foreign Relations Committee if control of the Senate changes in November.
That is a real shift, and I want to give it its due.
I also want to ask the questions this campaign is built on: What changed?
Why now?
And will the words become votes?
What he told POLITICO In an interview with POLITICO’s Daniella Cheslow, Coons said he is open to sanctioning two Israeli ministers, National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, and Netanyahu himself “if the Netanyahu government continues on its current path.” In his words: “that’s an indication of a fairly sharp break from where I would have been two or three years ago.” POLITICO put the arms question to him directly, noting that he has voted against resolutions to block sales of bombs or bulldozers to Israel.
His answer: “I felt as if when we were in the middle of regular attacks on Israel by Iran and its proxies was not the time to cut off military aid to the state of Israel.
But I think going forward, we need to have a thorough reexamination of our relationship.” He also said, “Look, Netanyahu himself has proposed a gradual phaseout of U.S. military aid funded by the United States,” and, “My goal is not to have a one-sided conversation where all I do is criticize Israel and Israelis.” POLITICO reports that after the Hamas attacks of October 7, 2023, Coons traveled to Tel Aviv to express his solidarity.
On September 23 of this year, he introduced a bill, with Senators Warren, Wyden, and Gallego, to sanction companies and people who help build Israeli settlements in the E1 area of the West Bank.
His own press release says “the United States must send a strong message that this behavior is unacceptable.” The record so far Here is what the Senate’s own roll calls show.
April 23, 2024: Coons voted yes as the Senate passed the $95 billion aid package for Ukraine, Israel, and Taiwan (H.R.
815), 79 to 18.
The Associated Press reported that it included $26 billion in wartime assistance to Israel and humanitarian relief to citizens of Gaza.
November 20, 2024: On three motions to force votes on resolutions disapproving arms sales and exports to Israel (S.J.Res.
111, 113, and 115), Coons voted no each time.
April 3, 2025: He voted no on S.J.Res.

He did not vote on S.J.Res.
33.
July 30, 2025: He voted no on S.J.Res.

April 15, 2026: He voted no on S.J.Res.

Those motions drew 40 and 36 votes, more than any earlier attempt on this list.
September 29, 2026: Coons voted yes on a motion to discharge S.Res.
852, a resolution requesting information on Israel’s human rights practices under section 502B(c) of the Foreign Assistance Act.
It failed, 47 to 51.
That last vote is a step, and I’ll say so plainly.
But asking for a report is not the same as voting to stop a shipment.
On the nine roll calls above on resolutions disapproving arms sales and exports to Israel, the record shows eight no votes and one missed vote.
The money behind the votes OpenSecrets, the research group that tracks money in U.S. politics, lists $258,166 in contributions to Coons from its pro-Israel category between 1990 and 2024, and $138,560 from the defense sector over the same period.
Those totals count PACs and individuals giving $200 or more, and OpenSecrets notes that the money comes from employees or PACs affiliated with each industry, “not from the companies themselves.” Many current and former senators appear above him on both lists.
I am not telling you those dollars explain any vote.
I don’t know that, and I won’t pretend to.
I am telling you the money and the votes belong side by side, in public, where Delaware can see both.
That is what accountability looks like.
Where I stand I spent years as a travel nurse, including months on a COVID unit.
I know what it takes to care for a broken body, and I know the people who sign off on the bombs rarely see that room.
POLITICO, in its interview, cited Gaza health authorities saying more than 74,000 Palestinians have died.
Every one of them was someone’s family.
When Congress sends weapons, Congress owns a share of what happens next.
I oppose forever wars.
I believe every weapons sale should meet the human rights standards already written into our own laws, with a recorded vote, out in the open.
And I believe the people who decide those votes should be funded by the people they represent, not by industries with a financial stake in what those votes decide.
My campaign is people-funded.
No Super PAC.
If the reexamination is real, Delaware will know by one test: the next time a resolution to stop a weapons sale comes to the Senate floor.
Words in an interview are a start.
Votes are the record.
As your Senator, I will vote my conscience on every war and every weapons sale, in the open.
