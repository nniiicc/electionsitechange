How to Vote → SPECIAL REPORT: They Are Tearing Down the Court Back to News & Updates Letter SPECIAL REPORT: They Are Tearing Down the Court T Travis Jack Stevens • Sunday, October 11, 2026 • 4 min read Dear Delaware, On Friday, Navi Pillay won the Nobel Peace Prize "for her efforts to promote peace and international law." She defended anti-apartheid activists in South Africa.
She served as a judge on the Rwanda genocide tribunal and on the International Criminal Court, and she was the UN's top human rights official.
That night, at a rally in Syracuse, President Trump called her "a person I never heard of" and "a mystery person." On Truth Social, he wrote that she "very obviously, hates Israel." He also reshared a post by his former impeachment lawyer, David Schoen, calling her "an unabashed hater of America and Israel." Hours after the prize was announced, the administration sanctioned the International Criminal Court itself.
Secretary of State Marco Rubio said, "Either the ICC will end its threats, or we will end the ICC," and vowed to take it apart "brick by brick." His statement didn't mention Pillay.
This didn't start Friday.
In February 2025, President Trump signed Executive Order 14203 sanctioning the court.
The order calls the court's arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his former defense minister "baseless." The State Department describes its sanctions as a response to the court's "illegitimate actions targeting the United States and Israel." Since then, the U.S. has sanctioned the court's then-prosecutor, both deputy prosecutors, nine of its eighteen judges, and the UN special rapporteur Francesca Albanese.
On Friday, Netanyahu praised the new sanctions.
The United Kingdom, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and Denmark issued a joint statement opposing them.
Now set that beside this war.
In September, a UN fact-finding mission found "reasonable grounds to believe" the United States committed the war crime of an indiscriminate attack in Iran.
Its findings include a strike on a primary school in Minab, where local authorities say 156 people were killed, 120 of them children.
That is a finding by investigators, not a court verdict, and it's made against the United States as a country, not against any individual.
The ICC has no case on the Iran war, because neither the U.S. nor Iran is a member of the court.
Separately, last year The Washington Post reported, citing two people with direct knowledge, that Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth gave a spoken directive before a September 2025 Caribbean boat strike: "The order was to kill everybody." Two survivors were killed in a follow-up strike.
Hegseth called the report "fake news." The admiral in charge told lawmakers there was no such order.
The chairs of both Armed Services committees closed their inquiries, saying they found no war crime.
A group of former military lawyers said killing shipwrecked survivors means "war crimes, murder, or both." Here is where I'm telling you my opinion, not a verdict.
No court has charged anyone, and I won't pretend one has.
I believe President Trump and Secretary Hegseth have committed war crimes in this war of choice.
I believe the campaign to dismantle the International Criminal Court is meant to shield Netanyahu, and to shield themselves, from ever having to answer for it.
You don't promise to take a court apart brick by brick unless you're worried about who it might one day hold accountable.
I spent years as a travel ICU nurse.
When something went wrong on a unit, the answer was never to fire the people who reviewed it.
The answer was to let the review happen.
A Senator from Delaware should defend the institutions that hold the powerful to account, no matter which country the powerful come from.
I will.
