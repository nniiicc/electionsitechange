How to Vote → Senate Stalls Ratepayer Bill Meant to Keep Data Centers Off Your Power Bill Back to News & Updates News Senate Stalls Ratepayer Bill Meant to Keep Data Centers Off Your Power Bill T Travis Jack Stevens • Thursday, October 1, 2026 • 2 min read Thursday, October 1, 2026 · 33 days to November 3 Yesterday the Ratepayer Protection Act fell short on a 57-43 cloture vote, three short of the 60 needed.
Cloture on the motion to proceed failed.
Sixty votes were required.
The House-passed bill stays stalled.
The measure was H.R.
9340, Senate Roll Call 254, at 12:51 p.m.
It would amend the Public Utility Regulatory Policies Act of 1978 to set a federal standard so large-load customers — including AI data centers — cover the full, incremental cost of grid upgrades needed to serve them, with financial assurances before those upgrades are built.
The House had passed the bill 417 to 3 on September 16.
Chris Coons and Lisa Blunt Rochester both voted no.
The bill's reach is narrower than the title.
It would require states to consider adopting that standard.
It would not force every state to adopt it, and it would not automatically change a Delaware electric bill.
Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called the measure toothless and said the chamber should make data centers cover their own costs by law, not by suggestion.
Roll Call reported the same limit: start considering within a year, finish within two — not a mandate to adopt.
No public Delaware dollar figure came with yesterday's cloture vote.
This short will not invent one.
The longer letter is the place for the kitchen-table standard: families should not subsidize AI data-center upgrades, and "consider" is not the same as "require." People-funded.
