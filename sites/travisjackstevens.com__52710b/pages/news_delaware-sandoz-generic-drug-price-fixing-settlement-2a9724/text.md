Did You Pay Too Much?
T Travis Jack Stevens • Saturday, October 10, 2026 • 6 min read News · Saturday, October 10, 2026 · Travis Jack Stevens, write-in candidate for U.S.
Senate, Delaware If you have ever picked up a cheap generic prescription and thought, at least this one won't hurt, read this first.
On Thursday, Delaware Attorney General Kathy Jennings announced that Delaware, as part of what Delaware describes as a coalition of 43 states and territories, is asking a federal court to approve a $400 million settlement with the generic drug manufacturers Sandoz Inc. and Fougera Pharmaceuticals.
The states allege the companies were part of a widespread, long-running conspiracy to artificially inflate prices, reduce competition and restrain trade on numerous generic prescription drugs.
Generic drugs are supposed to be the affordable option.
That is the whole promise.
The states say that promise was gamed.
What the states say happened The settlement resolves allegations.
It is not a verdict, and a judge has not yet approved it.
Sandoz has said the settlement contains no admission of wrongdoing.
Everything below about the conduct is what the states allege in their lawsuits, as described in the New York Attorney General's announcement of the same settlement.
The lawsuits allege that generic drug companies fixed prices, avoided competing with each other and rigged bids on more than 100 different generic drugs.
According to the New York Attorney General, executives at the companies met at industry dinners, lunches, cocktail parties and golf outings and stayed in touch by phone, email and text.
The complaints say they used phrases like "fair share," "playing nice in the sandbox," and "responsible competitor" to describe how they discouraged competition.
The states say the drugs span tablets, capsules, creams and ointments, and treat everything from infections to diabetes, cancer, epilepsy, multiple sclerosis, HIV and ADHD.
In some instances, the states say, the coordinated price increases were over 1,000 percent.
What the settlement would do Under the deal, Sandoz would pay $400 million over seven years, according to the company.
The Delaware Attorney General says that, counting earlier settlements Sandoz reached with other states, the company will pay about $469 million to resolve the claims of state enforcers.
The settlement also covers Sandoz's international affiliates, which the states accused of taking part in the alleged conduct and of fraudulently transferring assets to avoid liability.
Those are allegations too.
Sandoz also agreed to internal reforms: an antitrust compliance program with annual training for sales and management staff, and a Chief Compliance Officer.
Earlier settlements with Glenmark, Lannett, Bausch, Apotex and Heritage, and Heritage's parent company Emcure, total $96.5 million, according to Delaware's announcement.
The larger lawsuits against other companies are still going.
What it means for you If you bought a generic prescription drug between May 2009 and December 2019, you may be eligible for compensation.
The Attorney General says to check your eligibility at AGGenericDrugs.com , by phone at 1-866-290-0182, or by email at [email protected] .
Attorney General Jennings put it plainly: "If you paid an unfair price, you deserve your money back, full stop." The New York Attorney General's office, which is part of the same coalition, lists a claim deadline of March 8, 2027.
Pharmacy printouts, insurance statements and old receipts may help, though the claims administrator sets the documentation rules.
I could not find an announced payment amount per person, and I am not going to guess one.
The court still has to approve the deal.
Where I stand I was a travel nurse, including months on a COVID unit.
In a hospital, you do not get to tell a patient "that medication got expensive, so skip it." You see it later: the person who rationed pills, the person who stretched a prescription, the person who came in sicker because the bill came before the refill.
Nobody in that room can negotiate with a drug company.
I applaud Attorney General Jennings and the 42 other states and territories for pressing this.
A coalition that large acting together is what accountability looks like.
But I want us to be honest about the scale of what is allegedly at stake.
A $400 million settlement spread across dozens of states, for conduct the states say lasted a decade, over drugs millions of people take every day, is not the same as a price that never got fixed.
Money back years later helps, and it still does not make anyone whole for the pill someone skipped to make rent.
Here is what I believe: Generic drugs must be priced by competition, not by a quiet agreement over dinner.
Price-fixing in medicine should carry consequences big enough that no executive treats a settlement as a cost of doing business.
Health care is a right, and a nation of equal opportunity starts with people being able to afford what keeps them alive.
That is why I support universal health care.
The people who decide what a medicine costs should answer to the patient who has to pay for it, not only to shareholders.
If I am your Senator, I will push for strong antitrust enforcement in the drug supply chain and for transparency on what generic drugs actually cost to make and sell.
I will fight for patients over the companies that profit from their pain.
If you took a generic prescription in those years, check AGGenericDrugs.com and confirm the deadline there; New York's Attorney General lists March 8, 2027.
It is your money.
