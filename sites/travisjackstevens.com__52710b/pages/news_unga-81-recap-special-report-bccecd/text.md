Delaware Pays at the Pump. • Sunday, September 27, 2026 • 5 min read World leaders spent this week at the United Nations General Assembly in New York.
I spent years as a travel ICU nurse, and I watched the speeches the way I used to watch a monitor.
Tune out the noise and find out what is happening to the patient.
Here, the patient is the working family, and the vital signs are bad.
Secretary-General Antonio Guterres gave his final address and named four "tests of power": war, inequality, climate and AI.
He warned, "Life-and-death decisions must never be surrendered to machines," and called for independent oversight of AI.
He also announced that the UN had reached full gender parity in its senior leadership for the first time in its history.
That is proof that power structures change when someone decides they should.
Then President Trump took the podium.
He asked whether he should make a deal with Iran "or do I annihilate the Islamic Republic," and said he believed a deal would come right after November's elections.
On AI, he offered branding in place of guardrails: "Welcome to the new world of Super Intelligence - SI." When he called Cuba "an absolutely failed state," the Cuban delegation walked out .
The next day, Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian answered him: "The US president described us as terrorists.
We have been the victims of terrorism." He said Iran was ready to negotiate but would not accept "the language of force." US and Iranian officials exchanged messages through mediators, but no agreement appeared imminent .
When Prime Minister Netanyahu walked to the rostrum, scores of delegates walked out .
He called them "moral cowards." Outside, hundreds blocked traffic chanting "Let Gaza live," and organizers estimated more than 100 arrests.
Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas could not attend at all.
The US denied him a visa, so the Assembly voted 152 to 3 to let him speak by video.
At the end of the week, Reuters summed it up, as reported by Al-Monitor: "no breakthroughs on the world's most intractable conflicts." Delaware pays for that failure.
The record diesel prices of this war are tied to disruption in the Strait of Hormuz sparked by the U.S.-Israeli attack on Iran in February .
Today, AAA puts Delaware diesel at $6.4466 , the highest average ever recorded here, and regular at $4.3519, against $3.0619 #ago.
Diesel moves our food and our freight.
Families who heat with oil could see winter bills more than 31% higher .
This is how the forever-war economy works.
The threats are made at a podium, the bill arrives at a Delaware gas pump, and the people who profit never have to pay it.
I am running as a write-in candidate funded by people, not a Super PAC, because nobody who takes that money will tell you the truth about it.
I want diplomacy instead of endless war, and I want our tax dollars spent on healthcare, good jobs, housing and a democracy that answers to voters.
I will end where the week should have ended.
Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten opened his speech with Anne Frank, who wrote, "How wonderful it is that nobody need wait a single moment before starting to improve the world." He closed with his own words: "we have everything we need to change the world for the better and we don't have to wait another minute." He is right, and in Delaware we don't have to wait either.
