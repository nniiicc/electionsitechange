Why are you running for governor?
I didn't see a candidate on the ballot who gave me confidence to fulfill a clear and inspiring vision for Tennessee, I mostly saw the same messaging of opposition along party lines.
I love it here, and I have a vision for what Tennesse could be and how to get there: united in protecting our planet, our people, and our prosperity.
We have more in common than we think, and I designed my platform, The Volunteer Plan to reflect that.
A majority of my policies have at least 50% support from Democrats and Republicans.
We don't lack solutions that work for us, we just lack the political will to implement them.
I want to change that.
02.
Which way do you lean politically?
There's a reason I ran as an Independent Candidate and sought out and received the endorsement of The Green Party.
I think the two-party system requires division to sustain itself.
This division leads to authoritarianism with mediocre opposition, as many of us are now seeing in real time.
While I think there's important work happening within the parties to combat that, and I never judge a candidate by party affiliation alone, I also think that work outside the two party system is necessary to protect our vote, our freedom, and our free exchange of ideas and culture.
The future I want is one where a plurality of parties better represent all Tennesseans, and I'm working to make that future a reality.
That's why I reject the premise of the question: you cannot boil me or any one person down to a one-word identity.
We all contain multitudes and we deserve representation that speaks to us.
03.
What's your number one issue?
Tennessee has a host of issues right now from being in last place for school funding to nearly first place for hospital closures.
These are issues I have plans to address, but my number one issue is Climate Action for one reason: if we don't respond to our changing climate, I fear none of the rest will matter in the long term, or will be severely worsened.
The reality is, we can solve our problems and make climate progress at the same time.
After talking to other candidates and pleading with them to take the climate seriously, I decided that if they won't, I will.
Our children, and our children's children deserve a livable world, a beautiful world where we've transitioned from legacy systems of exploitation to evolved systems of regenerative design.
04.
What will you do about datacenters?
I oppose the buildout of new datacenters not only because they harms communities, but because I know that the people behind them seek power and control and wealth above our wellbeing.
I'm not anti-tech in general, as an engineer who's designed hundreds of products I know that the inherent danger of technology is it's power, and who wields it.
An industry who has invested 8x into AI what they've invested in climate technology is not an industry with the people's best interest at heart.
That's why I support a statewide moratorium until strict regulations are in place to make sure they only exist with community consent, are monitored for public and natural safety, and are blocked or shut down if they won't comply.
Any project that Tennesseeans don't want has no business being here.
05.
What will you do for Tennesseans Outside of the big cities?
The first thing I'm doing is visiting rural counties and listenting to what people want and need.
Better roads and bridges, local access to healthcare, local jobs and easier commutes, and affordable housing are some of the issues that come up most often.
I have policies in The Volunteer Plan to address all of them, and I've added more as I hear from you.
I also want to highlight that my number one issue, Climate Action, will be a boon for rural Tennesseans in all counties.
Farmers are our last line of defense against climate change, and protecting our food system is of vital importance.
That's why several of my climate policies pay farmers for things like capturing carbon, reforesting land, and protecting waterways.
It's work that needs to happen, so I'll support farmers who can help and benefit from joining forces to save our environment.
