The bill expands options for long-term care delivery outside of institutional settings.
Read the Full Bill 2024 Session Referred to Committee Sponsor This legislation would direct the state to shift Medicaid long-term care funding toward home- and community-based services, with a goal of allocating at least 50% of funding to non-institutional care.
It also includes changes to reimbursement rates and administrative requirements for home care providers, and supports expanded training for certain care workers.

McKenney sponsored legislation that would authorize the purchase of medical debt for qualifed low income persons, using excess APRA funds.

McKenney cosponsored legislation aimed to authorize trained pharmacists to prescribe and dispense self-administered hormonal contraceptives (bills and patches) to patients, aiming to increase access to birth control without requiring primary care visits.
Pharmacists would be required to complete a training program approved by the state board of pharmacy prior to being eligible to prescribe and dispense the contraceptives.
Although the bill did not pass, it laid the groundwork for subsequent legislation in later years.
