That means whether homeowners receive this incentive depends entirely on where they live.
I believe that isn't fair.
If we want to encourage clean energy, lower electricity costs, and give homeowners more control over rising utility bills, these exemptions shouldn't be optional.
Every New Hampshire homeowner should have the same opportunity, regardless of their ZIP code.
That's why I will fight to require every town in New Hampshire to offer the solar property tax exemption under RSA 72:62.
Coupled with the current federal 30% Residential Clean Energy Tax Credit, this exemption would make solar more affordable for families, help reduce monthly utility costs, and encourage greater investment in clean, renewable energy.
It's a practical step toward lowering our dependence on fossil fuels while helping New Hampshire homeowners save money and build a more sustainable future.
We also need to expand our scope and consider energy sources beyond fossil fuels for municipal use.
Doing so can benefit not only the environment, but also taxpayers and, when utilized correctly, our schools.
Arkansas provides a great example.
The Batesville School District utilized solar power along with energy-efficiency improvements, reducing its energy costs and creating significant long-term savings.
A portion of those savings helped fund teacher salaries.
At a time when taxpayers and public schools are too often being pitted against one another, we should be looking for fiscally responsible and innovative solutions that benefit both .
Investing in alternative energy is one example worth looking into.
