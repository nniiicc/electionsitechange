There is a growing sense of disillusionment with politics, and for understandable reasons.
Too often, it feels as though we're all pieces on a chessboard, moved by two teams focused more on winning than on the people whose lives are affected by the outcome.
Once the game is over, too many of us are left feeling like the pieces scattered across the board.
Sometimes it feels like the biggest difference between the two major parties is simply who we're told to blame.
But blaming entire communities has never solved a single problem.
It never will.
I'm a registered Democrat because it's the party that most closely aligns with my values.
That doesn't mean I agree with every Democrat or every decision made by Democratic leaders.
In a perfect world, we'd all be able to vote for the candidate we truly believe in instead of feeling forced to choose the lesser of two evils.
I'd like to see a government where people with different viewpoints work together to solve problems instead of treating compromise as weakness.
Good ideas don't belong to one political party.
They belong to the people.
I can't promise to change the culture of politics if I'm elected.
What I can promise is that my decisions won't be driven by political games, special interests, or personal ambition.
My focus will always be on what I believe serves the greater good.
I don't have big-money interests backing my campaign, and I won't build it by attacking already marginalized communities.
Real change starts with us.
We have to start seeing each other as people again.
We need to listen more, assume less, and remember that we all want safe communities, opportunities for our children, and the ability to build a good life.
We also have a responsibility to become informed.
Look beyond campaign slogans and advertisements.
Learn who you're voting for.
See which bills they sponsor, how they vote, and where their campaign contributions come from.
Knowledge is one of the most powerful tools we have for creating meaningful change.
We also have to look out for one another.
Too many people in our communities are falling through the cracks, and we can't keep looking the other way.
We all have a role to play.
Choosing not to vote doesn't improve our democracy, and neither does remaining uninformed.
I'd love to promise every voter:
- Affordable housing
- Help for our Senior Population
- Lower taxes and lower utility costs
- Restored mental health services
- Veteran support
- Safer communities
- Protection for vulnerable populations
- A cleaner environment
- Expanded renewable energy
- Strong public schools and support for teachers
- Equality and social equity
- Lower health care costs
- Reproductive care protections
- Death With Dignity
But I won't make promises I can't guarantee.
Meaningful progress requires leaders who are willing to work together instead of against one another.
Regardless of who holds power after the next election, I promise to listen, to work with anyone who wants to move our community forward, and to fight for policies that improve people's lives.
I may not be able to accomplish everything on my list, but I will always fight, and work towards making that vision a reality.
