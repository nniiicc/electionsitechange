Protecting Human Rights
As a white heterosexual female, I’m mindful of the privileges I get simply by being me.
I also recognize the responsibility I have to help address the unfairness that exists in my community and society which can make it harder for people of color, immigrants, and LGBTQA+ individuals to succeed.
Elderly adults and those facing poverty can also encounter extra challenges when it comes to getting the support they need.
Reducing obstacles.
As a registered nurse, I’m dedicated to helping my patients overcome obstacles to getting the care they need, especially those created by social inequalities.
Social injustices can affect health and contribute to chronic health problems.
For example, not having access to affordable, nutritious food can increase the risk of chronic health issues like diabetes and heart disease, which can effect our quality of life and put a strain on our healthcare systems.
In our tight-knit communities in particular, social injustice can be all around - sometimes even more subtle - which can really affect mental health.
Feelings of isolation and worries about racism, prejudice, and ICE might keep people from reaching out for the social, medical, mental and financial help they need.
Reproductive and gender affirming care decisions are personal, private, and should be protected.
Every Oregonian has the right to feel safe, love who they love, be who they are, and make choices about their own bodies without government interference.
Closing gaps.
I've watched the fear and desperation on a patient's face when they make the decision to die, not because there aren’t good treatments, but because they’re so overwhelmed by homelessness, personal safety concerns, and food insecurity that they can’t even think about the extra stress of cancer treatment.
People who are struggling with poverty, addiction, and homelessness often encounter obstacles that prevent them from improving their situation.
Our social system supports are designed to help those in need but then can penalize efforts toward gainful employment.
For example, when individuals in subsidized housing or receiving food stamps find better jobs, their rent usually goes up and their SNAP benefits are reduced.
Additionally, the changes brought about by the HR-1 (otherwise known as the Big Beautiful Bill) often mean they lose their health insurance coverage.
Ensuring that people working to improve their situation are given the support they need to succeed and reach stability also helps build our communities and decrease reliance on social systems.
Protecting the vulnerable.
Marginalized community members can experience barriers to opportunity.
Older adults and people with disabilities can be isolated with limited access to services.
Without computers, internet services or phones, people are unable to connect with support systems online.
Immigrants and low income residents can have difficulty finding housing options free from toxins and pests.
Let’s break free from these cycles and make sure every Oregonian has the protection and support they need to not just survive, but truly flourish.
We need to close the gaps between support and success, creating clear paths out of poverty.
