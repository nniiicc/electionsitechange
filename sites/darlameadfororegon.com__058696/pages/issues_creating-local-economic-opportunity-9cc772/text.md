Creating Local Economic Opportunity
People and families can thrive in safe, nurturing communities where they have access to education options, affordable housing, and employment that provides a living wage.
I believe we can achieve this while still maintaining the uniqueness of our small communities.
Funding public education.
To ensure our children and schools receive the support they need, we should focus on funding public education beyond federal dollars.
This includes boosting enrollment and exploring innovative approaches to address diverse learning needs within our public school system.
Teachers and professors are dedicated professionals that often spend their own money and time to ensure that their students receive the best education and care possible.
Colleges, trade schools, and even high schools should offer ways to succeed with scholarships, internships, residencies, and union-supported skilled trades.
Rural schools and roads are in disrepair and the budgets to fix them are insufficient.
We need to increase family homeownership so that the people paying for bonds for schools are the same people whose children and grandchildren are the ones attending them.
We need to protect the designation of professional job titles for educational funding from federal cuts.
We should expand on programs that offer student loan forgiveness in return for working a number of years in critical shortage and rural areas.
Achieving a livable minimum wage.
We need a realistic minimum wage so people can afford to buy their groceries and pay their bills while not stifling the economic growth of our small businesses.
We would all benefit from increasing the number of union wage jobs.
Creating affordable housing.
New housing developments in my community are not affordable for our children.
New construction should focus on housing that fits the community’s affordability standards, not just profitability for developers.
To help our communities grow, we need to upgrade outdated and inadequate infrastructure to support new development.
We should strive to stay within existing urban growth boundaries which means finding a balance between erecting new construction and repurposing existing buildings where possible.
People in subsidized housing or on rental assistance programs need pathways to long-term, stabilized housing options.
Those of us in small communities chose to live here.
We should protect our beloved communities from becoming tourist attractions filled with short-term rentals and revolving tenants owned by big companies that aren’t really invested in our towns.
Let’s focus on keeping the things that make living in a small community so special: knowing your neighbors, looking out for each other when we need it, and the sense of community we share at the grocery store, park, places of worship, or kids’ sports events.
I’ll bring my accounting expertise to help identify areas where our budgets could be improved, my analytical and critical thinking skills to explore new and existing solutions and work on putting them into action.
We don’t always need to reinvent the wheel; sometimes, we can learn from what other states are doing.
And most importantly, we need to keep checking in and making sure that the solutions we implement are working well for Oregonians and for future generations.
