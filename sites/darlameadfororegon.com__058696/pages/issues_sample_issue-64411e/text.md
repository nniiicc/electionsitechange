As an oncology nurse navigator, I coordinate my patients' care and support them through cancer treatment, both logistically and emotionally.
Part of my job is making sure they have insurance coverage.
When insurance companies delay or deny authorization for important tests, imaging, or life-saving treatments, I step in to resolve these issues and keep their care on track.
Unfortunately, I see these denials and delays far too often.I hear fear and anxiety in my patients' voices when they ask if they will survive long enough to get the insurance approval they need.
They wonder if their cancer will still be curable or if they might die before treatment even begins.
I see resignation and defeat in their eyes as they struggle to choose between risking their lives or getting treatment that could put their family's financial security at risk.
People with cancer should not have to worry about how to pay for their treatment.
The same goes for our children, parents, older adults, people with chronic conditions or mental health needs, your family, and mine.
Healthcare needs to be more affordable.
Your health insurance coverage and its quality should not depend on your employer.
The costs for doctor visits, lab work, imaging, medications, and hospital stays are too high.
Many Oregonians cannot afford their medications and sometimes skip important appointments or tests because of high deductibles or copays.
Watching people suffer and feeling unable to truly help them causes moral distress for nurses and all healthcare providers.
This distress can lead to burnout and drive people out of the medical field, making it even harder for patients to get the care they need.
I am passionate about separating insurance coverage from employment and strongly support a universal healthcare plan.
We need to make healthcare more accessible.
In rural areas, limited access to primary care puts extra strain on emergency rooms and hospitals.
When people can see a local primary care provider, they are more likely to seek help early, manage chronic illnesses, and avoid hospital stays.
Improving provider and hospital reimbursement through a single-payer insurance program will help keep small-town hospitals open and support more rural clinics.
We need to break this cycle and make real changes in how we provide medical and mental healthcare for Oregonians.
I will work to implement a more accessible and affordable healthcare system.
I will seek creative solutions in Oregon to ensure everyone has access to affordable care when and where they need it, including reproductive and gender-affirming care.
Healthcare is a HUMAN RIGHT.
If elected, I will prioritize fixing healthcare for Oregonians.
