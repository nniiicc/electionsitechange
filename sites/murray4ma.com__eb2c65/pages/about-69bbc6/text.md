Home About Priorities Endorsements Contact Volunteer Donate Meet Connor A lifelong advocate for community, accountability, and practical leadership, I am running to ensure the 18th Essex District keeps moving forward.
Rooted in the Community I grew up in Andover and attended Andover High School, where I was involved in show choir, acting, and rugby.
Those experiences helped shape who I am and connected me deeply to this community.
I went on to attend Iona University, where I graduated magna cum laude with degrees in political science and criminal justice, and was selected for the prestigious Presidential Fellowship program at the Center for the Study of the Presidency and Congress.
A Commitment to Public Service My path into public service began in 2018, in the aftermath of the Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School shooting.
I was a founding member of Freedom From Fear, a student-led coalition advocating to reduce gun violence in Massachusetts.
That experience showed me the importance of leaders who are willing to listen, engage, and act.
In college, I focused on policy and systems-level change.
I worked as an interpersonal violence peer educator and contributed to housing policy efforts with the Fuller Center for Housing of Greater New York City.
I also gained experience in Massachusetts government, interning for State Senator Vanna Howard and serving as Field Director for Representative Tara Hong’s successful grassroots campaign in 2024.
Delivering Real Results I’ve always believed that advocacy should lead to action.
In 2024, I was a main signatory on a nationally recognized op-ed on gun violence prevention that became the most widely published article across college newspapers in the country.
I’ve also worked extensively on climate and energy issues.
I’ve led initiatives with organizations like Citizens Climate Lobby and the Sunrise Movement, and I currently serve as a policy advisor to Massachusetts Interfaith Power and Light, where I focus on reducing energy costs and advancing renewable solutions.
In addition to my policy work, I’ve served as a nonprofit leader in my community.
I sit on the Board of Advisors for the Lowell Festival Foundation, where I help lead global vendor and scholarship initiatives for the Lowell Folk Festival, and on the Board of Directors for the Thomas Paine Historical Association, where I support efforts to expand public access to its historic site.
These roles have reinforced my commitment to service, collaboration, and delivering results for the communities I serve.
Why I’m Running Like many people in the Merrimack Valley, I’ve experienced firsthand how difficult it can be to build a future here.
After graduating, I returned home and currently work while continuing to pursue a career in public service.
I’m running for State Representative because I want to make sure that people who grow up here can afford to stay, build their lives, and succeed in the communities they love.
I believe government should work for the people it serves—and I’m committed to delivering real, practical results for our district.
