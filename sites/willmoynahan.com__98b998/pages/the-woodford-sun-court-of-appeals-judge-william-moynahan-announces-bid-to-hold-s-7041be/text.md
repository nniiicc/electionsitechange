Skip to content Home About Will Updates Volunteer Request a Yard Sign Donate Donate Navigation Menu Navigation Menu Home About Will Updates Volunteer Request a Yard Sign Donate The Woodford Sun: Court of Appeals Judge William Moynahan announces bid to hold seat 2 min read By Scott White Managing Editor Kentucky Court of Appeals Judge William Moynahan has announced his entry into the race to keep his judicial seat.
Moynahan was appointed by Gov.
Andy Beshear in April to fill the vacancy left by now Kentucky Supreme Court Justice Pamela Goodwine.
Woodford County is in the 5th Appellate District along with Fayette, Madison, Franklin, Scott, Jessamine, Clark and Bourbon counties, which Moynahan represents.
Since joining the Court of Appeals, Moynahan has reviewed appeals from trial courts across Kentucky.
“From my first day on the bench, my focus has been on ensuring every case is heard with an open mind and a commitment to justice,” Moynahan said in a news release.
“As a Navy veteran, longtime federal prosecutor and now appellate judge, my career has been dedicated to public service.
After more than 25 years serving my country, I’m now running to serve my home state on the Court of Appeals – protecting the rights of all Kentuckians.” A Laurel County native with deep roots in Central Kentucky, Moynahan graduated from Harvard University and then received a law degree from George Washington University.
He served more than 25 years in the U.S.
Navy as a strike aviator, flying 40 combat missions during Operation Iraqi Freedom.
He went to law school after his service, and then began a career as a federal prosecutor both in Washington and in Lexington as an assistant United States attorney.
Former Kentucky Lt.
Gov. and State Auditor Crit Luallen, who has deep Woodford County roots, praised Moynahan’s fitness for the role.
“In a time when public trust in our institutions is more important than ever, Will Moynahan has earned that trust.
He brings integrity, appellate court experience, and an unwavering commitment to applying the law fairly,” Luallen said in the release.
“Gov.
(Andy) Beshear wisely saw that Kentucky’s Court of Appeals would be stronger with Will on it.” Moynahan told the Sun his campaign will focus on continuing his record of fair and impartial decision making, upholding the Constitution and maintaining public confidence in the judiciary.
