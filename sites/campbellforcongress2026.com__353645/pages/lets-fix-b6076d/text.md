Social Security First and foremost, fraud remains a threat to both programs at individual and corporate levels.
The investigative agencies in each program must be allowed to pursue those that are defrauding American tax payers without interference from politicians.
Fraud, waste, improper payments and abuse cost tax payers tens of billions of dollars annually.
The 120th congress must demand that the Congressional Budget Office develop and publish a study to determine its true financial state independent of the partisan rhetoric.
Americans need to know the truth about a system that is the only income for a lot of retirees.
Increase funding to these programs through the following modifications.
Eliminate the limit of $184,500 where the FICA tax stops being collected.
Retain the benefit cap that is in place that limits the social security that is collected by the highest wage earners.
What is often overlooked when talking about FICA taxes is the equal contribution made by the employer.
To offer relief to employers, a corporate pay disparity coefficient will be created and applied.
For companies that reward their executive teams at levels much greater than their lowest earners, their multiplier will scale up starting at 1.
If a company rewards their workforce in a more equitable measure, their multiplier will start at 0.85 and scale downward as disparity drops.
This will benefit small businesses while causing boards of directors to rethink the value of their executive teams compared to their most vulnerable employees.
All forms of compensation, not just wages, will be used to determine the disparity.
Once the program is funded, then the coefficients will be adjusted downward keep from establishing a surplus with tax payers money.
Tax Code Compensation is income and it should be taxed.
For the vast majority of Americans, their entire compensation is taxed at the same rate with few ways to lower their taxable income.
For a much, much smaller minority, their income is taxed at higher rate, but there are multiple avenues to avoid taxes on their intentionally unrealized wealth.
One of the most intriguing options to avoid taxable income is through borrowing against assets such as stocks with interest rates much lower than the tax rate on capital gains.
And then on the same assets, when they pass away, the heirs receive a step up in basis which resets the value of the assets negating any capital gains that have been realized and would have been taxed.
It is time to close these avenues and redefine what is constituted as income.
If it is considered compensation, then it is income.
No more compensation packages that have less than 20% of total compensation as ordinary income with the remainder in unrealized wealth options.
This leaves 80% of income untaxed.
I don't know of an American that would find this burdensome.
Employers offering compensation packages with less than 67% ordinary income and more than 33% other income will have an additional tax burden on their earnings.
Publicly traded companies, not for profits and private government contractors that overly bias their compensation to reward executives at the expense of their lowest earners will also realize an additional burden in their taxes.
This is about getting compensation taxed equitably and eliminating billions of dollars in earnings that is never recognized as income.
Health Care Illness or injury is a major worry for majority of Americans.
The rising cost of healthcare and insurance premiums create significant burdens for many of us.
For me, I am overwhelmed by the number of people that are uninsured after the premium increases from cuts to the Affordable Care Act.
The nasty reality is that we all suffer when Americans are under or uninsured.
Consider you local hospital.
One in my area has an operating budget of roughly $370,000,000 annually.
For the sake of simple math, let's suppose they treat 100,000 patients per year.
The average bill for those 100,000 patients has to cover the annual costs.
That equates to $3700 per visit.
If a third of those patients are uninsured, then that leaves on 67,000 patients at $5200 per visit to cover the operational costs.
We all have our go to healthcare story, but I am talking about costs.
First we need to establish a sliding scale health insurance program that allows low income Americans access to the expensive insurance options.
Low premium, high copay options can appear attractive but one illness can set individuals and families back years.
You don't have to search long to find a family member or a neighbor that is making a monthly payment to a hospital similar to the amount of a car payment.
Second, medical debt cannot be sold to debt collectors for pennies on the dollar until similar offers are made to the patient.
If the debt can be sold for much less, then that is likely what the service was worth.
We all know nothing is worth more than someone is willing to pay you for it.
Third, while healthcare and insurance companies can invest in the markets, they cannot be traded in the markets.
Insurance companies should not be competing for investments based on earnings, cost cuttings and growth in the same manner as department stores and energy companies.
The growth and earnings is what drives denial of treatments and makes medications unavailable.
Finally, health savings accounts must be expanded to allow for insurance premium payments between retirement and medicare coverage.
