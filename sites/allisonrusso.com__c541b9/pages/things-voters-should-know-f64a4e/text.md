Independent polling has Allison Russo within one point of Robert Sprague, with one in six voters still undecided and most Ohioans saying they have not yet heard of either candidate.
It’s so important that our campaign’s message reaches these voters.
Our campaign is reaching voters through connected TV, OTT, and streaming video, as well as across Facebook and Instagram, statewide, with no preference for urban or rural.
We target individual voters, not regions.
We are focused on two groups of Ohioans.
Frustrated voters.
A large share of Ohioans are unhappy with the choices in front of them and have voted for both Republicans and Democrats in the past.
Voters under 40 in particular are disheartened with the direction of the Republican Party and willing to back individual candidates they believe have Ohio’s best interests at heart.
These voters respond to Allison’s record and to who she is, not to party labels.
Democrats who are less likely to vote.
Democratic voters are motivated this year.
The gap is between wanting to vote and getting it done.
We reach lower-propensity Democrats with messaging that makes voting easy: early vote dates, how to vote by mail, deadlines, and links to find a polling location.
This is how we show Ohioans we value their vote, and how we get past the jobs and busy days that keep people from being able to case their ballot.
The contrast Robert Sprague has moved from one statewide office to the next and has let Ohioans down at each stop.
He talks constantly about voter fraud and cannot point to examples.
He refused to admit Trump lost the 2020 Election.
He also supported handing voter information over to credit bureas and letting them monitor voter bank accounts.
Sprague supports removing ballot drop boxes in a state that already allows only one per county.
If he can’t be honest with you about the last election, he won’t be honest running the next one.
As Treasurer of Ohio, he’ll do anything to not be held accountable for the state of our economy and the cost of living in our state.
Polling memo Download the October 2026 memo from Change Research.
It covers where the race stands, how Russo compares to the top of the ticket, how undefined both candidates remain, and the profile of the nearly one in three Ohio voters who do not vote a straight ticket: women, voters under 50, voters of color, and independents.
