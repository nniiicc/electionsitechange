40+ years in Collin County · 4 years on the bench of the 401st District Court George Flint Candidate · Texas HD 70 Where He Comes From He did not inherit this.
He earned it.
George Flint was raised by a truck driver and a clerk.
What his parents could not give him in advantages, they gave him in example: show up, tell the truth, and do the work in front of you until it is done right.
That upbringing carried him from a working household to MIT, to Wake Forest, and then to SMU School of Law.
It is a long way to travel in one lifetime, and George never forgot the ground he started on.
He did not chase the credential.
He earned the trust behind it.
He brought that same standard home to Collin County, where he has practiced law for more than forty years.
He built his career here.
He raised his family here.
The community he now asks to represent is the one he has lived in, worked in, and answered to for four decades.
He is not a newcomer studying the district.
He is a neighbor who already knows it.
He built his career here.
He raised his family here.
He is not studying this community.
He belongs to it.
What He Proved Four years of being trusted to be fair.
In January 2021, George Flint took the bench of the 401st District Court as an elected State District Judge.
For the next four years, his job was not to take a side.
It was to hear every side, weigh the facts, apply the law the same way to everyone, and make the call that had to be right.
People who did not always agree with George Flint still trusted him to be fair.
That is the rarest thing in public life, and it is the truest measure of a person's judgment.
A courtroom does not reward the loudest voice.
It rewards the steadiest one.
That is the temperament George would carry to Austin: listen first, weigh honestly, decide under the law, and treat every constituent by the same rules.
Plano and Allen do not need someone learning how to make hard decisions.
They need someone who already has, thousands of times, with the stakes real and the record public.
There is a difference between holding an office and being trusted with a decision.
George Flint spent a career on the trusted side of that difference.
The judgment Plano and Allen deserve in Austin is the same judgment people relied on in his courtroom: fair, considered, and grounded in the law.
