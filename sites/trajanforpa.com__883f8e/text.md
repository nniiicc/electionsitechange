RB-04 · Rural Hospitals And Medicaid SUBJECT: Rep.
Jim Struzzi (R) · PA House District 62 The Record The federal budget law enacted in 2025 cuts federal Medicaid funding by roughly $1 trillion over ten years and tightens eligibility rules.
Pennsylvania officials estimate the Commonwealth will lose about $20 billion in federal Medicaid funding over ten years beginning in 2028 .
More than 737,000 Medicaid recipients live in rural Pennsylvania counties , where residents are typically older and more reliant on the program.
The Hospital and Healthsystem Association of Pennsylvania found 37% of the state's hospitals operating at a loss and projected 12 to 14 more closures within five years.
The law sets aside a $50 billion national Rural Health Transformation Program, but a spokesperson for Pennsylvania's Department of Human Services said those dollars will not fill the gap the cuts create.
In April 2026, Struzzi issued a statement applauding the extension of the Key-Con coal fired plant authorization, an action of the same federal administration.
Struzzi is a state legislator and cast no vote on the federal law .
The Medicaid reductions were enacted by Congress and signed by the President.
Suggested Message Pennsylvania is set to lose about $20 billion in Medicaid funding, and rural hospitals are the ones that absorb it.
Jim Struzzi has publicly applauded the administration that signed the law.
What is his plan for the hospitals Indiana County depends on?
Audience Seniors, rural households, hospital employees, families managing chronic conditions.

Return To Site Accessibility Statement Last Updated: August 21, 2026 Our Commitment Trajan Jones for PA State House is committed to making TrajanForPAHouse.com usable by everyone, including people with disabilities.
A campaign that asks for your vote should be one you can actually read, navigate, and act on.
Standard We Follow We aim to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA.
These guidelines explain how to make web content more accessible to people with visual, auditory, motor, and cognitive disabilities.
What We Have Done Text and background colors are tested for sufficient contrast.
All content is reachable and operable with a keyboard alone, including the navigation menu and every dialog.
Dialogs can be closed with the Escape key and return focus to the control that opened them.
Images that convey meaning have descriptive alternative text, and decorative images are hidden from screen readers.
Headings follow a logical order so screen reader users can navigate by structure.
Animation is reduced automatically for visitors whose devices request reduced motion.
Layouts reflow without horizontal scrolling down to a 320 pixel viewport width.
Known Limitations Some content is provided by third parties, including our donation processing through ActBlue and any embedded forms or maps.
We do not control the accessibility of those services, though we choose vendors with accessibility commitments where we can.
This site is a work in progress.
If something here does not work for you, that is a problem we want to fix, not a limitation you should have to work around.
Tell Us About A Problem If you encounter a barrier on this site, please contact us and describe what happened and what you were trying to do.
We will respond and work to resolve it.
