About Julie My husband and I have been married for 44 years, we have a wonderful daughter, and have three grandchildren.
Our son died in 2010 of celiac disease at the age of 26.
Since then, our daughter and my husband have been diagnosed with celiac as well.
We are grateful for widespread education among the medical community that has saved countless lives.
I studied landscape architecture, at West Valley College in Los Gatos California and sign language at Ohlone College.
I am still enrolled in the school of hard knocks.
I believe in continuing education and have attended Education Week at BYU every year since 2000 and have taken classes on DNA, music, the supreme court and family relations.
I am not a politician, but seeing the need to take immediate action to save the Great Salt Lake, the need to fight dishonesty and abusive party politics that has raised the cost of living exponentially, and the need to protect our environment, our resources, and our health, I took the leap to run for office.
Being a new comer to politics allows me to bring a fresh perspective to the legislature.
Supreme Court Justice Louis Brandeis said, "The MOST IMPORTANT OFFICE IN A DEMOCRACY IS THE OFFICE OF CITIZEN." The history of our country is about people who won against all odds.
The original 13 colonies had very little chance against the powerful English Empire, but despite being outnumbered, under-funded, and under supplied, the patriots moved forward, never gave up and the United States of America became the first nation of the people, by the people, and for the people.
I proudly claim the privilege of being one of those people.
We can work together to protect our air, water, public lands, and our most precious resource, our children.
We can reclaim Utah as a great place to live, work and raise a family.
