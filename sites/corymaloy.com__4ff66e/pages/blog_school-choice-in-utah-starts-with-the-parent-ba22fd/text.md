Part 2 of 2 Key points Utah Fits All Scholarship: 19,800 Utah families received one this year.
About 7,500 are still on the waitlist.
School choice in Utah means a neighborhood school, another district school, a charter, an online public school, private school, or home.
Utah charter schools are public schools.
About 13 percent of Utah students, no tuition, same per-student formula.
The scholarship takes no money from public schools.
It has its own appropriation.
The court case is on appeal and scholarships continue.
Alpine School District splits July 1, 2027.
Lehi and American Fork to Aspen Peaks, Saratoga Springs to Lake Mountain.
This year 19,800 Utah families used the Utah Fits All Scholarship to build an education around their own child.
About 7,500 more asked and are still waiting.
That waitlist is the part I keep thinking about.
7,500 families looked hard at what their child needed, decided something different would serve that child better, and were told they had to wait.
Some of those families have a student who was struggling in a traditional classroom.
Some have a child who learns better at the kitchen table than in a room with thirty others.
Some just needed help covering a tutor, a curriculum, or a class their child was ready for and their budget was not.
None of it is a line item anybody fights about at the Capitol.
All of it is a parent trying to raise a child the way they think is right.
Choice is bigger than any one program A Utah family can pick the neighborhood school, another district school, a charter, an online public school, a private school, or teaching at home.
All of it is choice.
Most families pick the neighborhood public school, and for most of them that is the right call.
Our district schools do the bulk of this work and they do it well.
Nothing about school choice is an argument against them.
Charter schools are the largest alternative, serving about 13 percent of Utah students.
They are public schools too, barred from charging tuition and funded by the same per-student formula.
A charter just runs on a different model.
Utah has charters built on classical education, dual language immersion, early college engineering, and daily arts instruction.
Utah Virtual Academy does it online for families anywhere in the state.
The Utah Fits All Scholarship covers the choices outside the public system.
It runs on its own appropriation and takes no per-student funding away from any school.
A district court ruled the program unconstitutional last year.
The case is before the Utah Supreme Court now, and the scholarships keep going out while the justices do their work.
I voted for this program, and I stand behind the appeal.
Those families made a decision about their own children, and I am not interested in taking it back from them.
Closer to home than the Capitol In July 2027, Alpine School District becomes three districts.
Lehi and American Fork go to Aspen Peaks.
Saratoga Springs goes to Lake Mountain.
House District 52 will sit across two brand new districts with new boards, new budgets, and new boundaries.
There is a lot still to sort out, and I am not going to pretend the transition will be simple.
But the reason voters approved it is the same reason I keep coming back to local control.
A smaller district means the person deciding about your child's school is somebody you can reach.
Alpine was the largest district in the state.
Nobody would call it close to the families in it.
Decisions about a child belong as close to that child as they can get.
A board you can drive to beats an agency you cannot call.
Different kids, different roads Every family in District 52 is different.
Every child learns differently.
A state that insists on one road for all of them is going to lose some kids who did not have to be lost.
Parents decide what their children are taught.
Not a school board.
Not a state agency.
My votes back that up.
The families who choose something different are not asking the state for permission.
They are asking it to get out of the way.
That is the whole idea.
