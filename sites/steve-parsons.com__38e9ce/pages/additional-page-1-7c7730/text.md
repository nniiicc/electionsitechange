Topics covered included: 1) Rural Economies ; Parsons: “To genuinely support rural economies, he proposed eliminating $181 billion in annual corporate welfare, arguing that these massive federal subsidies overwhelmingly benefit large corporations and put small rural businesses at a severe disadvantage.
He also called for a drastic reduction in federal regulations, explaining that while giant corporations can afford teams of specialists to navigate complex rules, small local companies cannot.” 2) Minimum Wage: Parsons argued strongly against raising the federal minimum wage, asserting that artificial wage floors trigger severe unintended consequences.
Citing historical employment data, he pointed out that after a minimum wage increase in 1957, teenage unemployment—specifically Black teenage unemployment—spiked dramatically.
He argued that such mandates can inadvertently allow discriminatory hiring practices to surface.
3) Inflation .
Instead of raising wages, Parsons proposed combating the cost of living by “neutering the Federal Reserve” to control inflation and halting the practice of paying interest to banks, a policy he labeled a “waste of taxpayer money”.
Cutting regulations (such as the 1920 Jones Act) will also reduce inflation.
4) Stock Trades : Parsons strongly agreed with the proposed stock trading ban, calling the practice by government officials “repugnant” and specifically criticizing the high volume of trades made by politicians.” 5) Privacy and Flock Cameras : Parsons, who answered first, acknowledged the role of public safety but expressed deep skepticism about current legal frameworks, noting that while some surveillance tools are deemed legal, those interpretations do not always match his own views on constitutional privacy rights.
To combat potential overreach, he proposed shifting the authority over surveillance from the federal government down to local municipalities.
6) Student Loans .
The American public has been sold a “bill of goods” regarding the absolute necessity of a traditional four-year liberal arts degree.
He criticized federal programs that blindly entice students into taking on massive debt without a guaranteed return on their investment.
To fix the system, Parsons proposed that higher education institutions need more financial accountability. … holding universities financially liable would incentivize them to steer students toward “more productive” degrees and technical certifications that ensure graduates can actually earn a living.
