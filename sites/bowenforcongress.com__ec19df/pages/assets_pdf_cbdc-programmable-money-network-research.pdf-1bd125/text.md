The documented network: Project mBridge The network at the center of this discussion is Project mBridge.
It is a wholesale, cross-border multi-CBDC platform developed through collaboration among the BIS Innovation Hub, the Central Bank of the UAE, the People's Bank of China's Digital Currency Institute, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, and the Bank of Thailand.
Saudi Arabia joined as a full participant in 2024. [O1-O3] The platform uses distributed-ledger technology to allow participating central banks to issue digital central-bank money and commercial banks to conduct direct cross-border payment and foreign-exchange settlement.
The 2022 pilot involved 20 banks and 164 real-value payment and foreign-exchange transactions totaling more than US$22 million. mBridge reached the minimum viable product stage in 2024. [O1-O3] Critical distinction: mBridge is principally a wholesale system for central and commercial banks.
It is not itself an ordinary consumer wallet that directly decides what an individual may buy.
Fitts connects this wholesale layer to separate retail CBDC, stablecoin, digital-ID, banking, and data systems.
The official mBridge documents do not describe the project as a global retail-control program.

Why the UAE is central to her account In the Solari interview “Currency Wars with Simon Dixon,” Fitts focuses on the significance of the UAE's use of mBridge.
Dixon characterizes the UAE as an important financial node linking Catherine Austin Fitts: CBDC Network Theory | Page 2 China and Hong Kong, the Gulf, emerging BRICS arrangements, commodity and energy trade, and Western capital markets.
Fitts adopts and develops that node-based interpretation. [F1] There is a documented factual basis for treating the UAE as strategically important:      The UAE was a founding mBridge participant. [O1-O3] In January 2024, the UAE initiated a Digital Dirham payment to China worth AED 50 million through mBridge.
The CBUAE described it as the first real-value cross-border CBDC payment between a MENA country and a country outside the region on an MVP-ready platform. [O4] UAE licensed financial institutions were onboarded to process cross-border CBDC payments with participating jurisdictions. [O4] The CBUAE is developing the Digital Dirham for retail, wholesale, and cross-border uses. [O5-O6] Official UAE materials discuss interoperability, smart contracts, tokenization, identity/KYC controls, traceability, access and value controls, and programmability as capabilities or design considerations. [O2, O5-O7] Fitts's inference: Because the UAE combines energy and commodity importance, a major international financial center, strong links to both Western and Asian capital, and early CBDC interoperability work, she views it as a potential bridge or routing node in a future international programmable-money architecture.
That strategic interpretation is plausible, but it is broader than what the UAE documents expressly claim.
3.
The two-layer system Fitts appears to envision Cross-border wholesale layer Platforms such as mBridge allow banks to settle central-bank digital currencies directly across borders without relying entirely on the traditional correspondent-banking chain.
A participating bank could settle one CBDC against another on a common platform, subject to the legal, governance, sanctions, access, and compliance rules of the participating jurisdictions. [O1-O3, O7] Domestic retail layer Within each country, individuals and businesses might use one or more of the following:      an official retail CBDC; regulated digital bank deposits; regulated stablecoins; government-approved or regulated wallets; or other tokenized financial instruments.
The possible U.S. route.
Fitts argues that the United States could avoid the politically unpopular label “CBDC” while obtaining similar practical capabilities through privately issued but federally regulated dollar stablecoins, regulated intermediaries, reserve requirements, digital identity, and payment controls.
In “Currency Wars” and “Programmable Money: Four Scenarios,” she treats this as a possible covert or indirect CBDC model. [F1, F5] Her architectural scenario can therefore be summarized this way: China, the UAE, Europe, and other jurisdictions may use explicit CBDCs, while the United States relies more heavily on bank money and regulated private stablecoins; the systems could nevertheless become Catherine Austin Fitts: CBDC Network Theory | Page 3 interoperable through international settlement rails.
This is a plausible scenario, not proof of a single centrally commanded system.
4.
The “control grid” conclusion Fitts's principal concern is not merely that money becomes digital.
Most modern money is already represented electronically.
Her concern is the convergence of:         programmable money and smart contracts; mandatory or pervasive digital identification; centralized identity verification and KYC systems; comprehensive financial surveillance; artificial intelligence and large-scale data infrastructure; health, tax, benefit, travel, and government records; automated rule enforcement; and the reduction or elimination of practical cash alternatives.
In her Tucker Carlson interview, Fitts defines the control grid as digital infrastructure capable of extraordinary surveillance and control, with programmable money at its center.
She argues that such money may carry enforceable rules and therefore could be made not to function when a person, location, transaction, or purpose is disallowed. [F2] The State of Our Currencies argues that the denomination or name of a future currency matters less than whether the surrounding system is centrally controlled and integrated with governance and resource-allocation mechanisms. [F3] Digital Money, Digital Control argues that cryptocurrency, blockchain, and stablecoin technologies can be absorbed by existing financial and governmental power structures rather than necessarily decentralizing power. [F4] Her warning that these technologies could be used for unprecedented financial surveillance or transaction control is technically credible and deserves serious scrutiny.
5.
Her more sweeping geopolitical theory Fitts goes considerably beyond the official mBridge documentation.
In her Tucker Carlson appearance and related Solari commentary, she presents a theory that a comprehensive programmable-money control system cannot function if people, capital, commodities, or governments can escape through unconnected financial jurisdictions.
She refers to those openings as forms of “leakage.” [F2] Her geopolitical theory can be summarized as follows:   Countries and banking systems outside an interoperable financial-control architecture provide routes around its rules.
Iran, Russia, and other partly disconnected systems may therefore be viewed as alternative channels or resistant nodes.
Catherine Austin Fitts: CBDC Network Theory | Page 4   Sanctions, political pressure, regime-change efforts, military conflict, and central-bank policy may be interpreted as tools for reducing those exits.
Apparent rivalry between Western and BRICS financial systems may eventually yield interoperable regional blocs rather than genuinely independent alternatives.
Evidence assessment: There is extensive evidence that payment systems, sanctions, reserve currencies, banking access, and geopolitical power are connected.
I did not find primary-source evidence establishing that the principal purpose of the wars or pressure campaigns Fitts discusses was to compel CBDC or programmable-money integration.
This part of her argument rests primarily on inference, pattern recognition, historical analogy, and interpretations offered by Fitts and sympathetic commentators.

Important methodological point: Fitts does not always provide a conventional footnote for every assertion in a spoken interview.
The strongest way to evaluate her argument is to separate (1) official technical documents, (2) her own explicit analysis, and (3) broader claims inferred from patterns or supplied by other commentators.

Where the official evidence stops The strongest supportable version of Fitts’s case  mBridge is a real multi-central-bank digital-currency settlement project. [O1-O3] Catherine Austin Fitts: CBDC Network Theory | Page 5     The UAE is a founding participant and has conducted a real-value AED 50 million Digital Dirham payment to China using mBridge. [O4] The UAE is developing retail, wholesale, and cross-border forms of the Digital Dirham. [O5-O6] Official designs and pilot materials contemplate interoperability, identity and access controls, smart contracts, tokenization, traceability, and programmability. [O2, O5-O7] Regulated stablecoins could provide some CBDC-like operational functions without being called a central-bank retail CBDC.
This is an architectural inference, not a settled legal or institutional fact. [F1, F5] What the evidence does not presently establish       that mBridge is a completed worldwide control system; that all participating countries have accepted a single political command structure; that ordinary retail purchases are governed through mBridge; that the UAE secretly administers a global CBDC; that Western and BRICS digital-money projects already operate as one integrated system; or that recent wars were primarily conducted to force countries into a programmable-money network.
The BIS qualification.
In October 2024, BIS General Manager Agustín Carstens stated that the BIS had “graduated out” of mBridge, that the project was not a “BRICS bridge,” and that it was not mature enough for full operation and remained years away.
That statement should be read alongside later and parallel official UAE materials describing an MVP platform, bank onboarding, and real-value transactions.
The fair characterization is that mBridge has demonstrated live wholesale settlement and reached an MVP stage, but it is not a universal global network. [O1, O4, O8] Bottom line Fitts has identified a real and consequential technological convergence: cross-border CBDC settlement, programmable domestic currency, digital identity, regulated stablecoins, smart contracts, tokenized assets, and increasingly centralized financial data.
Her warning that these technologies could be used for unprecedented financial surveillance or transaction control is technically credible and deserves serious scrutiny.
Her further conclusion - that these systems are components of a consciously coordinated global control grid, and that major wars and geopolitical conflicts are substantially designed to eliminate countries outside that grid - remains a theory.
It is supported mainly by inference, historical analogy, and interpretive commentary rather than documentary proof from the BIS, central banks, or governments involved.
