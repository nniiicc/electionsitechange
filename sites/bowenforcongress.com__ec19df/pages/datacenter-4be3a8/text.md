The liberty concern What I Mean by “The Infrastructure of Tyranny” My concern is not simply that a data center could collect or process private information.
My deeper concern is that the Box Elder project may be intended to supply the computing power for a tyrannical interoperable global digital-money system—one that combines central-bank digital currencies or regulated stablecoins with digital identity, programmable rules, and automated transaction controls.
Important parts of that architecture already exist.
Project mBridge has demonstrated cross-border settlement using central-bank digital currencies.
The United Arab Emirates is developing the Digital Dirham for retail, wholesale, and cross-border use.
Official materials discuss interoperability, smart contracts, identity and access controls, traceability, and programmability.
The United States could potentially obtain many of the same practical capabilities through regulated private stablecoins without formally calling the system a retail CBDC.
Programmable money is not merely money stored electronically.
It can be designed so that transactions are approved, denied, limited, frozen, taxed, or restricted according to identity, location, purpose, account status, or government policy.
When programmable money is connected to digital identity, financial records, artificial intelligence, and massive computing capacity, it could give governments and powerful financial institutions unprecedented control over ordinary economic life.
Why Kevin O’Leary’s UAE Connections Matter These unanswered questions take on greater urgency when viewed against the financial and technological relationships surrounding the project.
Kevin O’Leary has extensive personal, business, and investment ties to the United Arab Emirates and has publicly promoted Abu Dhabi’s M2 digital-asset exchange.
Separately, the UAE is a founding participant in Project mBridge, a cross-border central-bank digital-currency platform developed with China, Hong Kong, Thailand, and later Saudi Arabia.
The UAE has already used mBridge for a real-value Digital Dirham payment to China.
Those connections do not prove that O’Leary, M2, mBridge, and the Box Elder project are parts of one coordinated system.
They do establish a legitimate basis for asking whether a massive data center controlled by a UAE-connected developer could someday support interoperable digital-money, identity, artificial-intelligence, or transaction-control systems.
Before Utah commits its land, water, energy, and governmental authority, the public deserves a complete and verifiable explanation of what this facility will actually be used for.
That is what I mean by the infrastructure of tyranny: a system in which access to the financial system becomes a tool of control—where the ability to buy, sell, work, save, or provide for your family can be withdrawn by institutions ordinary citizens cannot meaningfully challenge.
Examine the Evidence The systems discussed here are not theoretical technologies invented for a campaign argument.
Cross-border CBDC settlement, digital identity, smart contracts, traceability, access controls, and programmable-payment capabilities are already being developed and tested.

The public-trust pledge If they approved it, they should not profit from it.
Carlton Bowen is calling on every public official who voted for or approved the Box Elder Data Center framework to make a simple public pledge: they will not seek, accept, or benefit from any private financial opportunity connected to the project or its future phases.
What that includes Contracts, commissions, consulting fees, employment, or vendor relationships.
Land transactions, water-right transactions, development opportunities, equity interests, or board seats.
Benefits through a company, family entity, trust, client, affiliated LLC, or future phase of the project.
This is not an allegation of wrongdoing.
It is a basic public-trust standard.
If no private financial interest exists, the pledge should be easy to sign.
If an interest exists, the public deserves to know now.

What is the Box Elder Data Center?
The Box Elder Data Center, formally known as the Stratos Project, is a major proposed energy and technology campus in Box Elder County involving large-scale land, power, water, infrastructure, tax, and public-accountability questions.
What is Carlton Bowen's position?
Carlton Bowen opposes the Box Elder Data Center.
The public still lacks complete answers about the water source and total water demand, what the facility will be used for, whether it could become part of the infrastructure for a tyrannical global programmable-money regime, the opaque MIDA approval route and later executive-order exemption, and whether decision-makers may profit privately.
What does Carlton Bowen mean by “the infrastructure of tyranny”?
Carlton Bowen is warning about the possibility of an interoperable programmable-money system in which access to the financial system becomes a tool of control.
In such a system, institutions could restrict or withdraw a person’s ability to buy, sell, work, save, or provide for a family.
His concern is whether the Box Elder Data Center could someday support the computing infrastructure for such a system.
Because the project’s intended uses have not been fully disclosed, he is demanding a complete and verifiable explanation.
Why do Kevin O’Leary’s UAE connections matter?
Kevin O’Leary has extensive personal, business, and investment ties to the United Arab Emirates and has promoted Abu Dhabi’s M2 digital-asset exchange.
Separately, the UAE is a founding participant in Project mBridge and has used the platform for a real-value Digital Dirham payment to China.
These connections do not prove that the Box Elder project is part of mBridge or a coordinated programmable-money system.
They do create a legitimate basis for demanding full disclosure of what this massive computing facility will be used for.
Is Carlton Bowen against data centers?
No.
Carlton Bowen is not expressing blanket opposition to all data centers, technology, private investment, jobs, or honest business profit.
He opposes this project because of its scale, unanswered water and use questions, special handling, approval process, and public-trust concerns.
What is the public-trust pledge?
The pledge asks officials connected to the approval process to state that they will not seek, accept, or benefit from private financial opportunities tied to the project or future phases.

What should officials disclose?
Officials should disclose any direct or indirect financial interest connected to the project, including incentives, land, water, contracts, employment, consulting, affiliated companies, family entities, trusts, or future phases.
