Ellin Leonard Answers Frequently Asked Questions
Does Ellin support the “Education Freedom Account” program?
I support ensuring that education funding is directed to the families who need it most.
As currently designed, the Freedom Account program lacks meaningful guardrails.
I believe it should have a firm cap and significantly revised eligibility criteria.
Public dollars should strengthen our public schools, not be diverted from them to subsidize tuition for families whose children already attend private or religious schools.
Responsible reform would keep this program focused on its intended purpose while protecting the classrooms that serve the vast majority of our students.
Does Ellin support adding an income tax on earned income?
I do not support a broad-based income tax.
Despite claims to the contrary, the majority of New Hampshire Democrats share that position, and I am committed to finding responsible ways to fund our priorities without imposing one on Granite State families.
Does Ellin think New Hampshire should lower business taxes?
I oppose the continued reduction of business taxes on large corporations, particularly out-of-state companies, while supporting appropriate relief for our small businesses.
Business taxes are a vital source of revenue for state government, and when it erodes, the burden shifts to local communities.
For roughly fifteen years, the state has been downshifting costs onto our towns and property taxpayers, and we need a more sustainable and balanced approach.
Does Ellin think the state should do more to encourage municipalities to remove zoning barriers to housing development?
I'm open to considering this, but I'd want to see the full picture before taking a position.
We need clear information on infrastructure needs and costs, and a credible plan for how it would be funded.
New Hampshire does need additional housing, and I believe our towns should have the flexibility to decide what works best for their communities.
Does Ellin support developing nuclear power for New Hampshire?
No, I absolutely do not support this.
I believe New Hampshire's energy future lies in renewables, and we should be actively encouraging offshore wind and expanding solar power in a big way.
I have serious concerns about nuclear power, including the unresolved question of where the waste would be stored.
Does Ellin support legislation to expand net energy metering?
Yes, I support expanding the net metering capacity caps.
New Hampshire already has some of the highest electric rates in the country, and net metering helps ease that burden by encouraging households and businesses to adopt solar energy.
It's a practical step that benefits ordinary people, supports local businesses, and moves us toward a cleaner, more affordable energy future.
Does Ellin support funding the Office of the Child Advocate?
Yes.
Protecting the well-being of New Hampshire's children is one of government's most fundamental responsibilities.
I support fully funding the agency that safeguards our children and provides oversight of the facilities that serve our children and teens.
How does Ellin think that public schools should be funded?
Right now, New Hampshire's reliance on local property taxes means school funding is driven largely by property wealth, not by student need or a community's ability to pay.
As a result, children in our wealthiest towns often have access to better educational resources than children elsewhere.
Every student in our state deserves a strong education regardless of their zip code, and I support a more equitable approach to how we fund our schools.
What does Ellin think about requiring proof of U.S. citizenship when registering to vote?
Only U.S. citizens should vote in our elections, and I am committed to protecting the integrity of our elections.
But eligible citizens should not lose their right to vote simply because they don't have a passport or birth certificate readily available.
We can safeguard our elections without creating unnecessary barriers for lawful voters.
Does Ellin want a state law cap how much local school districts can increase their budget each year?
No.
Decisions about school budgets are best made locally, by the communities that fund and depend on their schools.
The state should not be mandating how a town sets its budget, and I oppose this as a further encroachment on local control.
Where does Ellin Leonard stand on abortion?
I do not support banning abortion in the first trimester.
Decisions about a woman's pregnancy are among the most personal she will ever make, and they should be made by her, in consultation with her doctor and in line with her own values and faith.
I believe that government should not be inserting itself into these private medical decisions.
Trusting women to make choices about their own health and their own futures is a principle I hold firmly.
Nor do I support a ban on second trimester abortions.
I recognize that these are deeply serious situations, and they are rarely easy for anyone involved.
There are circumstances in which a mother's health is at risk, or in which a pregnancy is not viable, and families facing those heartbreaking realities deserve compassion and care, not political interference.
A one-size-fits-all law cannot account for the complexity of what these families endure.
At the end of the day, these decisions belong with a woman and her doctor, not with politicians.
I respect that people of good conscience hold differing views on this issue, and I welcome those conversations.
I am committed to protecting that freedom and to supporting the health and well-being of women and families in our community.
Does Ellin Leonard support the legalization of marijuana?
Granite Staters are already crossing state lines to purchase cannabis in Massachusetts, Maine, and Vermont, and when they do, they take their dollars with them.
That is revenue that could be supporting our schools, our roads, and our communities right here at home.
Rather than watching our neighbors benefit from a market our own residents are driving, I believe it is time for New Hampshire to take a practical, common-sense look at how we can keep those resources in our state.
By allowing licensed growers and regulated retail locations, we can ensure that consumers have access to safe, tested products they can trust, while creating new opportunities for local businesses and good-paying jobs.
A well-designed, carefully regulated market reflects the independent spirit of our state and our commitment to practical, responsible solutions.
I am eager to work with my neighbors, law enforcement, and public health leaders to get this right, and I believe we can build a framework that serves New Hampshire well for years to come.
Does Ellin support a new broad-based sales tax?
No.
I do not support this proposal.
It would place a significant burden on many New Hampshire businesses and increases costs for taxpayers, so I am not in favor of it."
Should New Hampshire increase the tax on cigarettes?
Yes.
I support an increased tax on cigarettes as long as they continue to be tied to the retail price index.
Higher prices encourage many smokers to cut back, which benefits public health.
I also believe we should legalize and tax cannabis.
Does Ellin agree that New Hampshire should add restrictions to the governor's powers during a state of emergency?
I do not support allowing the governor to circumvent the legislature in times of emergency, as happened during the COVID-19 pandemic.
Emergencies call for swift action, but they do not justify setting aside the legislative process and the checks and balances that protect the public.
Does Ellin believe that New Hampshire should increase public access to reports of police misconduct?
Yes.
The public has a right to know about inappropriate behavior of officers in their communities to allow for accountability at the local level.
Does Ellin support the option of mail-in ballots for all voters, not just absentees?
Yes.
Several other states have used mail-in voting successfully for years, and in a number of Western states it is the way the vast majority of voters cast their ballots.
It makes voting easier and more accessible for those who want to participate.
Should New Hampshire increase the size of solar panel installations that may participate in net energy metering?
Yes.
New Hampshire already has some of the highest electric rates in the country, and net metering helps ease that burden by encouraging households and businesses to adopt solar energy.
It's a practical step that benefits ordinary people, supports local businesses, and moves us toward a cleaner, more affordable energy future.
Does Ellin support New Hampshire providing student loan debt repayment programs for workers in industries with labor shortages?
Yes, I support this.
New Hampshire's economy depends on a skilled and dedicated workforce, and right now too many employers are struggling to find the talent they need.
To keep our communities strong, we have to attract workers with a wide range of skills, from the trades to technology to the professions that keep our families healthy and safe.
I believe we should be doing everything we can to make New Hampshire the place where talented people choose to live, work, and build their futures.
Student loan repayment programs are one of the most practical tools we have to make that happen.
They would be especially helpful in recruiting healthcare workers, as well as other professionals who need a certain level of education or training and often carry significant debt as a result.
By helping to ease that burden, we can bring more nurses, doctors, and skilled workers to our state and encourage them to put down roots here.
I recognize that we must design any program carefully and be responsible with taxpayer dollars, but I am confident that a thoughtful approach can strengthen our workforce, support our employers, and ensure that every community in New Hampshire has access to the care and expertise it needs.
What does Ellin think about the state adding tax incentives for affordable housing development?
Yes, I support this.
New Hampshire is in the middle of a housing crisis, and its effects reach nearly every corner of our state.
For too many young families, teachers, nurses, and working people, buying a first home has become a goal that feels out of reach.
When our children can't afford to stay in the communities where they grew up, and when employers struggle to recruit because workers have nowhere affordable to live, we all feel the consequences.
Part of the challenge is simple economics: right now, there is more money to be made building expensive homes than affordable ones, and the market is responding accordingly.
If we want more affordable housing built, we need to make it worth the investment for builders and developers, and I support practical incentives that encourage them to do so.
I recognize that any approach must be thoughtful, respect local character, and use taxpayer dollars wisely, and I welcome input from communities, builders, and families as we get it right.
But I am confident that by working together, we can open the door to homeownership for the next generation of Granite Staters and keep New Hampshire a place where people can afford to build a life.
Does Ellin support using state tax revenues for private and home-schooling costs?
No, I do not support this.
Our public schools are the foundation of our communities, and taxpayer dollars should go toward making them the very best they can be for every child in New Hampshire.
When we invest in strong classrooms, dedicated teachers, and the resources students need to succeed, we strengthen not only our schools but the towns they serve.
I do believe there are exceptions where families need other options, such as cases of bullying or other compelling circumstances, and in those situations support for private or home schooling can be appropriate.
But that should be the exception, not the rule.
I respect the commitment that parents bring to their children's education, and I welcome the conversation with families across our state.
At the end of the day, I will keep working to ensure that every child has access to an excellent public education, and that our tax dollars are used responsibly to make that possible.
