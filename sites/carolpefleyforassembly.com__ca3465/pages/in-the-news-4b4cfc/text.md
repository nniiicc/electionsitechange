Scott Wiener’s War on Neighborhoods: Stripping Local Control in Silicon Valley
People in Silicon Valley are finally starting to notice the repercussions of the housing legislation passed over the last few years—and it’s hitting home fast.
Cities like Willow Glen, Los Gatos, Morgan Hill, Scotts Valley, and Santa Cruz are being ordered to add thousands of new housing units to satisfy state housing mandates, regardless of whether these local infrastructure networks can handle them.
The character, safety, and unique identity of our local communities are being reshaped from the top down by Sacramento politicians.
Californians are waking up to a stark reality: these laws were never strictly about solving a housing crisis.
They are about forcing density, stripping local control from elected city officials, and ignoring fundamental public safety concerns.
The Mastermind Behind Forced California Density
Right in the middle of this top-down policy shift is State Senator Scott Wiener.
For nearly a decade, Wiener has made it his primary mission to eliminate traditional suburban developments and single-family neighborhoods across California.
He has publicly categorized single-family homes as "racist and exclusionary"—a stark contrast to his own upbringing in Princeton, New Jersey, one of the wealthiest, quietest, and most low-density communities in America.
After enjoying quiet streets, large yards, and neighborhood stability during his formative years, Wiener’s legislative agenda now aims to deny those exact living options to everyday Californians.
In Senator Wiener’s vision for California, families are pushed into dense stacked apartments, forced onto public transit, and taught to treat private front yards as a moral failing.
Danger Over Safety: Pushing High-Density into Disaster Zones
Wiener has introduced bill after bill designed to streamline high-density apartment construction, even in areas prone to natural disasters:
- High Fire Severity Zones
- Tsunami & Coastal Hazard Risk Zones
- Liquefaction & Soil Subsidence Zones
Legislation like SB 610, SB 647, and SB 677 was written to systematically override local safety decisions and allow large developers to bypass standard city planning oversight.

Legislative Inaction and the Local Impact
In February, while families were sifting through ashes following devastating wildland fires, Senator Wiener pushed forward SB 677—legislation designed to convert single-family parcels into multi-unit housing developments within disaster areas.
While local communities mourned their losses, Sacramento saw political momentum, and developers saw dollar signs.
My opponent, Gail Pellerin, voted YES on SB 677 alongside several other key housing bills that stripped local authority from our town councils and city officials.
Restoring Local Control to Our Communities
The pattern from Sacramento is clear: ignore environmental hazards, strip local authority, undermine CEQA protections, and force high-density housing at any cost.
Californians are paying attention, and it's time to demand accountability.
We must protect our neighborhoods, restore local decision-making to our elected city councils, and keep public safety as the top priority.
