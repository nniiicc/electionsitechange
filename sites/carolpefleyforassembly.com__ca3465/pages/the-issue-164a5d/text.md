Carol Pefley for Assembly Stop Sacramento's power grab Bill after bill, Sacramento has moved decisions about what gets built in our towns away from the people who live here.
Gail Pellerin voted yes on every one of these.
SB 423 2023 · Lets developers bypass local review for multifamily projects SB 684 2023 · Staff-only approval of subdivisions, no public hearing AB 1633 2023 · Lets developers sue cities that slow a project AB 976 2023 · Permanently bars owner-occupancy rules for ADUs SB 1211 2024 · Ministerial ADU approval, no public hearing Then came SB 79 — and she didn't vote at all SB 79 automatically upzones land near transit stops and is the furthest-reaching housing preemption law yet.
It passed the Assembly 43–19 on September 11, 2025.
Gail Pellerin was on the floor that day and voted on dozens of other bills.
On SB 79, the Assembly Journal records no vote from her — not yes, not no.
What these bills caused Families across the district feel unheard, and communities that once had a say in their own future are watching it be reshaped from 150 miles away.
Less local control City councils and county supervisors lose the final say on what gets built.
Limited community input Streamlined approvals cut public hearings short or skip them entirely.
Unwanted development Projects that don't fit the character of our towns move forward anyway.
No tax revenue for cities Mandated growth arrives without the funding to serve it.
Bankrupting our cities Roads, water, schools, and public safety are strained with no way to pay.
Is your current representative listening?
Gail Pellerin has represented Assembly District 28 while these mandates became law.
She voted yes on all five.
The result is a district where families, small businesses, and longtime residents are being priced out and planned around — without a real say in their own future.
Enough is enough.
Let's take our communities back.
