Our child welfare system should do more than respond after abuse, neglect, or family crisis has already occurred; it should strengthen prevention, expand access to trauma-informed care, support families before removal becomes necessary, and ensure children have a consistent voice when they enter the court system.
This plan creates a stronger continuum of care from prevention and early intervention through advocacy, treatment, reunification, proper placement, permanence, and aftercare so that children are protected at every stage and families have a real opportunity to succeed.
CHILD ADVOCACY AND FAMILY STABILITY PLAN Child Safety and Prevention Act Builds a stronger prevention system that identifies risks earlier, equips families and communities with the tools to protect children, and intervenes before preventable harm becomes a crisis.
Expands access to evidence-based child safety, parenting, digital safety, abuse-prevention, and healthy-development education for children, families, educators, and community organizations.
Creates stronger early-intervention pathways for families experiencing housing instability, substance-use challenges, mental-health crises, domestic violence, financial hardship, or other conditions that can place children at increased risk.
Encourages schools, healthcare providers, child welfare agencies, courts, and community organizations to coordinate prevention efforts so families are connected with help before problems escalate.
Child Advocacy and Representation Act Ensures that children involved in abuse, neglect, dependency, custody, or foster-care proceedings have meaningful representation and a consistent voice throughout the decisions affecting their lives.
Establishes stronger national standards for timely, independent, and child-centered advocacy in cases involving abuse, neglect, dependency, or removal from the home.
Expands resources for recruiting, training, supervising, and retaining qualified advocates and professionals serving children involved in the child welfare and court systems.
Prioritizes continuity so children are not repeatedly required to rebuild relationships with new advocates, caseworkers, or other professionals throughout lengthy proceedings.
Child Trauma Treatment and Recovery Act Creates a reliable system of trauma-informed care so children who experience abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, or family crisis can receive appropriate treatment regardless of income or geography.
Expands access to trauma-informed counseling, behavioral healthcare, crisis services, forensic services, and long-term recovery support for affected children.
Strengthens rural and underserved-area capacity through workforce development, transportation assistance, telehealth, regional partnerships, and incentives for qualified professionals.
Supports coordinated treatment plans that follow children across changes in placement, schools, providers, or legal status so care is not interrupted when their circumstances change.
Safe Families and Early Intervention Act Helps families address serious challenges before removal becomes necessary while maintaining child safety as the non-negotiable standard for every intervention.
Expands intensive in-home services, parenting assistance, mental-health treatment, substance-use treatment, domestic-violence services, and other interventions that can safely stabilize a family.
Allows targeted assistance with barriers such as transportation, housing instability, utilities, childcare, and other immediate needs when resolving those barriers directly contributes to a child's safety and stability.
Requires clear safety standards and regular case review so family-preservation efforts are never used to delay necessary action when a child faces an immediate threat of harm.
Permanency and Reunification Act Builds a child welfare system focused not simply on closing cases, but on achieving safe, stable, and lasting outcomes for children.
Strengthens reunification planning so parents understand requirements, have reasonable access to required services, and receive coordinated assistance in meeting court and child-welfare expectations.
Prioritizes safe and timely permanency through reunification, kinship placement, guardianship, or adoption based on the individual circumstances and best interests of the child.
Reduces unnecessary disruptions in placements, schools, healthcare, sibling relationships, and community connections while children move through the child welfare system.
Family Transition and Aftercare Act Recognizes that stability does not begin or end with a court order and provides continued support during the vulnerable period after reunification, permanency, or formal case closure.
Establishes transitional aftercare services for children and families following reunification, guardianship, adoption, or closure of a child-welfare case when continued support is appropriate.
Provides voluntary access to counseling, parenting support, childcare assistance, transportation, recovery services, resource navigation, and other stabilizing services during the transition period.
Creates rapid-response pathways that allow families to seek assistance when new problems emerge without requiring conditions to deteriorate to the point that another formal child-welfare case becomes necessary.
Childcare and Family Stability Act Removes practical barriers that prevent parents and caregivers from completing the work necessary to provide safe, stable, and permanent homes for children.
Prioritizes childcare assistance for families completing treatment, employment requirements, parenting education, court obligations, reunification plans, or other approved family-stabilization activities.
Expands access to practical family supports including transportation, nutrition, housing navigation, employment assistance, parenting resources, and essential child-care supplies.
Coordinates eligibility across assistance systems so families are not forced to navigate conflicting requirements, repeated applications, and administrative barriers while attempting to stabilize their households.
Child Welfare Coordination and Accountability Act Creates a more coordinated and accountable child welfare system so children and families do not fall through the gaps between agencies, courts, healthcare providers, schools, and community services.
Establishes stronger standards for information-sharing, case coordination, referrals, and continuity of services while maintaining appropriate privacy protections.
Requires better measurement of long-term outcomes including repeat maltreatment, placement stability, successful reunification, permanency, access to treatment, and family stability after case closure.
Directs resources toward areas with demonstrated service gaps and ensures rural and underserved communities are not left without adequate child advocacy, treatment, or family-support capacity.
