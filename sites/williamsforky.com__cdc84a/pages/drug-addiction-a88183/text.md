We need a system that gives people a real path to treatment and long-term recovery while giving officers the tools to target traffickers, violent offenders, and organized drug networks.
This plan strengthens treatment access, recovery, enforcement, rural behavioral health, and accountability so communities have more effective options than simply cycling people through crisis, jail, and relapses.
Drug Addiction Treatment & Recovery Plan Long-Term Recovery Act Treat recovery as a long-term process by supporting people beyond detoxification and short-term treatment with the services needed to remain stable and healthy.
Support residential and outpatient treatment, medication-assisted treatment, mental-health services, peer support, recovery housing, and continued case management.
Expand transportation, employment assistance, family support, and other services that help individuals remain stable after completing treatment.
Measure success through long-term outcomes such as recovery, stable housing, employment, reduced overdose, and reduced involvement with the criminal justice system.
No Wrong Door Recovery Act Create a coordinated recovery system where anyone seeking help can be connected to appropriate care regardless of where they first enter the system.
Develop regional systems connecting law enforcement, hospitals, EMS, courts, jails, treatment providers, and community organizations.
Create real-time information on treatment openings, detoxification, residential care, outpatient services, medication providers, recovery housing, and transportation.
Establish a single referral process so individuals and families are not forced to call multiple providers simply to determine where help is available.
Recovery Reentry Act Ensure that treatment, medication, and recovery support continue when someone leaves jail, prison, or another correctional setting and returns to the community.
Require federally supported reentry programs to establish a treatment and recovery plan before an individual is released.
Ensure continuity of prescribed addiction medication, behavioral-health care, peer support, and scheduled follow-up appointments.
Connect individuals with housing, transportation, identification, benefits, employment services, and community treatment immediately upon release.
Rural Behavioral Health Access Act Build regional treatment and mental-health infrastructure so rural families and law enforcement are not left without nearby places to turn during a crisis.
Fund regional crisis stabilization centers, substance-use treatment, psychiatric services, recovery housing, and mobile crisis teams.
Expand telehealth, transportation, and workforce incentives for mental-health and substance-use professionals serving rural communities.
Encourage multi-county partnerships among hospitals, EMS, law enforcement, courts, treatment providers, and community organizations.
Law Enforcement Treatment Access Act Give law enforcement officers immediate alternatives to jail when addiction, mental illness, or another behavioral-health crisis is the primary problem.
Create 24-hour referral systems allowing officers to connect eligible individuals directly with detoxification, treatment, stabilization, and recovery services.
Fund law-enforcement deflection programs, Quick Response Teams, peer recovery specialists, and behavioral-health co-responder programs.
Provide transportation funding so an officer does not have to choose between making an arrest or leaving someone without help because treatment is unavailable nearby.
Drug Court Expansion Act Expand access to Drug Courts and specialty courts that combine accountability, supervision, and evidence-based treatment to reduce repeat involvement with the criminal justice system.
Increase federal funding for Drug Courts, Mental Health Courts, Veterans Treatment Courts, and other behavioral-health diversion programs.
Support staffing, treatment, case management, drug testing, transportation, and rural access so courts can serve more eligible participants.
Protect successful specialty-court programs from disappearing because of temporary state or local budget shortages.
Drug Trafficking Enforcement Act Concentrate law-enforcement resources on the individuals and organizations that manufacture, traffic, and profit from dangerous drugs while driving violence in our communities.
Prioritize fentanyl, methamphetamine, counterfeit-pill, and organized trafficking.
Increase federal support for regional drug task forces, particularly in rural areas that cannot independently maintain specialized narcotics units.
Fund investigators, intelligence analysts, digital forensics, evidence processing, overtime, and cross-jurisdictional investigations aimed at disrupting major trafficking networks.
Public Health Recovery Outcome Voucher Act Reward treatment and recovery programs for producing measurable public-health improvements by allowing successful programs to earn flexible funding they can reinvest in their communities.
Create Public Health Outcome Vouchers earned through verified improvements such as treatment retention, reduced overdose, reduced recidivism, successful reentry, stable housing, employment, and timely follow-up care.
Allow qualifying treatment providers, specialty courts, recovery programs, and community organizations to redeem earned vouchers for expanded treatment capacity, staffing, transportation, peer support, recovery housing, prevention, and other approved public-health needs.
