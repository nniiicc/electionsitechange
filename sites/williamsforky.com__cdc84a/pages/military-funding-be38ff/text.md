Military families and service members should be able to rely on the programs Congress funds for their quality of life without those resources being redirected when other defense priorities arise.
This plan establishes protected funding directives for essential military obligations, strengthens transparency over how those dollars are spent, and requires greater congressional oversight before designated funds can be moved or reduced.
By treating these commitments as obligations rather than optional expenses, we can better protect military families, improve readiness, and ensure defense dollars are used for the purposes Congress intended.
Military Funding
Integrity Plan
- Turns responsible military savings into long-term investments in technology, equipment, infrastructure, and readiness instead of encouraging units to spend remaining funds simply because the fiscal year is ending.
- Allow units to transfer a portion of verified year-end savings into a protected multi-year Readiness Innovation & Investment Fund rather than surrendering all unused funding.
- Allow participating units to propose future investments in research, prototypes, equipment, maintenance improvements, training technology, and other projects that reduce long-term costs or improve readiness.
- Protect units from automatic future budget reductions solely because they generated legitimate savings, while requiring that savings come from efficiency rather than deferred maintenance, reduced training, or unmet readiness requirements.
- Encourages military units to pool savings and solve shared problems together by providing greater investment opportunities when multiple commands participate.
- Allow two or more units to combine their Readiness Innovation Fund balances for shared investments that provide measurable benefits across participating commands.
- Provide additional DoD matching funds for approved collaborative projects, giving units a larger return when they pool resources instead of pursuing duplicative solutions independently.
- Give participating units priority access to the resulting technology, equipment, facilities, or cost savings based on their contribution and operational need.
- Protects the education of military children by making education funding for military dependents a dedicated congressional obligation rather than a flexible source of Pentagon funding.
- Establish a separate funding for military dependents education appropriation that cannot be transferred or reprogrammed for unrelated Defense Department priorities.
- Require congressional approval before reducing funding already appropriated for classroom instruction, special education, staffing, school operations, or student services.
- Keep education for military dependents within the Department of Defense while separating its protected education funding from broader Defense-wide operating accounts.
- Requires money appropriated for safe military housing and barracks to actually be spent maintaining safe military housing and barracks.
- Establish protected funding floors for barracks, unaccompanied housing, family housing, and essential habitability maintenance.
- Prohibit transferring designated housing-maintenance funding to weapons, operations, or other programs without specific congressional authorization.
- Require annual public reporting showing identified housing needs, appropriated funding, actual expenditures, deferred projects, and unresolved health or safety deficiencies.
- Treats safe living conditions for service members as a required military expense rather than maintenance that can continually be deferred.
- Require dedicated funding for mold, plumbing, ventilation, sanitation, water, electrical, and other immediate health-and-safety deficiencies in barracks and military living quarters.
- Establish repair deadlines for documented habitability hazards and require alternative accommodations when those deadlines cannot be met.
- Prevent habitability remediation funds from being deferred or redirected while documented unsafe conditions remain unresolved.
- Protects health-care funding promised to service members and their families from being reduced to finance unrelated defense priorities.
- Establish protected funding levels for TRICARE beneficiary care, military treatment facilities, family health services, and dependent coverage.
- Prohibit transfers out of protected beneficiary-care accounts unless Congress specifically approves the reduction.
- Require DoD to disclose proposed health-care reductions, affected beneficiaries, and anticipated changes in access before funds can be redirected.
- Requires new weapons and equipment programs to account for what it will actually cost to keep those systems operational throughout their service lives.
- Require Congress to receive a complete sustainment plan covering maintenance, spare parts, software, infrastructure, training, and personnel before major procurement expansion.
- Establish sustainment funding requirements alongside procurement funding so new equipment does not crowd out maintenance of the existing force.
- Trigger additional congressional review when operating or sustainment costs substantially exceed approved projections.
- Helps military units across branches facing the same operational problem develop one shared solution instead of separately purchasing or developing competing solutions.
- Create a DoD process for units to identify common equipment, training, maintenance, logistics, technology, and infrastructure needs before making major independent investments.
- Provide enhanced matching funds when multiple commands jointly develop, test, or purchase a solution that can be adopted across the force.
- Require successful projects to publish costs, savings, performance results, and implementation guidance so other units can adopt proven solutions without starting over.
- Rewards units that find lasting ways to accomplish military requirements at lower cost without sacrificing readiness.
- Award participating units investment credits for independently verified savings produced through reduced waste, shared purchasing, resource conservation, process improvements, or other efficiencies.
- Allow accumulated credits to remain available across fiscal years and be combined with credits from other units for larger future investments.
- Measure savings over multiple years so commanders are rewarded for reducing long-term costs rather than simply producing a lower expenditure in a single budget year.
- Requires usable military property and supplies to be reused, transferred, or recovered whenever practical before they are destroyed or replaced.
- Create a DoD-wide process for identifying excess equipment, parts, supplies, and appropriate munitions that another unit or service can still use.
- Require a reuse, transfer, refurbishment, or cost-recovery review before serviceable government property is designated for destruction or disposal.
- Track the value of equipment recovered, transferred, refurbished, sold, or avoided through reuse and report those savings to Congress.
- Protects the maintenance infrastructure and workforce that keep military equipment operational during both peacetime and conflict.
- Establish protected investment for public shipyards, aviation depots, maintenance facilities, tooling, and critical equipment.
- Require long-term workload planning so depots, hangers, and shipyards can hire, train, and retain the skilled workforce needed for projected demand.
- Prevent maintenance infrastructure funding from being repeatedly deferred when documented capacity shortages are affecting operational readiness.
