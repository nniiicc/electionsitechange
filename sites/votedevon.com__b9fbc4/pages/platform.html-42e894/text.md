Education Affordability Opportunity How We Pay For It Priority 01 Protecting Public Education in Indiana Education School quality is the first thing families check when they’re choosing a community.
Public schools are being squeezed from three directions: Voucher dollars are leaving the public school system.
Property tax caps are stripping local funding.
Curriculum battles are driven by politics instead of evidence.
The problem $600M leaving Indiana’s public school system to fund private and charter schools next year, even though public schools educate 90% of Hoosier students.
Keep Public Funding in Public Classrooms The problem Private and charter alternatives draw from the same funding pool as the public schools that educate 90% of Indiana students.
The solution Devon will push to require funding for alternatives to come from a separate appropriation so public-school dollars remain in public classrooms.
Reverse Partisan School Board Elections The problem Noblesville voters made their preference for nonpartisan school boards clear, but state lawmakers overrode that local choice.
The solution Devon will push to reverse partisan school board elections and restore the community’s authority to choose how its schools are governed.
Make Every School Pay Its Share The problem Public schools must provide special education, transportation, and English-language services even when publicly funded alternatives do not share those systemwide costs.
The solution Devon will push to require every school receiving public per-pupil funding to contribute fairly to shared services so taxpayers do not pay twice.
Audit the Special Education Formula The problem Indiana’s special education funding formula has not received the scrutiny needed to ensure students with disabilities receive the protections and support federal law promises.
The solution Devon will push for an independent audit of the formula, identify funding gaps and workarounds, and demand accountability for how resources reach students.
Evidence Over Politics in Curriculum The problem Political pressure is increasingly shaping curriculum and assessment decisions that should be guided by classroom experience and student outcomes.
The solution Devon will push to put educator expertise and peer-reviewed evidence at the center of curriculum and assessment decisions.
Priority 02 Real Affordability.
Affordability When housing, utilities, childcare, and healthcare all rise while paychecks don’t, families feel the squeeze.
Indiana’s affordability crisis was built by policy — and it can be reversed by policy.
The problem +20% jump in Duke Energy bills in a single year — a choice made at the Statehouse, not the market.
Problem +20% Duke Energy hike Utilities The problem Duke Energy raised bills #% in one year because of choices made in Indianapolis.
The solution Devon will push to reform rate-tracker laws that lock in increases, stop billing families for data-center infrastructure, restore the state efficiency program, and cap costs as a share of income.
Problem $461K median home Housing The problem Teachers, nurses, and the people who keep this community running are getting priced out of it.
In Hamilton County, about one in five rental houses is owned by an out-of-state company.
In Fishers, nearly half of all rentals are owned by someone out of state.
Investors pay cash almost nine times out of ten, making it difficult for regular families to compete.
The solution Devon will push to limit how many single-family homes out-of-state investors can turn into rentals.
For seniors, property taxes are a bill that shows up whether they can pay it or not.
She will push to prevent that bill from forcing them out of their homes and to make it easier for first-time buyers to purchase a home here.
Problem 300+ providers closed Childcare The problem Since 2024, Indiana has frozen enrollment and cut provider payments by up to 35%.
More than 300 providers have closed, costing the state an estimated $4 billion a year in economic output.
The solution Devon will push to restore provider funding, expand tax credits for working families, and draw down every available federal matching dollar.
Problem $27K/yr family premiums Healthcare The problem Family health premiums are approaching $27,000 a year while Indiana’s doctors remain among the lowest-paid in the country.
The solution Devon will push to take hospital price caps further, enforce pricing transparency with real penalties, hold insurers accountable for improper denials, and expand telehealth access.
Problem 33,000+ face hunger Food Security The problem More than 33,000 Hamilton County residents, including 7,000 children, face hunger, and pantry demand has risen 30% in one year.
The solution Devon will push to streamline enrollment across food assistance and Medicaid, connect food access to primary care, and expand SNAP incentives at local farmers markets.
Problem $10K/mo nursing home cost Elder Care The problem A private nursing home room now costs nearly $10,000 a month, often without warning before a family needs care.
The solution Devon will push to lift the 40-hour cap on paid family caregivers, reform HEA 1127, reduce waitlists for home-based programs, and help seniors claim the federal benefits they’ve earned.
Problem 2009 last wage increase Wages & Tax Fairness The problem Indiana hasn’t raised its minimum wage since 2009, and it taxes the lowest-income families at nearly twice the effective rate paid by the highest earners.
The solution Devon will push to raise the minimum wage, fix the tax treatment of basic prepared foods, create an Indiana Earned Income Tax Credit, and tie corporate incentives to verified job creation.
Priority 03 Opportunity You Can Count On Opportunity Opportunity should mean more than a credential or a job title.
It should provide a path to rent, childcare, homeownership, savings, and the freedom to build something of your own.
After High School The challenge Indiana requires new diploma and workforce pathways without consistently funding the programs or counselors students need to navigate them.
The opportunity Devon will push to fund trade and workforce pathways, build pipelines between high schools and trade unions, and keep college within reach without decades of debt.
Building Your Own The challenge Unpredictable tariffs and subsidies tilted toward large corporations make it harder for local owners to plan, grow, and remain in their communities.
The opportunity Devon will push for stable trade policy, support Community Land Trusts that protect local businesses from rising rents, and reform subsidies that favor big corporations.
Protecting Opportunity The challenge SEA 1 strips communities of tools to fund local priorities, while broad tax rollbacks can undermine the schools and services families rely on.
The opportunity Devon will push to restore local authority, protect programs like NobleACT, and target property-tax relief to seniors and working families who need it.
Agriculture & Local Food Systems The challenge Farmland lost to development does not return, and beginning farmers face growing barriers to securing land and reaching local customers.
The opportunity Devon will push for land-access legislation, support conservation tools like the Teter Organic Farm model, and invest in farmers markets and local food systems.
Priority 04 This approach doesn’t require new money.
How We Pay For It We recover $23B in federal dollars Hoosiers already paid We stop losing $140M in penalties by 2027 Cost to you $0 new taxes needed The problem Indiana keeps paying for expensive outcomes through emergency rooms, nursing homes, and missed work instead of preventing those costs.
Administrative failures are projected to trigger $140 million in food-assistance penalties beginning in 2027, while childcare matching funds, utility savings, and summer food support go unused or are turned down.
The approach Devon’s approach is to fix state-created failures, recover the federal dollars Hoosiers already paid for, and redirect spending from costly outcomes to prevention.
Indiana has the resources; what it needs is the follow-through to use them for the people who paid for them.
