Perron is an Air Force veteran, career law enforcement officer, and former Southwick Selectboard member running for State Representative in the 3rd Hampden District as an Independent.
He brings nearly three decades of public service experience, including work as a union representative and negotiator, and a proven record of standing his ground and doing what’s right — even when it isn’t easy.
Perron decided to run because he is not the type of person who can sit back, complain, and do nothing.
After years of hearing people say they weren’t getting what they needed from government — and watching election cycles go by with little competition — he chose to step up.
Perron compares this campaign to the biggest “firsts” in his life: his first day of school, basic training, becoming a police officer, husband, father, and serving on the Selectboard — moments that required him to learn, grow, and earn it.
On the Selectboard, Perron led the effort to establish Southwick’s municipal aggregation program after researching why neighboring towns were securing lower electric rates for residents.
He also brought forth proposed zoning and policy changes to support responsible economic development and authored Southwick’s town flag policy to prevent constitutional and civil rights conflicts.
His campaign is focused on cost of living, government transparency, fair funding for Western Massachusetts, and practical solutions that put people over party.
My Key Issues Conservative Independent- People over Political Parties Conservative Independent — People Over Party I’m a Conservative Independent because I believe in fiscal responsibility, individual liberty, public safety, local control, personal responsibility, and accountable government.
Conservative also describes how I approach government: research the issue, understand the cost, consider the consequences, and don’t rush into change simply because it sounds good politically.
Independent means I answer to the people I represent—not a political party—and I’m willing to work with anyone, find common ground where we can, and respectfully agree to disagree where we can’t.
