What Nebraska’s Secretary of State Can Do...
Right Now Nebraska law is clear.
The Secretary of State is the chief election officer , responsible for supervising elections, enforcing the law, training county officials, and ensuring transparency across all 93 counties. ​ Yes, statutes set the calendar.
Yes, some changes require the Legislature. ​ But here’s what voters deserve to know: ​ There is significant authority already in place to strengthen election integrity without passing a single new law. ​ The issue isn’t legal limitations. ​ It’s a failure to fully use the authority that already exists.
What Can Be Done Today No New Laws Required End Mass Mail Ballot Requests There is no Nebraska law requiring statewide mass mailing of ballot request forms. ​ That was a policy decision, not a mandate. ​ A Secretary of State can: ​ End taxpayer-funded mass mailings Return to request-only absentee voting Reinforce elections as a deliberate, secure process Secure Mail & Early Voting Mail voting is part of Nebraska law. ​ But how it is administered is largely within the Secretary of State’s control.
That includes: ​ Signature verification standards ID-related procedures Training for county election officials Audit and compliance oversight A stronger approach means: ​ Higher verification standards Consistent statewide enforcement Reduced over-reliance on mail voting Enforce Chain-of-Custody & System Integrity Every ballot should be tracked and accounted for from request to final count. ​ The Secretary of State has the authority to: ​ Issue rules and guidance Inspect county election practices Enforce compliance with state law Oversee election systems and vendors That includes strengthening : ​ Ballot handling procedures Transfer and storage controls System access and accountability Nebraska voters shouldn’t be asked to simply “trust the system.” ​ They deserve to verify it .
Rein In Drop Boxes Drop boxes are not required across Nebraska. ​ The Secretary of State has the authority to: ​ Set uniform standards Strengthen security requirements Enforce chain-of-custody procedures Provide guidance on placement and usage This isn’t about eliminating access.
It’s about ensuring security and accountability wherever ballots are handled.
Maintain Clean, Accurate Voter Rolls Maintaining voter rolls is a core responsibility of the Secretary of State. ​ That means: ​ Proactive list maintenance Use of state, federal, and interstate data Removing outdated or ineligible registrations Providing transparency to the public Confidence begins with knowing only eligible voters are on the rolls .
What Real Leadership Looks Like The difference isn’t theory.
It’s execution.
A Secretary of State who fully uses their authority can implement meaningful reforms immediately.
Full Chain-of-Custody Transparency Statewide tracking for every ballot Digital logs with timestamps, seals, and handlers Public visibility into the process Security You Can See Surveillance at drop boxes and ballot processing locations Retention and availability of footage Clear documentation of ballot handling Training & Accountability Across All Counties Standardized statewide procedures Required certification for election officials Consistent enforcement of election laws Real Audits.
Not Check-the-Box.
Expanding audits based on the closeness of the race, not fixed percentages Conducting full hand counts of entire races in selected contests Using transparent, publicly verifiable random selection methods Requiring independent bipartisan observers at every stage Publishing complete methods, data, and results for public review Public Transparency Dashboards Regular reporting on voter roll maintenance Ballot verification and processing data Clear, accessible updates for the public No more “trust us.” You’ll be able to see it for yourself.
System Security & Vendor Oversight Independent security assessments Stronger accountability for election vendors Clear enforcement of standards This Isn’t About Changing the Law It’s about using the law .
Nebraska statutes provide the tools.
What’s been missing is the willingness to use them fully .
The Bottom Line The law sets the floor.
Leadership sets the standard.
