Democratic AG nominee says the Deloitte failure indicates the pitfalls of outsourcing core government services to private contractors
DALLAS, Texas — State Senator Nathan Johnson, the Democratic nominee for Texas Attorney General, said today the failure by a private vendor to process at least 178,000 voter registrations over the past eleven months underscores the importance of reviewing agency contracts for core state services.
Texas has currently in force nearly $600B in state agency contracts with outside vendors.
Votebeat and the Texas Tribune reported this week that the Department of Public Safety withheld registration records voters submitted while renewing a license or updating an address online, some for nearly a year, then released them to counties in a single wave weeks before Election Day.
DPS attributed the failure to an October 2025 software update installed by an outside contractor.
State Senator Paul Bettencourt, a Houston Republican, identified that vendor Sunday as Deloitte.
Texas has the second-largest electorate in the country.
“The state didn’t notice for eleven months that voter registrations weren’t getting through.
How is that possible?
How much is it costing taxpayers to fix?” Johnson said.
“I’m going to review state relationships with outside vendors to ensure that the interests of the people and of this state are protected.
Because as the attorney general, I represent state agencies when something goes wrong with the vendor.
Which increasingly seems to happen.
That old quest to eliminate waste, fraud, and abuse likely has some application in the vast $600B in state outsourcing and contracting.
Johnson emphasized that he’s not alleging corruption in fact underlies this latest failure.
“Organized corruption is difficult.
It relies on a lot of people to execute and get away with it.
But there is also another level of corruption, harder to see, that we are obliged to combat, and that is the weakening of the state to the point where even mistakes and neglect can, in various ways, be exploited to the detriment of the public interest.”
Johnson said the failure traces back to a deliberate choice by Texas Republican leaders.
He explained that the Texas government has been weakened and rendered vulnerable to manipulation, cost overruns, errors, and possibly intentional corruption-in-fact, by years of Republicans pretending to run a “small government”.
For many years Texas leaders have established a routine of appropriating money to agencies for the provision of essential state services, but then not allowing the agency to hire employees to do the work.
The only option available to the agency, then, is to hire an outside vendor.
“While state collaboration with private sector service providers is valid and essential to good government functioning, the scale and scope of this state’s outsourcing of essential government functions raises concerns, particularly in light of repeated failures.”
Overly cozy relationships between the state and vendors could make the state vulnerable to a set of individuals who are not required to answer to the public, while paying far more than the cost of providing the service in-house and surrendering meaningful oversight.
“Outsourcing at this scale, with this little oversight, is fertile soil for corruption,” Johnson said.
“Where we find wrongdoing, we will prosecute it to the fullest extent of the law.
And we will ensure that agency contracts are properly structured, to ensure adequate oversight and recourse in the event of errors, failure, and bad acts.”
Johnson’s anti-corruption platform calls for scrutiny of state contracting, conflict-of-interest review across agencies, and protections for state employees who report misconduct.
