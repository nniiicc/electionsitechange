Skip to content Facebook X-twitter Instagram Menu Contribute Dallas Morning News Dallas Morning News Endorses Nathan Johnson for Texas Attorney General October 15, 2026 There is at least one silver lining to Ken Paxton’s decision to run for the U.S.
Senate : It opens the office of Texas attorney general to new management.
The question is whether voters will elevate someone who can wash away the stench of the Paxton years .
The race pits two state senators against each other: Democrat Nathan Johnson of Dallas and Republican Mayes Middleton of Galveston.
Forget about Rs and Ds.
This is a choice between the candidate who vows to get back to the meat-and-potatoes duties of this office and the candidate who has committed to more culture wars.
We think most Texans want the former because they’re tired of the latter.
Their votes should go to Johnson.
Many of us forget about the core duties of the attorney general’s office because the dominant parties in Texas and other states have turned what should be an apolitical role protecting consumers and state agencies into a lawsuit cannon aimed at political enemies.
Paxton waded into outright corruption.
His top aides provided damning evidence that he had deployed his office to protect a political donor.
Legal talent has fled the office or been forced out.
If someone can restore honor to the attorney general’s office, it’s Johnson.
He became a state senator in 2019, after flipping a red district in Dallas County.
In the years since, we have commended him for his pragmatic style.
In an interview with us, Johnson, 58, said he’s promising voters he won’t politicize the office.
“I have pledged that I will sue Donald Trump if and when he breaks the law,” Johnson said.
“Otherwise, I have better and more important things to do with the time and the resources of the attorney general’s office.” To prove his point, Johnson highlighted instances in which he bucked fellow Democrats based on principle.
For example, his concern for free markets drove him to oppose a bipartisan “anti ESG” law in 2021 that barred the state from investing or contracting with companies boycotting the fossil fuel industry.
A federal judge this year ruled the law unconstitutional.
In 2023, citing property rights, Johnson sided against the Sierra Club and Texas officials who proposed saving a state park on private land by using eminent domain.
Johnson told us he wants to strengthen the enforcement of consumer protection laws, and he even praised some of the work that Paxton’s office had done going after social media titans.
However, he pointed out that Elon Musk, owner of X and a GOP donor, had not been a target.
Middleton didn’t respond to an invitation to meet with us or fill out our Voter Guide.
He seems interested only in speaking to the far-right echo chamber.
His legislative work has been focused on inserting religion into government, and he has declared that “there is no such thing as ‘separation of church and state’ in our Constitution.” In the primary, he ran as “MAGA Mayes,” confirming that he will be a puppet for his party, not an independent legal mind.
Texans of either party who want a competent and principled AG should vote for Johnson.
