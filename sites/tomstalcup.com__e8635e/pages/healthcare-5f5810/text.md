Skip to content Mon – Fri 9:00 -17:00 info@tomstalcup.com 877-943-4328 Twitter Instagram WhatsApp Log in Tom Stalcup Republican Candidate for Congress, MA-4 Home About Events DONATE Healthcare The rising cost of healthcare is not an accident—it’s the result of a system that allows pharmaceutical companies and entrenched interests to set prices far above what families and taxpayers should be paying.
In Massachusetts, many working families are paying hundreds to over a thousand dollars a month when you include premiums, deductibles, and prescription costs.
At the same time, we already have systems that deliver care at little or no cost—proving that lower-cost care is possible.
The problem is that too many families fall into the gap, forced to shoulder a burden that continues to grow year after year.
I will take on the root causes of these costs directly.
That starts with immediate, broad drug price negotiation—without artificial limits on which drugs are included.
We cannot continue with a system that protects monopoly pricing while families struggle to afford basic medications.
We also need to open the door to real competition, including allowing safe, equivalent generics to enter the market and ensuring that public investment in research translates into affordable prices for the public.
We can support innovation without forcing people to pay inflated prices at the pharmacy counter.
We must also ensure our kids don’t start to smoke or use drugs, and to do that we must begin, right now, to use funds from the burgeoning cannabis and vaping industries to fund aggressive prevention: implement hard-hitting local and national public messaging campaigns — like the highly successful ‘Don’t Be a Butthead’ anti-smoking effort at the turn of the century — that make it clear to kids it is not cool to vape, use nicotine, or consume cannabis; block all related advertising everywhere; and create strong disincentives for social media companies and influencers who portray positive or casual usage of any such products.
This campaign is about restoring balance, accountability, and healthy choices.
Healthcare should not be a system where costs rise unchecked while the burden falls on families, towns, and small businesses.
It should be a system that delivers high-quality care at a fair price—and that requires leadership willing to confront powerful interests and change the structure that allows these costs to persist.
That’s exactly what I intend to do.
