“Massachusetts has a housing crisis, and the cost of housing is the number one affordability challenge facing families across this Commonwealth,” said Campbell.
“Question 7 is a common-sense step toward solving it.
By removing the artificial barriers that prevent builders from constructing smaller, more affordable starter homes, we can open the door to homeownership for first-time buyers, young families, and seniors looking to downsize.
I am proud to vote Yes on Question 7 and I encourage every Massachusetts voter to do the same.” During her first term as Attorney General, Campbell has been a relentless advocate for affordable housing in Massachusetts: Enforcing the MBTA Communities Law: Campbell has enforced the landmark law requiring communities served by public transit to allow multifamily housing, opening the door to more homes in more neighborhoods and making it easier for families to afford to stay in the communities they love.
Most recently, the Supreme Judicial Court sided with Campbell, rejecting Marshfield’s challenge to the law and reaffirming that compliance is mandatory.
Taking on Corporate Landlords: Campbell sued five of the nation’s largest corporate landlords and the rent-pricing software companies they used to collude and artificially inflate rent across Massachusetts, because corporate greed should not be the reason working families cannot afford a place to call home.
Securing Homeowner Relief: Campbell secured a first-of-its-kind $10 million settlement for homeowners who were targeted by predatory mortgage lending practices, protecting families from the financial schemes that have stripped wealth from Massachusetts communities for generations.
Fighting for Housing Funding: Campbell has fought in court to protect federal funding for affordable housing programs, securing a full victory when the Trump administration tried to attach unlawful conditions to Fair Housing Assistance Program funding, and blocking the administration’s attempts to gut programs that keep Massachusetts families housed.
Blocking Unfair Utility Rate Hikes: Campbell has blocked $2.5 billion in unfair utility rate hikes, because making housing more affordable means lowering not just the cost of buying a home, but the cost of living in one.
