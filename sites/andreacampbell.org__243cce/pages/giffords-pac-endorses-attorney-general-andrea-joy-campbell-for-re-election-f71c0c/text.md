“With gun violence taking tens of thousands of lives every year, Bay Staters rely on their attorney general to hold those who endanger our communities responsible, enforcing the law without flinching.
Attorney General Andrea Campbell has been a courageous ally standing with victims of gun violence, and has led the charge to make Massachusetts safer,” said former Congresswoman Gabby Giffords.
“She will continue to hold gun traffickers accountable, fight the wave of ghost gun manufacturing, and refuse to let the gun industry abuse the system for its own gain.
I am proud to endorse Andrea Campbell for reelection as attorney general.” “I am deeply honored to receive the endorsement of Gabby Giffords and the Giffords PAC, and am inspired by Gabby’s leadership and commitment to turn her own tragedy into purpose,” said Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell.
“As Attorney General, I have worked to keep illegal guns off our streets, established a Gun Violence Prevention Unit, funded community-based organizations working to eradicate gun violence, and protected our gun safety laws to ensure every Massachusetts’ resident feels safe in their home, school and community.
I will continue to fight to protect the safety and wellbeing of Commonwealth residents.” Since taking office in 2023, Attorney General Campbell has made gun violence prevention a defining priority of her tenure.
She created the first-ever Gun Violence Prevention Unit in the Massachusetts Attorney General’s Office, doing critical work to enforce gun and consumer protection laws, support community-based gun violence prevention efforts, and protect commonsense gun laws from legal challenge.
Her office distributed $1 million to local organizations to address the public health crisis of gun violence, supported vital legislation to prohibit the distribution of ghost guns, and has continued to prosecute the organized criminal networks that put illegal guns on Massachusetts streets.

### About Andrea Joy Campbell: Andrea Joy Campbell’s journey to become Massachusetts’ first Black woman Attorney General didn’t follow a traditional path.
After losing her mother in a car accident as an infant and entering the foster care system while her father was incarcerated, Andrea was raised by a community of family and educators who believed in her potential.
Her twin brother, Andre, died in Department of Correction custody due to inadequate medical care – a tragedy that drives her fight for accountability and fairness in our justice system.
The first in her family to go to college, Andrea went on to graduate from Princeton and UCLA Law, serve as legal counsel to Governor Deval Patrick, and become the first Black woman to serve as Boston City Council President.
Raised in Roxbury and the South End, Andrea is a proud Boston public school graduate who attended five different schools, including Boston Latin.
Her story – of loss, resilience, faith, and opportunity – is an American story of what’s possible when the government works for people and when someone is in your corner fighting for you.
