“We are proud to join our union siblings in endorsing Attorney General Andrea Campbell for reelection,” said 32BJ SEIU Executive Vice President Kevin Brown, who leads the union in Massachusetts.
“The 22,000 janitors, security officers, airport workers, and other essential property service workers in Massachusetts who belong to our union are mostly immigrants and/or people of color.
From the bleakest early days of President Trump’s second term, the Attorney General has boldly defended the rights and dignity of people like our members against the administration’s vicious and terrifying assaults.
Before that, her support for the rights and needs of Uber and Lyft drivers was essential in helping them secure the first statewide union for rideshare drivers in the United States.
In these crucial acts and in so much else, she has proven her dedication to those who often struggle to get by in our society, and we are proud to stand tall in our support for Attorney General Campbell’s re-election.” “The over 85,000 members of 1199SEIU are proud to endorse Attorney General Andrea Campbell for re-election.
Andrea has been a consistent advocate for healthcare workers, with years of commitment to prioritizing the issues working families face on the job and in their communities.
Her dedication is what we need to protect residents from ICE’s terror, safeguard reproductive rights and access to care, and defend our democracy,” said Cari Medina, Executive Vice President of 1199SEIU.
“Attorney General Andrea Campbell continues to be a champion for the working class, and SEIU Local 509 is proud to endorse her for re-election,” said SEIU 509 President Dave Foley.
“Since day one, she has centered our values by putting working people first.
Under her leadership, she has held big corporations accountable for wage theft and worker misclassification, recovering $240 million in stolen wages and penalties to put money back into workers’ pockets.
Additionally, she has served as a vital advocate for protecting immigrants in Massachusetts against ICE and overreach from the Trump administration.
She has stood with us and the people we represent from the very beginning, and we are proud to stand in solidarity with our union siblings to endorse Attorney General Campbell for re-election.” “The members of SEIU are the backbone of this Commonwealth and I am deeply honored to have their support,” said Campbell.
“Essential workers deserve an Attorney General who fights for them as hard as they fight for the rest of us.
I will keep using every tool available to make sure every worker in Massachusetts is paid what they earn, treated with dignity, and protected from exploitation.” During her first term as Attorney General, Campbell has: Lowered costs for workers: Campbell has blocked $2.5 billion in unfair utility rate hikes, cracked down on junk fees draining workers’ paychecks, and sued corporate landlords for colluding to drive up rent.
Recovered stolen wages for workers: Campbell has taken over 3,000 actions against employers for wage theft and violations of sick leave, benefitting 183,000 workers and delivering more than $240 million in unpaid wages and penalties.
Fought for gig workers: Campbell secured a landmark, nation-leading settlement with Uber and Lyft, delivering a $34 per hour minimum wage, healthcare contributions, and paid family and medical leave for thousands of drivers and their families across Massachusetts.
Protected workers from exploitation: Campbell has enforced child labor laws, prosecuted labor trafficking, and cracked down on employers who misclassify workers to avoid paying them what they are owed.
Stood up to corporate power: Campbell has taken on the most powerful corporations in the country, and won.
A jury found Live Nation guilty of running an illegal monopoly at Massachusetts venues including House of Blues, MGM Music Hall at Fenway, and Leader Bank Pavilion.
She secured a $145 million settlement for workers exploited by Amazon’s delivery system and joined the FTC in suing Amazon for anticompetitive practices that hurt consumers.
She sued five of the nation’s largest corporate landlords for colluding to drive up rent.
She secured a $700 million settlement with Google for anticompetitive conduct.

### About Andrea Joy Campbell: Andrea Joy Campbell’s journey to become Massachusetts’ first Black woman Attorney General didn’t follow a traditional path.
After losing her mother in a car accident as an infant and entering the foster care system while her father was incarcerated, Andrea was raised by a community of family and educators who believed in her potential.
Her twin brother, Andre, died in Department of Correction custody due to inadequate medical care – a tragedy that drives her fight for accountability and fairness in our justice system.
The first in her family to go to college, Andrea went on to graduate from Princeton and UCLA Law, serve as legal counsel to Governor Deval Patrick, and become the first Black woman to serve as Boston City Council President.
Raised in Roxbury and the South End, Andrea is a proud Boston public school graduate who attended five different schools, including Boston Latin.
Her story – of loss, resilience, faith, and opportunity – is an American story of what’s possible when the government works for people and when someone is in your corner fighting for you.
