Capitalism has helped create extraordinary innovation, new industries, millions of businesses, and opportunities for generations of Americans.
Entrepreneurship and competition should remain fundamental parts of our economy.
But capitalism works best when there are rules, competition is real, workers have opportunities to advance, consumers are protected, and corporations are held accountable for their actions.
I support responsible capitalism: an economic system that encourages people to build businesses, invest, innovate, and become successful while recognizing that economic freedom must come with economic responsibility.
The goal should not be to punish success.
It should be to make sure the opportunity to succeed is available to more Americans.
Reward Entrepreneurship, Not Exploitation
America should be the best country in the world to start and grow a legitimate business.
Small businesses create jobs, strengthen neighborhoods, introduce new ideas, and provide competition to established corporations.
Federal policy should make entrepreneurship easier by reducing unnecessary regulatory burdens, expanding access to capital, simplifying appropriate tax requirements, and helping small businesses compete for government contracts.
At the same time, being successful should never place a company above the law.
Businesses that compete fairly, treat their employees responsibly, provide good products, and follow the rules should be encouraged to grow.
Companies that deliberately deceive consumers, manipulate markets, exploit workers, or repeatedly violate the law should face meaningful consequences.
Protect Competition and Challenge Monopolies
Capitalism depends on competition.
When a handful of corporations gain excessive control over an industry, they can potentially raise prices, suppress competition, purchase emerging competitors, and make it harder for entrepreneurs to enter the market.
As senator, I would support strong enforcement of antitrust laws and careful scrutiny of mergers that could substantially reduce competition.
The federal government should not protect corporations from competition.
It should protect the competitive marketplace itself.
A healthy economy should constantly create opportunities for the next generation of entrepreneurs to challenge today’s largest companies.
Make the Tax System Fairer
Responsible capitalism requires everyone to contribute fairly to the society that makes economic success possible.
Entrepreneurs and corporations benefit from roads, courts, educated workers, financial markets, public infrastructure, intellectual-property protections, emergency services, and countless other institutions supported by taxpayers.
Our tax system should encourage investment and entrepreneurship without creating special opportunities for extremely wealthy individuals or powerful corporations to avoid reasonable tax obligations through sophisticated loopholes unavailable to ordinary workers.
As senator, I would support simplifying the tax code, examining loopholes that primarily benefit the wealthiest taxpayers, strengthening enforcement against illegal tax evasion, and making it harder to hide income through abusive arrangements.
Success should be rewarded.
Tax avoidance should not be an industry.
Hold Corporate Leadership Accountable
Corporate accountability cannot stop at the bottom of an organization.
When serious corporate misconduct occurs, executives who knowingly directed, authorized, or concealed illegal conduct should not automatically escape responsibility while lower-level employees or shareholders suffer the consequences.
Congress should examine whether existing laws provide adequate individual accountability for executives involved in fraud, deliberate consumer deception, corruption, serious regulatory violations, and other unlawful corporate conduct.
The greater the authority someone possesses within an organization, the greater their responsibility should be for how that authority is exercised.
Support American Workers
Workers and businesses are not natural enemies.
Successful companies need productive employees, and workers benefit when businesses grow.
Responsible capitalism should encourage this relationship rather than turning it into a political battle.
Workers should have safe workplaces, fair compensation, protection against wage theft, and opportunities to acquire skills that increase their earning potential.
Congress should invest in apprenticeships, vocational education, community colleges, technical training, and workforce-development partnerships that prepare Americans for jobs in construction, advanced manufacturing, health care, energy, artificial intelligence, robotics, cybersecurity, and other growing industries.
The strongest long-term strategy for raising wages is building a workforce whose skills are increasingly valuable.
Encourage Companies to Invest in Their Employees
Government cannot dictate every business decision, nor should it.
But public policy can create incentives for companies to invest in their workers.
Tax policy and federal programs can encourage businesses to provide apprenticeships, professional certifications, continuing education, employee ownership opportunities, and other programs that help workers build careers rather than simply hold jobs.
When productivity and profits grow, workers should have opportunities to participate in that prosperity.
Protect Consumers
Free markets require informed consumers.
Companies should compete by offering better products, better prices, and better service—not by hiding fees, manipulating contracts, selling personal information deceptively, or making it intentionally difficult for customers to cancel services.
Congress can strengthen transparency requirements and consumer protections while avoiding regulations that unnecessarily burden honest businesses.
Consumers should be able to understand what they are buying, what it costs, and what obligations they are accepting.
Protect Americans’ Personal Data
Personal information has become an extraordinarily valuable commodity.
Technology companies, retailers, financial institutions, data brokers, and countless online services can collect enormous amounts of information about individuals.
Americans deserve meaningful control over that information.
I would support strong national data-privacy standards requiring greater transparency about what information companies collect, how it is used, how long it is retained, and when it is sold or transferred.
Companies that profit from Americans’ data should also have a responsibility to protect it.
Stop Corporate Welfare
Responsible capitalism applies to government as well as corporations.
Politicians should not condemn government intervention while quietly providing special advantages to politically connected industries.
Tax incentives, subsidies, grants, and government contracts should serve legitimate public purposes rather than simply transferring taxpayer money to companies with effective lobbying operations.
When government assistance is necessary—for example, during a genuine national emergency—it should include transparency, oversight, and appropriate conditions.
Capitalism should mean competition, not privatizing profits while taxpayers absorb losses.
Encourage Long-Term Investment
Public companies can face enormous pressure to deliver immediate financial results.
But some of America’s greatest companies were built by making investments that took years to produce returns.
Responsible capitalism should encourage long-term thinking: research and development, employee training, modern factories, infrastructure, cybersecurity, new technologies, and expansion into emerging industries.
Our economy becomes stronger when companies build lasting productive capacity rather than concentrating exclusively on the next quarter.
Defend the Right to Become Wealthy
Responsible capitalism is not anti-wealth.
Someone who creates an innovative company, takes enormous financial risks, employs thousands of people, or develops a valuable invention should have the opportunity to become extraordinarily successful.
That possibility is one of capitalism’s greatest incentives.
The question is not whether Americans should be allowed to become rich.
They absolutely should.
The question is whether the rules remain fair once someone becomes rich.
Economic success should not purchase immunity from taxation, competition, consumer protections, environmental standards, or the law.
Give Small Businesses a Fighting Chance
The neighborhood restaurant, independent contractor, family-owned store, startup, repair shop, manufacturer, and professional practice should have a genuine opportunity to compete.
Small businesses often lack the armies of lawyers, lobbyists, accountants, and compliance departments available to major corporations.
Congress should continually review regulations to determine whether they unintentionally protect established corporations by making market entry unnecessarily expensive for smaller competitors.
A regulation that only the largest companies can afford to comply with can sometimes strengthen the very corporations it was intended to regulate.
Smart regulation should protect the public without creating artificial barriers to entrepreneurship.
Capitalism With Responsibility
The debate about our economy should not be reduced to choosing between unlimited corporate power and government control.
There is another approach.
- We can defend private property while demanding corporate accountability.
- We can celebrate entrepreneurs while fighting monopolies.
- We can encourage wealth creation while closing abusive tax loopholes.
- We can support businesses while protecting workers and consumers.
- We can reduce unnecessary regulations while aggressively enforcing laws against fraud and corruption.
- And we can believe in free enterprise while recognizing that markets require rules to remain genuinely free.
As senator, I would fight for an economy where success comes from building something valuable—not manipulating the system.
Responsible capitalism means opportunity with accountability, competition without exploitation, and prosperity that strengthens the country as a whole.
America does not need to abandon capitalism.
We need to make capitalism work better.
