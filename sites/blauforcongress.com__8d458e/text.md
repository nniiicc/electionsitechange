About Shimon Denver is more than where I live.
It's who I am.
I was born and raised in Denver as the son of immigrants who came here to build a better life.
My parents chose Denver not just as a place to live, but as a community to serve and call home.
Their example taught me the importance of hard work, compassion, and giving back.
I have carried those values throughout my life, as a physician and small business owner.
Today, my wife and I are proud to continue building our future in this city I love.
Denver Roots I was born and raised in Denver's close-knit Sloan's Lake neighborhood, a city that has always been home.
My parents, George and Hermine, were immigrants who chose to build their lives here.
My father was a Holocaust survivor from Poland who served in the U.S.
Army during the Korean War before earning an engineering degree from Columbia University.
He helped build bridges, contributed to the Apollo space shuttle program, and later transitioned into the real estate business.
He loved Denver deeply, especially the mountains that surround our city.
He skied well into his 80s, and the outdoors remained one of his greatest joys throughout his life.
My mother was from France and worked alongside him while creating a home defined by generosity, hospitality, and the belief that there was always room for one more at the table.
Their love for Denver was expressed in countless ways, from supporting the arts and cultural institutions that make this city special to donating benches throughout the community, quiet reminders of their belief that public spaces belong to everyone and should bring people together.
They also taught me to value civic engagement, philanthropy, and service to others.
Those lessons stayed with me, and I have proudly continued that tradition by supporting many of the same arts and cultural organizations that make Denver such a vibrant place to live.
I still remember my mother making stacks of sandwiches before driving downtown because she never wanted to pass someone in need without offering a meal.
Those everyday acts of kindness taught me that serving your community isn't something you talk about, it's something you do.
Coming Home At 13, I left Denver to pursue my education.
Medical school, residency, and specialized training took me across the country, but I always knew I wanted to come home.
Returning in 2013 to complete my fellowship in Interventional Spine and Pain Management fulfilled a dream I had carried for years.
Since then, I've been proud to build my medical practice and dedicate my career to serving the community that helped shape me.
Listening to Denver As a physician, I've had the privilege of treating patients throughout the Denver metro area from every walk of life.
On a daily basis, I've heard their stories, the challenges of paying for health care, affording housing, raising children, caring for aging parents, and simply trying to build a secure future.
These conversations have given me a deep appreciation for both the resilience of our community and the challenges that so many families face.
As a small business owner and employer, I also understand the realities of running a business in today's economy.
I've experienced firsthand the challenges of meeting payroll, managing rising costs, navigating regulations, and caring for employees while continuing to provide high-quality care.
Small businesses are the backbone of our local economy, and I know how important it is that they have the opportunity to succeed.
I also believe Denver must remain a city where businesses of every size can thrive.
Too many longtime businesses have struggled or closed their doors in recent years.
We should be working to reverse that trend by fostering an environment that supports entrepreneurship, encourages investment, strengthens public safety, and helps local businesses grow and create good-paying jobs.
A stronger Denver begins with a stronger local economy.
A different kind of campaign Why I'm Running These experiences, as a physician and a small business owner, have convinced me that Washington has become too focused on political parties and not focused enough on people.
Too often, partisan politics and the pursuit of power come at the expense of solving real problems for real families.
That's why I'm running for Congress as an Independent.
I believe representatives should answer to the people they serve, not political parties, special interests, or Political Action Committees.
That's why I reject campaign contributions from Political Action Committees (PACs) and special interest groups.
Consequently, I am beholden to no one but the people of Colorado's First Congressional District.
I believe character matters more than party affiliation, and I believe Americans are best served by leaders who are willing to listen, find common ground, and solve problems together.
I am neither a Socialist nor MAGA.
I reject the ideological extremes on both the left and the right because I believe most people are looking for practical solutions, respectful dialogue, and leaders willing to put the country ahead of partisan politics.
I support a woman's right to make her own healthcare decisions, and I support the freedom of consenting adults to marry the person they love.
These are deeply personal decisions, and I do not believe government should interfere in women's healthcare or in the private lives of consenting adults.
If elected, I will represent every resident of our district, regardless of political party or whether you voted for me.
I intend to caucus with the Democrats and support Rep.
Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker because I believe that is the most effective way to advocate for our district and serve as a check on the Trump administration.
But caucusing with a party does not mean surrendering my independence.
I will support ideas that are good for Colorado and oppose policies that are not, regardless of which party proposes them.
