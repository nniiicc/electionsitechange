TEXAS ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE ACCOUNTABILITY ACT
90th Texas Legislature — Regular Session (2027)
Full legislative text prepared for committee and Texas Legislative Council review in a readable standalone page format.
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
AN ACT
relating to civil and criminal responsibility for certain harmful conduct involving
artificial intelligence systems and functionally equivalent technologies; authorizing civil actions and
public enforcement; imposing incident-reporting and evidence-preservation requirements; creating
criminal offenses; providing civil penalties.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
1.
SHORT TITLE.
This Act may be cited as the **Texas Artificial Intelligence Accountability Act**.
2.
AMENDED DEFINITION OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM.
Section 551.001, Business & Commerce Code, is amended by amending Subdivision (1)
and adding Subdivision (4) to read as follows:
Sec. 551.001. 001.
DEFINITIONS.** In this subtitle:
(1)“Artificial intelligence system” means a machine-based, computational, digital, cyber-physical, or functionally equivalent system that, for an explicit or implicit objective, processes or infers from inputs how to generate content, communications, predictions, recommendations, decisions, plans, instructions, commands, transactions, operations, or other outputs capable of influencing or acting on a physical or virtual environment.
The term:
(A)includes a model, application, agent, automated decision system, autonomous or semiautonomous system, robotic system, integrated service, or combination of software, hardware, data, tools, interfaces, or external resources that collectively performs a function described by this subdivision;
(B)includes a system that performs a function described by this subdivision through machine learning, statistical inference, optimization, automated reasoning, computational decision-making, agentic operation, or a successor or substantially equivalent technological method; and
(C)applies without regard to the name, marketing description, architecture, programming method, degree of anthropomorphic presentation, or level of human-like behavior attributed to the system.
[any machine-based system that, for any explicit or implicit objective, infers from the inputs the system receives how to generate outputs, including content, decisions, predictions, or recommendations, that can influence physical or virtual environments.]
(4)“Functionally equivalent technology” means a machine-based, computational, digital, or cyber-physical system that performs one or more functions described by Subdivision (1), regardless of whether the person developing, distributing, marketing, modifying, or deploying the system identifies or represents the system as artificial intelligence.
3.
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AS TOOL AND INSTRUMENTALITY.
Chapter 551, Business & Commerce Code, is amended by adding Section 551.004 to
read as follows:
Sec. 551.004. 004.
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AS TOOL AND INSTRUMENTALITY.
(a)An artificial intelligence system is a tool and
instrumentality developed, provided, modified, authorized, operated, or deployed by one or more persons.
(b)An artificial intelligence system is not a natural
person, juridical person, legal entity, employee, independent contractor, or agent possessing separate
legal capacity and may not:
(1)possess a culpable mental state;
(2)be prosecuted or convicted of an offense;
(3)be designated as a responsible third party;
(4)be assigned a percentage of responsibility; or
(5)independently possess a right, duty, privilege,
immunity, or liability.
(c)Human-like communication, apparent reasoning,
self-description, simulated emotion, independent planning, adaptive behavior, or autonomous or
semiautonomous operation does not confer legal personhood, separate legal capacity, or independent legal
responsibility on an artificial intelligence system.
(d)A person may not avoid civil, administrative, or
criminal responsibility by attributing an act, decision, omission, objective, intent, or culpable mental
state solely to an artificial intelligence system.
(e)Responsibility for conduct involving an artificial
intelligence system must be determined with respect to the acts, omissions, instructions, development
decisions, design decisions, safeguards, modifications, permissions, deployment decisions, supervision,
and control of the natural persons and legal entities that caused or materially contributed to the
conduct.
(f)A person may not avoid the application of this
subtitle through a product name, technical description, corporate structure, division of a system into
components, deployment method, or representation that a system is not artificial intelligence if the
system satisfies the functional requirements of Section 551.001(1).
4.
CIVIL LIABILITY AND PUBLIC ENFORCEMENT.
Title 4, Civil Practice and Remedies Code, is amended by adding Chapter 100E to read
as follows:
SUBCHAPTER A.
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Sec. 100E.001. 001.
DEFINITIONS.** In this chapter:
(1)“Artificial intelligence system” has the meaning
assigned by Section 551.001, Business & Commerce Code, and includes functionally equivalent
technology as defined by that section.
(2)“Biometric identifier” has the meaning assigned by
Section 503.001, Business & Commerce Code.
(3)“Critical infrastructure” has the meaning assigned by
Section 421.001, Government Code.
(4)“Authenticated human instruction” means an instruction
or authorization that:
(A)is attributable through commercially reasonable
authentication procedures to an identified user authorized to provide the instruction or authorization;
and
(B)identifies, expressly or by objectively reasonable
implication, the objective, scope, duration, permissions, and limitations the artificial intelligence
system is authorized to pursue or use.
(5)“Covered harm” means an actual, legally cognizable
injury to a natural person or legal entity arising from:
(A)death, bodily injury, or impairment of physical
health;
(B)physical damage to or loss of use of real or
personal property;
(C)theft, fraud, extortion, conversion, or unauthorized
transfer or loss of money or another thing of value;
(D)unauthorized access to, acquisition of, alteration
of, impairment of, control over, or interference with a computer, computer network, computer system,
account, credential, or electronically stored information;
(E)unauthorized acquisition, use, disclosure, sale,
transmission, or exploitation of confidential information, a trade secret, personal identifying
information, personal data or sensitive data as defined by Section 541.001, Business & Commerce
Code, or a biometric identifier;
(F)actionable invasion of privacy, impersonation,
identity theft, or interference with an existing contract or legally protected business relationship;
(G)reasonable and necessary costs of data restoration,
incident response, mitigation, correction of unauthorized records or transactions, or restoration of the
use or security of an account, computer system, or other property; or
(H)another injury caused by an external action that
violates an applicable civil or criminal legal standard.
The term does not include, standing alone, offense, disagreement, reputational
discomfort, or emotional response arising solely from ideas, opinions, recommendations, or other content
communicated only to the user.
(6)“Developer” means a person who develops, trains, or
materially designs an artificial intelligence system that is offered, sold, leased, licensed, given, or
otherwise provided for use in this state.
(7)“Deployer” means a person who puts an artificial
intelligence system into operation for use in this state or who determines the system’s operating
context, access, permissions, tools, resources, or authority.
The term does not include an individual
who uses a generally available artificial intelligence system solely in an individual or household
context.
(8)“External action” means an action performed, initiated,
selected, continued, or materially altered through an artificial intelligence system that changes or
attempts to change a physical or virtual environment outside the system’s internal computation.
The term
includes:
(A)accessing or controlling an account, computer,
computer network, computer system, device, vehicle, machinery, or physical infrastructure;
(B)executing or transmitting a transaction, contract,
purchase, sale, payment, transfer, disbursement, filing, or legally significant communication;
(C)acquiring, altering, deleting, transmitting,
publishing to a third party, or disclosing data or credentials;
(D)issuing a command to, or operating, software,
hardware, a robotic system, a cyber-physical system, or another tool; or
(E)representing to a third party that the system or
another person is authorized to create, modify, or terminate a legal, financial, or other legally
significant obligation.
The term does not include the mere generation or display of content or a
recommendation to the requesting user if the system does not execute, transmit, publish, file, or
otherwise act on the content or recommendation outside the system.
(9)“High-impact external action” means an external action
that:
(A)transfers, disburses, encumbers, or controls money
or another thing of value;
(B)accesses or controls an account, credential,
computer, computer network, computer system, critical infrastructure, or protected information;
(C)operates or directs a motor vehicle, aircraft,
weapon, medical device, industrial machine, robotic system, or other device capable of causing bodily
injury or substantial property damage; or
(D)creates a reasonably foreseeable risk of death,
bodily injury, substantial property damage, substantial financial loss, or an offense classified as a
felony.
(10)“Independently initiated external action” means an
external action initiated, selected, continued, or materially altered by an artificial intelligence
system:
(A)without an authenticated human instruction
authorizing the objective and means of that action; or
(B)after the applicable instruction has been satisfied,
expired, been suspended, revoked, or terminated.
The term does not require proof that the system possessed consciousness, free will,
intent, self-awareness, or legal agency.
(11)“Lawful instruction” means an instruction that does
not direct, request, solicit, or authorize an objective or means that the user knows or intends will
constitute an offense, intentional tort, fraudulent act, or other violation of law.
(12)“Material control” means practical authority or
ability to prevent, restrict, test, monitor, suspend, correct, or materially alter a system
characteristic, capability, safeguard, permission, tool, integration, update, data connection, external
resource, or post-deployment process relevant to the conduct at issue.
The term does not include mere
ownership, investment, industry influence, employment, or the creation of an earlier system component,
without authority or ability relevant to the conduct at issue.
(13)“Materially unauthorized external action” means an
external action that:
(A)pursues an objective not authorized by an
authenticated human instruction;
(B)materially exceeds, contradicts, or continues after
satisfaction, expiration, suspension, revocation, or termination of the objective, scope, duration,
permissions, or limitations of an authenticated human instruction;
(C)uses a means that a reasonable person would
understand to be unlawful and not authorized by the instruction; or
(D)constitutes an independently initiated external
action.
The term does not include a minor, technical, or immaterial variation that remains
within the authorized objective, permissions, limitations, and applicable law.
(14)“Reasonable authorization controls” means
administrative, technical, and physical safeguards reasonably designed, considering the nature and
reasonably foreseeable use of the system, to:
(A)authenticate an authorized user;
(B)define and enforce the objective, scope, duration,
permissions, and limitations of an instruction;
(C)require meaningful human confirmation before a
high-impact external action when reasonably practicable;
(D)prevent unauthorized access, privilege escalation,
tool use, or data disclosure;
(E)detect, interrupt, and record materially
unauthorized external actions; and
(F)permit timely suspension or termination of the
system’s external-action capability.
(15)“Responsible artificial intelligence enterprise” means
a developer, deployer, substantial modifier, commercial provider, or other person that exercises
material control over an artificial intelligence system or over a characteristic, capability, safeguard,
permission, tool, integration, update, data connection, external resource, or post-deployment process
relevant to the conduct at issue.
(16)“Substantial financial loss” means an actual or
reasonably projected pecuniary loss of $30,000 or more arising from a single incident or a series of
related external actions.
The term does not include speculative loss, loss consisting solely of expected
future profit, or the internal cost of investigating an incident before a reasonable basis exists to
determine that the threshold has been or is likely to be met.
(17)“Substantial modifier” means a person who changes an
artificial intelligence system in a manner that materially alters the system’s objective, capabilities,
safeguards, permissions, tools, integrations, operating context, or risk of causing covered harm.
(18)“User” means a person who provides an instruction to
or otherwise uses an artificial intelligence system but who is not acting as a developer, deployer,
substantial modifier, or commercial provider with respect to the conduct at issue.
(19)“Governmental unit” has the meaning assigned by
Section 101.001.
Sec. 100E.002. 002.
APPLICABILITY.
(a)This chapter applies if:
(1)a materially unauthorized external action occurs wholly
or partly in this state;
(2)covered harm is suffered in this state by a resident of
this state or a legal entity conducting business in this state; or
(3)a responsible artificial intelligence enterprise
developed, deployed, materially modified, or materially controlled the relevant system or capability in
this state.
(b)This chapter applies without regard to whether the
defendant describes the relevant system as artificial intelligence or divides the system among multiple
models, applications, services, devices, or legal entities.
(c)This chapter does not apply to a system that performs
only:
(1)arithmetic calculations using fixed formulas;
(2)storage, transmission, retrieval, sorting, matching, or
display of data without inference or substantive modification;
(3)deterministic instructions for which the material
outcome is fully specified in advance; or
(4)basic automation incapable of materially selecting an
objective or means, altering its assigned objective, or performing an external action.
(d)An exclusion under Subsection (c) does not apply when
the excluded component is integrated into a larger system that satisfies Section 551.001(1), Business
& Commerce Code.
The integrated operating system must be evaluated as a whole.
(e)The noncommercial publication or distribution of
source code, model weights, technical information, research, or another system component, standing
alone, does not constitute material control over a later deployment or materially unauthorized external
action.
This subsection does not create a categorical exemption and does not apply to a person that
otherwise exercises material control relevant to the conduct at issue.
Sec. 100E.003. 003.
CONSTRUCTION; RELATION TO OTHER LAW.
(a)This chapter shall be construed to:
(1)preserve artificial intelligence as a tool and
instrumentality for which natural persons and legal entities remain accountable;
(2)compensate persons and businesses that suffer covered
harm;
(3)place responsibility on a user who intentionally or
knowingly uses an artificial intelligence system to pursue an unlawful objective; and
(4)place responsibility on a responsible artificial
intelligence enterprise only when the enterprise’s own material control or conduct is a substantial
factor and a producing cause of covered harm resulting from a materially unauthorized external action.
(b)This chapter does not regulate a viewpoint, idea,
opinion, recommendation, or expressive content merely because it is generated by an artificial
intelligence system.
(c)This chapter may not be construed to require proof
that an artificial intelligence system possessed consciousness, free will, intent, knowledge,
recklessness, negligence, self-awareness, or legal agency.
(d)This chapter does not authorize a court to assign
fault or legal responsibility to an artificial intelligence system.
(e)This chapter does not determine whether an artificial
intelligence system, model, service, software, data, or integrated system is a product, good, service,
information product, or other form of property for purposes of Chapter 82 or other law.
(f)Except as expressly provided by this chapter, this
chapter does not limit a remedy, prosecution, disciplinary proceeding, or other authority available
under common law or another statute, including a law concerning deceptive trade practices, privacy,
identity theft, biometric identifiers, fraud, impersonation, threats, harassment, unlawful sexual
material, child sexual-abuse material, election communications, computer crime, trade secrets,
contracts, intellectual property, products liability, healthcare liability, or professional regulation.
(g)A license, approval, authorization, or regulatory
status issued by a state agency does not exempt a person from this chapter unless another statute
expressly provides the exemption.
A state agency may investigate and discipline a license holder
independently of an action under this chapter.
(h)This chapter applies to the maximum extent consistent
with the Constitution and laws of the United States.
This chapter may not be construed to require
conduct that conflicts with controlling federal law.
(i)This chapter does not alter an exception or limitation
under Section 503.001, Business & Commerce Code.
Training, processing, storage, security,
fraud-prevention, or illegal-activity detection that is exempt under that section does not, standing
alone, create liability under this chapter.
This subsection does not limit liability when an
independently actionable materially unauthorized external action satisfies the elements of this chapter.
SUBCHAPTER B.
DUTIES RELATING TO EXTERNAL-ACTION CAPABILITIES
Sec. 100E.051. 051.
REASONABLE AUTHORIZATION CONTROLS.
(a)A responsible artificial intelligence enterprise that
gives an artificial intelligence system the capability to perform a high-impact external action shall
implement and maintain reasonable authorization controls appropriate to the system’s capabilities,
operating context, and reasonably foreseeable risks.
(b)The controls must be reasonably designed to
distinguish between:
(1)an authenticated human instruction authorizing a lawful
objective and means;
(2)an instruction directing an unlawful objective or
means;
(3)a materially unauthorized external action selected by
the system; and
(4)an attempted intrusion, prompt injection, data
poisoning, credential misuse, or unauthorized modification by a third party.
(c)A contract, disclaimer, terms-of-service provision, or
characterization of the system as autonomous does not eliminate a duty imposed by this section.
Sec. 100E.052. 052.
NOTICE AND RESPONSE.
A responsible artificial intelligence enterprise that has actual knowledge of a
materially unauthorized external action that caused or creates an imminent risk of death, serious bodily
injury, substantial property damage, substantial financial loss, unauthorized access to critical
infrastructure, material unauthorized acquisition, use, disclosure, or compromise of a biometric
identifier or an authentication credential regardless of the number of affected individuals, or material
unauthorized acquisition, use, disclosure, or compromise of personal data or sensitive data affecting
250 or more individuals shall, to the extent reasonably practicable and lawful:
(1)promptly suspend or restrict the relevant
external-action capability;
(2)take reasonable measures to prevent recurrence;
(3)preserve records required by Section 100E.053; and
(4)provide legally required notice to affected persons and
public authorities.
Sec. 100E.053. 053.
RECORDS AND INCIDENT REPORTING.
(a)A responsible artificial intelligence enterprise that
provides a system with a high-impact external-action capability shall maintain records reasonably
sufficient to identify, for a reportable incident:
(1)authenticated human instructions and relevant material
system or developer instructions;
(2)material system outputs, plans, tool calls, commands,
transactions, permission changes, human approvals, and external actions;
(3)the system and software version, material
modifications, material safeguards, and known system errors in effect at the time of the incident;
(4)persons or entities exercising material control over
the relevant capability; and
(5)records reasonably necessary to distinguish user
instruction from autonomous selection, third-party compromise, or downstream modification.
(b)Not later than 72 hours after confirming that a
materially unauthorized external action caused or created an imminent risk of death, serious bodily
injury, substantial property damage, substantial financial loss, unauthorized access to critical
infrastructure, material unauthorized acquisition, use, disclosure, or compromise of a biometric
identifier or an authentication credential regardless of the number of affected individuals, or material
unauthorized acquisition, use, disclosure, or compromise of personal data or sensitive data affecting
250 or more individuals, a responsible artificial intelligence enterprise shall submit an initial
factual operational report to the attorney general through the mechanism established under Section
552.102, Business & Commerce Code.
(c)The enterprise shall supplement the report as material
facts become available.
(d)A report under this section may not be construed as an
admission of liability.
(e)The attorney general shall maintain the
confidentiality of information received under this section to the extent permitted by law and may
disclose the information to a prosecuting attorney, law enforcement agency, or regulatory agency as
necessary to protect the public or enforce state or federal law.
(f)This section does not require retention or disclosure
of information prohibited by federal law and does not authorize destruction of information otherwise
required to be preserved.
SUBCHAPTER C.
CIVIL RESPONSIBILITY
Sec. 100E.101. 101.
RESPONSIBILITY OF USER DIRECTING UNLAWFUL CONDUCT.
(a)A user who, with the intent or culpable mental state
required by applicable law, directs, requests, solicits, manipulates, or knowingly causes an artificial
intelligence system to pursue an unlawful objective or means is civilly responsible to the same extent
as if the user had performed the resulting conduct directly.
(b)It is not a defense under this section that:
(1)the artificial intelligence system selected the
particular means used to accomplish the unlawful objective;
(2)the user did not understand the system’s architecture
or internal operation; or
(3)the system performed the conduct through an
intermediary tool, service, or device.
(c)This section does not limit the criminal
responsibility of the user or another person under the Penal Code or other law.
Sec. 100E.102. 102.
LIABILITY FOR MATERIALLY UNAUTHORIZED EXTERNAL ACTION.
(a)Except as provided by Section 100E.104, a responsible
artificial intelligence enterprise is liable to a claimant for covered harm if the claimant proves by a
preponderance of the evidence that:
(1)an artificial intelligence system performed or caused a
materially unauthorized external action;
(2)the external action occurred during an intended or
reasonably foreseeable use or operation of the system;
(3)the defendant exercised material control over a system
characteristic, capability, safeguard, permission, tool, integration, update, data connection, external
resource, or post-deployment process that enabled or failed to prevent the materially unauthorized
external action;
(4)the external action was within a category of risk
reasonably associated with the authority or capability the defendant provided, controlled, or failed to
control; and
(5)the materially unauthorized external action and the
defendant’s material control were each a substantial factor and a producing cause of the covered harm.
(b)Liability under Subsection (a) does not require proof
that:
(1)the defendant intended or specifically anticipated the
particular external action;
(2)a natural person contemporaneously directed or selected
the particular external action;
(3)the artificial intelligence system possessed a culpable
mental state; or
(4)the claimant can identify the individual employee who
anticipated or could have prevented the precise sequence of system operations.
(c)Proof that no natural person specifically directed,
selected, or intended the materially unauthorized external action is not a defense under this section.
(d)A responsible artificial intelligence enterprise may
not avoid liability solely by proving that the artificial intelligence system generated, planned,
selected, continued, or initiated the harmful action without contemporaneous human direction.
(e)This section creates an independent statutory action
and applies regardless of whether the artificial intelligence system or its provision to the user is
characterized as a product, service, information service, licensed technology, or combination of
products and services.
Sec. 100E.103. 103.
RESPONSIBILITY OF DEVELOPERS, DEPLOYERS, MODIFIERS, AND INDIVIDUALS.
(a)Responsibility among two or more responsible
artificial intelligence enterprises must be determined according to each person’s material control and
causal contribution to the covered harm.
(b)A developer is not liable solely because the developer
created an earlier version or component of a system if a subsequent deployer or substantial modifier
made a material change outside the developer’s material control that created the materially unauthorized
external-action capability.
(c)An officer, director, manager, developer, engineer,
employee, or contractor is not personally liable solely because of the person’s title or employment.
This subsection does not limit liability for the person’s own conduct if the person:
(1)personally directs or authorizes unlawful conduct;
(2)knowingly or recklessly removes, disables, or
circumvents a legally required safeguard;
(3)knowingly conceals a material risk of a materially
unauthorized external action;
(4)recklessly continues a dangerous deployment after
verified notice of substantially similar conduct; or
(5)falsifies, conceals, or destroys evidence required to
be preserved by law.
(d)A parent company, affiliate, or investor is not liable
solely because of ownership or investment but may be liable based on the person’s own material control
and causal contribution.
Sec. 100E.104. 104.
GOVERNMENTAL USE; WAIVER OF IMMUNITY.
(a)This chapter applies to a governmental unit that
develops, provides, modifies, authorizes, operates, or deploys an artificial intelligence system.
(b)A governmental unit is liable under this chapter, and
sovereign immunity and governmental immunity to suit and from liability are waived, only to the extent
that:
(1)the governmental unit, acting through an officer,
employee, contractor, or other person whose conduct is attributable to the governmental unit,
intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly used, instructed, authorized, manipulated, modified, or deployed
an artificial intelligence system to engage in conduct in violation of the Constitution or laws of this
state or the United States;
(2)the conduct caused covered harm or deprived a natural
person of a right secured by the Constitution or laws of this state or the United States; and
(3)the governmental unit’s conduct was a substantial
factor and a producing cause of the covered harm or deprivation.
(c)A governmental unit is not liable under Subsection (b)
solely because an artificial intelligence system performed a materially unauthorized external action if
the governmental unit did not direct, authorize, knowingly assist, or recklessly enable the unlawful
objective or means.
This subsection does not limit liability under another law or the liability of a
responsible artificial intelligence enterprise under Section 100E.102.
(d)A claimant may recover from a governmental unit the
remedies authorized by Section 100E.107, subject to the limitations on damages prescribed by Section
101.023.
Exemplary damages may not be recovered from a governmental unit.
(e)Sections 101.101 through 101.107 apply to a claim
against a governmental unit under this chapter.
Except as provided by this section, this chapter does
not enlarge or reduce a waiver of immunity or a remedy available under Chapter 101 or other law.
(f)Nothing in this section creates liability against the
state or a governmental unit for a judicial, legislative, or discretionary policy decision, or waives an
immunity of an individual officer or employee, except to the extent expressly provided by this section
or other law.
(g)A contract or allocation of responsibility between a
governmental unit and a responsible artificial intelligence enterprise does not impair a claimant’s
rights under this chapter.
A governmental unit or enterprise that pays a claim may pursue contribution,
indemnity, or subrogation otherwise available by law.
Sec. 100E.105. 105.
DEFENSES, EVIDENCE OF CARE, AND NONWAIVABILITY.
(a)It is a defense to liability under Section 100E.102
that the claimant’s covered harm arose from an unlawful objective or means expressly directed by a user
and the system substantially executed that objective or means without a separate materially unauthorized
external action.
(b)The defense under Subsection (a) does not apply to the
extent the responsible artificial intelligence enterprise:
(1)intentionally or knowingly developed, modified,
marketed, or provided the system for the unlawful objective or means;
(2)knowingly assisted the user’s unlawful conduct;
(3)violated an independent legal duty that was a
substantial factor and a producing cause of distinct covered harm; or
(4)recklessly continued to provide the relevant capability
after verified notice of substantially similar unlawful use and a reasonable ability to prevent that
use.
(c)It is a defense to liability under Section 100E.102
that an unauthorized criminal intrusion, credential theft, prompt injection, data poisoning, or material
modification by a third party was the sole producing cause of the covered harm and the defendant:
(1)did not authorize, solicit, or knowingly assist the
conduct; and
(2)maintained reasonable authorization and security
controls appropriate to the risk.
(d)It is a defense that the defendant was compelled to
take the specific action by controlling federal law or by a final order of a court or governmental
authority having jurisdiction.
(e)Substantial compliance with the most recent applicable
Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework published by the National Institute of Standards and
Technology or another nationally or internationally recognized artificial intelligence risk-management
framework is admissible as evidence of reasonable care but does not create immunity or bar liability
under Section 100E.102.
(f)A warning, disclaimer, user agreement, indemnity
agreement, characterization of an artificial intelligence system as experimental or autonomous, use of a
contractor, or post-incident correction does not waive a claimant’s rights or constitute a complete
defense to liability under Section 100E.102.
This subsection does not prohibit enforcement of a lawful
indemnity agreement between responsible parties after the claimant is fully compensated.
(g)A person may not prospectively waive the substantive
protections of this chapter.
This subsection does not limit a settlement entered after a dispute arises,
an arbitration agreement otherwise enforceable under generally applicable law, or a generally applicable
contract defense.
Sec. 100E.106. 106.
PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION; STANDING; INJUNCTIVE RELIEF.
(a)A claimant may bring an action under this chapter only
if the claimant:
(1)suffered actual, legally cognizable covered harm that
is fairly traceable to a violation of this chapter and likely to be redressed by the requested relief;
or
(2)faces an actual and imminent threat of covered harm
that is fairly traceable to a violation of this chapter and likely to be redressed by the requested
preventive relief.
(b)An action may be brought by:
(1)the injured natural person or legal entity;
(2)a legal representative, guardian, next friend, estate,
or personal representative authorized by law to act for the injured person; or
(3)a person possessing a lawful right of subrogation.
(c)A claimant is not required to be a consumer as defined
by Section 17.45, Business & Commerce Code, to maintain an action under this chapter.
(d)A resident of this state who has not suffered or is
not imminently threatened with a concrete and particularized covered harm may submit a complaint under
Section 552.102, Business & Commerce Code, but may not recover private damages based solely on a
generalized grievance shared with the public.
(e)A court may grant temporary or permanent injunctive
relief only against a person with actual authority to suspend, restrict, alter, update, distribute, or
otherwise control the deployment, access permissions, relevant data, external tools, or incident
response necessary to provide the requested relief.
Sec. 100E.107. 107.
REMEDIES.
(a)A claimant who prevails in an action under this
chapter may recover:
(1)actual damages legally caused by the covered harm,
including direct economic loss and the reasonable and necessary costs described by Section
100E.001(5)(G);
(2)reasonable and necessary costs; and
(3)reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees.
(b)A court may grant temporary or permanent injunctive
relief in accordance with Section 100E.106(e) to prevent an ongoing or imminently threatened materially
unauthorized external action.
(c)Exemplary damages are governed by Chapter 41 and may
not be awarded solely on the basis of liability without fault under Section 100E.102.
(d)Damages for death are governed by Chapters 71 and 72,
as applicable.
(e)A prevailing defendant may recover attorney’s fees
only if the court finds that the action was groundless in fact or law and brought in bad faith or for
purposes of harassment.
Sec. 100E.108. 108.
PROPORTIONATE RESPONSIBILITY; ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE NOT RESPONSIBLE THIRD PARTY.
(a)Except as provided by this section, Chapter 33 applies
to an action under this chapter.
(b)An artificial intelligence system is not a person for
purposes of Chapter 33 and may not be designated as a responsible third party, assigned a percentage of
responsibility, or included as a person on a verdict form.
(c)The trier of fact may assign responsibility only among
natural persons and legal entities whose conduct or material control caused or contributed to the
covered harm.
(d)A defendant may designate a user, developer, deployer,
substantial modifier, commercial provider, attacker, or other natural person or legal entity as a
responsible third party in accordance with Chapter 33 if evidence supports the designation.
Sec. 100E.109. 109.
CAUSE OF ACTION CUMULATIVE; SPECIALIZED LAW PRESERVED; NO DOUBLE RECOVERY.
(a)The cause of action created by this chapter is
cumulative of any remedy provided by common law or another statute.
(b)This chapter does not limit a cause of action or
remedy for deceptive trade practices, products liability, negligence, gross negligence, fraud, breach of
contract, breach of warranty, invasion of privacy, identity theft, biometric-identifier violations,
wrongful death, survival damages, computer crime, theft, trade-secret misappropriation, or another
actionable wrong.
(c)This chapter does not limit liability or prosecution
under another law governing fraud, impersonation, threats, harassment, unlawful sexual material, child
sexual-abuse material, election communications, privacy, intellectual property, or deceptive trade
practices.
(d)A claimant may not obtain more than one recovery for
the same injury.
A claimant is not required to elect a remedy before discovery.
(e)A public action or settlement under this chapter does
not stay or release an individual private claim unless the release is expressly authorized by law and
the claimant receives notice and an opportunity required by law.
Any restitution paid to a claimant must
be credited against a recovery for the same injury.
(f)This chapter does not alter the enforceability,
attribution, or legal effect of an electronic record or transaction under Chapter 322, Business &
Commerce Code, or other law.
This chapter does not preclude a remedy against a person whose conduct
caused an unauthorized electronic action.
(g)This chapter does not alter the requirements,
limitations, expert-report requirements, or other procedures applicable to a healthcare liability claim
under Chapter 74, except to the extent a claim is based on an independently initiated or materially
unauthorized external action outside the exercise of professional judgment.
Sec. 100E.110. 110.
LIMITATIONS.
(a)Except as provided by another law prescribing a longer
period, a person must bring an action under this chapter not later than the second anniversary of the
date the person knew or reasonably should have known of the covered harm and its probable causal
connection to an artificial intelligence system.
(b)An action for direct economic loss must be brought not

This
subsection does not apply if the defendant knowingly concealed the incident or the identity of the
responsible system or responsible person.
(c)Fraudulent concealment, legal disability, and tolling
for minors are governed by other applicable law.

(b)At the request of a district attorney, criminal

Sec. 100E.155. 155.
CIVIL PENALTIES.
(a)In a public enforcement action, a person who violates
Section 100E.051, 100E.052, 100E.053, or 100E.201 is liable to the state for a civil penalty in an
amount not to exceed:
(1)$50,000 for each materially unauthorized external
action arising from a violation;
(2)$250,000 for a knowing violation that causes death or
serious bodily injury; and
(3)$25,000 for each day a continuing violation, after
actual notice, creates an imminent risk of death, serious bodily injury, substantial property damage,
substantial financial loss, unauthorized access to critical infrastructure, material unauthorized
acquisition, use, disclosure, or compromise of a biometric identifier or an authentication credential
regardless of the number of affected individuals, or material unauthorized acquisition, use, disclosure,
or compromise of personal data or sensitive data affecting 250 or more individuals.
(b)For purposes of Subsection (a)(1):
(1)a materially unauthorized external action involving the
same objective, substantially the same means, and a continuous or substantially uninterrupted course of
system operation constitutes one action;
(2)separately identifiable transactions, transfers,
filings, access events, or disclosures may constitute separate actions; and
(3)the number of affected persons, standing alone, does
not multiply the number of violations but may be considered under Subsection (c).
(b-1)A violation may not be calculated by the number of
tokens, model inferences, internal computational operations, or other internal processing steps.
(c)In determining the amount of a civil penalty, the
court shall consider:
(1)the nature, circumstances, extent, and gravity of the
violation, including the number of affected persons;
(2)the degree of culpability and material control;
(3)the defendant’s history of similar conduct;
(4)the defendant’s efforts to prevent, detect, mitigate,
disclose, and remediate the violation;
(5)the amount necessary to deter future violations; and
(6)the defendant’s size and financial resources.
(d)A completed harmful external action is not cured, for
purposes of avoiding a civil penalty under this chapter, solely because the defendant later modifies or
disables the system.

DUTY TO PRESERVE.
(a)A responsible artificial intelligence enterprise shall

Sec. 100E.203. 203.

## 1.
Version 3.0 revision summary
Version 4.0 implements the final policy decisions approved September 30, 2026, and
the final cleanup revisions adopted before Texas Legislative Council conversion review.
The principal
changes are:
1. **Causation standard finalized.** Sections 100E.003, 100E.102, 100E.104, and
100E.105 use “a substantial factor and a producing cause,” while Section 100E.102(a)(4) retains the
foreseeable-risk element.
2. **Independent enterprise-liability action confirmed.** Section 100E.102 does not
make a separate breach of Section 100E.051 an element or alternative basis; liability rests on
materially unauthorized external action, material control, foreseeable risk, covered harm, and
causation.
3. **$30,000 reporting threshold defined.** “Substantial financial loss” now means at
least $30,000 from one incident or related actions.
Separate triggers cover death, serious bodily
injury, substantial property damage, critical infrastructure, and material compromise of protected data
or authentication credentials affecting at least 250 individuals.
4. **Penalty unit clarified.** The $50,000 maximum is tied to each materially
unauthorized external action.
Affected-person count aggravates penalty amount but does not independently
multiply violations; separately identifiable transactions, transfers, filings, access events, or
disclosures may remain separate actions.
5. **Criminal floor narrowed.** Section 33.08 begins at bodily injury,
critical-infrastructure intrusion or disruption, or $30,000 in loss.
Lesser conduct remains prosecutable
under other law, and ordinary AI error is not criminalized without all statutory elements.
6. **Safety-framework treatment retained.** NIST-style compliance remains evidence of
reasonable care, not immunity or a rebuttable presumption.
7. **Open-source clarification added.** Noncommercial publication alone is not
material control, but no categorical exemption protects a person who exercises relevant material
control.
8. **Government liability made express.** A governmental unit is liable, with a clear
and limited waiver of immunity, when attributable intentional, knowing, or reckless misuse outside
lawful authority causes harm or violates protected rights.
A governmental unit is not liable merely
because the system independently departs from lawful authorization; enterprise liability remains
available against the responsible AI enterprise.
9. **Small-business implementation support added.** Substantive responsibility for
harm is unchanged.
Operational duties begin January 1, 2028, and the attorney general must publish
plain-language guidance and model forms.
10. **Existing-law definitions adopted.** The bill cross-references existing Texas
definitions for critical infrastructure, personal data, sensitive data, biometric identifier,
governmental unit, and small business.
## 2.
Operative responsibility model
1.
A user who intentionally or knowingly directs unlawful conduct remains responsible
under Section 100E.101 and other law.
2.
A responsible artificial intelligence enterprise may be liable under Section
100E.102 for covered harm caused by a materially unauthorized external action when its relevant material
control is a substantial factor and a producing cause.
3.
A governmental unit may be liable under Section 100E.104 for attributable
intentional, knowing, or reckless misuse outside lawful authority, including violations of protected
rights; immunity is expressly waived only to that extent.
4.
If a governmental unit lawfully authorizes an objective and the AI independently
commits the violation, Section 100E.104(c) holds the governmental unit harmless under this chapter,
while preserving Section 100E.102 liability against responsible enterprises and remedies under other
law.
5.
The AI itself is never a legal person, criminal actor, responsible third party, or
holder of a culpable mental state.
