OUR KIDS’ MENTAL HEALTH
Colorado is one of the healthiest states in America, but when it comes to our kids' mental health, we're not just failing — we're flunking.
Suicide is one of the leading causes of death for young people.
Dozens of Colorado counties have few to no child psychiatrist.
I know what childhood trauma does to a young person because I lived it, and I'm here to tell you this isn't a bumper sticker issue for me — it's personal.
As governor, my priorities are straightforward: drive down youth suicide, close the psychiatry gap in rural Colorado, crack down on youth substance abuse, and hold tech companies accountable to address the growing mental health risks associated with social media use.
In my first 12 months, I'll sign an Executive Order treating children's mental health like the emergency it actually is — with 90-day benchmarks, not annual reports that gather dust on the shelves of bureaucrats.
I will urgently expand telehealth counseling for underserved school districts, prioritize early identification and support in our schools, and work with faith communities and nonprofits who are already doing this work better than government — and doing it cheaper.
Legislatively, we need loan forgiveness to encourage child psychiatrists to rural counties.
While Colorado has made some recent progress on these issues, we must strengthen enforcement of our existing mental health insurance parity laws and go much further by passing a comprehensive Social Media Safety Act.
We also need full funding for the 988 crisis line to ensure every child in crisis gets a fast, human response.
Colorado has the resources.
We've just been too slow and too timid to use them.
That ends on Day One.
