Amy’s Affordability Agenda
Our families are feeling pinched with increased housing prices, the lingering effects of Trump Tariffs, increased gas prices due to Trump’s adventures in the Middle East and inflation that continues to hit working families.
In Olympia, I have fought predatory loan sharks, passed trailblazing legislation to increase housing supply and lower costs, and demanded tax relief for our most vulnerable.
My affordability agenda includes tax relief to the working class and small businesses, increased investments in housing, and pushing for quality childcare and early education through state funding and reform.
- There is an “arc” of housing for all of the people of our state as I see it, and that starts with the creation of lots of units.
High interest rates and development costs mean we need to repurpose existing buildings to create housing for families, and we need to do everything we can to ensure we have a competitive environment for home builders.
- State Government has a huge role to play in managing and spurring housing growth.
Housing has been one of my biggest priorities, and my work has helped turn the Seattle metropolitan area into the largest builder of affordable apartments in the country from 2020-2024.
- I’ve funded many affordable housing projects in the 48th District, appropriating $2M for the Illahee Affordable Housing project in Bellevue, $260K for the New Ground Young Adult Affordable Housing project in Kirkland and $3.2M for the Redmond Supportive Housing project
- In the 2026 legislative session, I was the prime sponsor of permitting and zoning reform bills that unleashed conversion of unused commercial spaces to residential units.
- I also supported the passage of a bill that allowed religious organizations to build affordable housing in residential neighborhoods, and redundant obstacles to housing construction.
- In the 2026 legislative session, I also voted for increased incentives and permitting reform to build dense housing near public transit corridors, like the Light Rail.
Developing housing along transit corridors helps our seniors age in their home towns, letting them downsize without leaving their neighborhood.
It also gives our young people the opportunity to own homes without having to own a car.
- Condos are the gateway to home ownership, because they allow for individual units to be bought or rented.
I will fight for the creation of a warranty system which allows condo owners to get repairs done quickly and fairly, as well as incentivizing the apartments we have built to be converted to condos.
- Once we establish this reliable revenue-producing development pipeline, we can build on our successes and spread the wealth to state programs and public-private partnerships that stabilize housing for low income families, immigrants, IDD, sober, and behavioral health communities
Owning a home makes THE difference in closing the generational wealth gap.
Increasing supply, lowering costs, and incentivizing condo growth will all make home ownership for young people an achievable goal rather than a pipe dream.
- Our district is heavily populated by tech workers, and recent layoffs, furloughs, and buyouts at Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Expedia, Zillow, Meta and other tech companies, along with the rise of AI, means that we need focus on expanding the economy with an eye towards AI-resilient work, entrepreneurship, and job growth.
Throughout history, communities that responded to economic hard times with investments in their people and their communities have thrived.
The innovation and entrepreneurship of the 48th District is one of our greatest strengths, and we should work with all our stakeholders, from community groups to corporations, to ensure we will be the home of the next generation of groundbreaking companies and startups.
Entrepreneurship leads to innovation, venture capital investment, and high-paying jobs in our area.
In the midst of intense economic instability we have the opportunity to lead by investing in "AI-proof" jobs which require human ingenuity and craftsmanship.
Expanding our education and vocational training system is essential in the face of job displacement due to AI.
- As the manager of a car dealership, I make sure our master certified technicians are always teaching new techs so that the next generation of auto technicians have a real-world foundation of skills and experience.
I will fight to apply this practice as much as possible across industries in our state.
- I will advocate to give tax incentives to those who train workers, and allow for Business & Occupation tax credits for money invested in training both for the trainer and the trainee.
- I will fight to make sure that small businesses get the same or more opportunities for partnership and growth assistance from the state as big companies.
I will ensure our grants and lending programs for small businesses continue to be robust.
- I will work to institute "Business in a box" programs to facilitate entrepreneurship through the State Department of Commerce.
This support would streamline licensing, bookkeeping, and tax returns, allowing innovators to innovate and service providers to provide service, rather than being bogged down by administrative tasks.
Apprenticeship, entrepreneurship, and vocational programs give our community the flexibility to adapt to changing economic conditions, and they should be an integral part of how we approach job growth, not an afterthought.
- In the last session, I took the lead in demanding a progressive tax code and tax relief for working families now, rather than years down the road.
I believe in the promise of a high-earners income tax, but I am fighting to ensure that the income threshold isn’t lowered as a crutch for future budgeting shortfalls.
I proposed two amendments to the state income tax passed in the 2026 legislative session:
- My amendment 2596 would have changed the bill's $1 million standard deduction so that it applied per individual, rather than $1 million per household.
- This amendment would have restricted the millionaire’s tax to true millionaires, rather than allowing a couple making $500,000 each to be required to pay half of their marginal income in state and federal income tax.
- My Amendment 2598 would have given the majority of the projected $3.9B in revenue from the high-earners income tax back to everyday Washingtonians by reducing the state retail sales tax rate from 6.5% to 5.75%.
- Since our State Supreme Court ruled against the constitutionality of a state income tax all the way back in 1933, I also proposed the passage of a state constitutional amendment authorizing a state income tax, and limiting the deduction at $1M for single earners.
- These amendments would have made sure the new income tax was not open to court challenges, future expansion to middle class earners, and made our tax code more progressive, rather than just raising taxes to fill budget gaps.
- I voted against the millionaire’s tax because it was unconstitutional, not progressive enough, and raised the possibility of broad middle class tax hikes in the near future.
In Olympia, I will continue to fight for a progressive income tax structure that actually relieves the tax burden for everyday people and working families—not 5 five years later pending a court challenge and ballot initiative, but in the present day, closing loopholes and righting wrongs that already exist in our code.
- My top tax priority next session is reforming the Business and Occupation (corporate revenue) tax so that the industry giants that call WA and the 48th LD home pay back their fair share to the communities that help them thrive.
- Our current corporate revenue tax structure allows WA-based businesses to declare their revenue in tax-haven states like Wyoming.
This is unacceptable—we cannot allow corporations to benefit from our district’s highly-skilled workforce and friendly business climate without keeping them honest and spreading the wealth.
- Hardworking small business owners do not have the benefit of fancy accounting and pay their Business & Occupation taxes here.
- I support the replacement of the B&O tax with a corporate income tax, as we have at a federal level, which only levies taxes on profitable businesses, and allows deductions for operating costs, reducing the relative tax burden for low-margin businesses like mom-and-pop grocery, retail, or restaurants compared to the giants with billion-dollar profits.
As a small business owner, I understand the challenges of entrepreneurship, and I will fight to level the playing field and reform the corporate revenue tax code in WA.
- A huge part of the gender pay gap comes from the fact that women are still the primary caregivers for children, often leaving them behind in the job market.
We can’t wait for the Federal Government to act to make progress in our state.
We need action on ensuring that quality childcare and early education are plentiful throughout our state for working families to be able to thrive.
This is an essential need for moms, dads, and kids.
In my next term, I will fight for comprehensive childcare reform.
My comprehensive childcare reform plan includes:
- Robust state funding for childcare and tax relief for the provision of childcare services
- A licensed and accredited childcare profession, with training in our technical colleges and an early career pathway to becoming a professional childcare provider in high school.
So many talented young people in our state want a career helping kids, but the pathway to that career is not clear.
- Business incentives for benefit plans that include childcare, for on-site childcare services, and for paying tuition for employees who want to become certified childcare providers.
- Tax subsidies for tenant spaces with childcare providers, making rent for providers affordable.
- The creation of a cooperative model for insurance to make insurance more affordable for childcare providers.
- State Government-provided services to childcare providers to help them navigate the regulatory environment at the state, county and city level.
- Minimum pay and workplace standards for workers, project labor agreements, and transparency on wages and working conditions.
These reforms will make this profession attractive to young people and will bring many retirees back to work doing what they love, which is teaching children.
It’s not solely the responsibility of parents and their workplaces to support a robust childcare environment.
We are all responsible for raising the next generation.
- Fighting Gun Violence and Protecting Public Safety I believe that fighting gun violence and protecting public safety are inextricable issues.
That is why in my time in the legislature, I have passed an assault rifle ban, a high-capacity magazine ban, and a permit-to-purchase policy, while continuing to be endorsed by our local police departments.
There is no either/or—public safety is about prevention, trust, and accountability.
I believe in a serious, realistic, and compassionate approach to public safety and gun safety that invests in well-trained, community-focused law enforcement while also expanding mental health and crisis response services.
I was lucky enough to spend much of my childhood in Australia.
On a recent visit, it was remarkable and noticeable that I had a certain relief in my heart that there were no guns in the audience.
People were having fun, and there were the usual concert shenanigans, but I was not concerned that a gun would be drawn and my children would be harmed.
We have more guns than people in the United States and firearm ownership is a very important part of being an American.
However, rights come with responsibilities and protecting our communities is nonnegotiable.
- I come from a family of hunters and fishermen and all weapons were stored safely.
Young people were trained properly.
Guns were treated seriously, handled carefully and were placed in safes when they were not in use.
- I drafted the “safe storage” bill introduced in 2025 to require secure storage of firearms in vehicles, to address the persistent problem of firearms being stolen from vehicles, then used in crimes or intimate partner and family violence and suicide.
- I passed HB 1562 in the 2023-2024 session, which adds safeguards to firearm purchases through firearm dealers.
These restrictions and safeguards are appropriate as firearms are often stolen from licensed firearm dealers and also straw purchasers, or illegal conduits for those who are not qualified to own a firearm to acquire them through deception.
- I will continue to fight for reasonable firearm violence prevention such as education around suicide risks, permits to purchase, and other measures so that very permanent decisions are not made in the heat of the moment.
We must have a robust mental health system and a community where we care enough about each other to pause rushed decisions so that we cannot harm ourselves or others when we are at our weakest and most vulnerable point.

Legislative Accomplishments
- Leveling the playing field for Public Housing Authorities:
- Amy was the prime sponsor of HB 1075, which enables public housing authorities to access financing tools available to private developers.
Access to capital and the costs of financing are one of the major barriers to housing development for public and private entities and this bill expands their bond and financing capacity.
- Rep.
Walen was also the prime sponsor of HB 1046, which cut red tape and freed public housing authorities to construct more “mixed middle” or working family housing.
Sustainable development and permitting and zoning reform:
- As prime sponsor on HB 1757 and HB 1042, Amy has enabled the large-scale conversion of existing constructions into housing.
Existing buildings are embedded carbon, and rather than tearing them down and filling our landfills with waste, getting in a long and costly line for permit approval and finding financing, they can be quickly modified and turned into housing for families.
From churches to office parks and strip malls these bills streamline and give tax exemptions for entities that do conversions.
- Rep.
Walen was also prime sponsor of HB 1576, which prevents the abuse of the “historic designation” process by requiring owner consent and for a building to have to be at least 40 years old to be “historic”—except for specific exemptions in historic districts such as Pioneer Square and the International District.
This bill is being used right now to develop housing that would otherwise have been blocked.
- Arbitrary occupancy limits obstruct the ability of multi-generational families and students, or other cooperative housing situations, to settle in a neighborhood.
As prime sponsor on HB 1054, Rep.
Walen prohibited this practice for cities and counties in condos, townhomes, and retirement communities.
When housing is scarce, we should get out of the way of people finding ways to affordably house themselves.

- Her work on a multi-family tax exemption program rewards communities who increase density in designated zoned areas.
