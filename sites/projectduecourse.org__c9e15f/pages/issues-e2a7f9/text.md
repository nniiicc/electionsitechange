Key Issues Enforce Accountability Demand Integrity Constitutional Reform Key Issues How Washington Avoids Accountability The problem is not that there are no rules.
It is that the rules are hard to check, enforcement is often run by the same people being watched, and penalties are so weak they barely matter.
When a system makes misconduct visible but rarely punishable, scandals pass and nothing changes.
Issue 01 / 18 Public Access to Financial Records Congress still files financial disclosures, but it does so in ways that make them hard to search, compare, or catch in time.
This reform turns paper compliance into real transparency with one public database, faster posting, and penalties that matter.
Read More → Issue 02 / 18 No More Self-Policing Washington let the Independent Counsel Law expire in 1999, removing themselves from any real accountability.
This reform restores Independent enforcement with the power to demand records, question witnesses, open investigations, and bring cases without waiting for permission from the people under scrutiny.
Read More → Issue 03 / 18 Stop Hidden Influence Federal lobbying rules promised transparency for decades and delivered almost none.
We will introduce bills that make disclosure real by exposing who's pressuring your representative, what they want, and whether the rules are actually enforced.
We plan to close the gaps that let influence operate in the dark.
Read More → Issue 04 / 18 Protect Whistleblowers Whistleblowers should be protected and a simple safeguard should exist in any serious system: report wrongdoing safely, investigate it seriously, and punish retaliation.
Every time.
Whistleblower protections exist to make people feel safe to report so wrongdoing is exposed early, corrected, and deterred.
Read More → Issue 05 / 18 Real Ethics for the Supreme Court Today, Supreme Court Justices remain essentially self-regulated.
Unlike other federal judges, they are not subject to the same external oversight process for misconduct.
And while federal recusal law requires that a Justice "shall" recuse in certain circumstances, the Justice alone decides whether to comply, with no review and no appeal.
