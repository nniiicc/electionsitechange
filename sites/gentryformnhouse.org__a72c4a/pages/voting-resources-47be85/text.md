Primaries & Early Voting Minnesota makes it easy to vote before Election Day.
Here's what you need to know for the August 11, 2026 primary.
How the primary works: Minnesota is an open primary state, you don't have to declare a party to vote.
But on your ballot, you'll need to pick candidates from just one party in partisan races.‍ ‍ Three ways to vote: By mail Request an absentee ballot at mnvotes.gov/absentee .
Mailed ballots must be received by your local election office by 8 p.m. on Election Day, a postmark alone isn't enough, so request and return yours early.
Early, in person Starting June 26, 2026 (46 days before the primary), you can vote absentee in person at your county election office: fill out an application, get your ballot, and vote right there.
New this year: "Early Voting" locations open starting July 24, 2026 (18 days out), where voting works just like Election Day: you get a ballot and feed it directly into the tabulator yourself.
On Election Day - Tuesday, August 11 Vote at your regular polling place.
Polls are open until 8 p.m., and anyone already in line by 8 p.m. can still vote.
Good to know: You can register to vote the same day you show up, if you're not already registered.
Track your absentee/mail ballot anytime at mnvotes.gov/trackmyballot .
Find your polling place or early voting location at mnvotes.gov/pollfinder .
Note: the November 3, 2026 general election will have its own early-voting windows opening roughly 46 and # days ahead of that date — check mnvotes.gov closer to the fall for exact dates.
Helping you vote because everyone’s voice deserves to be heard The poll finder on the Minnesota Secretary of State’s website will tell you the following information based on the address you provide: Next election, if one is scheduled Polling place location List of candidates and questions on your ballot Precinct and districts Caucus locations Find your polling location Check your voter registration status.
Using the Minnesota Secretary of State website, you can check your voter registration status by providing your name, date of birth, and street address.
Check your voter status Register to Vote There are many ways you can register to vote.
I recommend registering early to make voting as easy and convenient as possible for you.
You can register to vote or update your registration online using the the Minnesota Secretary of State website.
Minnesota Voting Information The Minnesota Secretary of State’s Elections and Voting website can be used to answer your questions about voter eligibility, voter registration, polling locations, and more.
The website is also available in multiple languages to be accessible to all our neighbors.
