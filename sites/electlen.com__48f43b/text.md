Rent is rising.
Groceries cost more.
Healthcare is harder to access.
The leadership in Atlanta has been doing it wrong
for over 20 years.
I believe our path forward needs these things:
- Targeted tax relief for essential service workers, retirees, and teachers;
- Water and resource management that prioritizes our rural communities, not industry;
- Economic incentives that deliver real local jobs and measurable benefits for taxpayers;
- Strong rural schools and better access to special education programs;
- Expanded healthcare access, including for those working multiple part-time jobs;
- Better access to essential services for veterans in rural areas;
…and more.
Anyone who works full-time should be able to meet their family's basic needs.
I'll put Georgians ahead of politics.
Georgia has the 8th largest GDP in the nation and the 29th strongest economy in the world.
But 44% of Georgia residents struggle for basic household needs like paying bills, doctor’s visits for each family member or even just household maintenance.
When you’re already working 40 hours each week or more and you still can't make ends meet, something is wrong.
I'm a widower and I know how illness can impact a family.
Georgia's success should not just be a story of economic strength.
It should be the story of quality of life.
We can achieve that with quality healthcare, nutritious food, and expanded essential services, including for veterans.
In this way, we'll build resilient communities and increase opportunities across the state.
Georgia's forests, farms and rivers are part of what makes it exceptional.
We can welcome new industries and help them to thrive while requiring responsible stewardship for our natural resources.
True strength in leadership sets expectations to protect our communities.
Good stewardship means preventing tomorrow's problems, not just cleaning them up.
Senator John McCain
