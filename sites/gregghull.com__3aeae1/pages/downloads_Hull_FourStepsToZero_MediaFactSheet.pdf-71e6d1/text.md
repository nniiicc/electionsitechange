Four years is the goal.
Legislative approval and fiscal, service and operational requirements determine whether and when each step can take effect.
The proposal does not establish a guaranteed start or completion date.
The single/joint bands would be $25,000/$50,000 at Step One, $50,000/$100,000 at Step Two and $100,000/$200,000 at Step Three.
Step Four would remove the remaining tax on eligible personal income.
Amounts apply after relevant deductions and exemptions, and people with income above a band retain its benefit.
Qualifying wages, salaries, retirement distributions, reasonable documented compensation for an owner’s actual work, and personal investment income would receive relief.
Operating-business profits, net rental profits, royalties and taxable business-sale gains would remain taxable under coordinated rules, with applicable expenses, deductions, exemptions and basis respected.
Corporate income tax and gross receipts tax would remain.
Before each step, the proposal requires funding for classrooms, student services, healthcare obligations, public safety, justice, refundable benefits and implementation.
It also requires recurring revenue after tax changes of at least 105% of recurring General Fund appropriations, reserves of at least 25% of recurring appropriations, and review of cash, other annual commitments and multiyear downturn risks.

New Mexico would phase out state income tax on eligible personal income through expanding tax-free bands, while continuing to tax net business profits.
Corporate income tax and gross receipts tax would remain.
What are the four steps?

Do I lose the benefit when my income exceeds a band?
No.
You keep the tax-free band on eligible personal income.
Existing marginal rates continue above it, with tax attributable to the exempt band removed.
There is no separate percentage rate cut.
Business income has a continuing tax calculation.
Is zero tax guaranteed in four years?
Four years is the goal.
The Legislature must approve the plan, and every step must meet its fiscal, service and implementation requirements.
No affordable start or completion date has been established.
A pause does not trigger automatic catch-up cuts.
October 5, 2026 7 Four Steps to Zero Hull For New Mexico Does every taxpayer eventually owe zero?
No.
The destination is zero state tax on eligible personal income.
Net business profits and the defined related income remain taxable.
Annual tax can also change with income, filing status and credits.
A person who owes no tax one year is not promised that result forever.
What income would receive relief?
Qualifying wages, salaries, retirement distributions, reasonable documented compensation for an owner’s actual work, personal bank interest, portfolio dividends, and gains on stocks or mutual funds held as personal investments.
What would remain taxable?
Operating-business profits, net rental profits, royalties and taxable gains from selling a business or its assets, after applicable expenses, deductions, exemptions and basis.
The proposal taxes the covered net amount, not gross sales or sale proceeds.
Changing an entity or payment label cannot turn operating profit into exempt investment income.
How would small-business owners be treated?
Reasonable, documented compensation for actual work would share the owner’s personal band with other eligible income.
Residual profits would remain taxable.
There is no automatic allowance, second band for each business or duplicate deduction for wages already accounted for.
Detailed compensation and loss rules and the amount of revenue retained still require analysis.
Business income uses the lower graduated brackets first.
At completion, ordinary business tax is calculated on residual business income alone.
Allowable returnwide deductions apply once against positive business income first and then any remainder against personal income.
Direct expenses follow their source.
Entity payments and owner credits must be coordinated before the first affected step.
October 5, 2026 8 Four Steps to Zero Hull For New Mexico Would refundable benefits disappear?
The proposal preserves eligible benefit formulas and funded TRD delivery, including after eligible personal-income tax reaches zero if necessary.
A return or claim process may remain.
A separate delivery system would require its own authority, funding, testing and a reliable transition.
Aren’t many lower-income taxpayers already paying no tax?
Some already have no ordinary tax; others have positive tax offset by credits.
Those are different situations.
With preserved refundable credits, a reduction in positive tax can increase an excess refund even for someone who already receives a refund.
Public grouped data do not establish the current proposal’s exact number of beneficiaries.
Would higher-income households receive larger cuts?
Larger eligible personal-income liabilities generally produce larger dollar reductions at completion.
Higher-income taxpayers also receive the earlier bands.
The plan does not claim otherwise.
It preserves refundable benefits, retains businessprofit taxation and makes funded services a condition of each step.
A matched distributional analysis remains necessary.
What gets cut to hold spending growth at 2%?
There is no assumed list of unnamed cuts.
Approximately 2% is a planning target.
The actual funded cost of protected services takes priority.
Any proposed saving must identify the program, change, recurring amount and service consequences.

How are schools and healthcare protected?
The model must cost and fund classroom instruction, student services, healthcare obligations, public safety, justice and other continuing commitments before finding capacity for tax relief.
A flat budget alone is not proof of protection when costs or needs increase.
