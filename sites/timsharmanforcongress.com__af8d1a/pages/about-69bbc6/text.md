English Español About Me I’m running for Congress as an independent because I think government should solve problems.
Too often, it doesn’t.
I’ve spent most of my life outside politics.
I grew up in London and studied Politics, Philosophy and Economics at Oxford University.
I moved to Virginia some three decades ago, married, had two children, made a home in Alexandria, and gladly became an American citizen.
My wife passed away in 2020 but my daughters and I continue to live in Alexandria.
I just “celebrated” my 62nd birthday.
America has been extraordinarily good to me, and I’m proud to call it my home.
My career has been spent largely in customer experience and helping businesses better understand and serve their customers.
You may be surprised to learn that YOU are a CUSTOMER of the U.S.
Government, and you should expect the best from it.
More recently, I founded trulyus.app, a startup built around a simple idea: trust, integrity, and treating people well still matter.
Those experiences have taught me something… when something isn’t working, you figure out why and try to fix it.
I look around and see problems we are perfectly capable of solving.
Housing has become astonishingly expensive.
Childcare can cost as much as a mortgage.
Healthcare and higher education place enormous financial pressure on families.
Groceries, insurance, utilities, transportation, and everyday expenses have climbed.
Young people wonder whether they’ll ever be able to afford a home in the communities where they grew up.
These aren’t abstract political arguments.
They’re the things people worry about when they wake up in the morning.
I don’t believe government can fix everything.
But I do believe it can do much better at the things we ask it to do, and that Congress should spend more time solving those problems and less time performing politics for an audience.
I also think something less measurable has gone badly wrong with our politics… we’ve forgotten the importance of kindness.
Kindness isn’t weakness.
It doesn’t mean avoiding difficult questions, pretending disagreements don’t exist, or refusing to say when something is wrong.
It means remembering that the person on the other side of an argument is still a person.
It means listening.
It means treating people with dignity, even if they’re your political opponents.
That matters to me personally.
I’m a widower and the father of two children, both daughters.
Losing my wife changed how I think about family, resilience, time, and what really matters in life.
I have zero interest in pointless fights.
I’m also not quite what people sometimes expect.
I love history, writing, my church, travel, vibe-coding, and good conversations with interesting people.
I’m a lifelong sporting clays enthusiast, own a pair of Italian side-by-side shotguns, and have spent many weekends breaking clays rather than talking politics.
I’m not running because I want a career in politics.
I’m running because I see problems worth solving, people who deserve to be heard, and a government that can work much better than it does today.
Government that works.
Politics that doesn’t get in the way.
More kindness as we walk through this world.
