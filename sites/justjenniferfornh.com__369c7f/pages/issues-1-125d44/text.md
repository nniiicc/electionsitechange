Home heating concerns As winter approaches, home heating concerns are top of mind for many especially in rural areas where homeowners rely on a mix of mostly wood and heating oil.
The cost of heating oil, while dependent on industry/individual company pricing, what are the options to lower costs?
The Republicans have killed a bill which would have added a carbon pricing ‘fee’.
HB278H.pdf Natural gas option, while a great idea, has issues due to other neighboring states not allowing pipelines.
It is also not a great option for rural homes however with natural gas in cities, the demand may lower thus decreasing pricing for those dependent on oil.
Nuclear energy is the cleanest option however this would take some time to build and establish.
There aren’t any easy answers for immediate relief however NH Republicans are committed to exploring options and saying “NO!” to added fees.
I’m committed to assist in finding ways to lower home heating energy.

Tourism, rentals and trailheads I had an interesting conversation with a voter about the tourism affects in the northern part of Carroll 2.
This district is beautiful and has many attractions available for visitors including ski/hiking/get-aways from the city/etc… Many have second homes, time shares, or rentals through hotels, bed and breakfast venues or through AirBNBs.
Housing options are limited and the balance of private home rentals vs affordability for residents is delicate.
More tourism means a boost to the local economy and businesses yet brings traffic and strain on services and resources.
I’m actively researching the needs among seven (7) unique towns.
Trail head locations in residential areas are also a concern for locals especially those who live on small country roads.
Many trailheads don’t have enough parking thus parking along the road which may interfere with residents and/or emergency services is a huge concern.
Seeing my own tourist visits through the eyes of a local resident, has widened my views.
I look forward to my hikes, dining and fun with respect to not visit when lots are full however I often see that many others do not.
I realize this has been a long-standing issue and as tourism increase, so too will these concerns.
