En estas votaciones de la Cámara, la mayoría de representantes demócratas votó de una forma—y la representante Quantá Crews (D, HD-26) votó de la otra.
El emparejamiento de votos proviene de una exportación basada en LegiScan para la sesión 2026; cada título coincide con el REFERENCE TITLE de la versión introductoria (impresa) en la Legislatura de Arizona, 57.ª legislatura, 2.ª regular (2026), y la columna de resumen parafrasea ese texto.
On these House floor votes, a majority of Democratic representatives voted one way—and Rep.
Quantá Crews (D, HD-26) voted the other.
Vote pairing comes from a LegiScan-based export for the 2026 session; each bill title matches the Arizona Legislature introduced (printer's) REFERENCE TITLE for the 57th Legislature, 2nd Regular (2026), and the summary column paraphrases that introduced text.
1.
HB2029 Feb 10, 2026 Water conservation grant fund; disclosure Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 20–4 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: The bill adds teeth to water-conservation grant applications: applicants must disclose projected savings in gallons, whether the water comes from groundwater or surface supplies, and how saved water will be used or disposed of.
That is basic accountability before the state subsidizes projects.
Twenty Democrats voted to require those disclosures before Arizona spends public money on water projects.
Crews voted no—blocking transparency that stops sweetheart deals from draining aquifers and rivers behind closed doors.
2.
HB2078 Feb 23, 2026 Reclamation plans; aggregate mining; notice Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 14–11 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: Introduced law requires new aggregate reclamation plans to come with written notice—and operator contact information—to homeowners within half a mile, unless the plan already went through a public process that reached those owners.
Families deserve a heads-up before pits, dust, and truck traffic show up at the property line.
Most House Democrats voted to put neighbors first.
Crews voted no—prioritizing operator convenience over the people who actually live beside these mines.
3.
HB2265 Feb 23, 2026 Courts; fees; assessments Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 23–2 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: This fee package caps certain court administrative assessments, blocks new court fees unless the Legislature explicitly authorizes them, and removes criminal jury fees and criminal appellate filing fees that fall on defendants who cannot afford them—shifting the burden off indigent people and appointed counsel.
Twenty-three House Democrats voted to stop squeezing poor families through the court system.
Crews was one of only two Democrats who voted no—keeping a predatory fee structure that funds government on the backs of people who cannot pay.
4.
HB2278 Feb 23, 2026 Financial assurance; reclamation; escrow; trusts Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 14–11 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: Hard-rock and aggregate operators still must post financial assurance for cleanup, but the bill lets them satisfy it through regulated escrow or trust accounts and apply earned interest toward reclamation—giving regulators clear, verifiable proof the money exists before taxpayers get stuck with abandoned pits.
Most House Democrats voted yes for that stronger accountability path.
Crews voted no—rejecting the same escrow-and-trust safeguards her own caucus backed.
5.
HB2762 Feb 24, 2026 Food products; labeling; cultivated cells Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 20–3 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: The introduced version (short title: Andy Groseta Act) requires any food consisting of or containing cultivated animal cells to bear the exact words: "This food product is derived from cultivated cells." Shoppers have a right to know what they are buying; one honest sentence on the label stops cultivated products from masquerading as conventional meat.
Twenty Democrats voted for that plain disclosure.
Crews voted no—helping producers hide what is in the package.
6.
HB2050 Feb 25, 2026 Radiologic technologists; radiologist assistants Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 19–3 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: The bill updates radiologic technology school curricula and exams to match national registry outlines, defines radiologist assistants' scope, and requires supervising radiologists to be "readily accessible by telecommunication" during procedures—plus it folds radiologist assistants into telehealth-related definitions so remote supervision rules stay coherent.
Nineteen House Democrats voted for those patient-safety upgrades.
Crews voted no—opposing a technical standard that keeps imaging procedures aligned with national training and real physician backup.
7.
HB2176 Feb 25, 2026 Health facilities; licensure; complaints; investigations Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 16–6 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: Democrats sent the Governor a nursing-home and hospital licensing bill that makes each deficiency cite the specific statute or rule, adds a written informal dispute-resolution path after inspections, and tightens license transfers when operators have harmed patients.
Sixteen Democrats decided those transparency and enforcement wins mattered more than the bill’s imperfect twelve-month window on some complaint investigations.
Crews voted no on the entire package—walking away from stronger citations, fair appeals after inspections, and tougher license transfers when operators hurt vulnerable Arizonans.
8.
HB2185 Feb 25, 2026 Homeowners' associations; lawns; drought Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 21–2 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: The bill forbids HOAs and condos from requiring overseeding or lawn watering during a drought year as already defined in state water law (A.R.S. § 48-4401)—when Central Arizona Project or other surface deliveries fall below eighty percent of normal under that statute’s formula.
Drinking water and survival come before turf aesthetics; green-lawn mandates in a shortage year are indefensible.
All but two House Democrats voted to put hydrology ahead of busybody board rules.
Crews voted no—standing with HOA power to waste water when the state is already in crisis.
9.
HB2438 Feb 25, 2026 Podiatric licensure compact Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 21–2 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: Arizona would join the interstate podiatric licensure compact so podiatrists in good standing in another member state can get an Arizona license without redundant hoops—cutting wait times for rural clinics, military families, and anyone stuck on long foot-and-ankle backlogs.
Twenty-one Democrats voted to expand access.
Crews and only one other Democrat voted no—blocking a standard reform the rest of her caucus supported.
10.
HB2665 Feb 25, 2026 Manslaughter; online advice; encouragement Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 14–9 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: "Cade's Law" adds manslaughter when an adult knows or reasonably should know they are communicating with a minor who is considering suicide and, through electronic means, encourages or advises suicide in a way a reasonable person would understand as urging the minor to die.
The statute deliberately leaves alone general discussion, art, counseling, and speech that is not direct urging to die.
Most House Democrats voted to hold adults accountable when they use phones and social media to push a child toward suicide.
Crews voted no—leaving Arizona without that protection and siding against a bill named for a Wisconsin teenager whose story drew national attention.
11.
HB2771 Feb 25, 2026 Community supervision; rehabilitation; costs Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 12–11 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: The bill sets a $65 monthly floor for community supervision fees (judges may reduce it for inability to pay), sends seventy percent of receipts to the Crime Victim Compensation and Assistance Fund and thirty percent to community corrections, and lets counties recover drug-testing and court-ordered treatment costs.
It moves money toward crime victims while still funding supervision—rather than letting fees balloon without accountability.
House Democrats split 12–11; a narrow majority voted yes.
Crews voted no—rejecting even that victim-heavy compromise and keeping people on supervision buried in debt.
12.
HB2594 Mar 4, 2026 Family court; address confidentiality Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 15–9 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: Survivors in Arizona's address-confidentiality program could keep their real street addresses sealed in family-court filings, and judges could not treat enrollment in the program as evidence of parental alienation, failure to co-parent, or instability.
Wrongful disclosure of a participant's address carries new misdemeanor or felony penalties.
Without sealing, abusers use court files to stalk survivors and children.
Fifteen House Democrats voted for those protections.
Crews voted no—leaving a door open for violent ex-partners to weaponize custody cases and find victims through the docket.
13.
HB4010 Mar 4, 2026 Genetic counselors; board; licensure Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 22–2 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: The bill creates a Genetic Counseling Advisory Board, licenses genetic counselors, and spells out practice: hereditary risk assessment, informed consent for genetic testing, psychosocial counseling tied to genetics, and training other providers.
Families navigating cancer risk, prenatal screening, and rare disease need licensed professionals—not whoever hung out a shingle.
Twenty-two Democrats voted for that standard.
Crews and one other Democrat voted no—opposing basic licensure her colleagues treated as essential patient protection.
14.
HB2035 Mar 9, 2026 DCS; kinship care placement; requirement Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 6–15 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Nay Crews: Yea Why it matters: Representative Fink's bill rewrites large chunks of kinship and foster placement law—pushing DCS toward placing children with relatives, adding fingerprinting and central-registry checks for kin, and tightening timelines and notices around removal and placement.
The package tilts too fast toward relative care and away from careful, safe reunification with parents—the reason fifteen House Democrats voted no and the House killed the bill on final passage.
Crews voted yes with the Republican minority anyway—backing a failed DCS overhaul that endangered the stability her own caucus was trying to protect.
15.
HB4004 Mar 9, 2026 DCS; abuse investigation; protective parent Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 8–13 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Nay Crews: Yea Why it matters: The bill would have barred DCS from declining to investigate solely because one parent is considered the "protective parent," required annual public reporting when investigations are declined, and narrowed when a report can be screened out—expanding mandatory probes in volatile custody situations.
That invites bad-faith hotline calls in divorce wars, buries caseworkers who already cannot keep up, and drags more families under state surveillance.
Thirteen House Democrats voted no; the House defeated the bill.
Crews voted yes with Republicans anyway—choosing a surveillance-first approach her own caucus rejected as dangerous overload.
16.
SB1107 Mar 9, 2026 Peace officer certification; alternative pathway Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 3–1 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: The Military Police to Peace Officer Pathway Act sets up an abbreviated AZPOST certification track for honorably discharged military police if Arizona's curriculum matches what they already completed, caps training fees, and requires licensing authorities to act within sixty days.
Honorably discharged military police have already done rigorous training; rural agencies and military families need predictable, affordable paths onto Arizona forces.
Three House Democrats voted yes that day.
Crews was the sole no—blocking the same expedited pathway for veterans that her colleagues supported.
17.
HB2914 Mar 10, 2026 Electronic monitoring; health care facilities Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 14–9 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: When nursing-home or assisted-living care is paid with public funds (including Medicaid), the resident or a representative may authorize optional in-room electronic monitoring—audio, video, or both—with consent forms, conspicuous signage, and privacy rules for roommates.
Taxpayer-funded care should never be a black box when families suspect neglect or abuse.
Most House Democrats voted to allow that transparency.
Crews voted no—siding with facilities that want allegations of mistreatment hidden behind closed doors.
18.
SB1270 Mar 23, 2026 Correctional officers; supplemental contributions Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) Dems (Sí–No) Dem Reps (Yea–Nay) 2–1 Dem majority Mayoría dem Dem majority Yea Crews: Nay Why it matters: Counties, the Department of Corrections, and similar employers could make optional supplemental contributions—up to $5,000 at defined career milestones—to defined-contribution retirement plans for correctional officers and related classifications.
Jails and prisons face brutal retention crises; optional retirement boosts are a small way to keep experienced officers on the job.
On a 2–1 split among voting Democrats, two colleagues voted yes.
Crews was the lone no—denying correctional staff even that optional tool to build retirement security.
