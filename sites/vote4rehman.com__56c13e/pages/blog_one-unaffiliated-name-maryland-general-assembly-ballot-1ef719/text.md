What it took A candidate running with a party puts their name on the ballot by filing a one page certificate of candidacy with the State Board of Elections.
That is the whole procedure.
A candidate running without one has to petition.
Maryland law prices ballot access at one percent of the district's registered voters, and every signature has to come from a registered voter who lives inside the district lines — lines that run from Ellicott City out through Woodbine and Mt.
Airy and into Clarksburg and Damascus.
Between April 19 and July 29, my neighbors signed — one at a time, in person, every one of them a registered voter living inside those lines.
Each signature was checked against the voter file before we filed, and every page carried a sworn affidavit from the person who collected it.
The State Board verified them and certified the petition on August 19.
The ballot was certified on August 31.
