This link leads off-site. es October 12, 2026 Governor Shapiro Joins Black Women Leaders from Across Philadelphia to Discuss the Stakes of the Midterm Elections and Moving Pennsylvania Forward Governor Shapiro is fighting to deliver results for Black Pennsylvanians and continue moving our Commonwealth forward Governor Shapiro : “Black women have been the backbone, the bedrock, the foundation of the Democratic Party for generations” PENNSYLVANIA – Last night, Governor Josh Shapiro sat down with Pennsylvania Democratic Party Vice Chair Jamila Winder for a fireside chat highlighting the power of Black women in deciding elections and the importance of ensuring Black voices are heard and represented across the Commonwealth.
Governor Shapiro has spent the last four years fighting to create opportunity for all Pennsylvanians — no matter what they look like, who they love, or who they pray to.
Through meaningful investments in Pennsylvania’s communities, Governor Shapiro is delivering results that are helping Black Pennsylvanians build stronger businesses, access quality healthcare, and get ahead in our Commonwealth.
As Donald Trump and his MAGA allies like Stacy Garrity continue to raise our costs and attack our fundamental freedoms, the Governor will continue to stand up for this Commonwealth.
Read below highlights from Governor Shapiro and Pennsylvania Democratic Party Vice Chair Winder’s conversation and watch here : On How Faith Unites Us: Vice Chair Jamila Winder: “I’ve seen you talk about how faith and family really guide your work.
So, can you talk to us a little bit about — you know this work is hard, and I can only imagine being the Governor of Pennsylvania, but you’ve always stayed grounded in the importance of family and how faith has guided this work.
Talk to us a little bit about that.” Governor Josh Shapiro: “One of the things that one of my best friends in the entire world, Pastor Marshall Mitchell, and I always talk about… while we have different religions, there is a through line of faith that connects us all.
And while my religion teaches us that no one is required to complete the task, neither are we free to refrain from it.
Meaning, each of us has a responsibility to do this work.
That may be from my religion, but I think it is connected through all different religions in the common bond of faith.
“So, the way I was raised by my parents, what the text has taught me over the years, and the way Lori and I are now trying to raise our four children, is all about service to others, about trying to leave something better than how you found it, about making sure that it’s not just our four kids with opportunity, but it’s your children as well.
That’s the attitude I brought to governing, and importantly, it’s what I’ve also tried to bring to politics — to find ways to lift up other people who are oftentimes ignored and left behind… I believe our politics are better when we have real representation around the table when all voices are treated with dignity and respect.
That’s what my parents taught me to believe, and that’s what my faith has commanded me to understand.” On Providing Access to Healthcare and Menstrual Products: Vice Chair Jamila Winder: “You and the First Lady have really been champions for women’s health over the last four years.
I know that I’ve personally worked with the First Lady on period products… It’s important in the Black community, with Black women.
So what does healthcare access mean for Black women, particularly when it comes to maternal and reproductive health?” Governor Josh Shapiro: “When Black women give birth in Pennsylvania, they are more at risk than any other women giving birth, and that is not okay. [Rep.
Morgan Cephas] has first educated people about this issue, sensitized to them, and then worked with me to pass a budget to ensure that Black maternal health finally got millions and millions and millions of dollars to help fund the needs of Black women all across Pennsylvania.
“Listen, leadership matters.
If Joanna McClinton is not the Speaker of the House, that funding never makes its way to my desk.
Black women are more at risk in Pennsylvania, but for that, this is why our politics matters.
You put these people in positions of power and authority, and then we, when we get the privilege of sitting at the table of progress for you, it’s your issues that are on the agenda, and it’s your items that get passed in the final packages.
And I’m indebted to Morgan Cephas, to Darisha Parker, and of course to Madam Speaker McClinton for helping us achieve this.” On Creating Opportunity for Black Pennsylvanians: Vice Chair Jamila Winder: “You always talk about the chaos and cruelty coming out of Washington.
So, despite… the rhetoric coming out of Washington, you still have found a way to deliver for Pennsylvania families by creating opportunity and fighting for economic security.
Can you talk a little bit about that?” Governor Josh Shapiro: “There is no better teammate when I sit at that table than Joanna McClinton, the Speaker of the House of Representatives… And because of her power and ability to hold firm, I want you to know what we’ve been able to accomplish, even with a Republican Senate: a 35 percent increase in public education funding throughout the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, 37 percent in the city of Philadelphia.
A new formula that drives those dollars out to the school districts that need it most.
Universal free breakfast for all God’s children when they show up at school.
A thousand schools have mental health counselors now because we stayed at the table and fought for our kids’ mental health.
Two thousand more police officers and a billion dollars more invested in preventing violence across this Commonwealth, and violent crime is down 19 percent across Pennsylvania, and our homicide rate in Philadelphia is below 1950s levels.
More jobs and economic growth in Pennsylvania over the last three years than the prior 15 years combined, including an incredible number of new jobs — over ten thousand just in the city of Philadelphia alone.
More economic opportunity for all.
On top of that, we’re using the power of this Commonwealth to lift up Black-owned businesses, doing more procurement, more business with Black-owned businesses than at any other time in our Commonwealth’s history.
On top of that, we’ve gotten a fund to invest in historically disadvantaged businesses, Black-owned businesses that for years were left behind now have millions and millions of dollars available to them… We got to find ways to work together, but we also have to understand that when you get to sit at the table, it’s not just about being at the table; it’s about getting stuff done when you’re at that table.
And that is what we’ve been able to do together.” On the Power of Black Women to Decide Elections: Vice Chair Jamila Winder: “This room is filled with brilliant, successful, engaged women who are leaders in their community.
As we continue to look at uncertain times ahead, what do you want to leave them with?” Governor Josh Shapiro: “I hope you appreciate your power.
Black women have been literally the backbone, the bedrock, the foundation of the Democratic Party for generations and generations.
And you have understood issues, and you have understood circumstances, and you have understood moments in time, even when a whole lot of other people didn’t… This group isn’t satisfied with just winning an election.
You want results… So, if Black women are the backbone of our party, have always bailed us out in tough times, you also have the power to put money in people’s pockets and change people’s lives by raising the wage here in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania.
It’s not just about electing people; it’s about delivering that result.
“I’ve always learned you run for office not just to win a race, but to change people’s lives.
It is how I was raised.
It is how this community here has raised me to believe that there is possibility, there is promise if we can find ways to work together.
But it starts with our politics, and it starts with all of you… You have a privilege, I believe, of being not just a powerful Black woman, but a powerful Black woman who has the privilege of living here in Pennsylvania, the place where it all started, and on November 3rd, the place where we will define how it goes.” ### Donate By Mail Shapiro for Pennsylvania PO Box 22635 Philadelphia, PA 19110 Paid for by Shapiro for Pennsylvania By participating, you agree to the terms & privacy policy for recurring autodialed marketing messages from Josh Shapiro to the phone number you provide.
