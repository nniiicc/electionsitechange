Priorities Everyone agrees that we need strong schools, good jobs, and a more affordable state.
But most candidates cannot tell you how they will make it happen.
These challenges are connected.
Roman is the only candidate with a long-term, comprehensive blueprint to make Michigan more affordable, prepare Michigan for the technological changes shaping our future, and ensure the next generation can learn, work, build, and thrive right here at home.
Cost-of-Living Crisis.
Housing Prevent large institutional investors from driving up housing prices and build more affordable single-family homes. ( For more details, click here. ) Protect Homebuyers: Stop corporations and large private equity firms from buying up neighborhoods.
Build More Starter Homes: Reward communities that approve attainable housing with increased revenue sharing.
Cut Building Costs: Streamline the permitting process and eliminate unnecessary red tape, saving tens of thousands for each new house.
Energy Wipe out years of future rate increases passed onto Michigan families. ( For more details, click here. ) Accountability: Set standards for performance improvements and require rebates for failure to meet the mark.
All-of-the-Above: Repeal mandates which prevent utilities from choosing energy sources with the lowest cost and highest reliability.
Fix the Grid: Reform property taxes so utilities aren't penalized for upgrading infrastructure.
Healthcare Stand up for people experiencing medical emergencies, prohibiting predatory practices from hospital monopolies.
Ban Monopoly Pricing: Eliminate loopholes exploited by hospital monopolies that preventing price competition with independent providers.
(For more details, click here. ) Reduce Medical Debt: Require hospitals to identify and correct billing errors before collections and offer affordable, income-based repayment plans to patients who cannot pay upfront.
Secure Medicaid: Strengthen oversight of fraud, waste, and improper payments, and use savings to expand access to care for Michiganders with permanent disabilities.
We can't tax and spend our way out of spiraling unaffordability.
Roman is focused on the structural factors driving up expenses every Michigander faces: housing, energy, and healthcare.
Multiple Paths In today's economy, a four-year degree is not the right choice for everybody.
Roman supports expanding access to: Career and technical education Skilled trades programs Apprenticeships ​ Roman will work to develop public-private partnerships which guarantee direct pathways to employment for Michiganders who complete these programs.
Real-Life Skills Many Michiganders graduate high school without knowing how to write a check, create a budget, or repay a loan. ​ ​ For high school graduation, students should have a basic understanding of financial literacy—specifically: Spending and budgeting Saving Credit and debt Taxes Insurance Education That Prepares.
Our system was built with the idea that every student should move at the same pace and follow the same path.
Roman believes in early intervention for struggling readers and personalized after-high-school plans for every graduate.
Strong Starts Students who fall behind in reading early are far more likely to struggle across every subject later on.
Roman will advocate to: Use evidence-based reading instruction grounded in phonics and the science of reading.
Streamline statewide assessments so schools spend less time testing and get clearer results.
Improve access to screening for dyslexia and other reading-related learning disabilities.
Provide one-on-one or small group tutoring based on need.

Make Things Here Bringing back advanced manufacturing is critical for our state's economy and our country's national security.
Economic Development: Refocus MEDC toward strategically important manufacturing, including aerospace, defense, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals, and medical technology.
Tax Reform: Transition commercial and industrial property taxation toward a land-value tax, eliminating the penalty on new construction, equipment, and productive investment.
Brownfield Redevelopment: Make it faster and less expensive for communities to clean up and reuse abandoned industrial sites, prioritizing properties with existing infrastructure for new manufacturing.
