OVERVIEW OF RACE
Party Spending:
Arizona law allows political party committees to make unlimited coordinated expenditures in support of its nominees.
The Hobbs campaign expects to benefit from coordinated expenditures from at least two political party committees, the Navajo County Democratic Committee and the Arizona Democratic Party.
Money spent by political parties goes much further.
For example, political parties can mail at a savings of up to $0.20 per piece versus organizations mailing with a bulk mail permit.
Additionally, the cost of advertising on television is about twice as expensive for an independent expenditure, whereas political parties making coordinated expenditures can get twice as many impressions for the same amount of money using the candidate rate.
Hobbs’ joint fundraising committee, Katie Hobbs Victory Fund, may accept unlimited contributions and may make unlimited transfers to any Arizona political party committee.
We are encouraging all donors looking to make large contributions to direct those to Katie Hobbs Victory Fund.
You may contact Isaac Lampner at isaac@katiehobbs.org for more information.
Key targets in the governor’s race:
NPA voters, non-college educated women, college educated men, and Hispanic men and women.
The winning negative message:
Andy Biggs is a Washington politician who will bring Washington’s cost-hiking agenda and the wrong priorities to Arizona if he is elected governor.
(BACKUP)
- Andy Biggs is a Washington politician who has repeatedly fought to take healthcare away from Arizonans, even though he has taxpayer-funded Washington politician Cadillac health insurance.
- He voted to increase insurance premiums for over 400,000 Arizonans, and 200,000 Arizonans lost their healthcare altogether because of his votes.
- The healthcare cuts he voted for this year have already led to the closure of 1 hospital, 9 health clinics, and have put 8 more Arizona hospitals at risk of closing – and still he says he wants “the more cut approach.”
- While serving in the Arizona State Senate, he opposed healthcare for children and fought against bipartisan efforts to expand access to affordable healthcare.
- He sponsored legislation to eliminate Medicaid in Arizona entirely, which would have ripped away healthcare from 1.8 million Arizonans, including hundreds of thousands of children.
- Andy Biggs is a Washington politician who cannot be trusted to protect Arizona’s seniors.
- He voted to raise the Social Security retirement age to 69.
- He voted to block Medicare from lowering the cost of life-saving medications, and opposed capping the cost of insulin.
- He has repeatedly voted against bipartisan bills that protect seniors from scams.
The winning positive message:
Katie Hobbs has lived the same struggles as everyday Arizonans and is working across the aisle to tackle the challenges politicians have ignored for decades.
Most importantly, she is standing up for the state of Arizona, and she always puts Arizonans first.
(BACKUP)
- Katie Hobbs is fighting to lower costs for Arizonans and put money back in their pockets.
- She has cut taxes each of the last four years for working families, veterans, and small businesses; cut utility costs; and cut red tape to make housing more affordable and eliminated the tax on rent.
- She canceled medical debt for nearly 700,000 Arizonans and launched AZRx, a program available to all Arizonans that saves up to 80 percent on prescription drugs.
- Katie Hobbs has experienced many of the same challenges Arizonans have.
- She grew up in a working-class family that struggled to make ends meet and sometimes relied on food stamps.
- She worked her way through school with jobs at Burger King and Pizza Hut and later drove Uber part-time to afford Christmas presents for her kids.
- She is a social worker who protected victims of domestic violence by helping run one of the largest domestic violence shelters in the country.
