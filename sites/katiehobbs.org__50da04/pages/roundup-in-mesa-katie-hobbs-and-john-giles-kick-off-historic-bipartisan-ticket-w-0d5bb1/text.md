Katie Hobbs’ platform
- When Democratic Gov.
Katie Hobbs picked former Republican John Giles as her running mate last week, her campaign pitched the move as an olive branch to Arizona voters from across the political spectrum.
- “We come from different political parties, but we share the belief that leadership is about bringing people together, delivering results, and doing what’s right for Arizona, not playing partisan political games,” Hobbs said at a campaign kickoff event in Mesa on Saturday.
- But beyond their voter registration, Hobbs and Giles appear in lock-step on most major issues.
- “Well, like I said, remarkably little or nothing, because we’re focused on these nonpartisan agenda items, transportation, education,” said Giles, a former Mesa mayor.
“There’s not a partisan approach to these very important challenges that Arizona’s facing.”
- Appealing to voters across the political spectrum is critical for Democratic candidates running for statewide office in Arizona, a state where independent voters make up more than one third of the electorate and Republicans make up the single largest voting bloc.
- But Giles, who was a registered Republican for four decades, argued there are plenty of current and former GOP voters disaffected with the state of their party.
- “So I felt like I needed to be there to advocate for people like myself that were being shoved out of the party a few months ago.
I realized that’s a lost cause right now,” he said of the decision to re-register as an independent this year.
- But he said he doesn’t view Republican voters themselves as a lost cause.
- “It means we share a lot of priorities; that I identify with them,” Giles said.
“And I think many of them feel like I do that they are being left behind by their party.
They’re being forced out.”
- At the campaign event, Giles argued the Republican tent “is getting smaller and smaller” as the party has become fixated on partisan hot button issues like President Donald Trump’s false claims that his loss in 2020 was the result of voter fraud.
- “When it came time to defend our democracy, Andy Biggs voted to put your votes in the toilet,” he told supporters, referring to Biggs’ vote against certifying Arizona’s 2020 presidential election results.
- Hobbs and Giles contrasted those positions with the “non-partisan issues” they want to tackle, such as water, education and economic development.
- “There shouldn’t be a Republican and a Democratic solution to that.
I mean, it’s a shared solution,” Giles said.
- “I’m going to spend my time out talking to Arizonans, and I have had more than 100 open press events just this year.
That’s not going away,” Hobbs said.
