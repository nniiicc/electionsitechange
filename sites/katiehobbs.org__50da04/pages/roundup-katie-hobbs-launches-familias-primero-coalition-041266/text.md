Katie Hobbs launched Familias Primero , a coalition consisting of over sixty Latino leaders, community members, and local elected officials working to re-elect her, including Maricopa County Supervisor Steve Gallardo, former Maricopa County Supervisor Mary Rose Wilcox, former State Sen.
Raquel Terán, President and CEO of the Arizona Hispanic Chamber Monica Villalobos, and President of Arizona Carpenters Local 1912 Fabian Sandez.
On the eve of the start of the general election, Familias Primero was the first coalition the Hobbs campaign launched, underscoring her commitment to earning the vote of every Arizonan and building a broad coalition of support.
Engaging Latino voters has been a top priority since day one of the Hobbs campaign.
Hobbs has consistently communicated directly with Spanish-language media about her efforts to lower costs for working families; the campaign began a historically early investment in Spanish-language television, radio, and digital advertising back in May; and Hobbs has met with Latino Arizonans and community leaders across the state from cultural celebrations to conversations with small business owners about the impact of rising costs brought on by Washington’s misguided policies.

State Affairs: Hobbs launches campaign to court Latino voters Gov.
Katie Hobbs’ reelection campaign launched its “ Familias Primero ” coalition on Monday, an effort meant to drive Latino turnout in Arizona ’s top-of-the-ticket race in November.
“I am focused on doing everything I can to deliver for Arizonans, and that includes Latino families,” Hobbs told reporters after the event.
“When I go across the state, whether I’m talking to Democrats or Republicans, whites or Latinos, rural or urban communities, I’m hearing the concerns about higher costs, about securing the border, about protecting our water, creating good-paying jobs, and those are the things I’m focused on delivering on.” Polls estimate that anywhere from 34 to 41% of Arizona’s Latino voters, who make up one-fourth of the state’s eligible voters, cast ballots for Trump in 2024.
But more recent numbers from UnidosUS show those voters are souring on the Trump administration, with 19% of Arizona Latinos who voted for the president saying they would not vote for him knowing what they know now.
Speakers at Hobbs’ event on Monday attribute that to the Trump administration’s immigration crackdown and what they see as its failure to move the needle on kitchen table issues.
All said Hobbs is the best candidate to win Latino voters back because of her record on both topics.
“She refused to let Arizona become a testing ground for immigration policies that divide our families and distract law enforcement from doing its job,” said Raquel Terán, a former state lawmaker and Arizona Democratic Party chair.
