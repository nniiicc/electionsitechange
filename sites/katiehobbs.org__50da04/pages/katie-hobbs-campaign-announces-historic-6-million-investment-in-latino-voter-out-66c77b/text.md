That investment reaches across every arm of the campaign.
Since the beginning of the campaign, Hobbs has invested in English- and Spanish-language mail and campaign literature, hired Spanish-speaking organizers and communications staff, regularly engaged Spanish-language media, and met directly with Latino voters, community leaders, and small-business owners across Arizona.
This builds on the campaign’s historically early investment in Spanish-language television, radio, digital, and streaming advertising, as well as English-language advertising that reaches Latino audiences.
“Latino families are an essential part of Arizona, and earning their votes means showing up, listening, and talking about the issues that matter in their everyday lives,” said Katie Hobbs.
“That’s what we’ve done throughout this campaign, and it’s what I’ve done as governor.
While Washington is driving up costs, I’ve remained laser-focused on cutting costs for Arizona families, and in a second term, I’ll keep fighting to make our state more affordable.” Hobbs’ first interview after launching the re-election campaign and the first interview announcing Hobbs’ running mate was with Univision.
In July, Hobbs launched Familias Primero as the first coalition of her re-election campaign, bringing together more than 60 Latino leaders, community members, labor leaders, and elected officials from across Arizona to organize and mobilize Latino voters.
Hobbs has also met directly with Latino small business owners to hear how rising costs are affecting their businesses and the families they serve.
In March, she visited Latino-owned food trucks at Comida Park in Tucson to hear firsthand how rising food and fuel costs were cutting into small businesses’ bottom lines.
In August, Hobbs and her running mate, John Giles, visited La Purísima Bakery in Glendale and spoke directly with managers about how rising costs were affecting the longtime Mexican bakery’s day-to-day operations and ability to thrive.
Hobbs has also made Spanish-language paid media central to her campaign from the outset.
In May, the campaign launched “No Se Rinde,” its first Spanish-language television, radio, and digital ads, as part of a multi-million-dollar statewide media campaign.
That was followed by a six-figure English- and Spanish-language buy during the World Cup, plus additional Spanish-language ads including “Pancitas Felices,” “Listos o No,” “Dinerito Extra,” and “De Puro Sudor” focused on lowering costs for working families.
That investment is matched on the ground.
Through Copper State Victory, the campaign has hired Spanish-speaking organizers who recruit Spanish-speaking volunteers, knock doors, and talk directly with voters across Arizona, with campaign literature available in both English and Spanish.
The campaign is also reaching Latino voters through English- and Spanish-language direct mail and has hired Spanish-speaking communications staff to work directly with Spanish-language media.
“From the beginning, we’ve made the investments necessary to engage Latino Arizonans across every part of our campaign,” said Nicole DeMont, campaign manager for Katie Hobbs.
“We’re reaching voters in Spanish and English on television and radio, online, in their mailboxes, at their doors, and in their communities.
This historic $6 million investment reflects the robust campaign we’ve built to earn Latino votes across Arizona.” As governor, Hobbs has focused on lowering costs for Arizona families.
This year, she signed a bipartisan budget providing $1.4 billion in tax relief, including a larger standard deduction for 88 percent of taxpayers, a new $6,000 deduction for Arizona seniors, and eliminating taxes on tips and overtime pay.
She has also helped eliminate nearly $1 billion in medical debt for approximately 700,000 Arizonans, lowered prescription drug costs through AZRx, worked to lower utility and housing costs, and expanded access to apprenticeships and community colleges that prepare Arizonans for good-paying jobs.
Hobbs has also strengthened border security and public safety, including launching Task Force SAFE and Operation Desert Guardian to crack down on cartels and criminal organizations, stopping more than 18 million fentanyl pills from reaching Arizona communities, and delivering more than $215 million to local sheriffs and police departments to combat drug smuggling, human trafficking, and other border-related crimes.
The campaign will continue expanding its Latino voter outreach and turnout operation through Election Day with additional paid media, mail, organizing, community events, Spanish-language communications, and direct voter contact across Arizona.
