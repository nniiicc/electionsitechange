On Charity Clark:
To the Editor:
Vermont Attorney General Charity Clark has been criticized for her decision to prosecute the individuals who disrupted operations at the Williston business park that houses Vermont’s ICE operations and other non-related offices.
She is right.
The critics are wrong.
We believe in what Henry David Thoreau called the duty to engage in civil disobedience.
We have no philosophical quarrel with the people deciding to disobey the police officers’ command to leave the premises and be arrested.
We have read the charging affidavit; the protesters made an informed decision to be arrested for a low-level misdemeanor.
They could have protested elsewhere, but they believed it important to make a larger point by virtue of their arrest and prosecution.
In his essay, “On the Duty of Civil Disobedience” Thoreau makes the point clearly: non-violent protest gains meaning only through arrest and absorbing the consequences.
Otherwise, it is meaningless theater.
It is also, as Attorney General Clark understands, an invitation for chaos with no consequences.
The attorney general does not have the luxury of charging those with whom she disagrees and not charging those with whom she agrees.
What if the protesters had been members of the hate-spewing Proud Boys?
We would demand their prosecution.
As attorneys we know that you don’t get to have it both ways.
If the Proud Boys should be prosecuted, so should these folks.
We understand the argument about prosecutorial discretion, but that discretion must be applied through the confines of the law, not politics.
We deeply admire the Attorney General’s courage and intellectual clarity in the face of difficult political cross-currents.
She understands both the deeper meaning of civil disobedience and the duty to apply the law even-handedly.
This demonstrates to us that Charity Clark is a leader in whom Vermonters can place their trust.
Senators Rob Plunkett and Seth Bongartz represent the Bennington Senate District in the Vermont State Senate.
Rob Plunkett is a career prosecutor with the Bennington County State’s Attorney’s office.
Seth Bongartz is a retired attorney with experience in criminal defense.
