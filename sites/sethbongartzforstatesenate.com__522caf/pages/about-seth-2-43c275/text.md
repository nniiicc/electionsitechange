About Seth, in his own words
I know a lot of people in the Bennington Senate District (Bennington County plus, Wilmington, Londonderry and Stratton), but I certainly don’t know everyone and not everyone knows me.
With this post, people who don’t know me can get a feel for who I am.
And for those who do know me, maybe it will fill in a few things.
I have always lived in Manchester.
In fact, I live on the property on which my four sisters and I grew up.
My sister Jenny and her son live next door.
Jenny and my sister Lauren run the family business our father started almost 70 years ago.
My oldest sister, Dulce, lives and works in California.
My youngest sister, Missy, recently retired from her long-held position of Town Clerk in Hinesburg, Vermont.
My wife, Chris, an RN, retired from her job as district director for the Health Department.
A year later, she started a second career as a school nurse.
Now fully retired, she still subs for school nurses when they need a day off here and there.
Our son, Reed, works for a non-profit with the mission of providing economic opportunity for communities across the country.
My Early Years in the Legislature
A few years after graduating from college, I ran for the legislature and was elected twice (1980 and 1982), serving on the Natural Resources Committee, the then Health and Welfare Committee, and, in my fourth year, on the Appropriations Committee.
I didn’t run for a third term in order to go to law school.
As much as I loved the legislature, I realized I needed to start a career.
Politics and policy aside, one very important thing happened while I was serving in the House: I met Chris.
Her mother, Mary Evelti, sat next to me in the House Chamber.
One day Chris stopped by after adjournment, while Mary and I were working at our desks.
It took a few years, but we were married in 1987.
In 1986 I ran for and was elected to the State Senate, serving all of Bennington County.
In the Senate, I served on the Health and Welfare, General Affairs, and Rules Committees.
Once again, as much as I loved it, I didn’t run for re-election at the end of my first term.
Things had changed: First, Chris and I had gotten married.
Second, we were going to have a baby.
Chris was working more than full-time for the Health Department, and I knew there was no way I could leave her home to juggle work and a newborn while I spent the winter weekdays in Montpelier.
One of my major accomplishments in the House was as the lead sponsor of the bill to close the notorious ten-acre loophole in Act 250 that almost encouraged developers to carve up farm and forest land in a particularly wasteful manner.
In the Senate, I was the first chair of the joint House/Senate Committee on Affordable Housing and a key proponent of successful legislation allowing mobile home park tenants the opportunity to buy the parks in which they live in the event of sale by the owner.
Since then, numerous parks have been purchased and are now run by their residents.
My Second Time in the Legislature
In 2020, 32 years and a career later, I ran again for the Vermont House of Representatives.
I was elected and served two terms before running successfully for the Senate in 2024.
During these more recent four years in the House, I served on the Natural Resources Committee, which provided me the opportunity to focus on regulatory changes to make it possible to build more housing across Vermont.
In the House, you serve on one committee.
In the Senate, because there are only 30 members (as opposed to 150 in the House), we each serve on two committees.
In the morning, I serve on Natural Resources and Energy, where I have been able to continue to focus on Housing in particular.
In the afternoon, I chair the Education Committee where I have been deeply involved in efforts to transform Vermont’s education system and to dramatically slow the rate of increase in the property taxes we pay for education.
A key feature of my tenure on the Education Committee has been to give voice to rural Vermont.
At the end of the 2026 session, I received a letter from the Rural Schools Community Alliance thanking me for my efforts on behalf of rural Vermont.
Legal Career
After passing the bar exam, I spent a couple years as the Southern Vermont Director for the Vermont Natural Resources Council before joining the law firm of Witten, Saltonstall and Woolmington, where I focused on environmental work, municipal work and, for a time, criminal defense.
I became a partner in the firm, recruited Ed Campbell to join us and, eventually, we moved the entire operation from Bennington to Manchester, where it continues today as Woolmington, Campbell, Bent and Stasny.
A few highlights of my legal career were to serve as: the attorney who won the first designation of an Outstanding Resource Water (the Battenkill), the attorney who achieved the first designation of a Class 1 wetland (the Dorset Marsh) and as the attorney who put together the Equinox Preservation Trust permanently protecting 800 acres of Equinox Mountain.
In 1996, I ran against the Republican incumbent for the State Senate.
I lost by a whisker.
While initially disappointed, I instead directed my public service to other areas, like serving as town moderator, serving on the board of Burr and Burton and founding the Northshire Land Trust.
Hildene
I would likely have stayed with the firm for the rest of my career had the opportunity to run Hildene not presented itself.
I was offered the job and started on January 1, 2002.
I was there for the next 18 years, until “retiring” at the end of 2019.
I am particularly proud of a few accomplishments at Hildene: I built a 34-person staff of smart, remarkably dedicated people who, in turn, built Hildene into a world-class cultural heritage site.
They are amazing and I love them.
We also built a summer jobs program for high school and college students that continues to make a real difference in the lives of a lot of young people.
And, we made Hildene a role model for good citizenship and for caring about others.
Now, because of our overall success, Hildene attracts on the order of 40,000 visitors each year and is a key economic driver for the area.
I also learned from my time at Hildene how difficult it is to be an employer.
We were able to get a small retirement program in place for our employees and we were able to provide health insurance.
But, while we were able to pay well for a non-profit, it was not what it should have been and the health insurance we were able to offer employees and their families was sub-optimal.
I learned how hard it is to do as much as we would have liked and how hard it was for our employees to make the living they deserved.
I gained valuable perspective on the difficulties faced by Vermont working families and Vermont employers alike.
Community Service
Over the course of my professional career, I remained active in my community and my state.
I helped start and was the first chair of the Manchester Community Land Trust.
We have completed numerous land conservation projects that will have a lasting legacy by preserving active farmland, forestland and nature’s beauty for future generations.
I have served as town moderator and for nearly twenty years on the board of Burr and Burton Academy – as chair for the last fifteen years.
I have been appointed by Governors and Speakers of the House to serve on commissions dealing with affordable housing and education funding.
I was a founder of The Shires, an effort to foster collaboration and a pooling of effort and resources across Bennington County in order to form a cohesive whole on both a social and economic level.
Recently, I received achievement awards from both the Bennington County Regional Commission and the Bennington Area Chamber of Commerce for my work on behalf of the area in which we live and work.
In 2020 I was elected to the House, where I have served on the Environment and Energy Committee for the last four years.
I am a recognized leader on both environmental protection and setting the stage for the construction of housing that is affordable for Vermonters.
Now, I believe I can contribute meaningfully as a member of the Senate, where I will continue the work I have started in the House.
We will need to be laser-focused on lowering property taxes and fixing our system for funding education, health care, helping our struggling small businesses and non-profits and building the overall economy.
It is going to be hard work and will require a whole lot of people working toward a common goal.
I want to help.
