Keeping our communities safe is one of government’s most fundamental responsibilities.
It requires supporting the men and women of law enforcement, protecting the rights guaranteed by our Constitution, standing with victims, and ensuring that serious and repeat criminal conduct carries meaningful consequences.
Throughout my years in public service, my approach to public safety has been grounded in a few basic principles: law enforcement officers should have the tools and resources they need to do their jobs; victims should never become an afterthought in our justice system; due process and accountability must apply to everyone; and the decisions made by prosecutors, judges, and elected officials matter.
Criminal justice policy is not theoretical.
Decisions about enforcement, charging, sentencing, pretrial detention, and prosecution have real consequences for victims, law enforcement officers, defendants, families, and entire communities.
There are legitimate disagreements about how our criminal justice system should balance punishment, rehabilitation, discretion, and second chances.
Those disagreements deserve an honest discussion based on the public record.
Supporting the People Who Protect Our Communities
Supporting law enforcement requires more than saying the right words.
Officers and investigators need modern equipment, strong communication systems, effective coordination among agencies, and laws that keep pace with emerging threats.
In Congress, I have supported efforts to strengthen protections for children from online exploitation and abuse.
I supported the bipartisan TAKE IT DOWN Act, which was signed into law in 2025 and created stronger federal protections against nonconsensual intimate imagery, including realistic AI-generated material.
I have also worked to bring public safety resources directly home to Virginia.
Earlier this year, I secured $448,559 for Mathews County to upgrade its public safety radio system and improve interoperability among local, regional, state, and federal agencies.
In a rural community, reliable communication is not a luxury.
It can determine whether law enforcement officers, firefighters, emergency medical personnel, and neighboring jurisdictions can communicate quickly and effectively when seconds matter.
Technology is changing both crime and law enforcement.
Our laws, investigative tools, communications systems, and public safety infrastructure have to keep pace.
Public safety should ultimately be measured by whether victims are protected, whether law enforcement officers have what they need to do their jobs, whether dangerous offenders are held accountable, and whether our communities are safer.
Prevention Is Part of Public Safety
Strong enforcement alone cannot solve every public-safety problem.
Keeping communities safe also means addressing substance abuse and mental health challenges, helping families in crisis, supporting young people so they never end up in the criminal justice system, and strengthening partnerships among schools, local governments, nonprofit organizations, community groups, and law enforcement.
The goal should always be safer neighborhoods and stronger communities.
I strongly support the law enforcement officers who serve in the line of danger every day to keep us safe.
And prosecutors and courts have an important responsibility to hold criminals accountable.
Communities also play a critical part in preventing crime, supporting victims, and shaping neighborhoods where people look out for one another.
Public safety works best when law enforcement has the resources it needs, victims are taken seriously, constitutional rights are protected, and accountability is applied consistently.
Those principles are not in conflict.
They reinforce one another.
That is the approach I will continue to bring to my work in Congress: support law enforcement, protect victims, uphold constitutional rights, demand accountability, and work toward safer communities throughout Virginia.
— Rob Wittman
