My story begins in Richmond with an act of extraordinary generosity.
When I was born, my birth mother made the difficult decision to place me for adoption.
For the first months of my life, I was cared for by the Children’s Home Society of Virginia in Richmond.
At eight months old, I was blessed to be adopted by two wonderful parents who gave me a loving home, a strong foundation, and opportunities I never could have imagined.
My father was a World War II Army veteran.
My mother was a schoolteacher in Hanover County.
They did not teach me about success through words alone.
They taught me through example.
They taught me that hard work matters.
That service matters.
That character matters.
And that every person deserves dignity and respect.
Those lessons shaped every part of my life.
Growing up in Henrico County, I worked wherever I could find a job.
I bagged groceries, stocked shelves, painted houses, worked at Kings Dominion, and flipped eggs as a short-order cook at Shoney’s.
I worked on fishing boats in Reedville and the Outer Banks.
I spent summers working in a tomato cannery in Westmoreland County.
None of those jobs were glamorous.
But every one of them taught me something important.
I learned that there is dignity in hard work.
I learned that communities thrive when people look out for one another.
And I learned that opportunities should never be taken for granted.
Those experiences eventually led me to Virginia Tech, where I studied biology and served in the Corps of Cadets.
The Corps reinforced lessons about leadership, discipline, responsibility, and service that have remained with me throughout my life.
After college, I began a career in public health, spending 26 years working for the Virginia Department of Health and serving communities throughout the Northern Neck and Middle Peninsula.
Along the way, neighbors encouraged me to become involved in local government.
I served on the Montross Town Council, then as mayor, later on the Westmoreland County Board of Supervisors, in the Virginia House of Delegates, and eventually in the United States Congress.
Looking back, none of that was part of some grand plan.
I simply came to believe that when a community invests in you, you have a responsibility to give something back.
That belief has guided me throughout my career.
Kathryn and I have lived in the same home in Montross since 1985.
It is where we raised our children and where we now enjoy spending time with our grandchildren.
Washington never built me.
Virginia did.
The values I learned growing up in Henrico, working jobs across the Commonwealth, serving communities throughout the Northern Neck and Middle Peninsula, and raising a family in Montross remain the same values that guide me today.
Public service has never been about titles or positions for me.
It has always been about gratitude and responsibility.
I am grateful to the birth mother whose decision gave me opportunities she hoped I would have.
I am grateful to the parents who raised me.
I am grateful to the teachers, mentors, coworkers, neighbors, and communities that shaped me.
And I am grateful to the people of Virginia who have entrusted me with the privilege of serving them.
My story began because someone made an extraordinary decision to give me an opportunity.
I have tried never to take that opportunity for granted.
This community helped shape who I am.
Serving it remains one of the greatest honors of my life.
— Rob Wittman
