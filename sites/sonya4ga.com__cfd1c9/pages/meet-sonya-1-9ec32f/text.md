DONATE MEET Sonya Halpern My career has spanned boardrooms, community halls, and Georgia's legislative chamber - but across every role the throughline has been the same: turning bold ideas into real solutions that expand opportunity for people around me I’m Sonya Halpern—a Georgia State Senator, businesswoman, nonprofit leader, wife, mother of three, and lifelong believer in the power of opportunity to change lives.​ That belief began at home. ​ Where it Started​ My parents were both first-generation college graduates from rural Mississippi who met at Tougaloo College, an HBCU that helped transform the trajectory of their lives - and mine.
They instilled in me the values of education, hard work, service and the belief that where you start in life should not determine where you finish. ​ They taught those values as much by how we lived as by what they said.
We went to church every Sunday, where my dad sang in the choir.
My father was a voracious reader, and in our house learning wasn’t confined to school.
We might be forced to come to the dinner table ready to discuss a newspaper article, recite a poem we had memorized, or share a favorite Bible passage.
My mother taught us a different kind of strength - one grounded in grace, compassion, and speaking up for what was right.
Her work as a social worker and helping people with disabilities showed me early that service wasn't something you talked about; it was something you did.
Summers meant heading south to Carriere and Clarksdale, Mississippi and Memphis, Tennessee to see my grandparents and extended relatives.
Some of my favorite memories are the simplest ones—snapping green beans on the back porch with Mawmaw, going out into the field checking on the cows with PawPaw, and learning how to play piano chords with my Grandma Katie. ​ After earning my undergraduate degree and MBA, I entered the job market during a recession and struggled to find work.
At 23, I moved into the YWCA in White Plains, NY, sharing a small living space with three other women who started off as strangers and commuting into New York City for a string of temp jobs Whatever I thought my life plan was going to be, that was not it.
I was frustrated, a little embarrassed, BUT determined.
It taught me how hard it can be to stand on your own when others are questioning your decisions - including yourself. ​ It was humbling.
But it reinforced something I still believe today: talent and determination matter, but so does community.
None of us succeeds entirely on our own. ​ ​ Building a Career, Building a Life Over the next decade, I built a career in sales, marketing, and business development, including helping launch ESPN's first digital advertising initiatives.
That work eventually brought me to Georgia, where I have built a life with my husband, Daniel, and our three children.
From the beginning, entrepreneurship has been part of that life.
Daniel has spent 30+ years building a successful business, which has given me both a front row seat and a personal stake in what it takes to navigate changing economic climates, manage uncertainty, and make the difficult decisions that come with running a business. ​ ​ Turns out, sales was pretty good preparation for life.
Learning how to listen, build relationships, make your case, and hear "no" without giving up prepared me for just about everything I've done since - from corporate leadership to non-profit boardrooms and community work, and eventually public service.
Different settings, different challenges, but the goal was always the same: solving problems and turning good ideas into real results.
Along the way, I learned that you can hold your ground without losing grace.
That blend of grit and grace is what led me to name my consulting practice Whiskey in a Teacup - a nod to the kind of leadership that can be both strong and steady, thoughtful and bold.
Rooted in Georgia For nearly 30 years, Georgia has been my home.
I have had the privilege of serving with several organizations including Habitat for Humanity, the YMCA of Metro Atlanta, and the National Black Arts Festival.
My commitment to civic leadership and the arts eventually took me to the national stage, when President Barack Obama appointed me to the President's Advisory Committee on the Arts at the John F.
Kennedy Center for Performing Arts in D.C., where I later became the first Georgian and first African American to serve as Chair.
Those experiences showed me firsthand what strong institutions and committed people can accomplish.
They also reinforced my belief that leadership is ultimately about leaving people and places better than you found them. ​ That work also showed me that some challenges are systemic and bigger than any one organization.
Education, healthcare, economic mobility, housing, and public safety require collaboration, good public policy and a government that works for the people it serves.
That realization eventually led me to elected office. ​ Why I Serve Today, I am proud to represent Georgia’s 39th Senate District, one of the most socioeconomically diverse districts in the state - and in the country.
Every day I meet people from different backgrounds, neighborhoods, professions, and life experiences.
Listening keeps me grounded.
I t reminds me that good ideas can come from anywhere and that wisdom is often found in places others overlook. ​ I don't believe in politics as performance.
My focus is simple: expand opportunity, solve problems and help create the conditions for people to thrive.
That is why I serve. ​ Moving Georgia Forward At my core, I am an optimist.
Not because I think progress happens automatically, but because I've seen what we can accomplish when people roll up their sleeves, tackle hard problems, and do the work.
Despite all the noise in politics today, I remain hopeful.
The people I meet every day are the reason why.
They continue to inspire me, and they remind me that Georgia's best days are not behind us — we still have the power to build them together.​ ​ ​ Make a Contribution Every dollar makes a difference. ​ Your support powers the organizing, outreach, and events that are building a stronger, more equitable Georgia.
