The Problem Here
Cotton, soybean, and corn producers across Gibson, Haywood, Crockett, Madison, and Henderson counties absorbed the cost of a tariff fight they did not start, while import prices, equipment, and land rent kept climbing.
Farming here is a generational legacy operating on a single-digit margin.
What It Means for TN-8
The Mississippi River corridor is this district's export ramp.
Keep it dredged, keep the roads to it in repair, and West Tennessee grain competes with anybody's.
What I'll Do in Congress
- 1Push emergency and bridge relief for producers hit by retaliatory tariffs, paid out on the crop-year calendar farmers actually live on — not eighteen months late.
- 2Defend and strengthen crop insurance and disaster programs in the Farm Bill, with coverage that reflects real West Tennessee input costs.
- 3Reopen and diversify export markets so no single trade dispute can wipe out a season, and hold hearings when a policy decision costs this district money.
- 4Fund the rural infrastructure farming depends on: river terminals, grain roads and bridges, rail spurs, and broadband for precision ag.
