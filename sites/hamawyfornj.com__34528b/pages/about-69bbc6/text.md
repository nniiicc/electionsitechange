Adam Hamawy About Me I am a doctor, veteran, small business owner, and proud father running to represent New Jersey’s 12th District.
I grew up working-class in Old Bridge and earned an ROTC scholarship to pay for my education at Rutgers.
I joined the New Jersey Army National Guard and worked my way through New Jersey Medical School.
While in residency, I treated victims and other first responders in a field hospital at Ground Zero on 9/11.
I rose to the rank of Lt.
Colonel in the Army and served as a combat trauma surgeon in Iraq at the height of the war.
One of my patients later became a United States Senator – Tammy Duckworth.
Since completing my service, I have worked in New Jersey emergency rooms and built my own practice in Princeton.
Over the last 30 years, I have volunteered to treat patients in war and disaster zones, including in Bosnia, Haiti, Sudan, Colombia, Belize, Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, Turkey, and Jordan.
In 2024 and 2025, I went on medical missions to Gaza, where I was trapped with my colleagues when Israel seized the Rafah Crossing.
I refused to leave until all my colleagues, including US green card holders and other nationals, were guaranteed safe passage alongside the American citizens in the group.
I was honored to be Rep.
Bonnie Watson Coleman’s State of the Union guest in 2025.
A Life of Service 1970 s When I was just seven months old, my family came to New Jersey for a chance at the American Dream.
I grew up in Plainfield and Old Bridge and attended public schools.
1989 I enlisted in the New Jersey Army National Guard, serving until 1991.
It was an opportunity to serve my country and help pay for college.
This began my over 20 years of service in the U.S. military.
1991 I graduated from Rutgers University, New Brunswick, with a major in biology.
1994 As a medical school student, I went on my first volunteer humanitarian medical mission.
I worked with the United Nations in Bosnia to deliver medical supplies to hospitals and witnessed genocide firsthand.
The following are excerpts from an interview I did with The Star-Ledger after my trip.
The Star-Ledger, 1996 Hamawy … spent five weeks of his 1994 summer vacation helping doctors ferry supplies throughout war-torn Bosnia because, “I didn’t want to just watch television.
I wanted to do something to help.” “We went out to hospitals around the area and in the mountains to check what supplies they needed and we tried to deliver them.” He said he learned doctors were targeted by Bosnian Serbs in shelling attacks and hadn’t been paid for two years — causing many to starve, but “I learned what being dedicated was all about.” “They didn’t stop helping patients,” Hamawy said.
“They are my role models.” 1996 I graduated from New Jersey Medical School, now part of Rutgers University, Newark.
2001 On 9/11, while a surgical resident in New York, I treated victims and fellow first responders at a field hospital at Ground Zero.
Watch the video on X → 2003 I began my active duty in the U.S.
Army.
2004 At the height of the Iraq War, I served as a U.S.
Army combat trauma surgeon.
Among the lives our team saved was a future U.S.
Senator — Tammy Duckworth of Illinois.
2006 Given the increasing number of wounded troops in Iraq and Afghanistan, the U.S.
Army's small handful of plastic and reconstructive surgeons proved insufficient.
As part of the recruiting effort to address the shortage, I was proud to be selected for additional specialty training after I returned from Iraq.
I was sent to the University of Texas Southwestern in Dallas, a top program in the field, where I began my career in plastic and reconstructive surgery.
2010 After the earthquake in Haiti, I joined medical teams caring for survivors.
2011 I retired from the U.S.
Army as a Lieutenant Colonel.
2013 I became a small business owner, launching my own medical practice in Princeton.
2014 I began over a decade of work in local emergency rooms as an attending surgeon at Penn Medicine Princeton Medical Center and Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital in New Brunswick.
I went on a medical mission to Jordan to assist refugees of the Syrian civil war.
This would be the first of four medical missions over the decade to support victims of the war.
2018 While running my medical practice in the Princeton area, I earned an MBA from Rutgers University, Camden.
2024 I went on a medical mission to Gaza, treating patients while bombs were falling.
While I was there, Israel seized the Rafah Crossing and our medical team was trapped.
When only healthcare professionals with American citizenship were granted safe departure, I refused to leave my colleagues with green card status and other nationals behind.
My refusal, along with that of two of my colleagues, helped bring enough media attention and pressure to ensure the safety of our full team.
The Washington Post, May 18, 2024 “I want our President to know that we are not safe,” he said.
“As a doctor, I cannot abandon the remaining members of my team, and as a former soldier, I cannot abandon my fellow Americans.” Hamawy and his colleagues don’t know when they might get the opportunity to leave.
In his statement, Hamawy apologized to his family for not going home.
“I know it hurts that I am not coming home this weekend, and I am sorry,” he said.
“But I know that you are proud that I am upholding my oath to never leave anyone behind.” In a message to The Post, he said, “if all the U.S. citizens left, what would that say about us as a country?
That is not who we are.” 2025 I returned to Gaza on another medical mission.
I was Congresswoman Bonnie Watson Coleman's guest at the State of the Union.
2026 I won the Democratic primary in New Jersey's 12th District.

Plan to Vote → Why I'm Running To build a government that works for you, not special interests In the richest country in the world, you shouldn't have to choose between taking your medication and paying your bills.
Your child's education shouldn't depend on their zip code.
The economy should work for people whose hard work builds and sustains our communities—not just billionaires and corporations.
Our country faces crises of affordability and authoritarianism.
We need a new generation of leaders with the courage to stand up to corruption and the competence to make life better for working families.
My Priorities Healthcare I believe healthcare is a human right, not a privilege.
I'll fight for Medicare for All, cancel medical debt, and take on insurers and Big Pharma so every family can afford care.
Education Public schools built my career — from Plainfield classrooms to Rutgers.
I'll fully fund public education, protect teachers, and make college and trade school affordable for every kid.
Civil Rights We face an authoritarian crisis, and too many Democrats are playing it safe.
I'll defend voting rights, reproductive freedom, and LGBTQ+ equality — and hold every administration accountable.
Economic Justice The richest country in history shouldn't leave families scrambling for rent and groceries.
I'll raise the minimum wage, tax billionaires, strengthen unions, and rein in corporate price gouging.
Foreign Policy As a combat surgeon who served in Iraq and on missions from Haiti to Sudan, I've seen war's true cost.
I'll put diplomacy first, demand human rights, and end blank-check wars.
Veterans I served as a Lt.
Colonel in the U.S.
Army, and I know the promises we break.
I'll protect the VA, expand mental health care, end veteran homelessness, and deliver the benefits earned.
Fighting Corruption & Fixing Democracy Congress answers to billionaires and lobbyists instead of us — by design.
I'll ban congressional stock trading, get dark money out of politics, and work to restore balance to the courts.
Climate Change From Haiti's earthquakes to New Jersey's superstorms, I've responded to climate disasters firsthand.
I'll fight for a Green New Deal, invest in clean energy jobs, and hold polluters accountable.
