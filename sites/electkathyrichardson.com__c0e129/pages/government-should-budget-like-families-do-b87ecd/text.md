Every family understands what it means to make difficult financial decisions.
Parents sit at kitchen tables deciding what bills can wait another week.
Small business owners delay purchases and reduce expenses when revenue falls short.
Retirees carefully monitor every dollar because they know fixed incomes leave little room for error.
People understand budgeting because they live it every day.
That is why so many residents are frustrated by what they see happening at the state level.
Washington’s budget has grown dramatically over the past several years.
At the same time, many families feel less financially secure, not more.
Residents continue facing rising costs for housing, utilities, insurance, groceries, transportation, and healthcare while also seeing continued discussions around additional taxes and fees.
Many people are asking:
When will the government start making the same prioritization decisions families make?
Government has important responsibilities:
- public safety
- infrastructure
- education
- healthcare systems
- protecting vulnerable populations
But responsible stewardship matters.
Taxpayer dollars should be managed with transparency, accountability, and long-term sustainability in mind.
People are increasingly concerned about spending programs where measurable outcomes are unclear or where funds cannot easily be tracked or accounted for.
State Auditor reports over the years have identified concerns involving accountability, internal controls, and management practices within various agencies and programs.
That should not be ignored.
Accountability is not anti-government.
It is good government.
Another growing concern involves long-term financial obligations and sustainability.
Washington, like many states, must balance pension obligations, public services, infrastructure needs, and future economic uncertainty.
Many residents are asking reasonable questions:
- What happens if revenue projections fall short?
- What happens if higher-income earners and businesses continue leaving the state?
- How sustainable is continued spending growth?
- Are we protecting future generations from unsustainable obligations?
These are not abstract policy discussions, they directly affect families, businesses, retirees, and young people trying to build a future here.
Washington has extraordinary potential, but long-term success requires responsible stewardship.
Families understand that you cannot indefinitely spend beyond sustainable revenue without consequences.
Government should operate with the same level of responsibility, discipline, and accountability expected from the people funding it.
That is not a partisan principle.
It is common sense.
