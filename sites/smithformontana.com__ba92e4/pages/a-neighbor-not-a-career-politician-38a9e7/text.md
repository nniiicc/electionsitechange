Before you open it, here is what I would like you to know.
What I did this year I have lived in the Heights for more than twenty-five years.
This year I knocked on [FILL: door count] doors here.
Most of them belonged to people who will not vote for me.
I knocked anyway, because that is the job.
Every door I missed got a postcard in my handwriting saying I came by and I would still like to hear what is on their mind.
Some people wrote back.
A few invited me for coffee.
One or two argued with me for half an hour on their own front step and then shook my hand.
That is a neighborhood.
It is the thing worth having, and we still have it.
What I heard Property taxes that keep climbing, and relief that came with a form some people never found.
A school where the bullying is not getting handled, and a parent who called and emailed and asked a specific question about their own child and never got an answer.
A power bill people are watching nervously while a company from out of state asks for thousands of megawatts, with the contract blacked out and the decisions moved to Helena.
A clinic too far away, and a wait too long.
A promise to a retired teacher that somebody in Helena would like to quietly forget.
An elderly neighbor going hungry.
A disabled worker, “laid off” and overlooked on new applications.
None of that is a Republican problem or a Democratic problem.
It is a Heights problem.
And all of it has solutions.
Why me I fix things for a living.
I have run a small business here since 2001, which means twenty-five years of making payroll, keeping promises that cost money, and getting told no on a Tuesday.
I serve on a disability-led board where the people affected direct the work, and where I am regularly the least expert person in the room.
That has been good for me.
I am working with the Heights Business Association to build a TIF district, so this part of town stops getting skipped when the money gets handed out.
None of that is a political résumé.
It is the reason I think I can do this job.
What the job actually is I will not promise you outcomes.
One legislator is one vote out of a hundred, and anyone telling you otherwise at your door is hoping you will not check.
What I will promise is the part that is entirely mine to give.
I will listen first.
I will show up.
I will tell you when I disagree with you, at your door, to your face, the way I have all year.
Solving one person’s problem is a good day.
Figuring out what keeps causing it and fixing that is a better one.
That is the whole reason I want this job.
Either way Whether or not you vote for me, I will still be your neighbor on November 4, and you will still be my constituent.
That does not change based on how you fill out a ballot.
Government belongs to us.
We reclaim it when we show up.
Your ballot is in the mail.
Please fill it out, sign the envelope, and send it back.
