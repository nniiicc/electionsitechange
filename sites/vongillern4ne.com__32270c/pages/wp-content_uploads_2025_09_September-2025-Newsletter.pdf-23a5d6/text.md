September 25, 2025 Greetings, neighbors!
Some people think of the time between legislative sessions as a “summer vacation”.
However, my colleagues and I in the Legislature have been taking advantage of the interim to continue the important work we were elected to do.
In this newsletter, I want to share several of the things I’ve been up to over the past couple of months: ➢ By the time you all are reading this, the Infrastructure Review Task Force will have met twice to discuss the complexities of Nebraska transportation infrastructure and financing.
This task force was created by LB 558, which I introduced this past legislative session to carve out a place for these critical conversations to take place.
Task force meetings include several Nebraska state senators including the Speaker, officials from Nebraska Department of Transportation, representatives from the executive branch, and experts and stakeholders in private industry.
The conversations have been profoundly informative about the ins-and-outs of Nebraska’s transportation.
I am looking forward to future work with this group to ensure Nebraskans have safe roads to move people and goods quickly and efficiently. ➢ I hosted a series of workshops this spring and summer for Nebraska senators and their staff to learn about all things property taxes—valuations and levies, property assessments, uniformity and proportionality, county and school finance, and more.
My gratitude to Jon Cannon of NACO and Colby Coash of the Nebraska Association of School Boards for this learning opportunity.
Over 100 attended the three sessions and were able to receive a first-class study in preparation for critical conversations about property taxes ahead of the upcoming legislative session and beyond.
I have approached these workshops as vital opportunities to ensure that everyone understands “the math” in order to develop good policy. ➢ I attended multiple conferences and conventions over the summer to take advantage of learning and growth opportunities.
Some highlights included an American Legislative Exchange Council (ALEC) conference in Indianapolis, Indiana, and a Council of State Governments (CSG) convention in Saskatoon in Saskatchewan, Canada.
At the ALEC conference in July, I met and conversed with lawmakers around the country to exchange ideas and learn about how other states are addressing their property tax challenges.
In Saskatoon, I was able to listen to and take part in discussions about the energy sector and its future, including both fossil fuel and clean energy as well as next-gen nuclear.
Saskatchewan is a leading producer of critical minerals that are needed for tech, and energy and expanding our friendship with our neighbors is important. ➢ I’ve had conversations with Omaha, Millard, and Elkhorn Public Schools over the summer about a number of matters, including their fiscal responsibility to taxpayers to manage their finances well.
Some of these conversations went more smoothly than others.
That being said, I believe strongly in the importance of maintaining productive dialogue with these educational leaders because they really are the “boots on the ground” in our community, working hard for our kids and for the future of our city, state and nation. ➢ I have had multiple meetings with the Aksarben Foundation, state and local chambers of commerce, and business leaders to discuss how to grow job opportunities in the metro area and plug the “brain drain” affecting Nebraska.
I am happy to report that there are many excellent efforts already underway to address this, and I look forward to sharing more later.
As the interim progresses and the 2026 session nears, my office will be working to develop and refine ideas for legislation to introduce that will meaningfully benefit constituents of District 4 and all Nebraskans by improving economic outlook and public safety.
In this effort, we will continue to collaborate with other senators and their staff of all political affiliations to formulate solutions that work.
I’d love to hear your thoughts on these issues—please reach out to my office with any ideas, suggestions, or concerns.
Sincerely, Senator R.
Brad von Gillern District 4
