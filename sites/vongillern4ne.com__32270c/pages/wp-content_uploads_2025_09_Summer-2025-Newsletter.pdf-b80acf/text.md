The 2025 Legislative Session was eventful and productive, especially for the Revenue Committee.
Our committee passed four major bills (LB 647, LB 649, LB 650, and LB 707) related to revenue, tax incentives, and economic development.
With LB 647, we took two significant steps to help Nebraska kids by passing the Adoption Tax Credit Act and expanding opportunities for educational savings accounts.
Under the Adoption Tax Credit Act, Nebraskans who adopt children and are eligible for the federal adoption credit can claim a tax credit against their Nebraska income tax equal to 10% of their federal credit.
This will translate, on average, to an extra $400-500 in the pockets of these loving Nebraska families.
Having two adopted children, I know that this credit is a small step, but an important one for families.
LB 647 will also allow funds saved in a 529 NEST account to be tax exempt when used to pay for tuition at a K-12 school, helping hardworking Nebraskans afford a quality education that aligns with their values without sending public funds to private schools.
LB 649 aims to draw high-paying jobs in the defense industry to Nebraska over the next 10 years by offering tax credits for defense firms that create jobs paying $90,000 or more in our state.
As efforts at the federal level shake up the defense industry in and around D.C. and Northern Virginia, this bill will help Nebraska attract high-value economic opportunity.
As a part of the means to close the budget gap for the upcoming biennium, LB 650 took a hard look at tax credits and incentives offered to businesses in Nebraska and employed a “last in, first out” approach to reduce or eliminate underutilized programs, delivering some $51M in savings to the state budget.
Finally, and of special importance for the people of the greater Omaha area, LB 707 delivered critical improvements to Nebraska’s Good Life District program for economic development.
The measures enacted in this bill will ensure that projects demonstrate financial viability and provide a meaningful return on investment for the state.
LB 707 passed unanimously in the Unicameral and closes a number of loopholes that could have cost the state tens of millions of dollars in the future.
The Legislature also passed the proposal contained in my bill, LB 329, to increase protections for students from sexual abuse by school employees and contract workers.
This issue was especially important to me as a family in our District 4 community came to testify in support of the bill and tell their heartbreaking personal story.
