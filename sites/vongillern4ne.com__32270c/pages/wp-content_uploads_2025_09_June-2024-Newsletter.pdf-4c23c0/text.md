I share a few updates on activities during the​ ​Legislative Interim below.​ ​On May 20th, the Building and Maintenance Committee took a visit to the Office of the​ ​Chief Information Office, better known as the 501 Building.
I serve as the Committee​ ​Chair.
Other members are Senators Clements, Dorn, Ibach, Hughes, and McDonnell.​ ​Mike Heaps of the State Building Division joined us along with Jason Jackson, Sarah​ ​Skinner, and Nick Pischel of the Task Force for Building Renewal.​ ​As a committee, we work with the Task Force in an observation role reviewing state​ ​facility improvements.
The 501 facility is located across the street from the State Capitol​ ​and houses the technology infrastructure for all state assets.
This includes hosting the​ ​cloud system, databases, employee information, state security systems, and building​ ​monitoring.
The building has seen numerous upgrades in recent years and is​ ​state-of-the-art, using energy-efficient measures to steward state funds.
The facility is​ ​very secure both in its physical makeup and its digital upkeep for the state.
The​ ​committee was very impressed with the security parameters involved in monitoring state​ ​sites and protecting state data systems.​ ​Additionally, the committee took a look at the impressive Chief Standing Bear​ ​monument located outside the Chief Standing Bear Justice Administration Building.
We​ ​learned about Chief Standing Bear’s legacy and how the project came to fruition.
The​ ​monument also features a sculpture created by artist Benjamin Victor.
On July 21st,​ ​2022, the building was officially renamed the Chief Standing Bear Justice Administration​ ​Building along with an unveiling of the sculpture.
This was all made possible under​ ​LB271 introduced by Senator Tom Brewer and former Senator Patty Pansing Brooks.​ ​The sculpture is located at 521 South 14th Street in Lincoln.​ ​I continue to work closely with colleagues as part of Governor Pillen’s Taxation Task​ ​Force.
We are busy with meetings in Lincoln, Omaha, North Platte, Norfolk and​ ​Columbus, working to solve Nebraska’s property tax crisis.
Governor Pillen has​ ​announced a “save the date” for a special session on this topic beginning late July and​ ​ending mid-August.
It is yet to be seen if a plan can be developed that will gain the​ ​needed 33 votes to pass.​ ​Finally, I want to wish all the dads out there a Happy Father’s Day!
