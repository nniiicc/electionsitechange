Join us on Friday, Oct.
16, from 1:30 to 3:30 p.m. at the Lincoln-Douglas Debate Museum Auditorium.
The event is part of Bob Fioretti's planned series scheduled during the same historic time frame as the Lincoln-Douglas debates of 1858.
Bob believes the goal is to carry forward an Illinois tradition built around candidates appearing before the public, discussing the issues directly, and giving voters a chance to hear from them in person.
All are welcome to attend.
Charleston will host the fourth debate in a seven-event series.
The debate will take place at the Lincoln-Douglas Debate Museum Auditorium The fourth debate is scheduled for the Lincoln-Douglas Debate Museum Auditorium in Charleston, a fitting location for an event tied to Illinois political history.
Event detail Information Event Fourth of seven Lincoln-Douglas Debate events Date Friday, Oct.

Location Lincoln-Douglas Debate Museum Auditorium, Charleston, Illinois Contact KAT@BobforIllinois.com, 773-828-9696 The event continues a series designed to mirror the spirit of the 1858 Lincoln-Douglas debates, when Abraham Lincoln and Stephen A.
Douglas appeared before voters across Illinois and argued directly over the major questions facing the country.
For Fioretti, the Lincoln-Douglas Debate format offers a reminder that public accountability has long been part of Illinois politics.
The series connects modern issues with an older Illinois debate tradition.
Attorney General Kwame Raoul was invited to participate Attorney General Kwame Raoul received an invitation to participate in the Charleston debate.
In that invitation, Fioretti's plan is to use moderators from both major political parties.
The stated goal is to promote a respectful format that gives voters a clear view of where candidates stand.
Fioretti believes that the debate series as an opportunity for civil discussion, substantive answers, and direct engagement with Illinois voters.
A Chicago Tribune editorial renewed attention on candidate debates The event comes as debate participation has drawn renewed attention in Illinois political media.
In a recent editorial titled “Who’s afraid of a big, bad debate?”, the Chicago Tribune Editorial Board emphasized the role of debates and public forums during election season.
The board wrote that candidate conversations can take several forms, including in person, virtual, or questionnaire-based formats.
The editorial also criticized candidates who decline opportunities to appear with their peers.
“During election season, the board hosts candidate endorsement conversations in person, virtually, or in questionnaire form.
We find it particularly disappointing when candidates refuse to show up to debates with their peers.” — Chicago Tribune Editorial Board, “Who’s afraid of a big, bad debate?” Illinois political history remains part of the Charleston debate setting.
The Charleston event is open to the public The Oct.
16 debate is open to anyone who wants to attend.
We are encouraging residents, voters, students, civic leaders, and members of the public to take part in the event by being present for the discussion.
The format is intended to give attendees a chance to hear candidates address issues directly in a shared public setting.
The debate is scheduled from 1:30 to 3:30 p.m.
The afternoon timing allows the event to serve as both a civic gathering and a historical observance tied to Charleston’s place in Illinois political history.
While the subject matter will be contemporary, the setting points back to a time when political debate was a major public event.
This tradition still matters because voters benefit when candidates appear publicly, answer questions, and address competing views.
The next step for the series will be continuing through the remaining debate locations after the Charleston event.
Bob Fioretti believes this overall effort as a way to promote civility, public access, and issue-focused discussion across Illinois.
