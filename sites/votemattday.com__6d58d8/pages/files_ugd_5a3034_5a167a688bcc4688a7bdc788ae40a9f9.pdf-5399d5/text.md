House of Representatives, Georgia’s 1st Congressional District Letter to the Citizens of Georgia’s 1st Congressional District Dear Citizens of Georgia’s 1st Congressional District, The Okefenokee Swamp and its sister refuge at Banks are more than wetlands — they are emblems of Georgia’s pride, perseverance, and natural beauty.
As your candidate for Congress, I am committed to protecting these lands through action, not rhetoric.
After years of under-resourced operations, it is time for a major reinvestment in the very people and facilities that keep this landscape alive.
I will work with Congress to secure a $10 million one-time federal allocation for the Okefenokee and Banks National Wildlife Refuges.
This initiative will:     Build a new operations and maintenance compound at Okefenokee; Equip the refuge with a complete modern heavy-equipment fleet; Fund infrastructure repairs to levees, roads, and docks; and Strengthen regional coordination with the Savannah Coastal Refuges Complex.
To sustain these investments, I will also push for annual budget increases of $2 million for Okefenokee and $1 million for the Savannah Complex, ensuring long-term capacity for tourism, education, and conservation.
These projects are not excess — they are essential.
By self-performing maintenance, habitat restoration, and emergency response, our refuges will save millions of taxpayer dollars every year, create local jobs, and reinforce the sovereignty of America’s public lands.
Together, we can secure a stronger, safer, more self-reliant future for Georgia’s wild heart.
Sincerely, Matt Day Candidate, U.S.

House of Representatives Georgia’s 1st Congressional District 3 Funding GA’s Okefenokee & Coastal National Wildlife Refuges Section A: The Case for Modernization The Okefenokee NWR is the largest refuge east of the Mississippi and the centerpiece of Georgia’s 1st District, which contains more refuges in one region than any other district on the U.S. mainland.
Years of under-funding have limited staff capability, delayed projects, and increased reliance on costly private contractors.
The Okefenokee alone sees over 400,000 visitors annually.
Modernization will give refuge personnel the ability to perform:      Fire prevention and suppression; Levee and culvert reconstruction; Road and dock maintenance; Habitat restoration and water-control operations; and Emergency relief following storms and floods.
