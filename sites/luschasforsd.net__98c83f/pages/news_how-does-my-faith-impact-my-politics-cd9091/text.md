2 Aug Sunday, 7:22 PM · 2026 How does my faith impact my politics?
“Tell me how your faith impacts your politics (which is distinctly different from having your politics impact your faith).” That was a message that I received from a constituent while I was campaigning for the primary election.
It is a fair question since I advertise myself as a Christian running on Christian Principles.
But it is a tough question.
I had to take some time to organize my thoughts and find the right words for an adequate answer.
I think, I got it now: I am an old-school Christian who believes that you should be able to tell I’m a Christian because of the way I behave, not because I tell you that I’m Christian.
But how exactly does it impact my politics and my campaign?
First let’s start with some Christian principles, and then get into specific examples from my campaign.
The 1 st Commandment: “You shall have no other gods before Me.” (Exodus 20:3) I put God first and don’t get upset or worked up about worldly issues.
Worldly issues can become like God to many people.
I strive to focus my attention on what matters above instead of fleeting worldly concerns.
For example, when my wife and I were cursed at or treated rudely while knocking a door, what did we do?
We prayed.
Without a doubt, the best moment while knocking doors during my campaign was when a constituent laid his hand on my shoulder and prayed with and for me.
The 9 th Commandment: “You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor.” (Exodus 20:16) Honesty in speech is just as important as honesty in action.
Actions need to be consistent with words.
My campaign is based on facts and principals.
All information that we use for the campaign and that we put on my website is checked and verified to the very best of our abilities.
My family has helped with several bills in Pierre during the last two legislative sessions – concerning Cash in 2025 & 2026 , concerning Data Centers in 2026 , and concerning Digital Money in 2025 & 2026 .
If you listen to the testimony for these bills, we brought statistics, handouts, and references for everything we presented to committee.
We had to be “right with God,” as one of the sponsors of one of our bills said, by telling the truth and not misleading the Legislators (like the lobbyists do!).
The Golden Rule: “Do to others as you would have them do to you” (Matthew 7:12) I strive to behave in the best way, no matter how others behave towards me.
For example, when I was screamed at or rudely had doors slammed in my face, I didn’t scream back.
I kept smiling, prayed, and kept walking.
Social Media and AI As an engineer, I have seen how social media and AI have been designed to make users addicted, unproductive, and controllable.
This is the primary reason why I did not and will not use social media during my campaign, nor did I or will I use AI to write any of my content.
My faith also plays a role in this decision.
God gave us our body, mind and spirit.
They are sacred gifts and should be honored by being used.
The idea that God, purely through his word, created the universe in which we abide, speaks to the sanctity of our own use of words—and why we should value them.
If I get elected to go to Pierre this approach will not change.
I will think for myself, create my own content and constituents can call, text, or email me to get in touch with me.
Bible verses can be interpreted in many ways, let me share with you some of my interpretations: “I have much to write to you, but I do not want to use paper and ink.
Instead, I hope to visit you and talk with you face to face, so that our joy may be complete.” (2 John 1:12) To me, this means in-person interactions are preferred over all other communication methods.
It’s the human connection that has been gifted to us by God.
“And Jesus grew in wisdom and stature, and in favor with God and man.” (Luke 2:52) It is unclear to me how we can grow in wisdom if we are using AI to think for us.
“If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given to you.” – (James 1:5) Ask God, not AI.
Examples of Christian Principles in My Campaign Below are examples of how I applied my Christian faith principles during my District 2 primary campaign.
I told the truth about where I live.
I told the truth about my occupation and employer.
I told the truth about my party affiliation.
I told the truth about why I am running for office.
I did not bribe or offer kickbacks to anyone for votes.
I did not take any monetary contributions to my campaign because money in politics is not good for freedom.
When I received an unsolicited check in the mail from a PAC, I used my own money on a stamp to send it back.
I did take all the prayers, though, that voters would offer.
I did not use social media or AI during my campaign.
When I heard that others posted misinformation about me on social media, I still didn't use it.
My actions are consistent with my words.
When I wasn’t invited to candidate events organized by the Chamber of Commerce or the School District, I prayed and held no grudges against the organizers.
My opponents mobilized their colleague to look up my children’s school records.
They then questioned how I could represent District 2 because of my children’s records.
I answered the colleague’s question truthfully and even transparently posted the answer on my website (under Platform ->School Choice).
The bills I would bring and the votes I would cast in Pierre will be consistent with the Scorecard and Platform outlined on this website.
(If you need an extra rack card to post on your refrigerator to hold me accountable, I’d be happy to drop one off.) My wife and I prayed at every door we knocked.
That’s right, 3000 people got prayers in our District.
Now that’s more powerful than any vote I could ever cast in Pierre!
