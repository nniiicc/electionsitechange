| TRUMBULL – State Representatives David Rutigliano (R-Trumbull) attended the 2026 Trade Internship Program graduation ceremony hosted by United Illuminating (UI) and Southern Connecticut Gas (SCG) on Wednesday, August 19, at the United Illuminating Training Yard, located in Trumbull.
The program gives local high school and community college students hands-on experience in the electric and natural gas utility industries, working alongside experienced field employees, union leaders and operations teams.
In addition to developing practical skills, participants gain firsthand exposure to careers in the skilled trades and the opportunities available through apprenticeships, technical education, and workforce training.
Rutigliano said programs like the UI and SCG internship are an important part of developing Connecticut’s future workforce while showing young people that a four-year college degree is not the only path to a successful career.
“College is absolutely the right choice for many students, but it isn't the only path to a good career,” said Rep.
Rutigliano.
“We need to do a better job showing young people all of the opportunities that are available to them.
The skilled trades offer good-paying, highly skilled careers where there is real demand for workers right here in Connecticut.” Rutigliano has supported efforts to expand workforce-development opportunities and strengthen the pipeline of skilled workers needed by Connecticut employers.
“For years, employers have been telling us they need more skilled workers,” Rutigliano said.
“Programs like this help connect that need with young people who are deciding what comes next after high school.
When students can actually work alongside people in these professions, learn the skills and see the career opportunities for themselves, it can open a door they may never have considered.” Rutigliano said developing a stronger skilled workforce is particularly important as Connecticut employers compete for electricians, utility workers, construction professionals, manufacturing employees and other skilled workers.
“Workforce development is economic development,” Rutigliano said.
“If we want Connecticut businesses to grow and stay here, we need a workforce with the skills to fill the jobs they are creating.
Programs like this are good for students, good for employers and good for Connecticut.” The graduation ceremony recognized students for completing the internship and highlighted the skills, training and career experience they gained through the program.
HARTFORD, CT — State Representative David Rutigliano, the Ranking Member of the General Law Committee, today joined Governor Ned Lamont and a group of bipartisan legislative colleagues for the signing of SB 5, the Connecticut Artificial Intelligence Responsibility and Transparency Act, a landmark law establishing clear safety standards for powerful artificial intelligence systems while positioning Connecticut to compete in an increasingly AI-driven economy.
"Artificial intelligence is rapidly transforming the way we live, work, and do business," said Rep.
Rutigliano.
"Many residents have concerns over this looming technology.
Connecticut has an opportunity to embrace innovation while ensuring appropriate safeguards are in place to protect consumers, especially children, promote greater transparency, and build public trust in these emerging technologies.
SB5 also contained strong protections for youth and the use of social media, including tools for parents and robust age verification system, initiatives I fully support.
The legislation creates a framework for responsible AI development and deployment, helping ensure that advanced AI systems are used ethically and transparently while encouraging continued investment and innovation throughout the state.
"We need to strike a balance between fostering technological and economic growth and addressing legitimate concerns about accountability and safety," Rutigliano added.
"This law demonstrates that Connecticut can be a leader in both innovation and responsible governance.
I appreciate Governor Lamont's leadership in signing this important legislation and look forward to seeing its positive impact on our state's future." The Connecticut Artificial Intelligence Responsibility and Transparency Act will prepare Connecticut's workforce, businesses, and public institutions for the opportunities and challenges presented by rapidly advancing AI technologies.

I join my colleagues in calling for long-term solutions to lower energy costs for state residents.
There are also some short-term proposals we’re offering to ease their burden now.
I am just as frustrated as the Trumbull families and seniors living on fixed incomes are with this massive increase in their electric bill, on top of all other rising costs,” said Rutigliano.

The proposals mirror those offered by the House and Senate Republicans in February, when they urged action as they reminded Democrats about the financial pressure on constituents caused by high electric bills.
Watch the press conference here.
The proposals outlined Thursday include: Limit all future Power Purchase Agreements so that no contract can be for more than 150% over the wholesale electric market price.
Study moving public policy charges off ratepayer bills and into the budget.
Redefine Class I renewable energy sources to include all forms of hydropower and all nuclear power to lower the cost of these energy sources.
Separate the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP) and the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) Cover the portion of rate increases associated with the moratorium on electric service shutoffs by reallocating remaining end-of-year American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds and examining budgetary options that could make available as much as $1 billion.
One of my priorities in Hartford is to make our state more affordable, especially by addressing utility rates.
These high rates are due to factors like our location and lack of natural resources, infrastructure upgrades, Connecticut’s high business costs, legislative policies, and decisions by state utility regulators.
The Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) and utility companies are in open conflict over rates, programs, infrastructure, and policy proposals.
Credit rating agency Moody’s has branded our state’s regulatory environment “inconsistent and unpredictable”.
The Governor has largely stayed out of the fray and hasn’t fully staffed PURA, leaving two vacancies on the panel.
Our system is broken.
Connecticut needs a comprehensive and bipartisan energy strategy, led by the Governor, now more than ever.
I joined minority party Republicans in addressing this crisis earlier this year as we offered proposals to deliver short and long-term relief, including redefining Class I renewable energy sources to include all forms of hydropower and all nuclear power, and studying ways to get more natural gas into New England to reduce our reliance on more expensive fuel-mix used to generate electricity.
Other Republican proposals: Provide autonomy for PURA.
PURA is a division within the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP).
I fear that environmental (and ideological) goals of Governor Lamont’s DEEP, such banning the sales of new gas-powered vehicles, aren’t aligned with the interests of ratepayers.
PURA should be removed from DEEP to provide it the greatest autonomy possible.
Unbundling the System Benefits Charge.
Your bill includes charges that pay for hardship protection measures, low-income conservation programs as well as other public policies.
We should consider funding these programs as part of the state budget appropriations process rather than including them on ratepayer bills.
Set reasonable caps on future power purchase agreements (PPAs).
To stimulate growth of renewable energy projects, the state has required utility companies to purchase clean electricity (such as wind and solar) at rates that are sometimes substantially higher than others available on the market.
Republicans proposed to improve this situation by capping future PPAs so that no contract can be for more than significantly over the wholesale electric market price.
Unfortunately, our ideas have been shot down by our majority party colleagues.
Last year, our proposal to study how electric distribution companies could be reconstituted to bring more competition into the market was also rejected.
Decisions made by the state have a significant impact on your monthly bill, such as roughly four years of moratoriums on service shutoffs (citing COVID-19), which socialized the cost of unpaid bills to all ratepayers.
It’s why Republicans insisted upon a provision that requires your electric bill to indicate the amount of your monthly cost that is attributable to these “public benefits” decisions.
The effect on ratepayers was among the reasons why I opposed the electric vehicle mandate sought by DEEP and Democrats, and why I opposed legislation requiring installation of electric heat pumps in thousands of homes.
Both would have added significant load to our grid, require massive infrastructure upgrades, and ultimately impact the “public benefits” tally on bills and create homeowner system upgrade costs.
Thankfully, those concepts stalled–for now.
But if approved, a draft PURA decision could allow utilities companies to recover from ratepayers the costs from another initiative tied to the state’s ambitious climate goals: a rebate program for residential electric vehicle charging stations We can’t continue to lurch from one crisis to the next.
I want you to know that I’ll continue to push for strategies to stabilize, and ultimately lower, electric rates for homes and businesses.
For now, if you’re using a third-party supplier for your electricity, you should compare whether your per kilowatt hour rate is more than the current standard offer rate from your utility company. | |
