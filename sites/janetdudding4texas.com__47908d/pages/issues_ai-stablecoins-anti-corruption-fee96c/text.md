Fine.
But if the public is being asked to carry the costs, then the public has every right to demand the rules.
AI is not just a tech buildout.
It is also becoming a money system.
And Texans should pay close attention to who is trying to get in on the ground floor.
One example is World Liberty Financial, a Trump family-backed crypto venture that has drawn national attention as it pushes into stablecoins and digital finance.
That is exactly why Texas needs clear rules now — before politically connected insiders can use their access, influence, or name recognition to corner the payment rails of the future.
This isn't a theoretical risk: it's a local reality This financial architecture—where a few insiders control the reserves and the rails—is exactly why we must scrutinize the ties between our representatives and the banking industry.
When a candidate sits on the board of a bank while supporting crypto-banking legislation, they aren't just 'supporting innovation'—they are positioning themselves within the very system they are tasked with regulating.
Follow the Money The influence doesn't stop at the bank board.
We see a revolving door of interests: from the massive donations flowing from tech billionaires like Elon Musk into lawyer-led PACs, to the candidates those PACs then fund.
When the same interests pushing for industrial buildouts like Terrafab are the ones funding the campaigns of our representatives, the public interest is no longer the priority.
The priority is the pipeline.
Most people hear “AI” and think about chips, servers, and giant data centers.
But underneath all of that is something just as important: the payment layer.
These are the systems that measure usage, move money, hold reserves, and collect fees as digital tools begin paying for services automatically.
That means this is not just a story about technology.
It is a story about who owns the rails, who collects the profits, and whether Texans get stuck paying for infrastructure while powerful insiders build private tollbooths on top of it.
Texas should not wait for a crisis before putting guardrails in place.
Texans Should Not Pay So Insiders Can Profit Texas is already being asked to absorb the costs of large-scale digital infrastructure: pressure on the electric grid, water use, local land-use impacts, and generous tax breaks for major facilities.
If taxpayers are helping support the physical buildout of the AI economy, then Texans should not also be asked to accept a future where politically connected private interests control the money layer underneath it.
That is the danger with private stablecoins.
A stablecoin is basically a private digital dollar substitute.
Supporters say these tokens can move money faster and cheaper.
Maybe so.
But the real questions are simple: Who issues it?
Who holds the reserves?
Who earns the interest?
Who collects the fees?
Who profits when these systems become part of everyday life?
If the answer is a closed circle of insiders, financiers, and politically connected operators, then Texans have a problem.
Stablecoins Are Financial Infrastructure Stablecoins should not be treated like a software app, a branding exercise, or a loophole for well-connected speculators.
If a private company wants to issue dollar-like digital money and use it in real transactions, then it should be regulated like critical financial infrastructure.
That means clear rules, real oversight, and no special favors.
What I Support If stablecoins are going to become part of the "Texas miracle," I support a Texas stablecoin framework that requires: Full 1:1 reserve backing in cash and short-term U.S.
Treasuries Independent audits and regular public reporting Immediate redemption at par Licensing and supervision for issuers operating in Texas Disclosure of beneficial owners, affiliates, and politically exposed persons A ban on misleading bank-like branding Strong enforcement authority when issuers break the rules If a company wants the privilege of operating a private digital dollar system, it should meet the highest standards for safety, honesty, and transparency.
No Private Tollbooths in the AI Economy This is the bigger issue.
As AI expands, more systems will be able to meter usage, charge fees, buy services, and settle payments automatically.
If a handful of private actors control those rails, they can make money at every step: issuing the token holding the reserves collecting fees steering users into preferred platforms using market power to shut out competitors cashing in on political influence That is not innovation.
That is rent-seeking dressed up as progress.
Texas should not allow essential money infrastructure to become a private cash machine for the well-connected.
Anti-Corruption Rules Must Be Strong Any company or network that wants to control digital payment rails in Texas should face strict anti-corruption rules.
I support: Full beneficial-ownership disclosure No hidden ownership through shell companies or pass-through entities Mandatory disclosure of affiliates and influence networks Recusal and divestment where public officials or candidates have conflicts No special treatment for politically connected firms Serious penalties for concealment, self-dealing, and abuse of office The rules should not stop with the front company.
They should also reach the networks used to hide who benefits: donors PACs and super PACs dark-money groups consultants lobbyists family entities shell companies subcontractors and pass-throughs If a political network is being used to hide who profits, Texas law should pierce it, expose it, and punish it.
Real Penalties, Not Wrist Slaps If powerful insiders break the rules, the consequences should be real.
I support penalties including: Forfeiture of profits and fees Monetary penalties tied to illicit gain License denial or revocation Debarment from state contracts Clawbacks where political influence was used to win special treatment Strong criminal penalties for bribery, concealment, and abuse of office Whistleblower rewards for exposing hidden ownership and corrupt influence networks The price of corruption should be higher than the profit.
Bottom Line Texans should subsidize private empires, reward insider dealing, or hand over the financial rails of the future to politically connected operators.
If stablecoins are going to become part of the Texas economy, they must be: fully backed fully transparent immediately redeemable strictly supervised free from hidden conflicts of interest In the Texas House, I will fight for guardrails that protect the public, not the powerful.
Texas should lead with common sense, transparency, and accountability.
Not private tollbooths.
Not insider cash machines.
And not one more blank check for the well-connected.
AI is not just software.
It depends on massive data centers that use electricity, water, land, and public subsidies.
So if Texas is going to host the AI economy, Texas must also demand accountability for the infrastructure behind it.
