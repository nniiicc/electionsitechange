Data Centers: Solving the Water Paradox & Defending Local Control
Texas is facing two water crises at the same time: we have too much "produced water" (industrial waste) and not enough water to drink.
For too long, we have viewed these as separate problems.
But there is a common-sense solution.
Data center experts have already confirmed that treated produced water can be used for industrial cooling.
If a data center wants to build in our community, the deal should be simple: Stop draining our drinking water and start using the produced water we already have.
If the technology exists to save our aquifers and rivers, then using produced water shouldn't be an "option"—it should be a requirement for entry into our communities.
But the most important part of this equation is Local Control.
I support economic growth and innovation, but I do not support "top-down" mandates from Austin that strip power away from local voters.
We need a seat at the table.
Our counties, cities, and groundwater districts must have the final say on:
- Whether a project is allowed to come in.
- How it manages its water and energy footprint.
- Where the infrastructure is built.
Innovation is welcome, but not at the cost of our autonomy.
We can welcome the digital economy without surrendering our common sense or our water.

My Policy Agenda
1.
Protect Local Control
I will fight to ensure that counties, cities, groundwater districts, and local utilities keep the authority to make decisions about where data centers go and whether local infrastructure can handle them.
I support:
- Local authority over siting and expansion
- Public hearings before major approvals
- Strong local review of water, wastewater, noise, vibration, and power impacts, with enforceable mitigation requirements
- The right of communities to say no when a project threatens local resources
The people who live there should have more say than than donors and their lobbyists in Austin.
2.
Put Water Protection First
Texas should not allow massive facilities to drain high-quality water supplies when safer alternatives are available.
I will push for policies that:
- Prioritize recycled produced water, greywater, and other non-potable sources
- Require data center build-out to include its needed infrastructure for water and wastewater
- Require full public reporting of water sourcing and total usage
- Include data center demand in regional and state water and energy planning
- Protect aquifers, rivers, and local wells from overuse
Our drinking water should be for people first, not corporate convenience.
3.
Make Corporations Pay Their Own Way
If a billion-dollar company needs new substations, transmission upgrades, pipelines, roads, or water infrastructure, taxpayers should not be stuck with the bill.
I support:
- Corporations paying their own state and local taxes
- Full-cost infrastructure requirements for large industrial users
- Utility rate structures that prevent residential customers from subsidizing data centers
- Buffering, sound control, and vibration mitigation must be built in and paid for by the developer, not shifted onto neighbors or taxpayers
- Impact fees or similar mechanisms when local systems need expansion
- Independent review of long-term grid and water costs before approval
No special deals.
No hidden subsidies.
No passing the bill to working families.
4.
End Blank-Check Tax Giveaways
Tax incentives should be rare, transparent, and tied to real public benefit.
I will work to:
- Review existing data center tax exemptions and abatements
- End giveaways
- Require clawback provisions if companies fail to meet job, investment, or community commitments
- Publish all subsidy agreements in plain language for public review
If a project is profitable, it should not need a secret handout.
5.
Demand Transparency and Accountability
Communities deserve facts before deals are signed.
I support:
- Public disclosure of projected and actual water use
- Public disclosure of projected and actual energy demand
- Annual environmental and infrastructure impact reporting
- Strong enforcement for permit violations or false reporting
If a company wants public trust, it can start with public honesty.
What I’m Fighting For
I’m fighting for a Texas where:
- local communities stay in charge
- water is protected
- energy is affordable and dependable
- families are not forced to subsidize global corporations
- economic development actually serves the public
We can welcome innovation without surrendering common sense.
Bottom Line
Development should respect local control, use water responsibly, relying on alternatives like produced water and greywater, and pay the full cost of its impact.
Texas should work for its people first — not for corporate insiders looking for one more special deal.
