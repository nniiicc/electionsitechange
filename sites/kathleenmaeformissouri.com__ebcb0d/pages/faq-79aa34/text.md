Which way do you lean politically, and what does being Independent mean to you?
“I believe I have a responsibility to provide clarity without asking people to rely on a party label,” and that each issue deserves individual evaluation.
My loyalty lies with the Constitution and with my constituents — not a political party.
Is a vote for an Independent a wasted vote?
“Any vote for the person you genuinely think will best stand for our community” is not wasted.
I promise to study the issues, ask difficult questions, explain my decisions honestly, and put the district first.
Can an Independent actually get anything done in Congress?
Yes.
Effectiveness in Congress is not determined uniquely by party affiliation — it depends on preparation, credibility, relationships, and a willingness to work with others in good faith.

How will you decide how to vote on legislation?
I treat policymaking as a systems problem: gathering evidence from multiple sources, understanding impacts on different groups, identifying risks, and weighing constitutional protections before deciding what best serves the district.
What if you don’t know enough about an issue to make an informed decision?
Good leadership begins by recognizing the limits of our own knowledge — and learning from multiple perspectives rather than relying on ideology.
What role should the federal government play?
The federal government should protect individual rights, ensure equal treatment, maintain security, and support essential systems — while exercising restraint to preserve local decision-making and personal responsibility. ↑ Back to top 03 Working With Both Parties Representing people who disagree with her, and working across the aisle in Congress.
How will you represent people who disagree with you?
I will represent the entire district, engage respectfully with differing views, and explain my reasoning transparently while remaining accountable to all constituents.
How will you work with Republicans and Democrats?
“I am not elected to choose a side before the conversation begins.

What can a U.S.
Representative actually do?
Representatives write and amend legislation, serve on committees, conduct federal agency oversight, advocate for district priorities, and help constituents navigate federal systems.
How will you stay accountable to voters?
By explaining my votes clearly, maintaining constituent communication channels, hosting town halls and community meetings, and remembering that the office belongs to the people.
How will you define success if elected?
