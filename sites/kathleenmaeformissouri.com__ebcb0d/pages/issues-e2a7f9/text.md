General Election · November 3, 2026
Missouri's 4th Congressional District
“A strong economy begins with strong communities.
Working Americans should be able to afford housing, food, healthcare, and retirement.”
Strong economies require supporting local enterprises and family farms facing rising costs and market concentration challenges.
Government should remove barriers to growth and enable fair competition.
- Support local businesses and family farms by cutting unnecessary barriers and encouraging fair competition
- Invest in infrastructure — roads, bridges, broadband, and utilities
- Strengthen workforce development through school, technical program, and employer partnerships
- Encourage competitive, innovative, community-supportive companies
Economic growth should be measured by improvements in working people’s lives and by community strength across Missouri.
“Education is one of the best investments we can make in the future for Missouri.”
Education enables stable, independent lives and community contribution.
Every child deserves quality education choices; every Missourian should have the chance to develop skills for a fuller life.
The education system must connect to workforce realities.
- Expand apprenticeships, trade education, and workforce training through employer and union partnerships
- Strengthen public schools while keeping career, technical, and higher education accessible
- Improve coordination between education and local workforce needs
- Encourage lifelong learning for skill development and career changes
Every form of honest work has dignity.
Strong education systems prepare adaptable Missourians to contribute and strengthen their communities.
“Good health gives people the freedom to work, care for their families, remain independent, and contribute to their communities.”
Healthcare access varies by distance, provider shortages, and hospital limitations — especially in rural areas.
Veterans face unique challenges accessing timely physical and mental healthcare.
- Strengthen rural healthcare — hospitals, clinics, emergency services, and telehealth
- Expand the healthcare workforce through training pipelines
- Improve veteran healthcare access and coordination between the VA and community providers
- Increase mental healthcare access and crisis response coordination
- Support preventive care and early intervention
Healthcare success means treating illness, enabling recovery and independence, and supporting family and community participation.
“Missouri’s rural communities are the backbone of our state and an essential part of our nation.”
Rural areas grow our food, steward natural resources, power local economies, and preserve tradition.
Geographic challenges — distance to healthcare, fewer opportunities, aging infrastructure, farm pressures — shouldn’t stand in the way of a stable future.
- Support family farms through agricultural research and university partnerships
- Invest in rural infrastructure — roads, bridges, broadband, water systems, utilities
- Strengthen healthcare, education, and workforce development access in rural areas
- Encourage competitive markets for farm and rural business innovation
- Support responsible stewardship of agricultural land and resources
Missouri’s agricultural heritage and its future are not competing ideas.
“A grateful nation has a responsibility to care for the people who dedicate themselves to protecting others.”
Many veterans and first responders face barriers to timely healthcare, mental health services, and essential support — complicated by long distances, provider shortages, and staffing challenges, especially in rural areas.
- Improve rural healthcare and mental health access for veterans
- Strengthen and expand veteran benefits oversight, reducing delays
- Support transition programs connecting veterans with education and employment
- Strengthen first responder workforce development and mental health resources
Congress must honor service by ensuring stable, fulfilling lives, access to care, and room to recover.
“Strong communities are built through jobs and infrastructure, but they are sustained through shared culture, creativity, and public life.”
Our cultural heritage reflects our history and our people — historic downtowns, family farms, working artists, and living traditions.
Public investment in cultural spaces supports education, tourism, and local economic activity.
- Support libraries, museums, parks, and historic preservation
- Encourage arts, recreation, and cultural programs that enrich community life
- Invest in public gathering spaces for families and neighbors
- Support partnerships that preserve tradition while enabling new cultural expression
Economic development builds material strength; culture gives communities their identity.
“Missouri’s rivers, lakes, forests, parks, wildlife habitats, and public lands are the heart of our state.”
Our natural resources provide clean water and air, support agriculture and local economies, and sustain hunting and fishing traditions.
Conservation means balancing environmental protection with economic activity.
- Support responsible stewardship of land, water, forests, and wildlife
- Encourage partnerships among farmers, landowners, researchers, and communities
- Invest in clean water systems, healthy forests, and recreation infrastructure
- Promote science-based innovation and practical conservation practices
Healthy wildlife populations depend on healthy habitats — future generations deserve continued access to Missouri’s natural heritage.
“Every American possesses constitutional rights that government has a responsibility to protect, not grant.”
Government must safeguard constitutional freedoms, apply the law fairly, and remain accountable.
A free society requires trust in equal application of the law and due process.
- Support legislation that respects constitutional limits and protects individual liberties
- Defend equal protection and due process for every American
- Strengthen federal agency oversight to ensure accountability
- Restore checks and balances that limit government power
The Constitution frames the relationship between people’s freedoms and limited government power; Congress must uphold that framework faithfully.
“Public office is a public trust — elected officials have a responsibility to listen to their constituents, explain their votes, and be accountable.”
Americans feel disconnected from their representatives when national politics overshadows local concerns.
Representation requires ongoing communication, transparency, and accountability — not just at election time.
- Hold regular town halls across the district, including rural communities
- Clearly communicate votes, priorities, and legislative decisions
- Maintain accessible constituent services
- Support transparency and strong congressional oversight
- Uphold equal application of the law
Effective representation begins with listening.
The office belongs to the people it serves.
“The United States has a role in promoting peace, protecting U.S. national security and allies, and preventing unnecessary harm whenever possible.”
Strong defense and principled diplomacy are complementary.
Military strength should deter conflict and defend our interests — decisions about military force carry profound human and economic consequences.
- Maintain a strong, well-trained, well-equipped military
- Support diplomacy and partnerships that reduce the likelihood of conflict
- Require clear objectives and congressional oversight before military force is used
- Honor commitments to service members and military families
- Evaluate the true economic and human costs of military action
Lasting security requires strength, discipline, accountability — and asking hard questions before risking lives.
