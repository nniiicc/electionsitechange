Join the crew → Send this page: Text it Share on Facebook Post on X macallister4congress.com/how-to-vote 1 Who can vote You may register and vote in Massachusetts if you are: A United States citizen ; 18 years of age or older on Election Day; A resident of the city or town where you register, at the time you register; Not under guardianship, not incarcerated in a correctional facility due to a felony conviction, and not disqualified by law because of corrupt practices in respect to elections.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
51, § 1 If you have moved within Massachusetts in the last six months and have not registered at your new address, you may still vote for national and state officers from your former city or town.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
51, § 1 Age 16 or 17?
You can pre-register now.
You will be added to the voter list automatically when you turn 18.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
51, § 47A 2 Register by Saturday, October 24 Registration for the November 3 election closes at 5:00 p.m. on Saturday, October 24, 2026 — the tenth day before the election.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
51, § 26 Three ways to register Online at the Secretary of the Commonwealth’s portal, if you have a Massachusetts driver’s license or ID — the portal uses the signature on file with the Registry of Motor Vehicles.
Register online → By mail — a mail-in registration form, signed and sent to your city or town clerk, postmarked by the deadline.
In person at your city or town clerk’s office or election commission, by 5:00 p.m. on October 24.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
51, § 33A Automatic registration.
If you have done a license or ID transaction at the RMV, or applied through MassHealth or the Health Connector, you may already be registered — check your status above rather than assume.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
51, § 42G½ 3 Three ways to cast your ballot A · Vote early in person — Saturday, Oct 17 through Friday, Oct 30 Every city and town offers in-person early voting for the state election from the seventeenth day through the fourth day before the election.
Hours and locations are set by your city or town — each municipality must offer weekday hours and at least one weekend of hours.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
54, § 25B Early voting hours and locations by town → B · Vote by mail — any registered voter, no excuse required Any registered voter may vote by mail.
Your application must be received by your city or town clerk before 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, October 27, 2026 (the fifth business day before the election).
Applications are accepted if signed on paper or submitted electronically with a signature written in substantially the same manner as your handwritten signature.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
54, § 25B Returning your mail ballot.
You or a family member may return it by: Delivering it in person to your city or town clerk’s office; Delivering it to an early voting location during early voting hours; Dropping it in your municipality’s secured drop box; or Mailing it.
Deadline.
Your ballot must reach the clerk before the polls close at 8:00 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3 .
A ballot mailed and postmarked on or before November 3 is accepted until 5:00 p.m. on Friday, November 6 .
Don’t cut it close — use a drop box.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
54, § 25B Vote-by-mail application → C · Vote on Election Day — Tuesday, November 3 Polls must open no later than 7:00 a.m. and, at a state election, close at 8:00 p.m.
If you are in line at 8:00 p.m., stay in line — you vote.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
54, § 64 Find your polling place → 4 What to bring — identification Massachusetts does not require most voters to show ID.
An election officer may ask for identification; a voter who cannot present it is still permitted to vote, though the vote may be challenged.
First-time voters: if you registered by mail after January 1, 2003 and have never voted in a federal election in Massachusetts , you must show one of the following when voting for federal office: A current and valid photo identification ; or A current utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck, or other government document showing your name and address.
Without it, you may cast a provisional ballot.
As stated by M.G.L. c.
54, § 76B Bring a photo ID or a piece of mail anyway.
It settles any question at the check-in table in seconds.
