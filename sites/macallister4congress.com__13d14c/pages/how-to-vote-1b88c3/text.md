You may register and vote in Massachusetts if you are:
- A United States citizen;
- 18 years of age or older on Election Day;
- A resident of the city or town where you register, at the time you register;
- Not under guardianship, not incarcerated in a correctional facility due to a felony conviction, and not disqualified by law because of corrupt practices in respect to elections.
As stated by M.G.L. c. 51, § 1
If you have moved within Massachusetts in the last six months and have not registered at your new address, you may still vote for national and state officers from your former city or town.

As stated by M.G.L. c. 51, § 33A
Automatic registration.
If you have done a license or ID transaction at the RMV, or applied through MassHealth or the Health Connector, you may already be registered — check your status above rather than assume.
As stated by M.G.L. c. 51, § 42G½
Every city and town offers in-person early voting for the state election from the seventeenth day through the fourth day before the election.
Hours and locations are set by your city or town — each municipality must offer weekday hours and at least one weekend of hours.

Any registered voter may vote by mail.
Your application must be received by your city or town clerk before 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, October 27, 2026 (the fifth business day before the election).
Applications are accepted if signed on paper or submitted electronically with a signature written in substantially the same manner as your handwritten signature.
Returning your mail ballot.
You or a family member may return it by:
- Delivering it in person to your city or town clerk’s office;
- Delivering it to an early voting location during early voting hours;
- Dropping it in your municipality’s secured drop box; or
- Mailing it.
Deadline.
Your ballot must reach the clerk before the polls close at 8:00 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3.
A ballot mailed and postmarked on or before November 3 is accepted until 5:00 p.m. on Friday, November 6.
Don’t cut it close — use a drop box.
Polls must open no later than 7:00 a.m. and, at a state election, close at 8:00 p.m.
If you are in line at 8:00 p.m., stay in line — you vote.
As stated by M.G.L. c. 54, § 64
Massachusetts does not require most voters to show ID.
An election officer may ask for identification; a voter who cannot present it is still permitted to vote, though the vote may be challenged.
First-time voters: if you registered by mail after January 1, 2003 and have never voted in a federal election in Massachusetts, you must show one of the following when voting for federal office:
- A current and valid photo identification; or
- A current utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck, or other government document showing your name and address.
Without it, you may cast a provisional ballot.
