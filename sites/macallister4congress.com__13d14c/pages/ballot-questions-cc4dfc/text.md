Massachusetts is the only state where both are exempt.
A YES vote opens those records, with exceptions for genuinely sensitive material.
Tyler votes YES You can pull the harbormaster’s records.
You can pull your town’s.
You cannot pull the House’s.
The people writing the rules should be able to show their work — if they can’t, it isn’t a rule, it’s a favor.
Open the books.

A YES vote allows it; a NO vote keeps things as they are.
No position — your call This is about how the state runs one of its own agencies.
I’m not going to pretend a fisherman has a stake in it either way.
Read it and decide it yourself.
Read the full text → 3 Question 3 State Primary Elections NO What it does Ends party primaries for state offices.
Every candidate goes on one September ballot and the top two — regardless of party — advance to November.
A YES vote makes the switch; a NO vote keeps the primaries we have.
Tyler votes NO Look at the map.
Top-two in this state means two Democrats on the November ballot across most of it and no real choice at all.
A primary is where a party picks its best fighter and then the voters get a contrast in the fall.
Take that away and November becomes a formality.
Keep the primaries.
Read the full text → 4 Question 4 Election Day Registration NO What it does Allows a person to register and vote on Election Day itself.
Today registration closes ten days before.
A YES vote adds same-day registration; a NO vote keeps the ten-day deadline.
Tyler votes NO Our clerks run a tight operation in 49 towns on razor-thin budgets.
Same-day registration asks them to verify eligibility while the line is out the door at 7:45 p.m.
That is how honest mistakes happen and how confidence gets lost.
Get the list right first.
Registration is one minute online and the deadline is October 24 — here’s how .
Read the full text → 5 Question 5 State Revenue Limit & Rebate YES What it does Tightens the cap on how much tax revenue the state may collect in a year and returns anything over the cap to the taxpayers who paid it.
A YES vote sets the new limit and the rebate; a NO vote leaves the current formula in place.
Tyler votes YES When Beacon Hill takes in more than it needs, that money belongs back on the dock, in the shop and at the kitchen table — not in a bigger agency.
This puts a ceiling on the take and sends the overage home.
Yes.
Read the full text → 6 Question 6 Natural Resource Conservation Fund NO What it does Creates a new state fund for land conservation, water quality and recreation, paid for by earmarking the sales tax collected on sporting goods, recreational vehicles and golf.
A YES vote creates the fund and the earmark.
Tyler votes NO Nobody is more for clean water and open land than a man who has fished it for forty-plus years.
But locking a slice of the sales tax into one fund forever ties the Legislature’s hands the next time a bridge, a harbor dredge or a school needs it — and lawmakers can still decline to fund it even if it passes.
Fund conservation in the budget, in the open, every year.
Read the full text → 7 Question 7 Single-Family Homes on Small Lots NO What it does Requires every city and town to allow a single-family home on a lot of 5,000 square feet or more where there is adequate infrastructure — a statewide floor that overrides local minimum-lot rules.
A YES vote imposes it; a NO vote leaves lot sizes to the town.
Tyler votes NO Truro is not Taunton.
What a town can carry — septic, water, roads, schools — is a question for the people who live there and the boards they elect, not a one-size rule from Boston.
We need starter homes badly; we get them by working with towns, not over them.
Keep zoning local.
Read the full text → 8 Question 8 Prohibit Retail Sale of Adult Recreational-Use Marijuana YOUR CALL What it does Repeals the 2016 law that allows licensed retail sale of recreational marijuana.
Possession of up to one ounce would remain legal.
A YES vote ends retail sales; a NO vote keeps them.
No position — your call Voters decided this ten years ago.
This asks whether you would decide it the same way today, knowing what you know now.
That’s a personal call and I’ll respect yours.
Read it and decide it.
Read the full text → 9 Question 9 Firearms Regulation — Chapter 135 of 2024 NO What it does A referendum on an existing law: Chapter 135 of the Acts of 2024, the sweeping firearms law the Legislature passed.
Read this one carefully — a YES vote KEEPS the law; a NO vote REPEALS it.
Tyler votes NO Massachusetts already had the strictest gun laws in the country.
Chapter 135 rewrote the rules for lawful owners anyway, and set the precedent that Beacon Hill can redraw a constitutional right whenever it likes.
I’m voting to repeal it — and on this question, repeal is a NO .
Read the full text → Then go vote.
