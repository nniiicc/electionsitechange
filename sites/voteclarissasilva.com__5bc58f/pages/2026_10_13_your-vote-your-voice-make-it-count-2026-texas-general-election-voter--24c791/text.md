Make It Count! | 2026 Texas General Election Voter Guide October 13, 2026 Every election is an opportunity to participate in shaping the future of our communities.
Voting is one of the most important responsibilities we share as Texans.
From statewide offices to local and judicial races, every election gives voters an opportunity to make their voices heard and participate in the democratic process.
Judicial elections are especially important because the decisions made in our courts can have lasting consequences.
Appellate courts review legal decisions, interpret the law, and help ensure that the judicial process is guided by fairness, consistency, and respect for the rule of law.
As Justice Clarissa Silva’s campaign continues across the 20 counties served by the Thirteenth Court of Appeals, we encourage every eligible voter to make a plan, stay informed, and participate in the November 2026 General Election.
Mark Your Calendar: Important Election Dates Early Voting: October 19–30, 2026 Election Day: Tuesday, November 3, 2026 Whether you prefer to vote early or on Election Day, planning ahead can help make the process easier.
Check your registration, review your ballot, and find the voting information you need before heading to the polls.
Your Texas Voter Toolkit The Texas Secretary of State provides official resources to help voters prepare for Election Day.
Visit VoteTexas.gov⁠ for information about: * Checking your voter registration status * Finding early voting and Election Day polling locations * Learning about acceptable voter identification * Understanding voting by mail and eligibility requirements * Accessing election information and helpful voter resources You can also visit My Voter Portal⁠ to review your registration information and available election details.
Remind Your Friends and Family to Vote Voting is important, and sharing accurate election information can help others prepare to participate.
Take a moment to remind your friends, family, coworkers, and neighbors about the upcoming election.
A simple text message, social media post, or conversation can help someone remember the dates and make a voting plan.
