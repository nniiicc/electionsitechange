Why Judicial Experience Matters
When voters consider a judicial race, one of the most important questions is simple: does the candidate have the experience needed to make careful, fair, and legally sound decisions?
For appellate courts, experience matters.
The Texas Thirteenth Court of Appeals reviews decisions from lower courts in both civil and criminal cases.
These cases can involve complex legal questions, detailed records, and issues that affect families, businesses, victims, defendants, and communities across South Texas.
That responsibility requires more than a legal title.
It requires discipline, preparation, sound judgment, and a deep respect for the rule of law.
Appellate justices must carefully review written arguments, trial court records, legal precedent, and the application of the law before reaching a decision.
Every opinion must be grounded in law and reason.
Justice Clarissa Silva brings more than eighteen years of legal and judicial experience to that work.
Before serving on the bench, she built a career in public service, handling juvenile matters and cases involving abuse, neglect, and exploitation of vulnerable Texans.
She later represented the Texas Department of Family and Protective Services, continuing her work on behalf of children and families.
In 2020, voters elected Justice Silva to the Thirteenth Court of Appeals.
Since then, she has reviewed civil and criminal appeals from twenty South Texas counties and authored more than 550 appellate opinions.
That record reflects the preparation, consistency, and careful legal analysis required of an appellate judge.
Judicial experience matters because appellate decisions carry lasting consequences.
Voters deserve judges who understand the law, respect the Constitution, and approach every case with fairness and impartiality.
Justice Silva’s record reflects a commitment to those principles and a continued dedication to serving South Texas with integrity.
