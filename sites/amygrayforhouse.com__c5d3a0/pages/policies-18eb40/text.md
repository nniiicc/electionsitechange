As a small business owner, I know what it means to make payroll, pay the rent and balance rising costs while taking care of your employees.
For many of us, our businesses represent years of hard work, late nights, weekends, and personal sacrifice and investment in our communities.
In Lansing, I’ll work to help small businesses afford employee health coverage, expand access to affordable loans and connect more local employers with funding for job training and apprenticeships.

Economic Growth & Small Business Support Making Housing More Affordable—and Helping You Stay in Your Home I am a homeowner, and my husband proudly serves as president of our subdivision’s HOA.
Across District 60, you can see the pride hardworking families take in their neighborhoods—in the well-kept lawns, carefully tended gardens and homes they’ve made their own.
These families deserve a home they can afford, and seniors deserve to retire without worrying about losing theirs.
I’ll work to expand Michigan’s Homestead Property Tax Credit by raising the income eligibility limit and increasing the maximum credit available to eligible households.
I’ll also fight for additional protection for seniors, including an enhanced refundable state tax credit when property taxes exceed 10% of their income.
No senior should have to leave a home they’ve lived in for 30 years because their property taxes have outpaced their retirement income.
And I’ll support expanding grants and low-interest loans for essential home repairs, so a failing furnace or leaking roof doesn’t force a longtime resident out of their home.
We can make housing more affordable while protecting our neighborhoods and keeping local communities in charge of zoning and development.

As a parent of two Chippewa Valley Public Schools graduates, I’ve seen how important strong public schools are to our children and our community.
Every student deserves a path to success—whether that leads to college, career and technical education, skilled trades or an apprenticeship.
In my conversations across District 60, I hear from parents and educators about the need for smaller class sizes, access to counselors and more individual support for students.
These are priorities I’ll bring to Lansing.

Our teachers, support staff, bus drivers, paraprofessionals and custodians deserve fair pay, safe workplaces and the resources to do their jobs well.
I’m proud to be endorsed by the Michigan Education Association (MEA), UFCW Local 951 and IBEW Local 58.
I’ll stand up for our educators and school employees while supporting opportunities for students to learn a trade, pursue an apprenticeship and build a rewarding career.
I’ll meet regularly with parents, educators and local school boards to hear their concerns and work together on solutions.
Every student deserves a safe, welcoming school—and a real opportunity to thrive.

Healthcare Access & Affordability When I knock on doors across District 60, health care costs come up again and again.
I’ve spoken with neighbors working two or three jobs whose employers can’t afford to offer health insurance—and who can’t afford Marketplace coverage themselves.
Others tell me they’re putting off routine health screenings because they’re worried about out-of-pocket costs.
College graduates and young adults are delaying dental care because even getting a cavity filled feels out of reach.
It shouldn’t be this way.
We work hard in Macomb County, and we should be able to afford basic health coverage and get care when we need it.
No one should have to wait for a manageable health concern to become an emergency because they can’t afford treatment.
As a small business owner, I understand that health care costs strain both employers and employees.
In Lansing, I’ll bring business owners, workers, health care providers and insurance companies to the same table to work on practical solutions that lower costs and improve access—including preventive care, dental care and mental health services.
I’ll bring the stories I hear at your doors into those conversations, because affordable coverage needs to mean more than having an insurance card.
It needs to mean being able to afford to use it.
