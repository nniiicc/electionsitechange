Jamie and Health At one point in her life, Jamie’s greatest accomplishment was being “one of the nicest patients” to her nurses.
In her early 20s, Jamie dealt with chronic hospitalization due to a severe, incurable ailment.
She would sometimes not be able to drink water or eat food for up to 5 days, relying instead on intravenous feeding/hydration and wracking up almost a hundred thousand dollars in medical debt in the course of a year, cumulatively.
She underwent multiple procedures and experimental trials, and had to take 18 pills per day, but still, her incredibly painful and debilitating condition only worsened over time.
With a tip from a friend, Jamie began researching the medical literature on her condition and only found one Japanese pescatarian study linking the condition to food.
Jamie then switched to a vegan diet, and has remained in remission for 9+ years.
Jamie understands what it is like to be too sick to work, too sick to care for yourself, and to need help, but can’t afford it.
Jamie also has a personal relationship in knowing what it means to care for a disabled loved one.
During her summers in high school, Jamie cared for a cognitively disabled child: changing his diapers, feeding him, and keeping him safe.
When her older brother was medically induced into a coma , Jamie stayed by his side in the hospital for days, reading to him and sometimes sleeping on the floor of the waiting room, while raising money from friends and family to pay his medical bills.
For Jamie, having quality healthcare and at-home support should be something that our nation collectively provides to those in need.
Since resolving her own condition and now needing no medications at all, Jamie pours back her energy into community.
When she bounced back from her hospitalization stays, she threw public events, poetry readings, hug marches , and volunteered for charity before moving into the charity sector to work full-time.
She also thanks every doctor, nurse, and medical researcher she meets for doing some of the most important work in the world.
