Cannae Partners bought the EmeryTech campus for $55.5M, under half what Blackstone paid in 2019.
Sutter Health's purchase of the Emery Yards site produced an $11.2M one-time transfer tax but permanently removed those parcels from the property tax rolls.
On Bay Street, leasing rose from roughly 30% to over 90% .
Worth watching: later vacancy reports and the Christie Avenue site now on the market.
A commercial parking fee idea A commercial parking fee idea.
In public comment on 2 June 2026 , former Planning Commissioner Stephen Keller proposed charging a fee for each commercial parking space instead of requiring a minimum number of spaces.
He said it “could generate some $15 million directly into our city coffers”.
That figure is one commenter's projection of roughly $15 million a year, not a staff finding.
The idea has not been placed on a council agenda or studied by staff, and no business response has been reported.
Worth watching: a staff memo on cost and on whether the fee would need voter approval.
Data centers and the zoning code Data centers and the zoning code.
At the Planning Commission's 29 August 2026 meeting, Commissioner Chafe asked how data centers would be treated , meaning large buildings that house computer servers.
Staff said a new one could fall in the Office/Technology or Mixed Use Residential zones and would likely need a major use permit, and noted that at least two data centers already operate in Emeryville.
The discussion was brief and produced no policy direction, and no application has been reported.
Worth watching: the topic returning to a commission agenda, or staff setting standards for noise, water and electricity.

Meeting format and recusals Meeting format and recusals.
On 2 December 2025, Vice Mayor Kaur proposed cutting the council to one meeting a month , down from two.
After objections from councilmembers and staff, who noted residents would get fewer chances to comment, the idea was dropped.
In May 2026 the council voted unanimously not to let advisory bodies meet fully remotely under SB 707 , a state law on remote public meetings, and voted 5-0 to keep its agenda order.
Mayor Kaur paused the Powell Street vote pending a state ethics advice letter.
Worth watching: that vote returning.
BART and AC Transit budget shortfalls BART and AC Transit shortfalls.
On 7 April 2026, BART District 7 director Victor Flores briefed the council that the train system faces a shortfall above $370 million from fiscal year 2027.
A regional sales tax measure is planned for November 2026, and if it fails, BART describes a possible 63% service cut.
Emeryville has no station but relies on MacArthur, Ashby and West Oakland.
AC Transit projects a $200 million four-year deficit, with cuts possible from 2027.
The city's fare-free Emery Go-Round shuttle is out to bid again.
Worth watching: the November vote.
Child Development Center funding and the school board race The Child Development Center and the school board race.
On 2 June 2026, the council authorized a $200,000 transfer of Measure F sales tax funds to the city-run Emeryville Child Development Center, where lower enrollment has reduced fees from families.
The same meeting recorded a $148,000 increase in Measure F collections.
In August 2026, the Emery Unified school board filing deadline was extended to 12 August because the seat holders had not filed, and five candidates qualified for three seats in the November election.
Worth watching: the vote and whether enrollment recovers.
Investment screens and agenda requests Investment screens and agenda requests.
On 2 December 2025, Councilmember Priforce pulled the annual investment policy off the consent calendar for separate discussion, and the council reaffirmed it unanimously.
Finance Director Friedrichsen said the city follows a Socially Responsible Investing framework screening fossil fuels, weaponry, labor practices and environmental compliance.
Portfolio manager Karl Meng said the policy is more restrictive than most cities'.
No list of what the screens exclude has been reported.
On 7 April 2026 , Priforce asked for future agenda items on sanctuary policy and a foreign conflict, with no outcome reported.
Worth watching: the next annual review.
