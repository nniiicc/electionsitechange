In May 2026, after 156 speakers and more than 360 written comments, the council rejected a larger master contract 8 to 1 while keeping the existing readers for a year, and whether the data can reach federal immigration authorities is still unsettled.
Residents write about Ward Street, where neighbors describe more than two decades without repaving, and about crossings on Claremont Avenue, Rose Street and California Street where people have died.
The city fills potholes in a median of four days and has voted to rebuild Hopkins Street.
To help close a deficit of about $30 million, the budget ended the Mobile Crisis Team, which answered mental health emergencies without police, and the winter shelter.
Illegal dumping is the most common report to 311, the city's line for reporting problems, with 6,096 reports in twelve months, and crews clear it in a median of one day.

Residents have raised this since at least October 2024 .
By a rough count, about 15 letters and petitions from at least nine residents reached council packets by August 2026.
The city's 311 line is how residents report problems.
In the city's 311 data , 45 requests in the "Engineering: Paving Issue" category were filed between October 2025 and September 2026, and 43 of them (96%) are still open, with a median age of 220 days.
Ordinary pothole reports, 649 in the data, close in a median of 4 days.
The status open is the city's own, and it does not say whether anyone has visited.
The city is putting real money into streets.
Voters passed the Measure FF parcel tax, a tax on each property, in November 2024, projected at about $15 million a year for paving and safety.
The June 2026 capital plan puts about $58 million into streets in fiscal year 2027-28 to improve 25 centerline miles, meaning 25 miles of street measured along their middle.
In July 2026 the council approved roughly 60 to 65 miles of repaving over five years.
A councilmember said that doubles the previous five years, the Daily Californian reported .
For the blocks residents name, the answers are short.
On 7 July 2026 the Interim Director of Public Works told Stuart Street residents the block "did not rank high enough" for the proposed plan for fiscal years 2027 through 2031, and that it remains on a list for future consideration.
On 7 August 2026 a council chief of staff confirmed that Francisco Street east of Sacramento Street "will get repaved much later".
Nothing we have tracked shows Ward Street or Cedar Street added to the schedule.
Where things stand: the citywide paving plan has grown, but for the blocks neighbors have written about, this has not been addressed.
Worth tracking What would show movement: Ward Street and Cedar Street appearing on the repaving schedule, Stuart Street and Francisco Street getting a named year, and fewer than 96% of paving requests still open in 311.
Repaving is a question of capital, and the city's own figure for unfunded Public Works needs is about $1.8 billion.
A direct appropriation request, which is how a member of Congress asks Congress to fund a named local project (called Community Project Funding), could cover part of the cost of rebuilding one or more of these streets, if the street meets that account's eligibility rules.
Costs for these four streets are not public yet.
Federal road money also reaches Berkeley through the Metropolitan Transportation Commission, the regional transportation agency, and whether a given residential street qualifies is worth checking street by street.
Because rebuilding Stuart Street triggers stormwater requirements, federal clean-water money that the state passes along through its Clean Water State Revolving Fund is a second route.
Which streets come first is Berkeley's call, made with the people who live on them.
The office would work with the city rather than arrive with a list.

Berkeley does not control either utility.

Worth tracking What would show movement is a published standard for how well utilities must patch a street, inspection results from the sites the city is visiting, a change in how sweepers handle gravel, and repaving on the blocks the residents named.
This is mostly not a money question.
