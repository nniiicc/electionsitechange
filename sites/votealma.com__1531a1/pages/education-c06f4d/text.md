Special Education Funding Special education is not optional.
It is a legal, moral, and educational responsibility.
Too often, local districts are left struggling to meet growing special education needs without adequate support from the state.
This places pressure on school budgets, taxpayers, families, teachers, and students.
Alma believes Connecticut must do more to responsibly fund special education so that every child receives the services they need without forcing towns to shoulder the burden alone.
Alma will advocate for: Increased state support for special education.
More predictable and responsible special education funding.
Relief for local taxpayers and school districts.
Services that help students succeed.
Respect for families navigating the special education process.
Better planning so towns are not left with unexpected costs.
Alma’s view: Every child deserves the opportunity to learn, grow, and thrive.
Alma's October 3, 2022 Comments to the Westport Board of Education On October 3, 2022, Alma Sarelli spoke before the Westport Board of Education about the placement of sexually explicit material in the "Banned Books" display at Staples High School.
Below is a video of her full remarks, along with her statement on the issue.
Alma has always supported the First Amendment and believes strongly in the freedom to read.
Her concern was never about a book's viewpoint or the removal of any title from the library.
It was about whether it is appropriate for sexually explicit material, including content describing how to find sexual partners online, to be prominently displayed and marketed to students as young as 12 and 13 years old.
As Alma said at the meeting: "It is one thing to have books available; it is another to market them so aggressively." She asked the Board to consider that a public school, funded by every taxpayer whether or not they have children enrolled, has an obligation to weigh parents' concerns about what is placed in front of young children, separate from any question of what belongs on a library shelf.
Alma believes parents and school boards can find common-sense, common-ground solutions on questions like this one, the same "Meet Me in the Middle" approach she will bring to Hartford as State Senator.
