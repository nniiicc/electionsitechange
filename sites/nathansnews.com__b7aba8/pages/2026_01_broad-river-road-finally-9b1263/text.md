While we all prepare for the winter storm heading our way this evening, I wanted to get this news out to you!
For YEARS, we have waited on Richland County to deliver on their local Richland County Penny Tax projects for our area.
Initially, I was pleased to see them deliver on the much needed safety improvement years ago at a dangerous intersection on on Kennerly Road (post from 2008 on Nathan’s News).
Since that time, outside of the improvements to “straighten” Kennerly and Kinley Road so they connected at the same traffic signal (this was more than a decade ago), we have seen NO TANGIBLE PROGRESS on the widening of Broad River Road.
You may recall in my role as a state official, I requested DOT to take over the project from the county.
However, in a unprecedented move, the county denied turning the project over to the state.
It’s still my belief today (and others) we would have been much further along had the county not denied that request.
Finally, last August, while at a meeting with DOT and local municipalities to hear of the “future of Dutch Fork Road/76,” I had had enough.
While the improvements they believe should happen in the years ahead are great, where is the project that was supposed to happen a decade ago?
I am usually a mild-mannered individual; but people in that meeting were shocked when I had choice words and a demand to SEE DIRT MOVING on the project!
Months later, that is about to finally happen.
(I was given a date of February 14th, 2026 and it appears the county and contractors may be delivering on that).
Since August, I’ve had utility companies and others call me with updates and it appears the ball is now moving.
Don’t get too excited though, it will take 1,095 days to complete!
But I’m relieved we will see dirt moving, poles eventually moving, and work happening up and down the road from Royal Tower Road (ie, Jim Hudson Toyota) through the US176 intersection (ie, Chickfila) The work is prohibited to have lane closures Monday through Sunday from 7am to 6pm (so that’s good!); but it will be interesting and continue to be crowded with all the local growth and developments the county and town of Irmo continues to approve out here in our neck of the woods.
Please know the work will be “multi-staged construction” (working mutiple locations throughout the project – not going from one end to the other in sequence) and will include the construction of a 5-lane roadway with 4 twelve foot travel lanes separated by a 15 foot median.
Please watch the video to hear of other improvements and infrastructure projects I mentioned last summer coming for parts of Broad River.
