Jim Risch provided support.
One of the most recent examples took place when the university’s Army Reserve Officer Training Corps, or “ROTC” program, was threatened.
A proposed Army restructuring put the University of Idaho’s status as a host institution in jeopardy.
Sen.
Risch didn’t simply voice his support for keeping the ROTC program at the U of I.
He took the matter all the way to the White House, personally bringing it to the president’s attention.
The president intervened, and a program that has been developing Army officers for generations was preserved.
Sen.
Risch’s leadership matters.
ROTC gives young people the opportunity to earn a college education while preparing to serve our country as a military officer.
Along the way, they develop leadership, discipline, responsibility and a commitment to something larger than themselves.
As a University of Idaho graduate himself, Sen.
Risch understood what was at stake.
More importantly, when the program needed someone with the experience and relationships to get something done, he acted.
There are plenty of things in politics that divide us.
Supporting young people who are willing to learn, lead and serve our nation as an Army officer shouldn’t be one of them.
I’m grateful Sen.
Risch went to bat for the University of Idaho and helped preserve this proud tradition for future generations.” Caroline Nilsson Troy, of Genesee, served in the Idaho Legislature and was the Special Assistant to UI President Scott Green for Governmental Relations.
