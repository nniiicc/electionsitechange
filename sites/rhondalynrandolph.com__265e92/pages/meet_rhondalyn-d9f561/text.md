Rhondalyn Randolph is a pastor, community leader, civil rights advocate, and lifelong public servant dedicated to strengthening people and communities across Western Kentucky.
A native of Madisonville and longtime Owensboro resident, Rhondalyn has devoted her career to service, leadership, and advocacy — working at the intersection of faith, justice, and community development.
Faith & Leadership Rhondalyn serves as Senior Pastor of Pleasant Point Baptist Church in Utica.
She made history as the first woman in Western Kentucky licensed and ordained to preach and pastor a Baptist church.
Under her leadership, the historic 150-year-old church was revitalized, expanded its outreach, and was officially recognized as a Kentucky historical landmark.
Her ministry has focused on building people, strengthening families, and preserving community history.
Civil Rights & Community Advocacy Rhondalyn serves as: Vice President — NAACP Kentucky State Conference and past President, Owensboro NAACP Branch (2014 -2025).
Her leadership has helped launch scholarships for students, expand health and youth initiatives, and advance reconciliation and civil rights efforts across the region.
She helped lead the successful effort to remove a 120-year-old Confederate monument from the Daviess County Courthouse grounds — replacing division with dialogue and progress.
She has organized peace and reconciliation rallies, supports domestic violence survivors, and works with local committees preserving Black history and promoting unity.
Media, Education, & Public Voice Rhondalyn is a regular columnist and commentator on faith, values, and community issues.
She has hosted television and cable programs and frequently speaks at conferences, churches, and civic events.
Her community leadership and various organizational roles in community service and programs for working families and people have led to her contributions as a content author and journalist.
She is a graduate of Owensboro Community & Technical College and Western Kentucky University and continues to seek out education and professional development to be a better leader and advocate.
Rhondalyn has completed the Leadership Owensboro program, 2000 and 2021.
Family Rhondalyn is a proud mother and grandmother who believes strong families are the foundation of strong communities.
She is a tireless advocate for her bigger family of Owensboro where she works tirelessly to improve the lives of everyone.
