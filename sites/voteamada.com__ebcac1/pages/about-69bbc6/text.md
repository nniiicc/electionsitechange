Skip to content for Minnesota House 39B Menu About Issues Donate Menu About Issues Donate About Amáda Amáda Márquez Simula is the first person of color elected to office in Columbia Heights, and only the second Latina mayor in Minnesota’s state history.
Community members encouraged her to seek office based on her steadfast volunteerism, solid management skills, and outspoken support for residents from all walks of life.
Following three terms as mayor, the community is encouraging her to bring her leadership to the Minnesota House of Representatives.
A Community Organizer Amáda is widely recognized as an active leader in the community.
Her involvement has only increased since taking office.
She volunteers daily, advocates for residents, and makes connections that have a positive impact.
More involvement… Endorsed by Your Neighbors Amáda has the support of the people in her community, as well as endorsements from several regional unions and organizations.
Her co-governance with state and federal officials has brought attention and funding to Columbia Heights.
More endorsements… A Track Record of Success Amáda Márquez Simula consistently demonstrates informed, fearless, and creative leadership.
A look at her track record shows the many ways she has helped keep property taxes as low as possible, made improvements to city technology and services, modernized public safety, created healthier spaces, and brought more representation into our local government.
See her full Track Record… Hard Work for Real Solutions Amáda has a long history of taking decisive action for those in the district.
Representation is not just words or appearances, but actual work that she does to mobilize teams, connect people with resources in her network, employ strategies that work, and pursue realistic solutions for the district and Minnesota at large.
See her full platform… Small Town Kid, Big Impact Amáda Márquez Simula was born in Madison, Wisconsin to a working-class family with big ideas about the future.
Her father was a childhood immigrant from Mexico.
Together with her brothers, she breathed new life into a fledgling restaurant, transforming it into one of the most successful venues in the area.
Read her full bio… How do you say ‘Amáda’ anyway?
Even among Latinos, Amáda is an uncommon name from a bygone era.
In Spanish, it means “beloved”, though colloquially the word is often used like “sweetheart” or “darling”.
In Spanish, the letter ‘d’ is soft and sounds like ‘th’ in words like ‘the’, ‘they’, and ‘them’.
If you can say ‘a mother’ you can say Amáda!
