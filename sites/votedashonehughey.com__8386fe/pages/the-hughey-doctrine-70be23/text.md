Congress AMERICA'S NEXT CHAPTER America is entering a defining new era.
The global balance of power is being transformed by conflict, energy uncertainty, artificial intelligence, cyber warfare, supply-chain vulnerabilities, and a worldwide race to control the technologies of tomorrow.
For too long, America has allowed critical parts of its economy and national security to become dependent on decisions made beyond our borders.
Our energy.
Our technology.
Our manufacturing capacity.
Our critical materials.
Our supply chains.
You cannot open new doors with old keys.
The Hughey Doctrine is a new framework for American foreign policy and national security built around a simple principle: America must reduce strategic dependence and build the energy, technology, manufacturing, infrastructure, and workforce necessary to determine its own future.
This is not a call to withdraw from the world.
It is a call to make America strong enough to engage with the world from a position of strength.
AMERICA'S NEXT CHAPTER ENERGY INDEPENDENCE IS NATIONAL SECURITY A nation cannot be truly secure if its economy depends on foreign governments or unstable global energy markets for the power it needs to function.
America must build an energy system that is reliable, resilient, diverse, and increasingly powered by American innovation.
That means advancing domestic energy production while investing in nuclear power, renewable energy, energy storage, grid modernization, and next-generation technologies.
Our objective should be unmistakable: Power the American economy with American energy.
Energy independence is not simply about lowering costs.
It is about protecting America’s freedom to act.
BUILD THE FUTURE HERE The technologies that define the next century are being developed today.
Artificial intelligence.
Robotics.
Semiconductors.
Quantum computing.
Biotechnology.
Cybersecurity.
Advanced manufacturing.
Space technology.
These technologies will determine economic competitiveness, military readiness, and national power.
America cannot afford to become dependent on foreign competitors for the technologies that will determine our future.
We must accelerate American research, development, investment, commercialization, and production.
Invent it here.
Build it here.
Deploy it here.
America should not simply compete in the industries of the future.
America should lead them.
REDUCE DEPENDENCE ON CRITICAL TECHNOLOGIES A nation that cannot produce the technologies, materials, and systems essential to its economy and national defense is vulnerable.
America must strengthen domestic capacity in: Semiconductors and microelectronics Artificial intelligence and robotics Advanced manufacturing Energy technology and storage Critical minerals and strategic materials Telecommunications infrastructure Cybersecurity Quantum technologies Biotechnology Space and defense systems The objective is not merely to keep pace with other nations.
America’s goal should be to lead the world in the technologies that define the future.
Strategic independence requires the ability to build what we cannot afford to lose.
MANUFACTURING STRENGTH IS NATIONAL STRENGTH Innovation alone is not enough.
If America invents the future but depends on other nations to manufacture it, we have not achieved true independence.
We need a modern American manufacturing ecosystem capable of taking an idea from an American laboratory to an American factory—and from that factory to customers around the world.
Invent here.
Build here.
Employ Americans here.
A strong manufacturing base means more than jobs.
It means stronger supply chains, greater economic resilience, stronger national defense, and the ability to respond when America faces a crisis.
What America must have, America must be capable of building.
PREPARE AMERICANS FOR THE AI ECONOMY The greatest technology in the world cannot create broad prosperity if American workers are not prepared to participate in the economy it creates.
We must dramatically expand access to education and workforce training in: Artificial intelligence Robotics Cybersecurity Advanced manufacturing Skilled trades Engineering and technical careers We cannot wait until workers have lost their jobs to begin preparing them for the next economy.
Preparation must come before disruption.
American workers must be leaders in the AI revolution—not casualties of it.
The AI economy should not simply produce more technology.
It should produce more opportunity for Americans.
DEFEND AMERICA'S DIGITAL INFRASTRUCTURE National security has changed.
America’s most important infrastructure is no longer protected only by physical barriers.
Our power grid, financial system, transportation networks, hospitals, communications systems, manufacturing facilities, and government institutions are increasingly connected to digital networks.
That makes cybersecurity a national-security priority.
America must invest in protecting critical infrastructure from cyberattacks, sabotage, technological disruption, and emerging threats.
Because the next major attack on America may not come by air, land, or sea.
It may come through a computer network.
STRONG ALLIANCES WITHOUT STRATEGIC DEPENDENCE America should maintain strong relationships with allies and partners around the world.
We should trade with other nations.
We should cooperate with other nations.
We should innovate with other nations.
And we should compete with other nations.
But partnership must never become permanent strategic dependence.
America must maintain the domestic capacity to protect its critical energy, technology, manufacturing, defense, communications, and infrastructure systems.
America can work with the world without becoming dependent on the world.
Strategic independence does not mean isolation.
It means having the strength to choose.
THE HUGHEY TEST Before America becomes strategically dependent on another country for a critical resource, technology, or capability, we should ask five fundamental questions: CAN AMERICA PRODUCE IT?
CAN AMERICA BUILD IT?
CAN AMERICA POWER IT?
CAN AMERICA PROTECT IT?
CAN AMERICA INNOVATE BEYOND IT?

Driven by American innovation.
Built by American workers.
Protected by American industry.
Secured by American technology.
We cannot wait for the next crisis to discover what America can no longer produce.
We cannot wait for a supply chain to collapse before we decide it matters.
We cannot wait for a cyberattack before we secure our infrastructure.
And we cannot wait for an economic transformation to begin before we prepare our workforce.
We must build America’s capacity now—before the crisis arrives.
This is not isolationism.
It is strategic independence.
America should remain engaged with the world.
We should trade, cooperate, innovate, and compete globally.
But America’s survival, security, prosperity, and future should never depend upon another nation’s willingness or ability to provide what America is capable of building for itself.
THE HUGHEY DOCTRINE A New American Foreign Policy for a Dangerous World Reduce strategic dependence.
Build American energy.
Lead the world in technology.
Rebuild American manufacturing.
Prepare American workers.
Protect America's future.
The world is changing.
The question is whether America will react to that change—or lead it.
I believe America must lead.
