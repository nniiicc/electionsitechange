In our communities, faith isn't a talking point used to score political points.
It is a quiet, foundational cornerstone of daily life.
It's woven into how we raise our families, how we look out for our neighbors, and how we keep ourselves grounded through the toughest seasons.
Ultimately, Montanans want something very simple when it comes to our beliefs: we want to be left alone to practice our faith as we see fit.
We don't need heavy-handed oversight or public institutions trying to manage our conscience.
True freedom means having the space to worship, live, and gather in peace.
A representative's job shouldn't be to reinvent our culture from a government desk.
It should be to protect the fundamental, constitutional liberties that allow our families and faith communities to thrive on their own.
That's Montana common sense.
