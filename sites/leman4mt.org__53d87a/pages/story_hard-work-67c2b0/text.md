But lately, Helena seems determined to make the tax code as complicated as possible.
We see it with the endless shifting of brackets and convoluted property tax overhauls that require piles of paperwork just to prove you live in your own home.
Instead of actually cutting the overall cost of government, the state keeps inventing new ways to shuffle the deck.
When bureaucracy grows, it always looks for creative ways to chip away at your hard-earned paycheck.
Rolling up your sleeves and putting in an honest day's labor should be rewarded, not penalized.
You should be able to keep what you earn rather than watching it get eaten up by creative taxing and state bureaucracy.
True strength doesn't come from a larger state footprint.
It comes from citizens active in service to their communities.
When a neighbor needs a hand, we show up.
We build our towns from the ground up through volunteer groups, local small businesses, and simple handshake agreements.
We don't need a government agency to manage our goodwill, and we don't need more state red tape making it harder to run a business, build a home, or clear a lot.
We need a representative who understands that a thriving Montana is built by workers, not by departments in Helena.
Rewarding an honest day's labor and letting you keep what you earn is the only way to keep our state strong.
That's Montana common sense.
