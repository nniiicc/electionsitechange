She is passionate about improving health, housing, and accountability in Northern Minnesota.
From Kettle River to Cloquet, Pine City to our rural townships — our communities deserve leadership that listens, protects public resources, and delivers real solutions.
WHY I’M RUNNING Northern Minnesota is at a crossroads.
Rising utility costs, limited access to health care, housing shortages, and major development decisions are impacting families, seniors, people with disabilities, and small businesses across District 11. ​ I’m running for the Minnesota Senate to bring transparent, community-driven leadership to St.
Paul — grounded in public health, local government experience, and a commitment to protecting our people and our land.
Health Care & Public Health Access to quality care should not depend on your ZIP code.
I support strengthening rural health systems, addressing long-term COVID impacts, and protecting seniors and people with disabilities.
Jobs, Workforce & Responsible AI Economic growth must create real jobs — not just profits for outside interests.
I support workforce development and responsible AI policy that protects workers and communities.
Transportation & Regional Connectivity Reliable transportation supports jobs, education, and health care access.
I support smart regional solutions that strengthen the Northland economy. ​ Housing & Infrastructure Safe, affordable housing and reliable water and sewer systems are foundational.
I will advocate for transparent infrastructure funding and practical solutions for rural communities.
Environment & Land Stewardship Northern Minnesota’s land and water are not expendable.
I support sustainable development and oppose poorly regulated projects that ignore community input. ​ Minnesota Needs a Five Year Data Center Moratorium Minnesota is being asked to make fast decisions about facilities that could shape our water use, electricity demand, land use, and local budgets for decades.
Large-scale and hyperscale data centers are not ordinary development projects.
They can require enormous amounts of power.
Some use significant amounts of water for cooling.
They can demand new transmission capacity, backup generators, roads, substations, and public services.
