New Poll Shows Shoffner Closes Gap, Signals Competitive U.S.
Senate Race in Arkansas
LITTLE ROCK, Ark.
(August 5, 2026) — A new statewide poll of likely Arkansas voters
shows the race for the U.S.
Senate is a statistical tie.
Democratic nominee Hallie
Shoffner’s campaign released a polling memo showing Shoffner is closing the gap with
incumbent Sen.
Tom Cotton, with just three percentage points between them, despite
more than $3 million in outside spending against her campaign.
The survey found Cotton polling at 46 percent support compared to Shoffner's 43
percent, a difference that falls within the poll's +/- 4 point margin of error, with 12 percent
of respondents still unsure.
The poll shows Shoffner has consolidated Democratic support, with 95 percent of
Democrats surveyed indicating they would vote for her.
Among Republicans, Cotton
receives support from only 78 percent, while 8 percent of Republicans already support
Shoffner and 14 percent remain undecided.
Shoffner also has a commanding lead
among Independent voters, securing 66 percent of their support, while 29 percent are
still unsure.
“This poll confirms what we've been hearing in every corner of Arkansas for months:
people want a Senator who will fight for them instead of playing politics as usual,” said
Anna-Lee Pittman, campaign manager for Hallie Shoffner’s U.S.
Senate campaign.
“Hallie understands what Arkansas families are up against because she's lived it, and
voters are responding to a campaign focused on lowering costs, holding Washington
accountable, and putting Arkansas first.”
According to the poll, more Arkansas voters view Cotton unfavorably than favorably,
and a majority, 52 percent, say he does not deserve reelection.
That includes nearly
one in four Republicans and three in four Independents.
The poll also found Shoffner is the only candidate with a net-positive favorability rating,
while 30 percent of voters do not yet recognize her, leaving significant room for growth
as more Arkansans learn about her.
"The most significant finding isn't simply that this race is within the margin of error," said
Tabitha Bonilla, Ph.D., Co-Founder and Head of Public Affairs for 2040 Strategy Group.
“It's that an incumbent senator with nearly universal name recognition remains below 50
percent while a challenger who is still introducing herself to voters is the only candidate
with a net-positive image.
Those are indicators of a genuinely competitive race."
Shoffner, a wife, mother and sixth-generation Arkansas farmer, has centered her
campaign on the issues voters identified as top priorities: government corruption, the
rising cost of living and education.
“After twelve years in Washington, Senator Cotton should be running on results,” said
Pittman.
“Instead, the corporate lobbyists, billionaire donors and Washington power
brokers who have backed his political career are spending millions to smear Hallie
because they know his record is weak.
As more Arkansans hear Hallie’s plans to put
Arkansas first, we fully expect the attacks to intensify.
Those attacks are meant to
distract voters.
But Hallie isn’t running to become part of Washington.
She’s running to
fight it.”
About the Poll
The survey was conducted by 2040 Strategy Group among 586 likely Arkansas voters
from July 17-25, 2026.
Results were weighted to reflect the state's likely electorate by
age, sex, race, region, income and party identification.
The poll has an approximate
margin of error of plus or minus 4.0 percentage points.
About Hallie Shoffner
Hallie Shoffner is a wife, mother, sixth-generation Arkansan, row crop farmer and small
business owner.
She’s running for US Senate to fight for her neighbors because
hardworking Arkansans shouldn’t have to choose between affording their life and
enjoying it; Arkansans deserve both health insurance and access to providers; and
every community should have first-rate infrastructure and a fighting chance: Arkansas
farmers have earned a forward-thinking Farm Bill, and every family is entitled to reliably
clean water, strong schools, affordable housing, high-quality childcare and elder care,
and broadband so they can compete in a global economy.
