AGRICULTURE & PRIVATE PROPERTY
THE PROBLEM
Farmers, ranchers and rural landowners are facing higher equipment, fuel, insurance and operating costs.
Owning land does not necessarily mean having the cash to absorb those costs.
Rural Missourians should not lose control of their property simply because a larger corporation or development project has more money.
THE SOLUTION
Protect private property rights while creating economic development that works with rural communities.
WHAT I WILL DO
Protect private property rights.
Support Missouri farmers and family farms.
Protect the right to repair and maintain equipment.
Review regulations that create unnecessary costs.
Give rural landowners a meaningful voice in major development decisions.
Require taxpayers to see the cost and benefit of major development incentives.
Education
THE PROBLEM
Missouri has fallen behind in adequately funding public education, while Missouri teachers remain near the bottom nationally in pay.
Rural schools are already making difficult financial decisions, including four-day school weeks in some communities.
At the same time, Missouri is putting additional taxpayer money into private-school voucher programs while public schools educate the vast majority of Missouri children.THE SOLUTION
Make public education a state responsibility and invest in the schools our communities depend on.The larger K-12 concern comes from the combination of federal budget proposals, changes to the Department of Education, federal funding uncertainty, and the additional pressure the OBBBA places on state budgets through Medicaid and SNAP changes.
Note that the federal government provides a smaller share of K-12 funding than states and local governments, but argues that the OBBBA's additional costs to states could make it harder for states to maintain education funding.
Cuts add to food insecurity due to cuts in the free or reduced lunch program.
WHAT I WILL DO
Protect and strengthen state funding for public schools.
Work toward competitive teacher and school-staff pay.
Protect rural schools from funding decisions that push costs onto local taxpayers.
Demand accountability and transparency for taxpayer-funded voucher programs.
Protect vocational education and extracurricular opportunities.
Monitor changes from Washington, D.C. so federal education cuts don't simply become another burden for Missouri schools.
COST OF LIVING, INFLATION & FIXED INCOMES
THE PROBLEM
Families are paying more for groceries, utilities, insurance, housing, fuel and health care.
For seniors and people living on fixed incomes, there is no extra paycheck to make up the difference.
A Social Security COLA may increase someone's monthly benefit, but Medicare premiums can also increase and reduce how much of that increase actually remains in their pocket.
THE SOLUTION
State government should stop adding unnecessary costs to household budgets and make sure Missouri's policies protect people who have the least room to absorb higher prices.
WHAT I WILL DO
Examine the effect of state taxes and fees on working families and fixed-income households.
Protect state programs that help seniors and people with disabilities meet basic needs.
Fight unnecessary increases in costs that Missouri controls.
Protect access to rural health care.
Support targeted property-tax relief for people who genuinely need it.
Evaluate legislation by asking what it will actually cost a family each month.
The question isn't just whether someone's check went up.
It's whether they have more money left after the bills are paid.
RURAL HEALTH CARE & HOSPITALS
THE PROBLEM
Rural hospitals and clinics operate under financial pressure while rural residents often have fewer choices for doctors, specialists and emergency care.
When a rural hospital struggles, the entire community feels it.
Federal health-care decisions can add additional financial pressure to Missouri.
THE SOLUTION
Keep rural health care available, financially sustainable and close to home.
WHAT I WILL DO
Support policies that strengthen rural hospitals.
Help recruit and retain rural health-care professionals.
Protect emergency and primary care.
Examine the state impact of federal Medicaid changes before Missouri passes those costs down to communities.
Fight unnecessary administrative costs.
Make rural health-care access part of Missouri's economic-development strategy.

ROADS, BRIDGES, WATER & BROADBAND
THE PROBLEM
Rural Missouri cannot grow without reliable infrastructure.
Roads, bridges, water systems and broadband aren't luxuries.
They're the foundation of everyday life and economic development.
THE SOLUTION
Invest in basic infrastructure before promising the next shiny economic-development project.
WHAT I WILL DO
Prioritize maintenance of existing roads and bridges.
Fight for rural infrastructure funding.
Expand reliable broadband.
Support rural water and wastewater projects.
Make infrastructure spending transparent.
Require major developments to help pay for the infrastructure they require.
WORKING FAMILIES & WAGES
THE PROBLEM
A person can work full-time and still struggle to afford housing, food, transportation, child care, insurance and health care.
When wages don't keep up with the cost of living, families fall behind even when they're doing everything they're supposed to do.
THE SOLUTION
Build an economy where work provides a realistic path to stability.
WHAT I WILL DO
Support workforce development connected to actual rural jobs.
Support small businesses and independent operators.
Examine policies that allow large corporations to shift costs onto taxpayers.
Evaluate wage and employment policies based on their effect on workers and small businesses.
Reduce unnecessary state fees and costs.
Make sure economic-development incentives produce measurable benefits for Missouri workers.
SENIORS, PEOPLE WITH DISABILITIES & FAMILIES WHO NEED HELP
THE PROBLEM
Programs such as Medicaid, SNAP and housing assistance can help children, seniors, people with disabilities and working families survive when circumstances make it difficult to get by.
Cutting assistance doesn't necessarily eliminate the need.
It can move the cost to hospitals, schools, charities, families and local taxpayers.
THE SOLUTION
Protect people who genuinely need assistance while creating opportunities for people who are able to become self-sufficient.
WHAT I WILL DO
Protect essential services for seniors and people with disabilities.
Protect children who depend on these programs.
Support employment opportunities for people who are able to work.
Oppose unnecessary barriers for people who legitimately qualify for assistance.
Examine the Missouri consequences of federal program cuts before shifting costs onto local communities.
Demand accountability for how taxpayer-funded programs operate..
TAXES, TAX BREAKS & STATE SPENDING
THE PROBLEM
Missouri has limited resources.
Every tax cut, exemption, credit or incentive affects the amount of money available for schools, roads, health care and other public services.
THE SOLUTION
Make taxpayers the first consideration, not an afterthought.
WHAT I WILL DO
Before supporting major tax legislation, I will ask:
Who benefits?
Who pays?
What does it cost?
What do we get in return?
I will:
Review existing tax credits and incentives.
Demand transparency about who receives taxpayer benefits.
Support independent fiscal analysis.
Protect essential public services.
Oppose shifting state costs onto local property taxpayers.
Require measurable results from major economic incentives.
