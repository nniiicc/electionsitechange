Dembo prevailed with an extremely strong performance in a competitive field of 7 candidates, including former state representative Cherlynn Stevenson and former city council member David Kloiber.
His campaign focused on fighting the corruption Dembo saw firsthand while working in Trump’s Department of Justice, lowering the cost of living, and ending the war in Iran that is killing Americans and driving up gas prices.
Zach Dembo, Democratic nominee for Kentucky’s 6th congressional district, released the following statement: “I’m absolutely humbled and grateful to be the Democratic nominee for Kentucky’s 6th congressional district.
Thank you to my fellow Democrats for a hard-fought race that was focused on the issues.
“As a first-time candidate, so many people counted us out, but together, we built a campaign from scratch, brought together a team of volunteers, supporters and voters all across this district, and then, we won.
“I’ve spent my career helping people, regardless of political party – in the classroom, in the Navy, and as a federal prosecutor fighting corruption.
I’m ready to hit the ground running in Washington to root out what’s broken and make that corrupt city actually work for people here in Kentucky.
That’s the representation that Central Kentucky has long deserved in Congress.
“That representation absolutely cannot be Ralph Alvarado, a professional politician who stands in lockstep with Matt Bevin and the corrupt politicians in Washington, and someone who will cut your healthcare and increase costs on working families, all to fund tax breaks for millionaires, billionaires, and his wealthy California donors.
“Tonight, we celebrate.
Tomorrow, we get to work.
This win shows we can prove the establishment class wrong.
We’ll do it again in November when we flip this seat.” As a first-time candidate, Dembo mounted an impressive campaign, raising more than the entire Democratic field combined in every quarter he was in the race and clinching the endorsements of major endorsements and public officials, including the National Education Association and former Lexington Mayor Jim Gray.
Recent polling suggests a close race between Republican nominee Ralph Alvarado and Zach Dembo in a district that has not elected a Democrat as representative in over 15 years.
While Donald Trump won the district by 15 points, Andy Beshear carried the district by 20 points just a year before in 2023.
