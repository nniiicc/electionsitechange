Put Washington Corruption
on Trial Agenda
As a federal prosecutor, I spent my career keeping my community safe and holding the powerful accountable to the fullest extent of the law.
Now, I’m running for Congress because we need someone who will take that fight to Washington and focus on the issues that matter.
Both parties have broken the public trust, and they must answer to the American people.
Throughout my career, I’ve sworn four oaths to the Constitution, and I took each one seriously.
That’s why I resigned from the Department of Justice when I saw corruption that benefited politicians over regular people.
It was a matter of principle.
This is a bigger problem than one Administration.
Trust in government is at an all-time low and costs are out of control.
Career politicians like my opponent Ralph Alvarado are the reason why.
While folks in Central Kentucky are struggling to get by, Ralph spent his time in elected office focused on himself.
In the state legislature, he cast votes that benefited his own business, putting his own profits ahead of protecting older Kentuckians facing substandard conditions in low-rated nursing homes he worked for.
And after joining disgraced former Governor Matt Bevin as his running mate – and losing – he abandoned Central Kentucky to take a taxpayer-funded job in Tennessee.
Ralph Alvarado won’t get rid of waste, fraud, and abuse in government because he and his wealthy corporate donors have participated in, and benefited from, it.
Sending yet another career politician like Alvarado who’s running for office for the sixth time to represent this district in Congress is like sending a fox to guard the henhouse.
But it’s not just Alvarado – too many elected politicians on both sides of the aisle go to Washington to enrich themselves, and they are willing to pass bad policies that increase costs for you or your family to do it.
I’ve spent my career fighting corruption, and I’m ready to continue the fight in Washington.
Here are the anti-corruption policies I stand for, so that Washington finally starts working for you:
Ban stock trading and prediction markets for members of Congress
While the rest of us are struggling to get by, our representatives are getting richer while in office.
Politicians are making hundreds of thousands of dollars trading stocks in companies they oversee and have private information on, making policies that will impact those companies’ bottom lines while voting to raise costs on working people.
I will push for a full ban on trading individual stock or cryptocurrency for members of Congress and their immediate families, to make sure they serve us, not their pocketbooks, and ban politicians from using prediction markets while in office.
We also need harsher penalties for rulebreakers.
Right now, when politicians violate existing legislation designed to protect against insider trading, they are fined $200, barely a slap on the wrist.
I support enhancing the US criminal code to ensure that kind of corruption is a crime with a harsh penalty and giving the SEC or DOJ the tools necessary to investigate and root out politicians pretending to serve us while lining their own pockets.
Ban Corporate PAC Donations
Kentuckians know that corporations and special interests have too much power over Congress.
Big corporations push their agenda using politicians they buy off through campaign donations, it is public corruption in plain sight.
That is why I have pledged to never take a dime of corporate pac money, so there is no question that I serve my community and my community alone.
In Congress, I will fight to ban donations from corporate pacs out right, so Congress starts serving us, not corporate special interests.
Institute a Lifetime Ban on Lobbying
We need to close the revolving door between our elected representatives and their next lucrative job influencing our political system.
In Congress, I would work across the aisle to ban lobbying by former federal officials.
Enact Term Limits
Congress shouldn’t be a lifetime job and politics shouldn’t be a career.
In Congress I will propose term limits of 12 years on members of the House and Senate and I will hold myself to that standard regardless of its passage.
Public office should be about service, not holding on to power.
Get Rid of Dark Money In Politics
When billionaires and corporations can pour unlimited money into campaigns without any transparency or accountability, ordinary voters get drowned out.
And to make matters worse, the politicians who directly benefit know who they owe.
As your congressman, I’ll introduce an amendment to end the Citizens United decision and get corporations out of our political fundraising system.
Codify the Justice Department’s Independence
The Justice Department should work for the American people, not the President.
The Justice Manual, the document that outlines the Department’s policies and procedures, should be codified so one figure can’t change it to their own benefit.
Independent redistricting commissions in every state
Independent redistricting commissions in every state: Voters should pick their elected representatives, not the other way around.
As politicians are in a race to the bottom, rigging the line of their districts to pick who votes for them, the rest of us are feeling left behind from both political parties.
If we’re serious about restoring trust in our elections, we need an independent, nonpartisan process for drawing district lines.
Never fly first class
This is a campaign about Central Kentucky, not about privileges or perks.
Voters trust us to be responsible stewards of their tax dollars, so we shouldn’t indulge in luxuries like first-class flights on their dime while the debt continues to climb.
Recuse yourself from any conflict
If you directly financially benefit from a vote, you shouldn’t be proposing policy to affect it.
Ralph Alvarado proposed – and passed – a bill to limit medical malpractice lawsuits while working as a Medical Director, a position where he profited when it passed.
That’s classic corruption that comes with a cost to the patients that can’t sue for justice when their rights are violated.
Make Housing, Healthcare, and Energy More Affordable
Developers, big tech, and pharmaceutical companies are cutting backroom deals with our politicians, and we’re all paying the price with ballooning housing prices, sky-high healthcare costs for ordinary folks, and increased strain on our power grid.
In Lexington, rent has climbed over 40% in just the past five years, with more than a quarter of families extremely cost burdened, meanwhile the big businesses in Lexington are posting record profits.
All across Kentucky, we’re seeing backlash against data centers and localities like Lexington are passing data center moratoriums; meanwhile big tech is bribing the federal government to pass laws banning states from regulating data centers altogether while they contribute millions to fund Trump’s inauguration – we need local control.
Pharmaceutical companies write their own pricing rules and insurance companies block coverage while their executives donate to politicians.
We need to make sure regulators answer to patients, not pharmaceutical executives, and increase the housing supply by cutting the red tape for developers and protect renters from the price gouging.
