The ad highlights the conditions in the nursing homes where Alvarado worked, which included a scabies outbreak, testimony from a family member that their loved one was “drugged up like a zombie” and patients left unbathed.
A Lexington Herald-Leader fact check confirms: “Yes, the ad is accurate.” The Herald-Leader writes in part : “ Yes, the ad is accurate .
Alvarado did serve in the Kentucky Senate while simultaneously working as medical director for Central Kentucky nursing homes that had serious quality of care issues , as reported by state inspectors.
As a senator, he sponsored bills and took positions that helped the nursing home industry, including the facilities that employed him , although Alvarado said he was helping the health care industry as a whole.
Alvarado was one of the gift recipients identified in the Medicare fraud case settled by the federal government and Nurses Registry, although he and the other doctors were not named as defendants in the suit.” The Facts: During Alvarado’s time in the Kentucky state Senate, he passed legislation that made it harder for families to hold nursing homes accountable for malpractice.
Also while in the state legislature, Alvarado was implicated in a Medicare fraud scheme .
The Lexington Herald-Leader has reported on Alvarado’s corruption for nearly a decade, and has verified that the claims made in the ad are true .
