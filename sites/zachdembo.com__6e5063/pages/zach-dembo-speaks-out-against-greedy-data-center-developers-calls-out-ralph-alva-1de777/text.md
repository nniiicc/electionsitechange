Dembo released his position on data centers a few months ago, calling for stronger regulations to prevent developers from shifting costs onto consumers, protect our water and land, and ensure local communities have a say.
Dembo has also called for a job retraining program in events where AI does displace workers.
“As Big Tech rakes in record profits, it is unacceptable for any data center developer to pass added costs onto Kentucky consumers,” said Dembo.
“In Congress, I’ll push for legislation that requires transparency and local input, holds companies accountable for their environmental impact, and makes sure Big Tech can’t offload rising energy costs onto Kentucky families.” Meanwhile, Dembo’s opponent profits off of Big Tech and data centers as he runs to assume a role to regulate them.
Alvarado’s financial disclosure shows he has up to $315,000 in stocks of data center developer companies while supporting a bill that gives massive tax breaks to those very companies as they look to build over Central Kentucky’s rural farmland.
Aside from a few shares of John Deere received as a gift from his grandfather, Dembo does not own any individual stocks and has released an anti-corruption agenda that includes banning congressional stock trading.
“We need folks who are in [Congress] for us, not for the big corporations or for their stock to do well,” said Dembo in a social media video.
“I will be a voice against corruption and for folks here in Kentucky.
We can’t have more self-serving politicians in D.C.” ### Let's Go!
