The government shouldn’t have a say in how and when you start a family or in what women choose to do with their own bodies.
As the son of two physicians, I know how private and sacred the relationship between a doctor and her patient is.
There’s no reason to have the government in that exam room when a patient is facing one of the most difficult decisions of her life.
A woman who wants to have an abortion in her first trimester should have that right, and the extreme bans in place in states like Kentucky only means more health risks, expense, and unfairness for working class folks trying to make their own family planning decisions.
Abortions should be safe, legal, and rare, and the vast majority of Kentuckians agree, as we saw with the anti-abortion Amendment 2’s resounding defeat in 2022 (including by 20 points here in the Sixth District).
Kentucky’s current law is among the most extreme in the country, and Washington Republicans are pushing to make it the law of the land nationally.
I won’t let that happen.In Congress.
I would be a vote to codify Roe v.
Wade, which worked for over 50 years, and protect fertility treatments like IVF.
I’d also want to make sure we are truly a pro-family country by fighting for things like universal pre-K and affordable child care, expanding the child tax credit, and creating incentives to reward companies that offer sufficient parental leave for their workers.
