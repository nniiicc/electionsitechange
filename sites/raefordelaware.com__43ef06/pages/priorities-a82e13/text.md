My policy platform is rooted in my commitment to bringing fresh ideas and government accountability to Dover.
It focuses on the issues I hear the most from our community.
Build Streets for People, Not Just Cars Our streets should work for everyone, but years of designs that only prioritize vehicles have left us with unwalkable roadways and increased traffic-related harm.
Investments in accessible streets that are safer for all people, no matter their transit choice, generate economic, health, and environmental benefits making our community a more desirable place to live.
Advocate for solutions that reduce traffic speeds and increase safety for all people, whether they’re walking, biking, or driving.
Fare-free public transit that encourages ridership and decreases traffic.
Advocate for substantial investments in building a safer, more accessible transportation network for drivers, bus riders, cyclists, and walkers alike.
Provide funds to local communities to add low-cost improvements to problematic intersections, removing the red tape between people and their own streets.
Decrease Housing Costs Delaware’s housing market is leaving people behind.
As rents and the costs of owning a home continue to rise, families are feeling the squeeze.
We can ensure every Delawarean has affordable and stable housing.
Incentivize local, small-scale developers that can focus on infill developments and meeting community needs where they are at, making us more adaptable to changing economic winds Use public investments to create more affordable housing units.
Eliminate exclusionary zoning rules that increase the cost of living and decrease our ability to build affordable housing.
Implement rent stabilization to limit how high rent can be raised each year.
Incentivize the development of "pattern book" housing plans that set design standards to streamline the approval process and construction of affordable homes.
Create a progressive property tax policy to protect homeowners from drastic increases and help people keep their homes.
Remove the ban on land value tax from our state constitution.
This will allow more fair taxation throughout our state.
Ensure seniors can age in place and lower-income families can stay in their homes through expanded repair programs and tax assistance programs.
Increase the climate and disaster resiliency of housing, with a specific focus on the flooding we face as a coastal state.
Public Education that Meets the Needs of All Students A strong public school can serve as the core of a community, but right now our schools aren’t getting what they need to effectively serve all students and their families.
Educators aren’t paid enough, and there’s too much waste in our systems.
Our schools can continue to be lively community resources if we are smart about how we design them.
Fund universal Pre-K so students can start school ready to learn and parents can afford to go to work.
Provide free school meals for every child in Delaware because no child can learn on an empty stomach.
Fewer computers, more creativity in the classroom Increase apprenticeship programs to support people changing careers later in life and graduates who prefer trades to college.
Ensure they provide living wages.
Fund universal community college, trade schools, and jobs training programs.
Implement the equitable, weighted funding model that has been recommended for years.
Ensure it delivers more resources for students who require more support like children with disabilities or neurodivergence, English learners, and students from low-income families.
Use state funding to secure competitive salaries and benefits for teachers and school staff, no matter their district.
Clean Land, Air, and Water as a Right No human can live without clean air and water.
Yet in Delaware, we consistently rank terribly for both air and drinking water quality.
I am a fierce advocate for our environment because our lives depend on us doing better.
Pass a Green Amendment to protect every resident's right to clean air, fresh water, and healthy soil.
Create incentives and plant supply programs for people to convert grass to native plant pollinator gardens; this will help conserve water, reduce the use of weed control chemicals added to our land, help clean our water table, and support agriculture and gardens in our state by supporting our native insect populations.
Demand accountability from corporate polluters to fund clean ups and reparations to the people and communities they harm.
Push for a swift transition to a diversified electrical grid powered by solar and wind that can bring down energy prices.
Create strict, state-wide restrictions on data centers and other energy hogs to prevent them from sucking up all our water and electricity.
Fund a statewide effort to replace aging water pipes and remove lead contamination from our water system.
Incentivize research into, and usage of, architectural designs that support our local environment and are resilient to climate changes.
Regulations for AI & Data Centers As a software engineer, I would bring to the state legislature an in-depth understanding of the current and future development of AI.
In particular, it is imperative that we keep our school and healthcare systems supported as they navigate this change, and that we proactively introduce legislation to protect people from the worst effects of decreased learning outcomes, job loss, and higher energy prices.
When the Project Washington hyper-scale data center was first proposed in Delaware in the summer of 2025, I attended the first town hall on the topic.
I then joined Sierra Club’s Delaware chapter in canvassing the neighborhoods around the proposed site and educating community members on the proposal and how they could voice their concerns to their county councilpeople.
I stayed closely involved with the process through canvassing and advocacy, and was in the room when New Castle County Council finally passed the commonsense data center ordinance, ensuring data centers are set back from our daycares, hospitals, and houses.
You can see my public comments around the data center ordinance discussed at the County level.
The major concerns I have, and what I’ve heard from residents at the doors and at the town hall, centered on how data centers will increase our electric bills, affect our sleep and health, reduce our water quality and availability, decrease our property values, pollute our air with diesel generators, and harm our struggling wildlife populations.
At a state level I would: Support a data center moratorium until all 3 counties have basic, commonsense data center regulations in place and we have a plan for how our state’s electric grid will withstand the increased load Create a special tariff for large-load customers, including data centers, with demand of more than 75 megawatts Mandate strict regulations on water consumption by data centers, with such requirements as: large facilities paying higher water rates for perceived high usage, large facilities paying for increased monitoring of the wastewater they return to the system (as we know wastewater from data centers often has higher concentrations of harmful elements in it after cycling through so-called “closed loop” processes) Phase in renewable power purchase agreements specifically for data centers Require noise pollution assessment and reporting requirements, with a requirement to establish a baseline of decibel levels prior to construction, with an eye towards how it impacts public health and migratory patterns of animals.
(Note: we have base noise management requirements at the county level as it relates to land use; however, the state can require additional consideration for public health.) Require that any data center project must include a project labor agreement with the Delaware Building and Construction Trades Council An Economy that Benefits Working People In order to meet the basic needs of its residents and attract new workers, Delaware must build a strong and diverse economy with the power to adapt to a rapidly changing world.
Diversify our state’s revenue streams and ensure our critical programs remain supported as federal funding is withdrawn.
Limit the development costs that energy providers are allowed to pass on to their consumers.
Expand our energy sources, with an emphasis on co-op development, and create pathways for Delaware to be a net energy exporter rather than an energy importer.
Push for a livable wage and champion union protections.
Advocate for fair-pay agreements on large state-funded projects to ensure that our local workforce and minority-owned businesses are properly represented.
Stand up to billionaires like Elon Musk and refuse to surrender our economy and our legal code to tech oligarchs.
Create a new copyright-like right for people to claim their own likeness, and allow them to have legal claim against unauthorized use of their likeness in AI-generated content.
Right to a Healthy Life Health care costs are rising, and tens of thousands of Delawareans are set to lose access to Medicaid as a consequence of the most recent federal budget.
Meanwhile, the loss of federal funds means some providers in Delaware are shutting their doors, leaving many Delawareans without access to health care in-state at all.
I believe that health care is a human right, and all Delawareans deserve the ability to live a healthy life without driving for hours to see a specialist, or having to choose between their medication and putting food on the table.
Advocate for financial support for assisted living facilities and in-home nurse aids.
Further fund community medical clinics to incentivize preventative care, lessening the burden on our ERs and hospital systems.
Amend our state constitution to protect access to reproductive and gender-affirming health care.
Safeguard access to vaccines, ensuring that anyone who wants a vaccine can get one.
Democracy and Human Dignity Democracy and the rule of law are under threat from an aspiring dictator and the politicians who are standing back and letting it happen.
Every day, whether it’s the persecution of our immigrant neighbors, attacks on the transgender community, or some new abuse of the legal system in order to target a political enemy, the Trump administration is setting horrifying new precedents in cruelty and destructiveness.
This has created a hostile environment not just federally, but in our state government as well, with many hesitant to act too boldly for fear of angering the GOP.
I wholeheartedly understand these fears, and I also believe that Delaware deserves leaders who are able to meet this moment with confidence, strength, and a plan.
Shore up the protections our state offers against abuses of power from other states and the federal government.
Amend our state constitution to permit no-excuse absentee voting (vote by mail), early voting, and same-day voter registration.
Restore the voting rights of people who were previously convicted of felonies and have now completed their sentences through a constitutional amendment.
Protect the rights and lives of the people who have immigrated here Prohibit federal immigration agents from hiding their faces and identities Make Delaware a sanctuary state for the LGBTQ+ community Community-Centered Justice For too long, our criminal justice system has operated in a way that systematically dehumanizes the people within its grasp, and perpetuates cycles of harm and recidivism.
Something has to change.
I want to see our neighborhoods invested in each other, and for our systems to recognize incarcerated people and returning citizens first as members of a community, rather than as offenders whose criminality is some inherent character trait.
Build a foundation of rehabilitation and reintegration underneath our justice system Support incentives to replace school police officers with counselors and promote alternatives to prison time.
Push for fines and fees reform, and sponsor legislation to dismantle the outdated funding of government services off the backs of disenfranchised and under-resourced Delawareans.
Transparent Government Systems A government of the people should, by all rights, be transparent to its people.
Still, our government is often unnecessarily and sometimes deliberately opaque about vital aspects of its operations and services.
We cannot expect people to trust government if their government is unwilling to trust them with the truth.
Increase transparency in law enforcement by supporting community review boards and allowing public access to misconduct records, so people know as much about their neighborhood’s armed officers as they do about their doctors and teachers.
Ban direct corporate donations to political campaigns to ensure our elected officials are accountable to their constituents, not their donors.
Prioritize Health and Safety in Consideration of Port Expansion My core belief is: we should not need to trade our health for jobs.
I do not personally support the port expansion project, as I am worried about the environmental impacts in our community, which is already overburdened from historical pollution.
As a state legislator, I will fight to ensure that economic development happens alongside, not at the expense of, the health and safety of surrounding neighborhoods.
Currently, the port expansion marketing has focused on the number of jobs that will be created, but leaves out the impact to the local communities.
Our state must grow the number of good union jobs, and a community benefits agreement to keep the jobs local and protect the health and safety of surrounding neighborhoods must also be central to negotiations.
People in our community deserve livable wages that don’t require them to accept that their community be poisoned in exchange, whether through increased truck traffic on our roads or contaminants in our water dug up through dredging.
That is possible, and is also why community benefits agreements have to be the foundation for a project of this magnitude.
Good paying union jobs can and should include protections for the very environment that those same union workers live and work in; we should not need to trade our lives for jobs.
Additionally, as AI increases the pace of automation, it is crucial that our state legislature passes forward-thinking AI regulations to protect jobs and ensure that these newly created jobs do not come at the expense of our air, water, and health.
We need forward-thinking AI regulations at the state level.
