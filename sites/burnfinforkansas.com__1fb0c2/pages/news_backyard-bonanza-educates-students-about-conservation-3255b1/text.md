The digital version was erased between changes in ownership, but the archived story can be found through the Finney County Library digital database .
Sixth-grader Kristin Roth, while looking and learning about animal tracks Wednesday during the Party for the Planet Earth Day celebration at the Finnup Center for Conservation Education, said the event to her was all about the animals and the environment.
Kristin, who attends Bernadine Sitts Intermediate Center, said she learned about the value of recycling.
Recycling is really important to our environment, Kristin said, who added that she had attended the Earth Day event before as a younger student.
Learning about recycling, the environment and the earth were the primary topics for 1,200 to 1,300 third- to sixth-grade students Wednesday at Lee Richardson Zoo.
Students from Garden City, Lakin, Scott City and Holcomb attended the Earth Day event.
The Backyard Bonanza -themed event featured displays, activities and demonstrations by zoo staff, volunteers, local and regional exhibitors that taught area students about water, wildlife, recycling, energy efficiency along with games and crafts.
I hope to learn about some more animals, Kristin said.
I get to be with kids and learn about the environment.
Whitney Buchman, curator of education for the Finnup Center for Conservation Education, said students were told what type of trees and plants are ideal to plant in southwest Kansas while showing them techniques for the proper ways to plant seeds.
The students experienced seed bombs, which are clay balls filled with seed and soil, so no planting in the ground is needed.
You just throw it and and let the seeds germinate, Buchman said.
Buchman said the event is in its 26th year.
There were 13 exhibitors, including the Garden City Recycling Center, Garden City Fire Department, Black Hills Energy, Finney County Transit and the Finney County Public Library.
Aaron Gonzalez, a sixth-grader from Bernadine Sitts, said the event taught him about the value of trees and taking care of the environment.
We are learning about the animals and how we can help in cleaning up the Earth, Aaron said.
We are looking at ways on how to (and what we can) recycle.
Julia Barco, also a Bernadine Sitts sixth grade student in Becca Burnfin s class, said events like Earth Day are good ways to learn about the environment to save the Earth.
I think this is a great idea to educate us on doing more for the Earth, Julia said.
Richard Renner, a juggler and clown, traveled from Lawrence to Garden City to show students his Recycle Cycle, described on his website as a crazy art car made of junk and recycled stuff that lights up with action, sound and excitement.
The Recycle Cycle is created entirely from reclaimed or recycled parts, and is intended to be a rolling lesson in the value of the environment.
Bernadine Sitts fifth-graders Gissel Perez and Mia Ortega played the makeshift drums set up on the back of Rennner s cycle, while singing the rock song, We will Rock you.
Both students said that Earth Day was a great event, and Perez said it was fun to bang away on the drums.
Renner said the idea for his cycle is to learn something about recycling and have some fun at the same time.
He told students every item on his cycle was recycled, and items some consider trash are actually valuable resources.
Laughing Matters, starring Jay and Leslie Cady, entertainers from the Kansas City area, presented Juggling the Earth s Natural Resources.
The duo combined juggling and comedy to educate children about the importance of protecting the things we have today so we can have them around for tomorrow.
As Jay juggled items, Leslie snatched them out of the air, and when finished she had filled up a plastic bag of recyclable products that often end up thrown away.
The first Earth Day was held in Washington, D.C., in 1970 to call attention to the declining health of the planet.
The zoo has made Earth Day celebrations an annual tradition since 1990 to help current and future generations learn how to be better stewards of the Earth.
