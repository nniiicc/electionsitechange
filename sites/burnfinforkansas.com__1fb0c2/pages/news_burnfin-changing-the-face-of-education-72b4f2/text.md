Rebecca (Becca) Burnfin was more than a teacher on Monday.
In fact, she's more than a teacher everyday, she's her students' biggest advocate.
On Monday, Burnfin was a registrar for students in her social studies class, helping them through the voting registration process, a preview to a mock-election on Tuesday.
Burnfin walked her students through properly filling out a voting registration form, after which, Burnfin greeted them with hellos and smiles.
I always want them to know that I m going to be their biggest advocate and I think that they really appreciate that, Burnfin said.
Burnfin, who teaches science, social studies, and writing, is one of six finalists for this year's Crystal Apple Teacher Recognition Program.
Burnfin said she really struggled in school until she met a sixth grade teacher.
I knew that's exactly what I wanted to be.
She was so passionate and kind, she said.
From then on, I knew exactly that I wanted to be a sixth grade teacher, specifically.
Burnfin's sisters also are teachers, and when she was young, she always wanted to play school with the other little kids who lived in her cul-de-sac.
The most rewarding part of being an educator for Burnfin is when a student tells her they really like an activity, or they understand what she is teaching them.
Burnfin also considers herself an atypical teacher.
She s not a lecturer, and she doesn't stand in front of the class.
She facilitates through small groups, helping students who need it the most.
For her higher order students, she challenges them.
I work with them typically less, allowing them that time to do big projects to extend their learning, Burnfin said.
I feel like that's a pretty huge thing because it's hard to let go of that control of your classroom.
Burnfin believes in letting her students take control of their learning.
I m very open on what it is they want to learn about.
After all, this is their education, Burnin said.
As long as I can build it into the curriculum.
I don't think I've ever said no and not been able to follow through.
Burnfin, who has been at Bernandine Sitts Intermediate Center for the last four years, said she knows that it's not exactly how everyone sees education, but it's rewarding just having students get excited, laugh or be silly and enjoy their education.
She also uses alternative seating in her classroom.
Chairs and desks are not arranged in the typical classroom setting.
There are casual home chairs and swivel and roller chairs.
There are even exercise balls that are used for seats.
Alternative seating is something she picked up from Model Schools, and the philosophy behind it is students are more engaged if they have choices as to where and how they sit, Burnfin said.
Model Schools is a program whose purpose is to assist districts in their efforts to integrate technology into the curriculum as a tool to improve student achievement.
Burnfin builds a connection with her students about their lives inside and outside of school, as well as their interests.
One way she has connected with her class is by letting them make up their own holidays.
This year her class has Chancla Fridays.
Chancla is Spanish for sandal or house shoe.
On Chancla Fridays, her students wear their sandals or house shoes to class.
I have a paraprofessional in my class and originally, Chancla Friday just started off with sandals, and she let them know that the actual meaning of chanclas is old house shoes, so we got to kind of be culturally informed, Burnfin said.
The kids really enjoy wearing chanclas on a random Friday.
It s something that they created and why stop them from doing something so much fun they did it on their own?
Burnfin also tries to inspire her students to chase their dreams.
She lets them know that she struggled in school herself, but they need to keep trying and pushing to make their dreams come true.
I know that mine are, even though I wasn't from the wealthiest family and I didn't have the most awesome opportunities.
But if they keep trying, I'm always their advocate, she said.
When Burnfin found out she was a Crystal Apple finalist, she cried, which she said she's kind of embarrassed about.
She said it's an honor to be nominated as a young teacher.
I also think it sends a huge message about new teachers who want to change the way that education looks and what the classrooms look like.
I feel like I'm a catalyst for change, Burnfin said.
Even if I don't win, it gives me the thought that what I m doing isn't bad, and what I m doing is changing the face of education and I couldn't ask for anything more.
If she won the Crystal Apple, it would mean her students have worked hard.
The recognition would be more for her students than herself.
I challenge them every day and they challenge me even more, so I really feel like winning would be for them, Burnfin said.
