The Garden City Telegram
The Dog Days of December campaign, intended to spread awareness of abandoned animal needs and raise material donations for the Finney County Humane Society, is the brainchild of two girls whose motto might as well be, "No dog left behind."
The campaign was part of an innovative, student-directed project series introduced by Becca Burnfin, a sixth-grade social studies, science and writing teacher at BSIC.
But what started as a class project quickly became something bigger for Adeleena Unzueta and Yesenia Arteaga, whose love for animals spawned a charity exercise that spread across the school.
On Thursday morning, the students of Burnfin's 10 a.m. class were joined by a couple of special guests: Rocky, a pit bull-bull dog mix, and Blitzen, a Lhasa Apso mix — both of whom are current residents at the local animal shelter.
The kids flocked around their new furry friends, who were more than excited to get a little extra loving at a time of day when the animal shelter is usually closed.
The animals' presence was meant to drive the message home during Adeleena's and Yesenia's presentation.
With about $300 in donations of dog food, cat food, pet beds, towels, toys, shampoo, collars — all things called for in their presentation — and a humorously dilapidated doghouse before them, the girls told their classmates about some alarming statistics.
According to the girls' presentation, which was backed up by FCHS Executive Director Nikki Spanier, approximately 3.9 million dogs are euthanized each year to make room in animal shelters across the nation, while 2.7 million are annually abandoned.
Still, without the animal shelters, the girls explained, stray dogs often starve to death or are run over in the street.
But even though adoption can help rehome dogs that have become strays, the girls emphasized the importance of taking the adoption process seriously.
"Dont take this idea of adopting lightly," Yesenia told the class.
"Adopting a dog isn't just a Christmas present to get and then put away forever just because they are old.
Dogs from shelters are like miracles because they have been through so much, and when you adopt you can help them forget about everything they have been through."
Burnfin modeled the project rubric after a Google initiative called Genius Hour, through which Google employees are allowed to use a portion of their working hours to tackle projects of special interest to them but that also yield benefits to Google's corporate environment.
She said she was introduced to the concept two years ago during a district trip to Florida, where USD 457 teachers reviewed ways to make their classrooms more innovative.
"So the idea into the classroom is if you give students their own time to do something that's their own passion project, that they'll have more buy-in, they'll actually learn things that you never anticipated them to learn, and then they would actually be more connected to the classroom itself," she said, explaining that she introduced the project to her students in August when they spent four weeks researching the meaning of passion, how to find passion in their lives, and how to brainstorm constructive ideas that fit their passions.
Each student group was given a question to consider that couldn't be answered through a simple online search.
Adeleena's and Yesenia's question was two-part: "How can we help abandoned dogs?" And, "How can pet owners take the best care of their animals?"
Those questions prompted the girls to contact Spanier in late October to coordinate a presentation and donation drive with the help of her suggestions.
Putting their best professional foot forward, the girls held a phone conference with Spanier in the classroom in preparation for the event they dubbed Dog Days of December.
The girls worked with the school's student council to put up posters and get the word out for students throughout the school to participate in a campaign that focused on the importance of volunteer efforts at animal shelters, the meaning of animal adoption, and best practices for pet care.
"The idea of the project is to change something, your life, your ideas of what it was before, and in this case bring awareness to something that is really important in our community," Burnfin said.
"I started getting nervous that it was so big," she said of the girls' project.
Spanier said it was nice to see kids taking steps toward helping local abandoned animals, adding that everything at the FCHS comes from donations.
And with Christmas approaching, Spanier reified the girls' assertion that the holidays are not a time for impulsive pet adoption, "because they usually come back a couple weeks later if they're given as Christmas gifts.
We emphasize: Don't adopt for Christmas unless you've thought about it for six months or more."
Just on Wednesday, four dogs were brought to the shelter after their family moved away and left them, Spanier said.
A neighbor fed the dogs for three weeks until it became time to leave town for Christmas.
Now, the dogs live at the animal shelter, which reaches capacity on a weekly basis and transports animals to Denver and Kansas City once a week for adoption purposes.
Blitzen, a loveable blonde mop about 8 years old with wide eyes and a penchant for kisses, was brought to the animal shelter on Tuesday after he was found walking the streets with nobody to claim him.
Rocky, a big 6-year-old lapdog who likes cats more than dogs and isnt self-aware about his size, has been at the shelter since August.
"He just needs a home where someone will be home with him more than a couple hours a day," Spaniard said of Rocky, noting that both animals are up for adoption.
Dog owners Adeleena and Yesenia said the special attention given to their project means a lot, and they have aspirations of volunteering with FCHS.
They say they see a lot of abandoned dogs around town, but "every dog has a chance of being saved."
"I think that what they need to know is that dogs are miracles and they need to help them a lot, and if they have a dog they need to take care of it," Adeleena said.
Yesenia continued, "Because the dogs are there most of their life to do a lot of things.
They're a big part of their life.
It's just like having another family member in your family."
