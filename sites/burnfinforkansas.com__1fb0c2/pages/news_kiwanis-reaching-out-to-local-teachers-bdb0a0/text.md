Meet Becca Issues Photos News Volunteer Yard Signs Contribute News / 🌻Kiwanis reaching out to local teachers 15 Mar Sunday, 12:45 PM · 2015 🌻Kiwanis reaching out to local teachers By Steven Tendo The Garden City Telegram Note : This story was originally printed in The Garden City Telegram in 2015.
The digital version was erased between changes in ownership, but the archived story can be found through the Finney County Library digital database .
A local fraternal organization on Monday provided funds for teacher projects that otherwise might have had to have been paid for out of the teachers pockets.
The Kiwanis Sunrisers Club came to Bernadine Sitts Intermediate Center on Monday to award five teachers at the school with $200 grants to be used for classroom projects.
While the event showcased the teachers projects, the Kiwanis also used it to appeal for more applications from teachers.
There is a lot of money in the organization s coffers, and only a fraction of it is taken in a year, according to Chandra Lay, director of recruitment for the Girl Scouts of Kansas Heartland and a Kiwanis board member.
The Teacher Mini Grant program was started by Kiwanis Club member Mario Lopez in 2010.
The five teachers who received the mini grants included: Tom Rishel, BSIC choir teacher, who will use the money to purchase a portable CD player to go on tour with in the spring; Penny Unruh, who will use her grant to aqcuire bookmarks or Irlene Syndrome overlays; Kathy Caine, whose project is titled, Conquering Common Core ; Becca Burnfin, who will use her money to purchase digital writers; and Michelle Law, whose project is the F.U.N.
Number Line System.
Law, who teaches fifth-grade math, said, F.U.N. stands for fluency and understanding numbers.
It is an open number line and a visual representation for sharing students thinking strategies and developing number sense, said Law, who added, I was excited because this was the first time I ve won one of these.
The school district does grants, but I ve never won any of those.
The Teacher Mini Grant program already awarded money for projects in other schools.
Jennie Wilson Elementary School s projects were place value disks, reading and math center activities and Common Core math plane and solid shapes.
Victor Ornelas Elementary School bought educational apps for iPads; St Mary Catholic School purchased guided reading groups; Plymell Elementary School acquired magnetic erase boards; and Edith Scheuerman Elementary School used its money for a classroom pet project named Buddy the Reader Rabbit.
Ginny Ortiz, a teacher at Georgia Matthews Elementary School, said the $200 her class received will help her fourth-graders, as well as her students in years to come, master mathematical thinking.
Her project, The Why of Mathematics, will make her students better, she said.
We have long taught our students procedures in how to solve math problems, but never the meaning behind it, she said.
Other schools with projects included Alta Brown Elementary School, St.
Dominic Catholic School and Garden City High School.
The other members of the Garden City Sunrisers Kiwanis are Chuck Allen, Bob Baker, Stan Bechtle, Darin Bradstreet, Windy Brown, David Coltrain, Shawna Deal, John Geil, Ralph Germann, Beverly Glass, C.W.
Harper, Dianne Hunsberger, Terry Hunsberger, Belinda Lansink, Don Linville, Ray Mai, Ray Mann, Cliff Mayo, Randall McVey, Dick Monical, Roger Ratliff, Ron Schwartz, Brian Schwindt and Will Votypka.
We would love to get to a point where our funds run dry, but that has not happened Lay said.
This year, only 14 programs have been awarded funds by the club so far.
The Kiwanis want even more schools and teachers to apply for the funds.
There are so many out there, and we have funds to give, Lay said.
We need teachers to know about this money.
The Kiwanis Club raises money through its flag program.
On the holidays of Memorial Day, Flag Day, the Fourth of July, Labor Day and Veterans Day, the Kiwanis go around placing flags on residential front yards.
The funds are not unlimited, but the flag program is sufficient for the Kiwanis to collect enough funds to operate on, Lay said.
Each member has 40 to 50 homes on their route, and there are nine routes.
The residents pay $30 for the flag program for the year.
This year is the 100th anniversary of the Kiwanis movement.
Kiwanis International was founded in 1915 in Detroit, and became an international organization with the creation of the Kiwanis Club of Hamilton, Ontario, the following year.
