The digital version was erased between changes in ownership, but the archived story can be found through the Finney County Library digital database .
Rebecca Burnfin, Dr.
Jennifer Herdman, and Wendi Terpstra are the 2016 Crystal Apple winners.
About 100 people, including friends, family and co-workers of the the six Crystal Apple finalists congregated in the ballroom of the Clarion Inn for the ceremony and banquet.
The three recipients received $1,000 checks, certificates, crystal apples and iPads donated by Cox Communications.
Burnfin, a sixth-grade teacher at Bernadine Sitts Intermediate Center, thanked the Garden City Area Chamber of Commerce, host of the event, and her principal Barb Hauschild for allowing her to be herself in the classroom each and every day.
She also thanked her former and current co-teachers, and her husband, James Levi Burnfin, and her 18-month old son Chase Thomas.
And lastly, she thanked her students.
When I fall short, I know my students are there to pick up the difference, Burnfin said.
Each and every one of you has changed me in some way.
You mean the world to me and have taught me something small or large.
I have adapted my teaching because of you and because you are so forthcoming with your desire of what you want for your education - all too often we don t give you guys enough credit for taking control of your own education and the effort that you give to us - you are truly the reason I am here tonight.
Herdman, a chemistry teacher at Garden City High School, said it was amazing to be recognized in the fashion of the banquet.
She thanked various people, including GCHS principal Steve Nordby for allowing her to implement a new class, material science, at the high school.
She also thanked her students.
My kids are my everything, Herdman said of her students.
They teach me new stuff everyday.
They make my day - it's exhausting, it's challenging.
It's everything I want it to be and so much more.
They keep me coming back.
She also thanked her parents, who attended from Kansas City, for always believing in her.
They definitely instilled education and the importance of it from the beginning, Herdman said while fighting back tears.
As my dad would say, before every soccer game and still does, You just got to go out there and have fun and play hard.
That s what I hope they re (students) doing in the classroom.
Terpstra, an English teacher at GCHS, said she was thankful to receive the Crystal Apple honor.
Though this has been out of my element to be congratulated and recognized, it's been very rewarding, Terpstra said.
She also thanked her husband and kids for helping her over the years.
Grading English papers doesn't happen very quickly, in case you didn't know, and every trip we take, every vacation, I m carting around a big bag of papers and sometimes they get graded and sometimes they don't, Terpstra said.
My kids and husband have been so understanding and I want to thank you guys.
Terpstra said the students make life so much fun.
It s been a joy to be with them every day and to watch them succeed in life, Terpstra said.
Michelle Baier, Holcomb High School; Sarah Drubinskiy, Charles Stones Intermediate Center; and Kimberly Freeland, Abe Hubert Elementary School, the other three Crystal Apple finalists, received $250 checks and certificates.
Lara Bors, USD 457 Board of Education board member and the banquet's presenting sponsor, thanked all of the finalists families for acting as support systems.
Teachers, I hope when you look out tonight, you realize that you are not alone in your classroom.
The community is standing behind you and supports you in all of your endeavors, she said.
While there will only be three winners named tonight, in reality, there are thousands of winners - the students of USD 457 and USD 363.
Shelby McNutt, GCHS journalism and photography teacher and a 1995 Crystal Apple winner who acted as master of ceremonies for the banquet, said the event shines a big spotlight on the best educators in Finney County.
These six ladies have shown dedication, enthusiasm, and professionalism in our classrooms every day, McNutt said.
