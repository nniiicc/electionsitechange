Too often, state leaders promise tax relief while shifting responsibilities onto local governments, leaving cities, counties, and school districts to fill the gaps.
Special education funding provides one of the clearest examples.
Kansas special education funding formula was set into law in 2005 and mandates the state legislature to funding 92% of the excess costs of special education services.
Since 2012, the state legislature has not met that mandate, leaving districts across the state making up the difference, and often that is in the millions of dollars.
Just in Garden City, the state fell short of their mandated 92% funding by $6.67 million in 2024-25.
