For me, 2026 brings a new adventure–running for State Representative.
I’m starting this blog to document the journey, including what I experience, what I learn, and the realities of stepping into the political arena.
Along the way, I’ll also share thoughts on everyday life and the moments that shape it.
I promise to always be real with you.
I hope you’ll follow along and, more importantly, get involved—whether that’s with my campaign or with another candidate or issue you care deeply about.
Our democracy only works when we pay attention, ask questions, and use our voices.
Voting matters.
Speaking up matters.
And together, we can do better.
For my very first blog post, I chose a topic I believe we need now more than ever: hope.
Recent studies from the Wall Street Journal–NORC and the Pew Research Center show that an overwhelming majority of Americans believe their children will not have lives as good as their own.
Many also feel that the idea of hard work leading to success is no longer true.
For too many, the American Dream feels like it’s fading from view.
At the same time, cynicism has become a common coping mechanism, while hope has been reduced to a cliché on cutesy signs.
I believe that hope deserves better than that.
We must consciously cultivate hope—not only for our own health and well-being, but for the sake of future generations.
Optimism is the belief that the future will be better, and I am fortunate to be an optimistic person by nature.
Hope takes it one step further.
Hope is an action verb.
It is the belief that we have the power to help make the future better.
And hope gives us more.
The presence—or absence—of hope is one of the strongest predictors of overall well-being.
Research shows that hope can improve mental health and even physical health.
Simply put, hope helps us thrive rather than just exist.
So, with so much happening in the world that can make us feel hopeless, what do we do?
First, we need to let go of cynicism.
Jumping straight from cynicism to optimism can feel impossible, so let’s aim for skepticism instead.
Skepticism doesn’t assume everything will turn out perfectly, but it also doesn’t decide that all hope is lost.
I’ve had many moments in my life that felt hopeless, yet there was always at least a tiny glimmer of hope in the darkness.
Just as a spark can grow into a roaring fire, that glimmer can lead to brighter days.
We also need to intentionally focus on the positives—both in the world and in our own lives.
It’s often said that people talk far more about negative things than positive ones.
What if we changed that?
What if we shared “positive gossip” instead?
To cultivate hope, we must be able to envision a better future and see a possible path toward it.
That hasn’t always been easy for me.
More than once, the future I envisioned and planned for exploded right in front of my eyes.
Over time, I’ve learned to stay grounded in the present and trust that God will take care of what’s ahead.
That trust has allowed me to hold on to hope for tomorrow.
For me, hope also grows when I focus on others.
As Mr.
Rogers famously said, “Look for the helpers.” Finding the helpers—and striving to be one—restores hope.
I’m also incredibly fortunate to work in an elementary school.
Few things push back negativity like hearing children laugh and watching them play.
If you don’t have children in your life, consider volunteering at a school or in your church nursery.
It’s hard not to smile when you’re surrounded by their unbridled joy.
Finally, we can lean into our faith.
That’s where I’ve leaned the hardest.
In moments when I’ve felt like I was drowning, I’ve felt God’s presence keeping me afloat.
That faith continues to give me hope—even when the waters feel deep.
Hope isn’t something we wait around for—it’s something we practice.
Remember, it’s an action verb.
It shows up in small choices, in how we speak to one another, and in how we show up for our communities.
I don’t have all the answers, but I do know this: giving up on hope isn’t an option.
I believe in people, in community, and in the idea that our best days don’t have to be behind us.
Even when the world feels heavy, hope reminds us that better days are possible—and that our actions matter.
I’m choosing hope as an action; choose it with me.
