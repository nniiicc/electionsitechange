I was taught that the people in our state capitols and Washington, D.C. were there to look out for us.
Over time, I realized the truth–only a few of them are actually doing their job, which is simply to represent their constituents to the best of their ability.
Like many of you, I felt disgusted and disillusioned.
I was busy with my life, and I didn’t think there was anything I could do.
I’m not running for State Representative because I’ve always dreamed of holding office.
I’m doing this because I am tired of watching hardworking people get ignored while political parties play games and line their pockets.
No one is coming to fix what is broken in our communities except us.
So that means we need to work together, we need to communicate, we need to VOTE folks into office that truly care about the communities and people they represent.
I’m not a career politician.
I’m your neighbor.
I know what it feels like to worry about paying the bills—because I’ve lived it.
I know what it’s like to juggle insurance red tape and massive medical bills while trying to find the best care for my husband and son, all while teaching full-time and getting supper on the table every night.
Healthcare is a mess, and we need common-sense solutions that actually help everyday families.
As a mother and a teacher, I worry every single day about safety in our schools.
Not a day goes by that I don’t think about how I’ll keep "my kids" safe in an emergency.
I worry about the survival of our small-town schools when voucher programs siphon money away from public education to fund private schools or pop-up schools built for profit.
Here in England, our public school is the “choice.” Parents should expect and deserve a great education for their children right in their own community.
We should be fortifying public education, not destroying it.
I’m running as an Independent because representatives should answer to their community, not to political parties playing games.
Parties are so polarizing these days, and I don’t care for all the hateful rhetoric when I know we can all agree on so many of the things that matter.
My pledge is simple: People Over Politics.
What that looks like is representation that you can TRUST and a representative who is ACCESSIBLE and actually LISTENS.
It takes vulnerability to put your name on a ballot, but our rural communities are worth fighting for.
