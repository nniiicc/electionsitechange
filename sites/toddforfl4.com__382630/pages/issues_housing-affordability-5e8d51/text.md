Volunteer Contact Home ❭ Issues ❭ Housing Affordability Housing Affordability Housing Affordability: How We Broke It — and How to Fix It The problem is simple: Homes are now nearly 9 times more expensive relative to wages than they were in 1970.
What Happened: 1971: End of the gold standard → fiat money era begins 1986: Tax changes made single-family rentals a favored investment (27.5-year depreciation) → investor class takes over 2008–2012: Foreclosure crisis → institutional buyers lock in the gains Investors openly brag about using depreciation to generate "tax-advantaged free money" with no real cash outlay — phantom deductions that shelter rental income while home prices skyrocket.
The result: Housing became a financial asset for the few instead of a place to live for the many.
My Approach: Close the 1986 depreciation loophole that treats single-family homes as tax-advantaged investments.
If possible through legislation, end the ability of investors to get 30-year mortgages on rental properties — the best small business loan in existence that regular business owners like a muffler shop could only dream of.
We don't need more subsidies that inflate prices further.
We need to attack the root causes, restore balance between shelter and investment, and support local supply and first-time buyers through transparency and competition.
True affordability comes from making housing a home again, not a speculative asset for the few.
