Campaign Finance Reform
The 2024 federal elections cost $14.8 billion nationwide, largely driven by corporations, billionaires, and Super PACs that expect favors in return.
This flood of money stems from two key 2010 decisions: - Citizens United v.
FEC opened the door to unlimited *independent expenditures* by corporations, unions, and others. - SpeechNow.org v.
FEC (a lower court ruling that never reached the Supreme Court) went further by removing contribution limits to independent-expenditure-only groups — directly creating today’s Super PACs.
The result?
A system where a handful of ultra-wealthy donors can drown out everyday voices with massive ad spending, while politicians spend more time dialing for dollars than solving problems for their districts.
Polls consistently show strong bipartisan support for reform: 78% of Americans across party lines want to limit Super PAC money (NYT/CBS).
Yet too often, candidates only offer vague calls to “overturn Citizens United” — a step that would likely require a constitutional amendment and faces steep odds.
A Practical, Achievable Fix:
The Abolish Super PACs Act There’s a smarter, more direct path that works within current law and could force the Supreme Court to reconsider the SpeechNow ruling: The Abolish Super PACs Act (H.R. 2352 / S. 4602) would restore reasonable $5,000 per year contribution limits to Super PACs.
It directly targets the unlimited big-donor funding that SpeechNow enabled, without needing to fully overturn Citizens United.
This is real, targeted legislation — not hand-waving.
If passed and challenged, it creates the clean test case needed to rein in Super PACs.
As your representative, I will cosponsor and aggressively advocate for this bill, along with complementary reforms like stronger disclosure rules and limits on coordination between candidates and outside groups.

Single Issue Bills
One of the sneakiest ways both parties deceive voters is by packing bills with unrelated "pork" to buy votes.
"Must-pass" legislation gets loaded with giveaways, allowing politicians to brag about wins back home while ignoring the core issue.
Aside from the budget, every vote should be a clean up-or-down on a single topic—no more skirting rules with pork-filled continuing resolutions that hide corruption.
Stop the DC Revolving Door
Elected officials from both parties should serve the public, not line up cushy post-office jobs that sway their decisions.
Sweetheart deals—like corporate board seats, lobbying gigs, or media contributor roles—erode public trust and influence policy on everything from healthcare to defense.
This extends to unelected regulators jumping between government and industries they oversee.
Sweeping bipartisan reform is needed to ban these practices and take back our democracy from corporate control.
