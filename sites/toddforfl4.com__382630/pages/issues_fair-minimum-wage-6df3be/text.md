Volunteer Contact Home ❭ Issues ❭ Fair Minimum Wage Fair Minimum Wage America was built on a simple deal: work hard, play by the rules, and you’ll earn enough to support a family with dignity.
That deal is broken.
Today, millions of full-time workers still live in poverty, rely on food stamps, and choose between rent and medicine—while the companies they work for post record profits and get billions in tax breaks.
We subsidize poverty wages with our tax dollars.
That’s not free enterprise.
That’s corporate welfare.
We can fix this.
We can end the rigged game and make hard work pay again—without crushing small towns or ignoring skyrocketing rents in our cities.
The Fair Minimum Wage Act – A Common-Sense Solution One National Floor That Automatically Keeps Up No more waiting 15 years for Congress to act while costs explode.
The federal minimum wage will adjust every year using the same Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA) formula we already trust for Social Security and military retirees.
Inflation goes up → your wage floor goes up.
Automatically.
Predictably.
No politics.
Regional Adjustment That Follows Real Life One size never fit all.
A single national number either crushes rural communities or leaves city workers behind.
Instead, we use the military’s proven Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH) data—the same system that already adjusts pay for service members in Jacksonville versus San Francisco.
We don’t pick a specific rank or family size.
We simply take the published percentage change in local housing costs for each area and apply it to the national baseline.
Your wage floor rises with the real cost of living where you actually live and work.
Rural Mississippi stays affordable.
Miami, Orlando, and Tampa finally catch up to their rents.
Remote Work Rules That Can’t Be Gamed If you work for a New York firm but live in Iowa, common-sense rules (already used in tax and payroll law) ensure you’re not paid poverty wages just because you’re remote.
The higher of your home area or the company’s main location applies—closing loopholes from day one.
No More Taxpayer-Funded Corporate Welfare Any large employer who pays below the regional floor loses special tax breaks—executive pay deductions over $1 million, accelerated depreciation, certain credits.
They can afford to pay their workers or they can afford to pay the government.
Either way, the rest of us stop picking up the tab.
What This Means in Real Life A full-time worker in the Panhandle or rural North Florida finally sees steady, automatic raises that keep pace with real costs.
A server in Jacksonville or a warehouse worker in Orlando gets a floor that actually reflects Florida’s housing market—not Washington’s one-size-fits-all guess.
Small businesses aren’t forced to compete with mega-corporations that offload their payroll onto food stamps and Medicaid.
Taxpayers save billions.
Local economies get a shot in the arm when working families spend real money at local shops instead of sending it to corporate shareholders.
This Isn’t Handouts.
This Is Honest Rules.
We’re not asking corporations to be charitable.
We’re telling them: if you want the roads, ports, courts, and educated workforce the taxpayers provide, then stop making us subsidize your labor costs.
When a full-time job again pays enough to live where you work, and when getting sick doesn’t mean losing everything, people pull themselves up by their bootstraps—because they finally have boots.
That’s not progressive.
That’s not conservative.
That’s American.
