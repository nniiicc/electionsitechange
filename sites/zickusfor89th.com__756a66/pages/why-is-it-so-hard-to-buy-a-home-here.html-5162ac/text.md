You should have a chance to live near the school where you teach, buy a first home in the community where you grew up, or downsize without leaving family behind.
Too many housing choices are out of reach.
I want to change that.
On the Allendale planning commission, I worked on the pressures that neighborhood and university housing place on our township.
Saying “local control” is only a starting point.
We still need to make room for homes people can afford.
Here’s what I’ll work on First, work with residents, communities, builders, and employers to find suitable places for smaller houses, townhomes, apartments, and homes that let older residents stay nearby.
A good proposal should get a clear, timely review.
Local input and sensible standards belong in that process.
Unnecessary delays don’t help a family looking for a home.
Sometimes the obstacle is a road, a utility connection, or the cost of preparing the site.
I’ll work with local officials to examine those costs and the financing tools that could make a project workable.
Then show you the results.
How many homes got built?
What do they cost?
Can working households afford them?
Spending money or approving a plan isn’t enough.
A local example worth learning from Ottawa County approved a financing plan for a proposed 84-unit townhome development on a long-vacant Hudsonville site, including 12 units reserved for income-qualified households.
Officials identified infrastructure costs, site preparation, and earlier zoning restrictions as barriers.
They worked on those specific problems.
That’s the kind of practical work I want to pursue.
The county’s 2025 housing assessment projects a need for 12,526 additional homes for sale and 3,938 rentals by 2030.
It also found that 88% of new for-sale homes permitted since 2021 were priced above $400,000.
We need choices across a much wider range of budgets.
There are other ideas to examine: accessory dwelling units in Massachusetts , more types of homes on residential lots in Oregon , and housing financing in Washington .
I’d judge each by whether it fits our communities and helps people find a home.
Joe Moss’s homepage, reviewed for this site’s October 3 comparison , names local zoning and housing as priorities.
It doesn’t set out comparable steps for increasing attainable housing.
I want you to be able to examine what I’m proposing and hold me to the results.
Roads, water, sewer capacity, and the people living next door belong in the plan from the start.
