Instead, you’re on hold, filling out another form, or calling an office that can’t help.
I live with a rare autoimmune illness.
During COVID, keeping me safe changed everyday life for Charles and our daughters, too.
The whole family felt the isolation.
I want finding help to be easier.
One clear next step.
Someone who can explain your options.
Support for the person doing the caregiving.
Start with the person who needs help A referral is useful only if you can find someone who takes your insurance and has an appointment.
Families need clear information about primary care, specialists, urgent care, and mental health services.
They need to know whom to call when they don’t already have a provider.
I’ll work to bring providers and community services together around those questions.
A useful resource should point you to local counseling, crisis services, substance-use treatment, and disability support, and explain how to reach them.
Calling five offices that can’t help shouldn’t be the price of getting started.
The caregiver needs care, too Appointments.
Medications.
Meals.
Transportation.
Paperwork.
Those jobs can gradually fill a family’s day.
I want caregivers to be able to find respite care, home-health services, transportation, and support for themselves.
Families should be able to learn about elder-care options before a fall or hospital stay makes every decision urgent.
That includes understanding the differences between home care, assisted living, skilled nursing, rehabilitation, and hospice, and getting qualified help with coverage and planning.
Medical, legal, and financial decisions need the right professional.
Finding that person should be easier.
Help families find their way through A prescription can create another round of calls about coverage, prior authorization, or cost.
A young person with a disability may need help moving from school-based services into adult programs.
These are moments when a family needs coordination, rather than another handoff.
I’ll work to help residents find trusted information in one place, with clear routes to care, benefits help, transportation, prescription assistance, and caregiver support.
We should ask whether people reached the service they needed and how long it took.
You’ve already got enough to do.
