For years, the traditional idea of success after high school was pretty simple: graduate, go to college, get a job.
But that path does not fit every student, and it does not fit the workforce needs of West Michigan anymore.
Ottawa County schools are increasingly moving toward a broader idea: every student should graduate with a realistic next step.
That might mean a four-year university.
It might mean community college.
It might mean a skilled trade.
It might mean an apprenticeship, an industry certification, or going directly into a career with a local employer.
The important part is that students have choices.
Career and Technical Education Is Growing Ottawa Area Intermediate School District has been expanding Career and Technical Education because demand has been growing faster than available space.
Careerline Tech Center has had significant student waitlists, and OAISD says its expansion is designed to add nine new programs and increase enrollment capacity by roughly 30%.
A new Health Sciences campus opened in 2024, a Fire Academy opened in fall 2025, and HVAC-R is scheduled to open in fall 2026.
That matters because these are not simply elective classes.
CTE students can earn industry-recognized certifications, college credits, and hands-on experience while they are still in high school.
For some students, that can mean graduating with a much clearer path into healthcare, construction, advanced manufacturing, public safety, automotive work, technology, or other high-demand careers.
More Learning Should Happen Outside the Classroom One of the most promising changes is the expansion of work-based learning.
During the 2023–24 school year, more than 1,000 Careerline Tech Center students participated in work-based learning through partnerships with local employers.
Those opportunities include internships, apprenticeships, mentorships, and job-based training.
That kind of experience can answer an important question before a student graduates: Do I actually want to do this for a living?
It also lets students develop skills that are difficult to teach entirely in a classroom: reliability, communication, teamwork, problem-solving, and working with customers or supervisors.
College Should Be One Path, Not the Only Path A stronger school system does not mean discouraging college.
It means making college one of several good options.
Ottawa Area ISD's Early College program allows students to extend high school for an additional year while earning both a high school diploma and an associate degree through Muskegon Community College.
Required college costs and textbooks are covered for students in the program.
At the same time, CTE students can earn college credits and industry credentials without necessarily pursuing a traditional four-year degree immediately after graduation.
The better question for students becomes: What education or training do I need for the life I want?
Career Exploration Should Start Earlier Students should not have to reach senior year before they start thinking seriously about careers.
OAISD has been expanding career exploration into younger grades through programs such as futurePREP'd and Career Camp.
The futurePREP'd initiative uses project-based learning and partnerships with businesses and community organizations to give students experience solving real-world problems.
That matters because career exploration is often most useful before students have already made major decisions about classes, college, or training.
Schools and Employers Need to Talk to Each Other Ottawa County has a strong economic base in manufacturing, healthcare, construction, agriculture, skilled trades, technology, and small business.
Schools should know what those employers actually need.
OAISD works with local employers to keep CTE programs aligned with changing industry needs and uses labor-market information to help guide career counseling and program development.
That relationship benefits both sides.
Students get training connected to real jobs.
Employers get access to workers who already have useful skills.
And families get another reason for young adults to stay in West Michigan instead of feeling that they have to move somewhere else to build a career.
Special Education Students Need Clear Paths Too Preparing students for life after school also has to include students with disabilities.
OAISD provides transition services and Young Adult Services programs designed to help students move from school into employment, independent living, further education, and community participation.
A successful school system should be measured by whether every student is moving toward a more independent and meaningful future.
Teachers Need Support for This to Work Expanding career pathways cannot simply mean adding more responsibilities to teachers.
Educators need training, planning time, modern equipment, partnerships with employers, and access to people who understand rapidly changing industries.
The goal should not be to turn every classroom into vocational training.
It should be to help students understand why what they are learning matters.
What Should Come Next?
Ottawa County already has much of the foundation in place.
The next step is making those opportunities easier for every family to understand and access.
Expand CTE capacity until long waitlists become the exception.
Create more apprenticeships and paid work-based learning opportunities.
Make dual enrollment and Early College easier to navigate.
Introduce career exploration earlier.
Help students earn useful credentials before graduation.
Strengthen partnerships between schools, colleges, unions, skilled trades, and local employers.
Give parents clearer information about earnings and education requirements associated with different career paths.
Continue strong support for special education and transition services.
Every Student Should Leave High School Knowing What Comes Next Not every student needs the same future.
They need a good path to their own future.
Ottawa County schools are already moving in that direction.
The opportunity now is to make sure every student can take advantage of it.
