Affordability Wages for Working Oregonians A Corporate Public Cost Recovery Plan A single adult working full time in Oregon needs $26.46 an hour to cover basic needs without public assistance, and Oregon's minimum wage falls short of that figure by as much as $12 an hour in nonurban counties.
Large, profitable employers routinely pay wages low enough that their own workers must turn to SNAP and the Oregon Health Plan to get by, shifting labor costs onto Oregon taxpayers.
This Plan Will Make Large Employers Pay for the Cost They Push Onto Taxpayers: • Impose a Wage Gap Public Cost Recovery Surcharge on every Oregon employer with 50 or more employees. • Calculate the surcharge continuously from 0% at the living wage to 100% at the state minimum wage, so every raise lowers an employer's bill. • Base the surcharge entirely on wage and hours data already on file with the state, never on employee enrollment in Medicaid or SNAP. • Exempt employers with fewer than 50 employees entirely. • Phase the surcharge in at 50% for employers with 50 to 249 employees for two years before rising to full rate. • Apply the full rate immediately to employers with 250 or more employees. • Recalibrate the surcharge every year against updated living wage and public assistance cost data.
Funding Public Programs Through Employer Accountability Modeling projects the surcharge would recover between $700 million and $3.4 billion a year, funding that flows directly into the public programs large employers' wages currently strain.
This Plan Will Direct New Revenue to the Programs Low Wages Strain: • Direct 50% of surcharge revenue to the Oregon Health Plan to offset the costs low wages generate. • Direct 30% of surcharge revenue to SNAP administration and emergency food assistance programs. • Direct 15% of surcharge revenue to wage support and workforce training grants for low-wage and displaced workers. • Reserve 5% for state agency administration of the program. • Publish every covered employer's headcount, share of workforce paid below the living wage, and total surcharge paid by name each year. • Impose civil penalties of up to three times the underpaid surcharge for late or false wage band reporting.
Implementation Pathway This Plan Will Roll Out Over Four Years: • Secure legislative authorization and complete agency rulemaking in Year 1. • Formally adopt the Public Cost Proxy and Living Wage benchmark by rule in Year 1. • Begin phase-in surcharge collection for the 50-249 employee tier in Years 2 and 3. • Begin full-rate collection for employers with 250 or more employees and publish the first annual transparency report in Years 2 and 3. • Apply the full rate across all covered employer tiers and conduct the first statutory review of the Policy Intensity Multiplier in Year 4.

Healthcare for Working Oregonians A Universal Public Option Plan Oregon faces a structural healthcare affordability crisis.
Even with the Oregon Health Plan (OHP) and ACA exchanges, tens of thousands of Oregonians remain uninsured, underinsured, or financially strained by rising premiums, deductibles, and network instability.
This plan expands the Oregon Health Plan into a universal public option available to every Oregon resident, guaranteeing free coverage for lower and middle income families while capping premiums for higher earners at affordable levels.
This Plan Will Guarantee Affordable Coverage for Every Oregonian: • Expand eligibility for the Oregon Health Plan to all Oregon residents, regardless of income, employment status, or preexisting conditions. • Guarantee comprehensive coverage modeled on existing OHP benefits, with no deductibles for core services. • Simplify enrollment by building automatic eligibility through OHP's existing infrastructure. • Cap premiums on a sliding income scale, charging $0 a month to residents below the median income. • Limit the maximum monthly premium to $350 for an individual and $500 for a family of four. • Preserve private insurance as a choice, so no Oregonian is forced off their existing plan.
Financing the Plan Without Burdening Working Families Financing relies on targeted revenue sources designed to correct market failures rather than burden working families or small businesses.
Strengthening Oregon's Economy and Health System Decoupling healthcare from employment status strengthens both the workforce and the health system that serves it.
This Plan Will Strengthen Oregon's Economy by: • Building on Oregon's existing Medicaid infrastructure rather than the cost and risk of a new bureaucracy. • Reducing administrative waste, since public plans typically operate with lower overhead than private insurers. • Encouraging entrepreneurship and small business formation by reducing job lock tied to employer health coverage. • Delivering immediate premium relief, reduced medical debt, and increased disposable income for working families. • Stabilizing rural hospital systems through guaranteed coverage pools and more predictable provider reimbursement.
Implementation This Plan Will Be Phased In Responsibly: • Secure legislative authorization and align necessary federal waivers in Year 1. • Scale OHP's administrative capacity to prepare for expanded eligibility in Year 1. • Phase in eligibility expansion, premium scaling, and revenue mechanisms over Years 2 and 3. • Achieve universal eligibility and stabilize the program by Year 4.

The proposal does not interfere with federal foreign policy authority.

This Plan Will Keep Oregon's Ports Out of Active Conflict Zones: • Appoint a Port of Portland Commission majority committed to ethical export oversight. • Adopt a Port rule prohibiting the shipment of military grade weapons, ammunition, explosives, and military only weapons components. • Restrict that prohibition to nations actively engaged in sustained hostile military operations with credible international findings of humanitarian law violations. • Base every designation on documented findings from established bodies, such as international courts, United Nations agencies, or recognized human rights institutions. • Establish an independent advisory review panel of international law experts to guide determinations. • Conduct periodic reassessments of designated conflict zones rather than relying on one-time findings.

DARK MONEY STOMP OUT DARK MONEY IN OREGON ELECTIONS BE IT ENACTED BY THE PEOPLE OF THE STATE OF OREGON: Article XI of the Constitution of the State of Oregon is amended by adding a new section 16 that reads: Section 16.
Oregon Corporate Powers and Privileges.
(1) Findings and Purpose.
The people of the State of Oregon find and declare that: (a) All political power is inherent in the people.
Corporations and other artificial persons are creations of law that exist only by virtue of powers the State affirmatively grants, and their legal status and privileges are conditional grants held subject to the State's reserved authority to define, limit, revise, or withdraw them.
An artificial person has no vested right to the continuation of any grant of power. (b) Oregon's previous broad grants of corporate powers have been construed to include political spending power.
This section redefines those grants and makes their contents explicit, so that the privileges the people confer are not used to compromise or corrupt the integrity of the people's political process. (c) The State's law on corporate powers shall not grant political spending power or election voting power to artificial persons.
That absence is an attribute of the artificial person, not a rule of place; no artificial person, wherever organized, holds political spending power or election voting power within this State.
This section leaves the constitutional rights of natural persons wholly undisturbed. (d) This section shall be broadly construed in favor of democratic self-government and the rights of natural persons.
(2) Powers of Artificial Persons. (a) An artificial person organized under the laws of this State exists only by authority of the State and has no powers or privileges except those authorized by this Constitution or by statute enacted consistent with this section.
An artificial person's organizational documents may further limit the exercise of its powers. (b) Every artificial person possesses only artificial person powers and possesses no power to exercise or purport to exercise political spending power or election voting power, except as provided in paragraph (c) of this subsection.
On the effective date of this section, all powers previously granted by the laws of this State to any artificial person are revoked in their entirety and replaced by the artificial person powers granted under this subsection.
The revocation and grant take effect simultaneously, so that no artificial person is without powers at any time.
That absence of political spending power is not confined to acts within this State; it is an attribute of the artificial person wherever the artificial person acts. (c) Notwithstanding paragraph (b) of this subsection, political spending power may be exercised by a political committee registered under Oregon law or federal law, provided that the political committee exists and operates solely for that purpose, claims no state-conferred privileges other than limited liability and particularized tax treatment, and is subject to the reporting, disclosure and disclaimer requirements applicable to such political committee under Oregon and/or federal laws. (d) Within this State, no artificial person, wherever organized, may exercise or purport to exercise political spending power or election voting power, except as provided in paragraph (c) of this subsection. (e) Nothing in this section defines, limits, withdraws, or otherwise affects the capacity of an artificial person organized under the laws of another jurisdiction to exercise political spending power or election voting power directed at elections involving candidates or ballot measures of a jurisdiction other than this State.
As to such an artificial person, this section governs only its powers and conduct within this State. (f) Nothing in this section, solely by reason of the revocation and grant of powers under paragraph (b) of this subsection, may be construed to impair the continued existence, legal status, or state-conferred privileges of an artificial person; to affect its ability to initiate, defend, or participate in legal proceedings or to take any action necessary to wind up and liquidate its business and affairs; to affect any license, permit, approval, registration, or authorization otherwise held by the artificial person; or to invalidate, impair, or modify any contract, debt instrument, security, or other legal obligation lawfully entered into before the effective date of this section, except any such obligation that purports to authorize political spending on or after the effective date.
(3) Consequences and Enforcement. (a) An artificial person that exercises or purports to exercise political spending power in violation of this section: (A) Acts ultra vires, and the act is void from the time it occurs, may not be ratified, validated, or given effect by consent, waiver, estoppel, reliance, course of dealing, or any other doctrine, and creates no enforceable rights, obligations, or defenses; (B) Forfeits all state-conferred privileges as a matter of law, including limited liability, perpetual duration, succession in organizational name, and any limitation on personal liability; and (C) Shall forfeit to the State an amount equal to the funds expended, contributed, transferred, or disbursed in that exercise or purported exercise of political spending power, valuing any thing of value other than money at its fair market value. (b) Any violation of this section is subject to civil action by any member or shareholder of the artificial person, by any Oregon elector, and by the Attorney General.
A civil action may obtain declaratory relief, injunctive relief, forfeiture, and confirmation and enforcement of the termination of state-conferred privileges.
Courts shall recognize that successful enforcement, undertaken without pecuniary gain particular to the plaintiff, constitutes a substantial public benefit. (c) An artificial person whose state-conferred privileges are terminated may be reinstated only pursuant to procedures established by law and only upon full forfeiture under paragraph (a)(C) of this subsection and certification of future compliance with this section. (d) For an artificial person organized under the laws of another jurisdiction, termination under this section reaches only the authority, legal status, state-conferred privileges, and limitations on liability conferred by the laws of this State.
This section does not alter the law of another jurisdiction or the formation, existence, or status of an artificial person under that law. (e) The Legislative Assembly shall, during its first regular session following the effective date of this section, enact procedures for reinstating terminated privileges to artificial persons that forfeited in accordance with the forfeiture provisions of this subsection and complied with the ordered equitable relief.
(4) Conflicts, Construction, and Severability. (a) The provisions of this section supersede any conflicting provision of this Constitution. (b) No provision of this State's corporation or entity statutes may be construed to grant political spending power to an artificial person except as provided in paragraph (c) of subsection (2) of this section.
Any grant of power, authority, or capacity that could otherwise be construed to include political spending power is superseded by this section and shall be given no effect. (c) If any provision of this section or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the invalidity does not affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect without the invalid provision or application.
Each section, subsection, paragraph, and subdivision is severable, individually or in any combination. (d) A power, privilege, or capacity withdrawn or not granted by this section may not be revived, reinstated, or implied by operation of law or judicial construction. (e) If a holding that any provision of this section is invalid would otherwise have the effect of conferring political spending power on an artificial person, it is the preference of the people that the artificial person instead possess no powers at all rather than acquire political spending power.
(5) Definitions.
As used in this section: (a) "Artificial person" means every entity or legal arrangement whose existence, legal status, collective capacity, power to sue, or limited liability is conferred by the laws of this State, including, without limitation: (A) Business corporations; (B) Nonprofit corporations; (C) Limited liability companies; (D) Unincorporated associations, limited-liability partnerships, statutory trusts, professional corporations, cooperatives, and any successor form; and (E) Foreign entities that are authorized to transact business, are otherwise transacting business, or hold property in Oregon.
Any entity or legal arrangement organized under the laws of another jurisdiction that directly or indirectly undertakes, finances, or directs acts constituting political spending power in this State is conclusively deemed to be transacting business in this State for purposes of jurisdiction and enforcement.
(F) The term does not include the State, any agency, authority, instrumentality, or political subdivision of the State, or any wholly publicly-owned and controlled public body corporate and politic. (b) "Artificial person powers" means the powers necessary or convenient for an artificial person to carry out lawful business, charitable, cooperative, or organizational purposes under the laws of this State, excluding political spending power and election voting power.
Neither political spending power nor election voting power may be deemed necessary or convenient under any circumstances. (c) "Election voting power" means the legal capacity to vote in a government election.
The term does not include voting in the internal governance of an artificial person by its shareholders, members, partners, directors, managers, or trustees. (d) "Foreign entity" means an artificial person organized under the laws of any jurisdiction other than the State of Oregon. (e) "Funds" includes money or any thing of value. (f) “Political spending power” means the legal capacity to, directly or indirectly, pay, contribute, expend, transfer, or disburse money or anything of value to support or oppose: (A) a candidate, political party, or political committee, including independent expenditures; or (B) an initiative, referendum, recall, referral, or other question for the ballot that has been approved, authorized or otherwise made eligible for circulation for signatures or has been qualified, certified, or otherwise approved for presentation to electors on a ballot of any jurisdiction. (g) "Political spending power" does not include the legal capacity to: (A) Gather, prepare, produce, publish, broadcast, host, disseminate, or distribute any news story, commentary, or editorial through the facilities of a broadcasting station or of any print, online, or digital newspaper, magazine, blog, or other periodical publication, unless the broadcasting, print, online, or digital facility is owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by a candidate, a political committee, a political party or its subunit at any level, or any combination of such persons or entities; (B) Endorse a candidate or ballot measure or petition; (C) Use resources to communicate an endorsement to members, shareholders, employees, or officers; (D) Use de minimis resources to communicate an endorsement to the general public, provided that, in the case of a candidate endorsement, the public communication of the endorsement is not coordinated with the candidate, the candidate's committee, or their agents; (E) Communicate with members, shareholders, employees, or officers regarding supporting or opposing a candidate, political party, political committee, ballot measure or petition for a ballot measure, provided that: (i) The communication is not distributed, republished, promoted, or otherwise made available to the general public; and (ii) A "member" is an individual or entity that has affirmatively taken action to join the organization; or (F) Use resources for the establishment and administration of a political committee affiliated with the organization or for soliciting contributions to the political committee from members, shareholders, employees, or officers of the organization. (h) "State-conferred privilege" means any benefit that exists only because the State confers it on an artificial person, whether by Constitution, statute, rule, or otherwise, including, without limitation, limited liability, perpetual duration, succession in organizational name, and tax treatment.
