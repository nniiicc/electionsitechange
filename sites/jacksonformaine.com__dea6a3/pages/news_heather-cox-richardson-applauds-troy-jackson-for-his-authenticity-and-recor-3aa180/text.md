PORTLAND, ME — Fifth-generation logger, former Maine Senate President, and Democratic nominee for U.S.
Senate Troy Jackson joined Mainer and historian Heather Cox Richardson for a Community Conversation in Portland earlier this week.
Mainers travelled from across the state to hear the conversation about Jackson’s campaign and what’s at stake in the Maine Senate race.

The pair spoke about Jackson’s record as former president of the Maine Senate and his policy stances, notably his support for Medicare for All, universal childcare and an end to the Iran War.
Healthcare in particular was top of mind for Jackson, a former logger from Allagash, because his son’s partner recently gave birth to his first granddaughter.

Richardson encouraged voters to cast their ballots for Troy Jackson and asked him about his experience building coalitions in Augusta.
Jackson also said his top priorities if elected would be passing Medicare for All, universal childcare, and lowering the cost of prescription drugs.
He also talked about ensuring Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program benefits are funded.
"I think you pay for it by where we put our money.

Jackson also talked about U.S.-Canada relations, and slammed President Donald Trump's ongoing trade disputes with the country that Maine's economy relies heavily on.
"I have yet to meet anyone who thinks that blanket tariffs on Canada are a good idea.
I think people may argue that one or two things should change, but putting tariffs on Canada unilaterally is idiotic," Jackson said.

During the event, Richardson asked Jackson about his blue collar background and his motivation to fight for the working class.
