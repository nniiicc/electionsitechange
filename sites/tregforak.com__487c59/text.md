Governor Built not bought Meet Treg Raised by a single mother, Treg learned early the value of hard work, responsibility, and solving problems when there was no easy answer.
He started working young – delivering newspapers, detasseling corn, and working agricultural jobs, and later worked nights while putting himself through college.
He attended the U.S.
Air Force Academy before earning his bachelor’s degree in political science and law degree from Brigham Young University.
After law school, Treg came to Alaska and began building a legal career that spanned both the private and public sectors.
He worked in commercial litigation and later served as counsel for McKinley Capital Management and senior counsel for ASRC Energy Services.
In 2018, he joined the Alaska Department of Law as Deputy Attorney General overseeing the Civil Division.
He became Alaska’s Attorney General in 2021 and served in that position until August 2025.
Treg and his wife, Jodi, have also built their life in Alaska.
They are small-business owners and have raised their six children in Anchorage.
That experience has given Treg a personal connection to many of the issues Alaska families face, from education and public safety to the cost of living and creating opportunities that allow the next generation to stay and build lives of their own in Alaska.
As Attorney General, Treg’s work frequently centered on building partnerships and using Alaska’s Department of Law to address statewide and national issues.
The department participated in more than 80 legal challenges involving federal actions during his tenure.
He also served as chairman of the Attorney General Alliance, a bipartisan organization representing attorneys general from states and territories across the country.
As chairman, he launched a cybersecurity initiative focused on consumer protection, critical infrastructure, data privacy, artificial intelligence, and emerging digital threats.
His emphasis on collaboration extended to other areas of the department.
During his tenure, the Department of Law formalized a more accessible process for Alaskans to request investigative grand juries and expanded its engagement with Alaska Tribes through dedicated Civil and Criminal Division tribal liaisons.
Now, Treg is running for governor because he believes Alaska’s best days are still ahead.
He and Jodi have built their family, their business, and their lives here, and they want their children and every Alaskan family to have the opportunity to do the same.
For Treg, that means building safer communities, stronger schools, a growing economy, responsible resource development, and a government that works better for the people it serves.
His focus is not simply on what Alaska needs to push back against, but on what Alaskans can build together for the next generation.

Meet Candi Candi learned early that the circumstances you are given do not have to determine what you build with them.
She survived polio as a child, spending time in an iron lung and leg braces.
With the encouragement of a mother who refused to let others define what her daughter could accomplish, Candi went to school, grew independent, and developed the persistence that would shape the rest of her life.
She built a career in the energy industry, spending roughly two decades with ARCO Alaska and working on the North Slope.
What began in an administrative role grew into work that relied on Candi’s ability to understand the details, solve problems, and help teams make consequential decisions.
Then she built something of her own.
In 2007, Candi founded Northern Solutions.
What began as a small workforce-solutions business grew into an Alaska company providing engineering, machining, manufacturing, specialized labor, and oilfield support.
Northern Solutions now operates facilities in Anchorage and Deadhorse, and its team has developed patented technology for drilling, workover, and intervention applications.
But Candi’s story is about more than building a company.
She built a family while building a career.
She created jobs, trained and mentored young Alaskans, gave talented people opportunities to put their skills to work, and encouraged employees to bring new ideas to the table.
One of those ideas ultimately grew into patented technology, the kind of Alaska ingenuity and grit that comes from giving people the opportunity to create, solve problems, and take ownership of their work.
Through every stage of her life, Candi has approached challenges much the same way: understand the problem, bring good people together, and build something that works.
Candi has spent her life building in Alaska.
Her next chapter is about helping build Alaska’s future.
