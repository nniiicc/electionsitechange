Heads up: I’m a write-in for the GENERAL election on November 3.
The July primary is over — this is the one that counts.
Early ballots go out October 7 , so if you vote early, this needs to be on your radar before then.
You don’t have to take my word for any of it.
Arizona’s Secretary of State publishes the official list of write-in candidates for this election: see the state’s own list here .
I’m on it.
On your ballot, write: MICHAEL DORLAND and fill in the oval next to it.
Find it Find the race for U.S.
Representative — District 6 on your ballot.
Write it Find the write-in line at the bottom of that race and write in Michael Dorland.
Fill it Fill in the oval next to the write-in line.
This part is not optional.
The Warning ⚠️ Write the name AND fill the oval.
Both.
A write-in name with no filled oval doesn't get counted — that's the law, and it's the most common mistake.
One more thing: in that race, only mark the write-in.
If you fill in more ovals than allowed, it's an "overvote" and the whole thing gets thrown out.
So write my name, fill my oval, and leave the printed names alone.
Does a write-in vote even count?
Yes — because I filed the paperwork to make it count.
Arizona only counts write-in votes for candidates who've officially registered as write-ins.
I did.
Your county posts the list of official write-in candidates online and at every polling place — my name's on it.
Votes for unregistered write-ins (your dog, a sandwich, your favorite fictional senator) don't count.
Mine does.
Write it in correctly and it's a real, counted vote.
When & How (covers mail and in-person).
However you vote, it works the same.
Most Arizonans vote by mail — ballots go out in October, the return envelope is postage-paid, and you can mail it or drop it at any drop box or voting location.
If you vote early or in person, you'll fill in the oval the same way.
The write-in line is on every version of the ballot.
