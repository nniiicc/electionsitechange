Read the whole messy résumé → I’m running with no party affiliation.
Not because I think I’m above it, but because I don’t want to owe anyone a vote before I’ve read the bill.
Independents don’t get a primary, a donor list, or a team telling them how to vote.
That’s the point.
If I get this wrong, it’s on me.
If I get it right, it’s also on me.
Either way, you’ll know exactly who to hold responsible.
And I’ll be straight with you about the odds: write-in candidates don’t usually win.
I’m not doing this because I think I’m going to.
I’m doing it because somebody should show that you still can — that a person can put their own name down, say what they actually think, and not need a party or a donor’s permission to do it.
If that talks one other person into running for something, anything, that’s the whole return.
Winning is frosting.
Inspiring is the cake.

Honesty (Even When It Costs Me) I will tell you the truth, even when a comfortable lie would be easier for me.
That doesn't mean cruelty; you lead with kindness, then you tell the truth.
But you don't get a representative who lies (even by omission; I hate those types of lies the worst) because the honest answer polls badly.
Don't Make Things Worse Things are hard, and the last decade proves they can always get worse.
The first job of anyone handed power is to not accelerate the slide.
I'm not promising to fix everything.
I'm promising not to "make it worse," and you'd be surprised how rare that promise is.
Critical Thinking/Common Sense Critical thinking and common sense aren't two separate things; they're the same thing from both ends.
Logical reasoning with no common sense argues its way into nonsense; common sense with no thinking is just "everybody knows" and ignores years of dedicated investigation.
You need both to have a functioning society.
Example: a ceasefire that doesn't stop the bombing isn't a ceasefire.
I'll think things through using both.
Caring Being human is hard enough as it is.
Going out of your way to make other people's lives worse is just petty, and it's one thing nearly every ethical and religious tradition on earth pretty much agrees isn't cool.
Decency isn't a policy position.
It's the floor.

Why You Might Pick Me (No Pressure) Someone asked me what makes me uniquely qualified for this job.
Here's my honest answer: I'm not.
I think I'm at least qualified — but not uniquely.
I'm certain there are people all over District 6 who are just as qualified as me, and plenty who are more.
They just aren't running.
And honestly?
I hope a platform of honesty, common sense, and critical thinking isn't unique.
Because if caring about those things makes me stand out — that just makes the whole thing a little sadder.
So no, I won't tell you I'm the only one who can do this.
I'll just tell you I'm willing to, I'll be honest about it, and I'll own it when I get it wrong." Michael Dorland - with help from people who wanted to make sure I didn't oversell it Please Don't Donate (We Mean It) We’re not taking donations.
On purpose.
Federal rules say the moment a campaign raises or spends more than $5000, it has to register with the FEC and start filing paperwork.
We’d rather spend that energy talking to you than talking to accountants.
So this campaign runs on roughly nothing — a website, some streams, a few park benches, and a penguin.
If you want to help, it’s free: tell one person how to write me in.
That’s worth more than money to a campaign like this anyway.
