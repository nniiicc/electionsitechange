Dear friends and neighbors,
As the people who have always had my back and supported me, I want you to know first that I’m running for re-election.
When I first ran in 2018, my goal, aside from getting elected, was to represent Avon and Canton with enthusiasm, an ear for listening to voters, and fresh ideas for how to move Connecticut forward.
I was also deeply concerned about a presidency that seemed to be headed away from deeply-rooted American values: justice, freedom, and taking care of one another.
Those concerns are greater now than they ever have been, and I won’t abandon the job or the causes we care about most.
My service over the last five years has focused on what my constituents told me they needed.
That’s why the first bill I passed returned millions of taxpayer dollars to seniors by exempting most of their individual retirement accounts from personal income tax.
I followed that up with a bill to help people with disabilities gain meaningful work.
And I brought over a million dollars back to the district, including money for Canton’s Rails to Trails and Avon’s town-wide broadband project.
In my second term, as I listened to the needs of my constituents, I wrote and passed the Purple Alert.
Several of my constituents experienced what it was like to have an adult child with IDD go missing and found out there was no alert tailored to their needs.
Though I was initially told that we didn’t need another alert, in the first two weeks it went live, more than a dozen purple alerts were issued.
I helped Avon get money for updated softball fields and Canton’s Town Hall Auditorium to have an HVAC system that worked.
In my third term, I successfully championed a bill to finally start funding our statewide food bank at a level comparable to our neighboring states.
I successfully passed an affordable housing bill (eventually), which will provide housing opportunities for seniors who want to downsize, young families who want to buy their first home, and an opportunity for our teachers, police officers, and firefighters to live in the communities they serve.
I also brought back even more money to fix the pool liner at Mills Pond Park, and money to update Avon’s Department of Public Works breakroom for our amazing public workers.
Perhaps, most importantly, I advocated we put aside $500 million to address the cuts the Trump Administration enacted, which are harming people here in Connecticut.
We must protect our schools, our unhoused population, our hungry neighbors, and people who get their health insurance from our healthcare exchange.
In solidarity,
Eleni
