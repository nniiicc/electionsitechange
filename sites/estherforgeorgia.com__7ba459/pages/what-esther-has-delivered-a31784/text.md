Sponsored by Esther and signed into law in May 2026, HB 1283 brings a proven, life-saving model to Georgia.
Developed alongside advocates Jill Hollander, Julianna Peterson, and Fulton County DA Fani Willis, this legislation directly addresses the systemic gaps Esther witnessed during her 30 years representing abuse survivors.
Historically, survivors have been forced to navigate a traumatizing maze of separate agencies.
Family Justice Centers (FJCs) consolidate critical services into a single location:
- Co-Located Services: Law enforcement, prosecutors, victim advocates, and civil legal aid work together.
- Minimizing Trauma: Survivors of sexual assault, trafficking, and child abuse can access medical exams and resources in a quiet, safe space without retelling their stories to dozens of strangers.
- The 10-Year Kit Extension: Thanks to an amendment by Rep.
Karen Lupton, the preservation period for sexual assault evidence kits is extended from 12 months to 10 years for survivors not yet ready to report.
Trauma doesn't run on a police timeline, and this gives survivors the space they need to heal.
- Protecting Law Responders: Domestic violence calls are highly volatile and dangerous for officers.
By offering survivors a safe path to plan their exit during the day, FJCs help de-escalate crises before they turn into dangerous midnight 911 calls.
The Bottom Line: This model saves the lives of survivors and police officers alike, protecting families on both sides of the badge.
