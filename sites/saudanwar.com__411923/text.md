Beyond the Senate
The person behind the votes.
Long before he was anyone's senator, Saud was the doctor who showed up - in the clinic, at ground zero, in disaster zones, and across the lines that divide us.
The same steady hands, the same open heart.
That's who casts the votes.
For 20 years, Saud has chaired the Department of Internal Medicine at Manchester Memorial and Rockville General Hospital.
He still cares for the very neighbors he represents - so he feels our community's struggles the way a doctor does, and he keeps seeing patients and helping people through their health challenges.
In the country's darkest hours after September 11, Saud showed up to protect and fight for us - helping organize the volunteer medical response, and later testifying before Congress on keeping Americans safe.
The American Red Cross recognized the work; for him, it was simply what a doctor does.
When COVID overwhelmed hospitals and ventilators ran out, Saud helped design a simple splitter that let a single machine breathe for as many as seven patients.
He didn't patent it or sell it - he gave it to the world for free, and it reached roughly 100 countries.
He never chased recognition, but it found him anyway - the FBI Director's award for community leadership, the American College of Physicians' Legislator of the Year, and the Anti-Defamation League's Torch of Liberty Award.
