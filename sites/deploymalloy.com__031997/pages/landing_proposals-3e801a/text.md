Here are a few proposals for a way forward for a better Vermont.
Answers to the many questions I get asked Help first time homebuyers Propose up to $25,000 in federal down-payment assistance for qualifying first-time buyers, paid at closing.
Set income and home-price limits, require the home to be a primary residence, and require repayment for an early sale, with hardship exceptions.
Pair assistance with housing rehabilitation and infrastructure investments to increase available homes.
Repair infrastructure and bring existing homes back into use Prioritize bridges, flood resilience, drinking water, wastewater systems, and reliable rural broadband.
Propose a refundable home rehabilitation tax credit covering 20% of eligible costs, up to $15,000, for qualifying primary residences.
Focus on essential repairs, accessibility, and returning vacant homes to occupancy, with income limits and safeguards against subsidizing luxury renovations.
Provide property tax relief Propose an annual refundable federal property-tax credit of up to $8,500 for qualifying homeowners, prioritizing people age 65 and older and households facing high property taxes relative to income.
Limit the credit to a primary residence and property taxes actually paid after state relief, with income-based phaseouts and rules preventing duplicate federal tax benefits.
Refundability would allow eligible people who owe little or no federal income tax to benefit.
Reward work with student loan relief Create a Work and Stay Student Loan Credit providing up to $5,000 annually for five years, paid directly toward qualifying student loans.
Tie assistance to work in designated shortage occupations or underserved communities, including nursing, teaching, agriculture, and skilled technical fields.
Include income limits, proportional assistance for qualifying part-time work, and protections against receiving duplicate relief for the same loan payments.
