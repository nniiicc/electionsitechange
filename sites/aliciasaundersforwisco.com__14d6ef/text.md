Childhood Meet Alicia Saunders Meet Alicia Saunders Moved by a sense of duty after 9/11, Alicia enlisted in the Navy her senior year of high school.
She started boot camp at Recruit Training Command Great Lakes in August 2002.
After training, she was assigned to Naval Air Station Whidbey Island, Washington, where she worked on aircraft, handling inspections, fueling, washing, and launch procedures.
After t he birth of her first child, Alicia deployed aboard the USS Harry S.
Truman (CVN-75) in 2005 as part of Operation Iraqi Freedom.
Her Navy service finished at AIMD Whidbey Island, where she maintained aviation support equipment and managed its records.
When she came home from deployment, her child saw her as a stranger.
That settled it.
Her service mattered to her, but her kids mattered more.
Alicia received an honorable discharge in August 2006.
Her awards include the National Defense Service Medal, the Global War on Terrorism Service Medal, the Global War on Terrorism Expeditionary Medal, and the Navy and Marine Corps Good Conduct Medal.
Military Service After the Navy, Alicia raised her two children at home.
In 2007 she used her GI Bill benefits to pursue a political science degree at UW Fox Valley, where she co-founded a campus club to help fellow veterans navigate the GI Bill.
Two years in, she transferred to UW Oshkosh, then made the call many single parents face and put her education on hold to focus on her kids.
Alicia worked her way through a string of jobs familiar to a lot of people in this district.
She started on overnight retail stocking, then moved to delivering aftermarket auto body parts and on to warehouse work, inspecting returned parts and handling warranty claims.
In 2018 she became a package delivery driver for FedEx, working through the heavier loads, longer hours, and supply and staffing shortages that came with COVID.
From there she ran a delivery and pickup route servicing businesses with their uniforms and floor mats, and today she works in industrial sanitation. from night shift to navigating covid-19 A Return to Civilian Life After her 2024 campaign, Alicia didn't step back from the work.
She spent the next two years doing constituent advocacy on her own time, connecting neighbors with the resources and legislators they needed and helping veterans cut through red tape on housing, property tax relief, and bringing home those still missing from past wars.
She lobbied in Madison for VOCA funding for domestic violence survivors, and she worked with legislators on both sides of the aisle to get things done, because the people who needed help didn't care about party.
She took on a district leadership role with a veterans service organization, all while working full time on early hours.
People who know her have started asking if she ever sleeps.
She's running again because the problems she heard about over those two years still haven't been fixed.

The “Why” Behind Alicia’s Candidacy What Does Alicia Stand For?
Alicia will expand Wisconsin's veterans property tax credit so more disabled vets can stay in their homes, increase funding for county veteran service offices to cut claim backlogs, and support dedicated veteran housing and mental health programs.
She will back state-level property tax relief for service-connected disabled veterans.
Veterans Alicia will support and push to pass the right to repair bill for agricultural equipment, requiring manufacturers to make diagnostic tools, parts, documentation, and software available to owners and independent mechanics at fair cost.
Right to Repair Alicia will tie state business incentives to local hiring and fair pay, cut fees and permitting red tape that fall hardest on small businesses, and target tax relief to small and Main Street businesses rather than large corporations.
Small Businesses Alicia will work to end the state sales tax exemption for data centers, give local communities clear authority to reject these projects, and require any approved development to pay its full share into local schools and roads rather than recapturing it through TIF financing.
She will support limits on large-scale water withdrawals that threaten local supply.
Farmland and Water Alicia will defend prevailing wage on public projects, protect the right to organize without retaliation, and support project labor agreements on large public builds.
She will require that public subsidies to employers carry local hiring and wage standards.
Unions Alicia will raise the state minimum wage, add wage anti-compression protections so increases lift workers just above the floor, and expand child care assistance.
She will support prescription drug cost controls and study a guaranteed income pilot modeled on programs tested in other states.
Cost of Living Alicia will vote to codify abortion access in state law and protect access to birth control and IVF.
She will defend Wisconsin's new 12-month postpartum Medicaid coverage and work to expand maternal health care access across the district.
Reproductive Rights Alicia will back nondiscrimination protections in employment and housing covering sexual orientation and gender identity, and oppose any rollback of existing civil rights protections.
