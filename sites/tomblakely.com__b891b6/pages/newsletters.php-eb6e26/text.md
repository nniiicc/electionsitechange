Newsletter for July 31: Healthcare #2 .
Healthcare costs don’t stop at the doctor’s office—they drive up school budgets, municipal costs, business expenses, taxes, and the prices Vermonters pay every day.
Tom argues for pursuing universal healthcare, such as Medicare for All , while also expanding community-based care and access to health services.
Newsletter for July 23: Data Centers .

Newsletter for July 15: Healthcare .
Tom points out that health care is different from ordinary consumer goods and services: when you are sick or injured, you cannot simply wait for prices to fall or shop around for a better deal.
Meaningful reform will require thoughtful government planning, responsible regulation, and sustained investment to expand access while keeping costs under control.
Newsletter for July 9: Energy .
Renewable energy—especially solar, wind, and geothermal—is now cheaper and faster to deploy than fossil-fuel generation, even when battery storage is included.
Vermont can expand solar without sacrificing farmland or scenic landscapes by prioritizing rooftops, parking lots, canals, rail corridors, floating solar, and agrivoltaics.
Newsletter for June 4: Issues Overview .
Tom introduces his top priorities: healthcare affordability and access, energy costs, and education/property taxes.
High healthcare and energy costs ripple through the broader economy, while education costs are complicated by healthcare expenses, staffing needs, and Vermont’s declining student population.
He argues for local input and state support rather than top-down mandates, and asks residents of Bennington and Pownal to share their priorities so he can better represent them.
Newsletter for March 30: Failure to Communicate .
Tom focuses on Act 181, saying that the parts supporting infill development in cities and towns are reasonable, while questioning provisions such as the “road rule” and the added burdens on some rural landowners.
While Vermont needs to balance environmental protection with economic growth, housing, and jobs, his main concern is how poorly the provisions of the law were communicated to constiutents.
