Skip to content Stephen Boyle – Community Notes About Facebook Instagram Public Transportation Dependency Despite the outgoing mayor’s claims of how he turned Detroit around, a majority of people residing in the city will refute that.
I guess with 12 years as Mayor, Mike Duggan believes he’ll run successfully for Governor of Michigan.
One of his campaign promises in 2013 when he was elected was that he would “fix the buses” and work through recovering from a municipal bankruptcy forced by the State of Michigan.
He certainly has committed to complying with corporate guidance & leadership.
He’s an example of capitalism shining. 🙄 While shadows cast are upon the poorest of the public.
While the article I’m referencing below says 70% of Detroit residents find work outside the city… it could truly use mentioning how difficult it is to find work outside the city with the transportation schedules that DDOT and SMART currently provide.
Our buses run with closing time (last run) set at 8pm for most of the neighborhoods with reduced access, 10pm for some fortunate neighborhoods, and around the clock 24 hour access is guaranteed to FEW neighborhoods.
Expanding 24 hour access was a purpose of the Connect 10 initiative by DDOT, for 10 routes to run 24 hours within the city.
Access to the suburbs via SMART from within Detroit ends at midnight.
These end of service hours are shorter on Sundays and holidays – days when many lower wage employees are requested or demanded to work.
Counting on suburban jobs for urban center residents is ludicrous, but the status quo think nothing of making that normal.
The automotive industry indoctrination lives on to this day – 60 years after globalization had many corporations offshoring labor operations seeking the lowest wages globally.
Note the following except from this recent CNN article : The city has experienced an influx of White, Hispanic and Asian residents over the last decade.
But Detroit, long a majority Black city, has seen its Black population has decline.
Since 2013, the largest loss in population has been among Black residents, with 72,000 fewer over that span.
The median income in Detroit is $38,000, half the median income of its broader region.
Only 26% of Detroit’s households are middle class, compared to 39% in the region.
And 18 out of Detroit’s 139 square miles of land are still vacant, according to Detroit Future City, a nonprofit organization.
High-paying jobs are often held by people who live in the suburbs and commute into Detroit — while the salaries of Detroit residents, regardless of where they work, are lower.
Nearly 70% of Detroit-resident workers traveled outside the city for jobs.
“Future” Plans Of Dereliction I was present for several Detroit Future City presentations and read through their analysis/plan for the city which evolved from 2008-2012.
That plan was to reduce community & municipal services in certain areas of the city of Detroit.
It didn’t get into the details of impact to those LIVING in the areas, but I believe alluded that residents would relocate to suit their needs.
The plan seemed to pose that those staying might be embracing a less urbanized existence.
The city moved zoning changes forward allowing for agricultural use.
Recently the solar farms are claiming land to offset municipal energy usage.
Note the entry of the solar farms doesn’t assist energy needs of the residents near them.
The city is offering a one-time stipend to OWNER-RESIDENTS within the area of the solar farm.
Poor people often don’t have the means to relocate easily.
During my most impoverished years (2008-2017), as I moved from place to place, I ended up unable to transport possessions and left many things behind.
This isn’t something any family should experience because it’s traumatizing to pick up and go taking only what you can carry.
Imagine the trauma for children with dearly loved toys and their friends adopted and lost with each move.
Living in Detroit, some families find themselves moving to enable work conditions to be met.
It’s easier to rent than own if you’re moving frequently.
And even with Detroit real estate being considered affordable by many – many within the city can’t qualify to own a home.
Home ownership has shifted from about 80% in the 1950s wet 1.8 million residents to 20% or less now with 650,000 residents.
You might note the owner-residents from 2 paragraphs up.
Solar farms aren’t helping anyone that rents or their landlords.
Detroit just held municipal elections with mixed results.
Some of the long term incumbents remain in office, most notably the City Clerk just elected to the 7th term (28 years) in office.
Can things change?
It’s going to take innovative engaging dialog & exposure with 80% of potential voters that choose not to participate in elections.
The 20% that do vote are mostly seniors and stable gentry of the city.
May our young people rise up into claiming a renewed city spirit created through their innovative approaches.
Civics has to be learned in the streets and the creative class are the best messengers.
