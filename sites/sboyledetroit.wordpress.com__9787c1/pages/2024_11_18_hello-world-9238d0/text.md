I’ve found the political landscape is full of people that promise to hear you.
Once in office they will create focus groups of people (whom they typically already know) to set a desired path.
That traditional approach doesn’t really get into natural Diversity, Equity, Inclusion (DEI).
It can paint it as such by selecting the representatives who best exemplify the effort.
I believe we need more direct democracy.
Advances in technology make it possible.
Although doing things the old way, where people meet, discuss, and choose collectively.
This is rarely done in our households in present society.
It requires getting to know you through the communities that you’re part of… even if it’s just staying home.
Communities are people with common interests who are united in those, some are declared and many more are demographically discovered.
Democracy At The Root
In democracy, an elected official is a representative from a community that interfaces with governance of that community.
The representative is an access point to government which should be serving your interests.
You’ll find me talking about access to opportunity quite often in my campaigns.
Access is something we need to discuss more in community.
People dismiss so many opportunities thinking they haven’t got the access needed.
Back in 2014 I joined the Green Party of Michigan.
At the time I was around many social justice activities and saw active engagement and leadership from Greens.
That year I chose to run for US Congress in District 14.
I can’t say it was the best office to seek.
I made a big commitment moving from activism to political change.
My activism brought an insight: legislative change sets the laws / ordinances / policies that allow for a better society that serves everyone.
The biggest problem the public faces is discovering how to interface effectively with these government agencies that truly hold agency over their lives.
So many processes and filings to make, and fees for access as well.
The 4 pillars of the Green Party of US 1 are:

- Democracy
I continue to run for office to provide a listening for the public on issues that impact their access to opportunities.
