A Divergent Proposal Supporting Community
Stephen Boyle proposes a transformative approach to the Michigan Department of Transportation’s (MDOT) I-375 project in Detroit.
This highway displaced the base of the Black Bottom and Paradise Valley neighborhoods.
These were historically vibrant hubs for Detroit’s Black community, during the 1950s construction of the I-75 corridor.
Boyle’s plan seeks to alter this legacy of displacement while fostering sustainable urban development.
Boyle’s idea is to cap the existing I-375 freeway, then redevelop the underground as a parking facility.
This facility would include free shuttle services to downtown businesses.
Utilizing connections to various transit services existing downtown such as MoGo (a bike-share program) and the Detroit People Mover.
Above the capped freeway, he envisions the re-establishment of the Black Bottom Business District.
This development prioritizes descendants of the families and businesses displaced by the original highway, promoting economic restitution and cultural revitalization.
Overall the project would transition this area of downtown Detroit to be more walkable, which would in turn encourage shopping.
Boyle’s coined term, “Ditches of Denial,” critiques the intentional placement of below-grade highways.
Highways like I-375 and I-75, were designed to shield motorists from the visible destruction these projects caused in Detroit communities.
His approach contrasts with MDOT’s current plans by emphasizing historical justice and community-centric urban planning.
