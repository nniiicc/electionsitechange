Skip to content Stephen Boyle – Community Notes About Facebook Instagram Taxes City residents need to put the economic development organizations on notice when it comes to the length of time for tax abatement.
Time after time they arrive to the Economic Development Committee of City Council, championing a business prospecting for tax abatement, often requesting the maximum time possible, 12 years.
It would be far better to have a logarithmic scale reducing tax abatement as the business grows.
Statistically many businesses don’t continue more than 5 years from the date founded.
Some of the reasons include lease arrangements of 3-5 years which can make staying in the original location impossible.
Turnover of young businesses is also prevalent.
It leaves city residents wondering when, if at all, will the city benefit from the business rising and thriving.
Zones Withholding Taxes We should also be concerned about tax incentivized financing (TIF) zones and their tax captures.
These zones prevent taxes collected from being delivered to the city as a whole.
This limited tax accessibility means funding of city services is choked.
One way the lack of funding impacts is a failure to offer taxes into public transit for the business’s employees to commute for work.
I saw recently Mayor Duggan applauding the arrival of COSMO into The District, which is a TIF zone.
If the startup was outside that zone the city could benefit from taxes .
These new businesses are focused on corporate growth, without realizing a need to empower employees participating in making the business grow.
This can be benefit packages, transportation assistance, and in-person team building activities.
Detroit needs to go through holistic healing so all parts of the city work on coexistence.
Access to Opportunities is vital to growth and it’s not been given sufficient attention.
Developing a Future Residential property taxes will go up around streetscape developments.
But the Dexter streetscape becomes a burden without having businesses already settled in thriving.
I’m concerned how that was handled as we’re looking to develop the Fenkell Business Corridor between Livernois and Wyoming.
There are many boarded up business fronts along Fenkell.
City inspectors keep putting notices on them for blight and delinquency.
Let’s not spend funds in the current playbook of dereliction to demolish to develop .
Save what exists and transform it.
At present Detroit’s jobs with most growth around them are demolition, development / construction, and private security.
But is this even true?
Many of these employers are from outside the city of Detroit, creating a satellite office (or shell company) within Detroit for hiring, payroll, and tax purposes.
