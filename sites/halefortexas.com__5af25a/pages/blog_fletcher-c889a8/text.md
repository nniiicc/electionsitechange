EXHIBIT A—SAME POLICY, OPPOSITE VOTES
The LNG reversal.
In 2024, the House voted to restore approvals for American liquefied natural gas exports—an industry deeply relevant in this district.
Rep.
Fletcher voted no.
Twenty-one months later, the House voted on the same policy again.
This time, she voted yes.
- February 15, 2024 (Biden administration): H.R. 7176, Unlocking our Domestic LNG Potential Act.
Roll Call 52, 224–200.
Fletcher: Nay. https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/202452
- November 20, 2025 (Trump administration): H.R. 1949, the same LNG export policy.
Roll Call 304, 217–188.
Fletcher: Yea. https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2025304
The policy didn't change.
The White House did.
And even after voting yes, she declined to cosponsor H.R. 1949.

EXHIBIT D—WHAT SHE WRITES, AND WHAT SHE DOESN'T
Where you learn what a member cares about is in what they choose to write.
Her own bill: freeze hiring at ICE.
The Freeze ICE Act (H.R. 7392)—introduced and led by Fletcher, with Rep.
John Garamendi of California as co-lead—would impose a hiring freeze on U.S.
Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
She also voted against the Secure America Act and its $38.5 billion for ICE (S. 2, Roll Call 214, June 9, 2026, 214–212, Fletcher No). https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2026214
Her flagship effort: the abortion-travel bill.
The bill Fletcher is best known for—the Ensuring Women's Right to Reproductive Freedom Act—guarantees the right to cross state lines, out of Texas, for an abortion.
It passed the House in July 2022 when her party held the majority, then died in the Senate.
She has re-filed it every Congress since; it is pending today as H.R. 4099.
She paired it with the Reproductive Health Travel Fund Act (H.R. 4265)—federal grant dollars to pay for the travel, lodging, and meals of those trips.
And she cosponsored and voted twice for the Women's Health Protection Act—September 2021 (Roll Call 295, 218–211) and July 2022 (Roll Call 360, 219–210).
Here is the problem: the right her flagship bill guarantees is not Congress's to grant.
Justice Kavanaugh wrote in the Dobbs decision itself that a state may not bar its residents from traveling to another state for an abortion.
Re-filing it every two years doesn't change a single law in Texas—it makes a statement.
Strip away the part that restates rights Americans already hold, and what's genuinely new is the money: federal taxpayer grants to pay for abortion travel.
Not energy, in the energy capital of the world.
Not flooding.
Abortion. https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4099

EXHIBIT F—WOMEN'S SPORTS & THE EQUALITY ACT
She voted no, twice, on the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act.
In its own terms, the bill recognizes sex for Title IX school athletics as based solely on reproductive biology and genetics at birth, and makes it a Title IX violation for federally funded schools to allow individuals of the male sex to participate in athletic programs designated for women or girls.
It passed the House twice—219–203 in 2023 (H.R. 734, Roll Call 192) and 218–206 in 2025 (H.R. 28, Roll Call 12). https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2023192 and https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/202512
She cosponsored and voted for the Equality Act, twice.
The Equality Act adds sexual orientation and gender identity as protected classes across federal civil-rights law.
The text she put her name on provides that an individual may not be denied access to a shared facility—the statute lists restrooms, locker rooms, and dressing rooms—in accordance with the individual's gender identity.
She was an original cosponsor and voted yes in both 2019 (Roll Call 217) and 2021 (Roll Call 39). https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2019217 and https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/202139
Vice Chair of the Equality Caucus, and a mid-campaign cosponsorship.
She serves as a Vice Chair of the Congressional Equality Caucus.
And on February 11, 2026, in the middle of this campaign, she added her name to H.Res. 1058, the "Transgender Bill of Rights," which calls for adding gender identity to the Civil Rights Act, guaranteeing access to school athletics and facilities consistent with gender identity, and codifying access to gender-affirming care nationwide.
Both are her choices, on the record. https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-resolution/1058 and https://equality.house.gov/about-cec/membership
