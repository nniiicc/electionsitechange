Data Centers Among Top Concerns for Tri-County Residents
Energized by three open-forum town halls held in August, Scott Grund, candidate for the Ohio House of Representatives, is looking forward to the next round starting this month.
Grund, who is running to represent District 98 in the Ohio House, says last month’s events in Coshocton, Knox and Holmes counties gave him a chance to hear directly from residents what concerns them the most.
Voters from both sides of the political aisle attended the free town halls, which he says is the primary goal for the events because it’s the best way to encourage a truly open and organic conversation.
“People were thrilled to have someone actually ask them what’s on their minds,” Grund says.
“That was pretty encouraging.”
Topics ranged from hot-button issues like data centers, automated license plate reader systems—like Flock cameras—and property taxes, to other sensitive subjects, like education and access to health care.
Often, the events would turn into residents conversing with each other about the issues, their concerns and what they were reading in the news.
Grund helped keep the conversations moving by offering his own insights and researched data.
“These days, it’s very easy to let our emotions dictate the direction and the temperature of our political discourse,” Grund says.
“I want to make sure that whatever we discuss, we keep everything rooted in facts and information.”
That fact-based research is a top priority of his as the representative for District 98, he says.
If elected, Grund plans to build a network of subject matter experts throughout the tri-county area in all key areas, including education, health care, workforce development and economic growth.
Such a network will keep him informed and focused on what all tri-country residents need—not just what benefits special interests, he says.
“I’m running because I believe people in this area have been ignored for too long.
And they need a choice for change,” Grund says.
“The focus lately has been to enrich the wealthy corporations and donors instead of the residents.
It has to change.”
Grund invites the community to again join him and their neighbors for more town halls this month and into October.
Two events are scheduled for Mount Vernon on Sept. 17 and Oct. 15 from 6-8 p.m. at the ReStore/Habitat for Humanity meeting room at 405 W.
Chestnut St.
Another is scheduled in Coshocton on Sept. 22 from 6-8 p.m. at the Roscoe Village Visitor Center 600 N.
Whitewoman St.
Tri-county residents are invited to attend any or all of the town halls to join the conversation and dig deeper into the issues that concern them most.
