Meet Scott Events Issues Education In 2021, the legislature passed the Fair School Funding Plan (FSFP) which is a bipartisan approach to school funding based on how much districts must spend to educate every child, including those who need different or additional support.
The FSFP works because it is based on the actual cost of educating kids, sharing that cost between the state and local communities by considering communities’ ability to cover the costs through local taxes.
In 2025, Ohio was four years in to the six-year plan, when the FSFP would provide an additional $3 billion to schools in Ohio over the next two years, when the formula was calculated using updated costs, updated local property and income tax information, and more accurate weights that more closely correspond to the costs of special education and English language services schools must provide.
However, the legislature effectively altered and underfunded in the 2025 state budget , as lawmakers departed from the full formula to use older data and alternative spending targets .
Lawmakers did not fully implement the final phases of the original FSFP calculations, creating an estimated $2.75 billion gap compared to what full phased-in inputs would have provided over the biennium!
Analysis from groups like Policy Matters Ohio shows that over 60% of districts receiving net state increases under the revised 2025 adjustments feature low or very low student poverty, while high-poverty districts faced relative funding reductions.
