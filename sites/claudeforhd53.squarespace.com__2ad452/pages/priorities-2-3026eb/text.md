03 🌿Nature Coast & Local Economy Our waters are who we are.
I will protect them.
The problem 7 of 8 Springs Coast springs exceed safe nitrate levels — SWFWMD.
Septic and fertilizer runoff contributes an estimated 5 million pounds of nitrogen to springs annually — DEP.
Weeki Wachee is struggling with algae, water withdrawals, and nutrient runoff, threatening the $800M+ Nature Coast economy — Visit Florida.
The Water Management District board overseeing our springs isn't elected by the people who depend on this water.
All nine members are appointed by the Governor, rather than elected by Nature Coast residents — SWFWMD / Florida Statute 373.
Development continues to put pressure on springs and waterways.
Questions remain about whether post-permit monitoring and enforcement adequately protect water quality, particularly as septic systems are added near Weeki Wachee and Homosassa — Tampa Bay Times, October 2025.
Hyperscale data centers are expanding into Florida, potentially placing additional pressure on water resources, electricity supplies, and infrastructure.
Florida Statute 145.031 ties county commissioner compensation partly to county population.
As population increases, the statutory salary formula can increase commissioner pay.
Although commissioners are not paid for approving individual developments, this creates the appearance of a financial incentive for population growth, even when residents are concerned about overdevelopment and environmental damage.
Tony's solution Provide incentives for native ground covers and Florida-friendly landscaping to reduce water consumption and nitrogen runoff.
Establish a moratorium on new development that threatens our springs and waterways, using legally enforceable environmental criteria.
Require full transparency and accountability for environmental spending — no backroom deals, no polluter carve-outs.
Make Water Management District board members elected, not appointed , giving residents a direct voice in who oversees their water resources.
Oppose further hyperscale data center development in Florida.
Reform Florida Statute 145.031 to eliminate population growth as a factor in determining county commissioner salaries.
Establish a fair and transparent compensation structure that is not tied to county population increases.
Require greater transparency in development approvals, including public disclosure of projected impacts on water resources, infrastructure, and the environment.
