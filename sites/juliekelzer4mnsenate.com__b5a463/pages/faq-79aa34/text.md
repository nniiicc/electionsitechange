Got Questions?
Julie has participated in many forums and interviews.
Here are her answer to some of the most common questions:
Tell us a little about yourself.
I am a mother to a 20yr old son and 9yr old daughter, I have noticed the education difference with an 11yr gap.
I have been in the customer service industry since I was 14, including salon management.
Where I had to organize, lead and coordinate stylists to have our salon running efficiently.
Problem-solving, clear communication and encouragement were needed everyday.
During the pandemic the salon franchise was sold yet again, each sale meant more work to achieve the same pay while still working nights and weekends.
When salons were shut down, My daughter was diagnosed with speech apraxia.
In order to coordinate her care and being home in the evenings for my son to be more involved with his homework, I started a home cleaning business to prioritize my children’s needs.
Why are you running for the State Legislature?
People deserve a choice.
Our future generations matter: policies made now, will affect generations to come.
For too long, representatives have voted in the best interests of large businesses and corporations.
People are struggling with costs continuing to rise.
I want to be a voice for the people over the party, vote in their best interest and improve our daily lives.
The government needs to be: of the people, by the people, for the people.
If elected, what will be your priorities?
Healthcare: Affordability and expanding rural access to specialists.
I found out last fall how badly mental health care access is lacking statewide.
Specialists are an hour or more away depending on the specialty you need, it isn’t possible for everyone to travel that far.
Expanding telehealth would help in many areas of care.
Affordability: Healthcare, housing, childcare, food and energy costs are continually rising, my bank account can’t continue to afford it: something has to change for the better.
Education: Our children deserve a Quality Education.
Class sizes are growing, food quality has gone down (working with our local farmers to help supply our schools will benefit all), schools are short-staffed impacting our children’s learning environment, budget cuts are damaging their educations.
What differentiates you from other candidates?
I’m living the struggles that government policies have created on an income less than lower middle class.
I live on a tight budget and have to make tough decisions daily, there’s no money left to save anymore.
Our legislature needs to do the same with our tax money and invest it back into communities.
Compromising and changing beliefs based on others perspectives to accommodate everyone has been ongoing throughout my working career and while campaigning.
What experience to you have that will help you if elected?
Working in salons involved; effective communication, fast paced problem solving, and precise technical skills under pressure.
Successful management required integrity, sharp problem-solving and conflict resolution skills, being accountable for my decisions and holding others accountable for theirs.
Collaboration, compromise and team work are necessary to keep the business running smoothly.
I would bring these same skills to the legislature and work with both sides to achieve the best outcome for we the people.
How would you address disagreements in the legislature?
By listening and hearing what other legislators have to say on a matter.
Take their perspective into account and come to an agreement that the majority are ok with.
Learning how to compromise is what is needed most.
Compromising is not something we see often today and it needs to become the norm.
We need elected officials that work for and represent us.
I was taught that, government is of the people, by the people and for the people.
I would love the opportunity to bring that to our district.
What are the biggest concerns affecting Minnesota?
The cost of housing and childcare.
Home daycares, which are affordable, are closing due to excessive regulations.
We need to make it easier for them to operate.
Evaluation of state regulations for housing need to be addressed.
What is necessary for protection of the workers and home owners and quality of the home?
Remove the rest.
Building more basic starter homes will also lower the cost, not everyone wants a massive house.
How will you manage the budget?
Federal cuts from the BBB are projected to cause a $2 billion shift to the state in the next 4 years.
Start by keeping the programs that help the people, then start cutting what doesn’t.
In 2019: corporations made up 10.1% of tax revenue, Individual Income Tax 31.9%, Property Tax 22.9%. 15 Fortune 500 companies in Minnesota had a combined profit of $547.26 Billion.
Corporations need to contribute their fair share.
What will you do to support agriculture in rural Minnesota?
I believe that our small family farmers deserve better from our government.
Tariffs are hurting many that I have spoken with.
Meanwhile Corporate farms are being given the go ahead to expand against the concerns farmers have voiced.
Input costs are through the roof.
Farmers already have to hope for the best with the weather, they shouldn’t have to worry about policies by our government straining their pockets as well.
Stability is what so many want and I agree.
Farmers are the backbone of our country and need to be treated with respect, they know what they need to be able to operate efficiently, without additional regulations making their passion to work the land and raise animals more difficult.
What would you do the help address fraud in Minnesota?
Fraud needs to be caught before having tax payers robbed of their money and hurting the people those programs are intended for.
SF334 which will upgrade our computer programs into the Modern times is a great start.
I would like to thank Glenn for actually voting yes.
Implementing oversight at every level is crucial.
I want to see more government transparency, we have the right to know where our money is going.
Any Closing Remarks?
People deserve a choice.
Our children and grandchildren's futures matter: policies made now, will affect us and them.
For too long we’ve had representatives voting in the best interests of large businesses and corporations.
Raising taxes isn’t a solution.
Small businesses and locally owned farms are going out of business.
People are struggling to survive with the cost of everything continuing to rise.
I will be a voice for the people over the party, vote in the peoples best interests and improve our daily lives.
The government needs to be: of the people, by the people, for the people.
