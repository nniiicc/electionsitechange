Erika Creydt Advances to the General Election With Growing Grassroots Momentum Campaign Staff Aug 5 3 min read WHATCOM COUNTY, Wash. — Dr.
Erika Creydt advanced from the primary election in the race for Washington State Senate’s 42nd Legislative District, carrying a message focused on affordability, stronger schools, accessible healthcare, support for local businesses, and better-paying jobs.
“I am proud, deeply honored and humbled by the support we received,” Creydt said.
“Democrats, Independents and Republicans are getting behind my campaign because they want a better quality of life for themselves and their children, and they know I’m committed to putting party politics aside and addressing the issues.” While voter turnout in the primary was low, Creydt said the results demonstrate that momentum is building behind her campaign and its focus on the everyday concerns of Whatcom County residents.
“Our grassroots support is growing every day, and so is our team of volunteers,” Creydt said.
“We are grateful for everyone who voted, volunteered, donated, displayed a sign, made calls, knocked on doors or shared our message with a neighbor.
Now, we are going to work as hard as possible to earn the support of a majority of voters in the general election.” Creydt’s campaign is centered on making Whatcom County a more affordable place to live, work, raise a family and retire.
She has consistently highlighted the pressures residents face as housing, groceries, utilities, transportation and healthcare consume a growing share of household income.
“Housing affordability is one of the greatest challenges facing our community,” Creydt said.
“Young adults are being priced out of the communities where they grew up, working families are struggling to find homes they can afford, and seniors on fixed incomes are worried about whether they can remain in their homes.” Creydt supports responsibly increasing housing supply, encouraging the development of housing types that meet the needs of local residents, streamlining permitting and removing unnecessary barriers while protecting Whatcom County’s farmland, water and environment.
Her priorities also include making sure public schools have the funding they need.
As an elected member of the Blaine School Board, Creydt has firsthand experience with the financial and operational challenges facing local school districts.
“Our schools should have stable funding, and every student deserves an excellent education,” Creydt said.
“That includes properly supporting special education, strengthening career and technical programs, and expanding pathways to college, apprenticeships and living-wage careers.” A clinical psychologist and healthcare professional, Creydt has also made access to healthcare a central part of her campaign.
She supports addressing provider shortages to ensure residents can obtain timely physical, behavioral and mental healthcare.
“Access to healthcare means very little when people cannot find a provider or must wait months for an appointment,” Creydt said.
“We need to strengthen our healthcare workforce and ensure people can receive care before their needs become a crisis.” Creydt has pledged to support Whatcom County businesses and farms by improving predictability, reducing unnecessary regulatory burdens and creating an environment in which employers can remain, expand and create better-paying jobs.
“Our local businesses are struggling with rising costs and layers of regulation,” Creydt said.
“When employers can grow, they create jobs, support families and invest in our community.
We must support the businesses already here while attracting new employers that offer living-wage careers.” Creydt said she will continue building a broad coalition of voters who want practical, accountable and balanced leadership in Olympia.
“People are tired of political extremes and division,” Creydt said.
“They want leaders who will listen, work with people who have different perspectives and remain focused on improving life in their communities.
I am committed to doing that work with integrity and representing every person in the 42nd District.” Creydt’s campaign will continue to focus on voter outreach, volunteer recruitment and community engagement efforts ahead of the general election.
“Momentum is on our side, but we are not taking anything for granted,” Creydt said.
“We will keep listening, keep showing up and keep working to earn every vote.
Together, we can improve Whatcom County’s economy and make it a more affordable place to live and run a business.” Dr.
Erika Creydt is a clinical psychologist, local business owner and elected member of the Blaine School Board.
She is running for Washington State Senate in the 42nd Legislative District to improve housing affordability, lower the cost of living, strengthen public schools, expand access to healthcare, support local businesses, attract living-wage employers and bring greater accountability to Olympia.
