Meet Bobby NEWS Issues ACtion center Get Involved Events STORE X DONATE Bobby Charles Dominates 2nd Televised Maine Governor Debate October 8, 2026 Republican Nominee Bobby Charles Puts the Mills–Pingree Record on Trial in Bangor — on Taxes, Addiction, Children’s Well-Being and Insider Dealing ‍ BANGOR, Maine -- Republican nominee Bobby Charles dominated the second televised debate of the Maine governor’s race Thursday night, pressing Democrat Hannah Pingree on the record she shares with Gov.
Janet Mills and refusing to let the hour stay limited to rehearsed talking points.
In an hour hosted in Bangor by the Portland Press Herald, Maine Public and NEWS CENTER Maine, Charles tied Pingree to higher property taxes, higher electricity bills, a projected budget hole, and last-in-the-nation children’s well-being -- and he did it until the moderators stopped him. ‍ The Portland Press Herald’s live coverage said Charles spent the night connecting Pingree to Mills on housing costs, health care, immigration enforcement and the budget shortfall, while Pingree spent it blaming President Trump and independent Rick Bennett blamed the two-party system.
That was the contrast Charles wanted, and he owned it. ‍ "You don’t invite the arsonist to put out the fire.
Hannah Pingree and Janet Mills destroyed our state with higher property taxes, higher electricity bills, and needles in our streets.
Hannah did that.
As Governor, I’ll fix it." --Bobby Charles, on the debate stage in Bangor, October 8, 2026 ‍ On the budget question itself, Charles was more specific.
“Hannah was the right hand” of the administration that “put us in all these problem sets,” he said.
Inviting that same team back “makes no sense whatsoever.” Bad policies, he said, would be “just compounded again.” ‍ WHAT HE PUT ON THE TABLE ‍ Charles did not arrive with atmospherics.
On the opening housing question, he laid out a concrete list: zero property taxes for 100 percent disabled veterans, no public housing for noncitizens, and seizure of Chinese-linked marijuana grow houses so the buildings can be turned into housing for Mainers.
On the budget, he blamed Pingree and the Mills administration for the shortfall and named the cuts: an immediate hiring freeze, a restructuring of the Department of Health and Human Services and the education department, and an end to what he called “insider contracts.” ‍ He rejected Pingree’s proposal for a constitutional amendment to raise property taxes on homes owned by out-of-staters.
The Press Herald called his follow-up — a shot at Pingree for running against Trump instead of against Maine’s own record — a candidate for the line of the night.
Asked by Maine Public’s Kevin Miller whether the Charles who ran close to Trump in the primary, or a later version, would govern, Charles denied he had changed his tune and said he would confront the president where Maine’s interests required it. ‍ On data centers, he refused the fashionable answer.
He said he is “agnostic” on bringing more of them to Maine.
Pingree answered with a moratorium “until we have the strongest laws in the country.” On tribal sovereignty he said he would listen to the people, rather than pledge a signature on a bill modeled on the one Mills vetoed.
On abortion he told the state the issue is not the one deciding this election — “This is not the number one issue” — and kept the hour on affordability, safety and the record. ‍ THE LINES THAT TRAVELED ‍ Within minutes of the debate, the Charles campaign posted the exchanges that defined the night.
