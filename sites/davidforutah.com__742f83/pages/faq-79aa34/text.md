The Salt Lake Tribune asked us both the same questions.
Read the Q&A → WHAT IS YOUR POSITION ON PROP 4?
The legislature should respect Prop 4.
In 2018, we created rules for fair election maps (Prop 4).
The legislature’s response was to ignore the fair maps and pass their own gerrymandered ones.
I will fight to amend the Utah State Constitution to clearly direct an independent commission, rather than the state legislature, to draw the maps.
Learn More: Ban Gerrymandering for Good WHAT IS YOUR POSITION ON THE INCOME TAX CUTS?
Working Utahns need a tax cut.
The recent cuts went mostly to the top.
Utah’s six recent income tax cuts cost more than $1.4 billion every year and almost exclusively benefit the wealthy.
As a result, everyone’s property taxes are rising.
Utah already taxes poor people at a higher rate than the wealthy.
Income tax cuts make this worse.
My goal is to make taxes more equal across income levels by cutting sale tax on essentials.
My plan does not include any tax increases.
Learn more: About the Tax Cuts , Cut Taxes for Working Utahns , Utah’s Budget and How I’d Change It WHAT IS YOUR POSITION ON DATA CENTERS?
Data Centers should not be built in Utah.
Utah is not a good place for data centers for the following reasons: ✸ We don’t want them here ✸ They consume a lot of energy ✸ They create noise and light pollution ✸ They do not create long-term jobs ✸ They are not worth the tax breaks I support pausing all development of data centers until we can pass laws that will protect the environment and the will of local Utahns.
Read my adjustments to the proposed Utah County data center ordinance here.
Read my public comment about it here.
WHAT IS YOUR POSITION ON EDUCATION FUNDING?
Utah ranks dead last in per-pupil spending and that has to change.
Reinvesting even just half of the recent income tax cuts could move Utah to around 46th in the nation.
Income tax is constitutionally earmarked for education, so when we cut income tax, we limit our ability to properly fund education.
Teachers need smaller class sizes, more materials, and more aides.
Instead, Provo and Springville schools are facing cuts to their budgets, staff, and programs.
Learn More: About the Tax Cuts , Utah’s Budget and How I’d Change It , Tracking Utah's K-12 Education Funding , Free School Meals HOW WOULD YOUR HOUSING PLAN ACTUALLY WORK?
Low-interest loans so everyday Utahns can add units for sale on property they already own.
Take my house as an example.
I own my home and the mortgage is tough to get paid each month.
My attic would make a great second unit.
But I can’t afford to finish it, especially with interest rates where they are.
Under my plan, I’d get a state loan with little or no interest and finish the attic.
Then I’d sell the new unit.
I pay the state back and anything left over goes toward my mortgage.
The foundation, roof, and utilities are already there.
That makes it one of the cheapest and fastest ways to add an affordable unit for sale.
The state gets its money back and a family gets a place they can actually afford.
I’m sick of only rich people benefiting from housing development.
Everyday Utahns should be the ones building housing and reaping the reward.
Learn More: Small Developer Program WILL YOU MAKE IT EASIER TO BUILD HIGH-DENSITY HOUSING IN MY NEIGHBORHOOD?
No.
My plan is about small additions, not high-rises.
We have a real shortage of affordable homes, but I don't want high-rise apartments in the middle of our neighborhoods.
Developers behind projects like the Vesper amphitheater and the high-density housing going up around Provo need to win over the people who live here.
What’s the point of building something the community doesn’t want?
I believe in incremental development: small changes that add units without changing the feel of a neighborhood.
It is perhaps the most efficient way to increase housing supply since all the infrastructure is already there.
Learn More: How My Housing Plan Works , Cut the Cost of Housing WILL YOU RAISE MY TAXES?
No.
My plan does not include any tax increases.
I have studied the Utah budget extensively and I believe that my plans would be fully paid for by projected growth of net revenue and cuts to corporate welfare, programs that unfairly benefit the wealthy, and administrative inefficiencies.
I will fight to actually cut taxes for working families.
Learn More: Utah’s Budget and How I’d Change It , Cut Taxes for Working Utahns , Utah’s Current Grocery Tax Rate WHAT WOULD YOU DO ABOUT THE GREAT SALT LAKE?
Declare a state of emergency until we get it done.
We have to reduce our consumption of water and that means real action from the Legislature such as modernizing our water rights law, helping framers switch to water-wise crops, helping residents switch away from sprinklers and Kentucky bluegrass, and ultimately I believe we need to declare a state of emergency to save the Great Salt Lake.
Learn More: Save the Great Salt Lake , Great Salt Lake Strike Team Report WHAT IS YOUR POSITION ON ICE?
Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) should be abolished.
ICE has killed hundreds of people since it was created in 2003.
ICE harasses our communities, our workplaces, our hospitals, and schools.
They functionally answer only to the president; they spy on us as they cover their faces, and they are rarely accountable for their actions.
ICE’s existence directly conflicts with our constitutional rights.
Violations of immigration law are a civil offense and should be handled through the civil courts, not this brutal and unaccountable police force.
I will do everything in my power as a state legislator to protect Utahns from ICE.
Learn More: Immigration Enforcement Dashboard , 10 Reasons Why Congress Must Defund ICE DO YOU TAKE CORPORATE MONEY?
No.
I refuse every cent of corporate money.
I will put forward legislation to prohibit corporations from contributing to political elections in Utah, using the design from the Transparent Election Initiative in Montana or SB2471 in Hawai'i.
Utahns deserve a legislature run by the people, not special interests.

No.
I would not.
AIPAC typically gives only to federal candidates.
Regardless, I would not accept their money.
While the legislature does not control foreign policy, I would support efforts to repeal laws that require companies contracting with the state to provide a written certification that it is not currently engaged in a boycott of Israel.
The legislature should focus improving the lives of Utahns, not supporting a country committing genocide.
Learn More: UN Report on Israel’s Crimes of Genocide ARE YOU A DEMOCRAT?
Yes, after a lifetime as an independent.
I’m also a member of the Working Families Party, which fights for everyday people.
I’m frustrated with both major parties and a two-party system that barely gives voters a choice, so I’m glad to see third partiesoffering more options.
You can call me a libertarian progressive.
I want government out of your personal life and I think decisions should be made as close to home as possible.
I also want a government that taxes and spends responsibly to improve the lives of everyday people, and that actually enforces the laws that protect workers and renters.
If you believe the two-party system needs to end, please join me in fighting to establish thigns like Ranked Choice Voting, fusion voting, or non-partisan elections.
Learn More: About Me , Working Families Party , Take Back People Power , Endorsements WHAT IS YOUR POSITION ON FLOCK CAMERAS?
High-tech A.I. cameras like those of Flock Safety should be banned from our state.
Flock Safety is a powerful network of cameras that tracks, logs, and shares your movements with the federal government.
This database allows local police officers to bypass the typical warrant process required by the 4th amendment to track people.
There have been countless cases of this power being abused, and I believe Flock cameras substantiate mass surveillance.
