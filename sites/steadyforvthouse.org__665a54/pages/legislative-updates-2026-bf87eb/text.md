Depending on the bill, floor times could be long if there was a lot of debate.
On Friday, May 29th, Governor Scott concluded the biennium and we adjourned at 7:46 p.m.
The education reform bill passed on the floor after the Governor and the Education Committee made some consolidations, which now allow districts to voluntarily merge and share services to cut costs rather than forcing consolidation.
Both H.951 and H.949 passed, which set the annual property tax rates.
The House closed up shop at 7:46 p.m.
JRH12 passed on the floor.
This Joint House Resolution from the State of Vermont authorizes limited remote voting for members of joint committees through the end of 2026.
Each member is permitted to vote remotely for up to three days, provided they notify the committee chair or co‑chairs and the committee clerk.
Votes cast remotely will be recorded as such and will count toward the committee quorum.
The committee clerk is responsible for tracking the number of remote voting days utilized by each member.
This resolution makes it possible for a member with a disability or who cannot get access to the committee room to be allowed to vote remotely.

I voted yes.

I voted yes.

I voted yes.

I voted yes.
S.64 – Optometry Scope of Practice Expands the scope of practice for optometrists, allowing them to perform certain in‑office advanced therapeutic procedures, such as minor eyelid surgeries, ocular injections, and laser treatments.
To perform these procedures, optometrists must obtain a new “advanced procedures specialty” license by completing additional education, clinical training, and examinations.
I voted yes.

H.955 – Next Steps in Transforming Vermont’s Education System This is major landmark education reform legislation passed to transform the state's public education system without enforcing mandatory school district mergers.
It speeds up the implementation of a new foundation formula by July 2029, caps tuition increases paid to public and approved independent schools beginning in 2028, and relies on incentives and state oversight rather than forcing communities to merge.

I voted yes.
This annual “Yield Bill” sets the state's education property tax benchmarks, yields, and non‑homestead tax rates for the upcoming fiscal year.
The legislation balances one‑time funds and Education Fund surpluses to mitigate the impact of rising school budgets on property taxes.

Respectfully, Representative Steady 2026 Legislative Update: May 12 - 15 2026 Legislative Update: May 12 - 15 Legislative Update – Week of May 12 - 15 , 2026 May 19, 2026 Brenda Steady • State Representative, Chittenden-25 House District Legislative Update The Human Services Committee continued work on several significant bills this week: H.657 – An act relating to various programming and requirements within the Department for Children and Families H.657 originally began as a narrowly focused bill titled: “An act relating to enabling unaccompanied homeless youth to obtain certain services without parental consent.” As the bill moved through committee, it was expanded into a broader DCF omnibus bill , and the title was changed to reflect its wider scope.
While this is a common legislative process, I continue to have concerns about transparency when a bill’s title and scope change so substantially.
The bill still includes the original provisions allowing unaccompanied homeless youth age 16 and older to access key services without parental consent.
It creates a certification process where youth verified as homeless by school liaisons, shelter directors, or homeless service providers receive a standardized DCF form.
With this certification, youth may obtain: medical and dental care mental health services driver’s licenses housing school enrollment banking services The bill clarifies that DCF will act in place of a parent or guardian for these purposes.
I remain concerned about the unintended consequences , including the possibility that some youth may leave home to access services without parental involvement when the issue may be family rules or conflict rather than true homelessness.
S.193 – An act relating to establishing a forensic facility This bill establishes a forensic treatment facility for certain criminal‑justice‑involved individuals.
The goal is to provide appropriate mental health treatment, substance use disorder intervention, and other specialized care needed to prepare a person to stand trial.
Age Well Presentation We heard from Jane Catton, CEO of Age Well , who provided an overview of Older Americans Act funding.
Age Well’s programs help older Vermonters live with dignity, health, and independence — an essential part of our state’s aging‑services network.
H.293 – Health equity data reporting and registry disclosure This bill focuses on transparency, data accessibility, and research use within Vermont’s public health registries.
It aims to improve data quality while balancing public health needs with individual privacy protections.
H.660 – FY2027 Opioid Abatement Special Fund appropriations We reviewed Senate changes to the Opioid Abatement Fund.
I was disappointed that the Senate added over $1 million in additional funding for the Burlington overdose prevention site, especially given that more than $2 million has already been designated for this project.

H.566 — Sealing Court Diversion Records This bill changes how records from post‑charge court diversion programs are handled.
Instead of being expunged, records will be sealed once a person successfully completes diversion—hidden from public view but still available for limited, authorized use.
The bill also expands adult diversion to include certain municipal ordinance violations under these same sealing provisions.
H.907 — Legislative Review of State Reporting Requirements H.907 revises and extends the Legislature’s process for reviewing state reporting requirements.
It delays scheduled repeal reviews and exempts certain reports from automatic expiration.
The goal is to ensure appropriate legislative oversight of agency and program reporting while reducing unnecessary churn.
H.694 — Bennington Town Charter Amendments This bill approves amendments to the Town of Bennington’s charter regarding the removal of the Town Manager.
The changes allow a contract‑based exception to the current requirement for notice, cause, and a hearing.
This gives the Town legal authority to adopt the removal process approved by Bennington voters.
H.639 — Genetic Information Privacy Act H.639 creates new protections for Vermonters’ genetic data.
Direct‑to‑consumer genetic testing companies must obtain separate, express consent for how genetic information is collected, used, stored, shared, marketed, and destroyed.
The bill also grants consumers the right to access and delete their data, limits sharing without consent, and prohibits discrimination against individuals who exercise these rights.
