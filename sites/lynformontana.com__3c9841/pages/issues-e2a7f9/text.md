Not bureaucrats.
Not special interests.
Not politicians looking for a talking point.
Montanans hunt, fish, hike, graze, work, and raise families around these lands.
They’re part of who we are.
But too often, decisions are made far away by people who don’t understand how Montanans actually use the land.
Trails get closed.
Access gets limited.
Working uses get blocked.
Revenue that could help schools dries up.
Then homeowners are told to pay more in property taxes.
That’s not conservation.
That’s bad management.
Real conservation means protecting the land while keeping it open, healthy, and useful for Montana families.
That means local voices should matter.
It means responsible grazing and timber management.
It means reducing wildfire risk.
It means keeping access open for your family.
And it means making sure public lands serve the public.
Action Steps Return more decision-making power to Montana.
Keep trails open and accessible.
Support responsible grazing, timber, and resource management.
Protect school funding.
Oppose federal overreach and restrictive policies that lock Montanans out.
Your lands.
Your access.
Your vote.
Health Care ▼ You’re Paying More.
Care Is Getting Harder to Find.
Health care should work for the people who truly need it.
The elderly.
The disabled.
The chronically ill.
Families in crisis.
But right now, too many Montana families are paying more while getting less.
It’s harder to find a doctor.
It’s harder to get timely care.
Taxpayers are being asked to cover more and more.
And resources are being stretched in ways that don’t make sense.
That’s not fair to working families.
It’s not fair to the vulnerable.
And it’s not fair to our children, who’ll be stuck paying the bill if we don’t fix it.
We need practical reform.
Focus help on the people who truly need it.
Cut fraud and waste.
Encourage work where appropriate.
Give Montana more flexibility to set priorities that fit our state.
The goal should be simple.
Protect the vulnerable.
Respect taxpayers.
Make care easier to get.
Action Steps Prioritize Medicaid for the elderly, disabled, chronically ill, and truly needy.
Review growth in enrollment among able-bodied adults.
Control costs and reduce waste.
Support work requirements where appropriate.
Help families access care without making shortages worse.
Your health.
Your tax dollars.
Your children’s future.
Education ▼ Your Child Deserves a School That Works Parents know their children better than any system does.
Every child is different.
Some thrive in a traditional public school.
Some need a charter school.
Some need a different learning environment.
Some need more hands-on training.
Parents should have real choices.
Montana has made some progress, but families still have too few options.
That’s wrong.
Your child shouldn’t be trapped in a school that isn’t working just because of your ZIP code or because powerful interests don’t want competition.
We should strengthen public schools.
But we should also be honest.
Healthy competition and innovation help everyone.
Schools should focus on the basics: reading, writing, math, critical thinking, and preparing young people for real life.
That also means building better connections between schools and local businesses so students can find good jobs, learn skills, and avoid crushing debt.
Education should serve students and families.
Not unions.
Not bureaucracy.
Not special interests.
Action Steps Expand real school choice.
Support strong public schools and quality charter options.
Focus on reading, writing, math, and critical thinking.
Create more internships and career pathways.
Make colleges share responsibility for student loan outcomes.
Your child.
Your choice.
Your vote.
Improving Our Economy ▼ Your Paycheck Should Come First Real prosperity doesn’t come from government.
It comes from workers, small businesses, farms, ranches, builders, and families who get up every day and make Montana work.
But every new tax, fee, and regulation takes another bite out of your paycheck.
That means less money for groceries.
Less money for gas.
Less money for savings.
Less money for your kids.
Politicians love to brag about new bills they passed.
But the better question is this: What did they repeal?
What burden did they remove?
What did they do to make life easier for Montana families?
We need an economy that rewards work, supports local businesses, and gives young people a reason to stay here.
That starts with getting government out of the way.
Action Steps Cut unnecessary taxes.
Reduce burdensome regulations.
Repeal rules that slow job creation.
Support small businesses and local hiring.
Build an economy where Montana families can stay, work, and thrive.
Your job.
Your paycheck.
Your family’s future.
Rule of Law ▼ Safe Communities Don’t Happen by Accident Montana families want safe neighborhoods.
Fair rules.
Equal justice.
Common sense.
That shouldn’t be controversial.
But we’ve all seen what happens when cities stop enforcing the law.
Crime rises.
Disorder spreads.
Families feel less safe.
Police are blamed for doing their jobs.
And regular people pay the price.
We don’t need big-city experiments in Montana.
We need laws enforced fairly and consistently.
No political favoritism.
No selective enforcement.
No judges rewriting laws from the bench.
No treating public safety like a political game.
The rule of law protects everyone.
Families.
Workers.
Businesses.
Victims.
Law enforcement.
And the communities we call home.
Action Steps Fully support law enforcement and military.
Demand fair and consistent enforcement of the law.
Reject soft-on-crime policies that have failed elsewhere.
Stop judicial activism and legislative overreach.
Keep Montana communities safe, stable, and strong.
Your safety.
Your rights.
Your community.
Your vote.
