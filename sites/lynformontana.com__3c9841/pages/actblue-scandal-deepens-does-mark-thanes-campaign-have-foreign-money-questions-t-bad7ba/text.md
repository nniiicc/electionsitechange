Articles ActBlue Scandal Deepens: Does Mark Thane's Campaign Have Foreign Money Questions to Answer?
As ActBlue Executives Plead the Fifth, Mark Thane's Fundraising Platform Faces New Scrutiny The ActBlue scandal just got worse.
And Montana voters in House District 89 have a right to ask what it means for the race.
On Thursday, ActBlue co-founder Matt DeBergalis refused to answer questions from three House committees.
He pleaded the Fifth Amendment in a closed-door deposition.
He walked out after just 30 minutes.
This isn't the first ActBlue executive to take that route.
Back in June, CEO Regina Wallace-Jones did the same thing before the Committee on House Administration.
Investigators say she repeated one line over and over: she was declining to answer on the advice of counsel, citing her Fifth Amendment rights.
Here's why it matters.
ActBlue is the main fundraising platform for Democrat candidates nationwide, processing billions in donations.
Congress is investigating whether the platform's fraud prevention broke down badly enough to let foreign money flow into American campaigns.
Rep.
Jim Jordan revealed that ActBlue's own board chairman admitted roughly $# million in donations processed by the platform appeared to be of foreign origin.
Jordan's response: "I'd have taken the Fifth too." Rep.
Bryan Steil, who sat in on Thursday's deposition, said ActBlue's entire fraud prevention team has resigned or been fired.
He said it's still not clear whether the platform ever fixed the problems that let this happen.
My opponent, Rep.
Mark Thane, uses ActBlue to fund his campaign.
That's not a minor detail.
Every dollar Thane raises through ActBlue runs through a platform that two of its own top executives now refuse to explain under oath.
A platform whose board chairman has already admitted tens of millions of dollars in donations may not have come from where they were supposed to.
Montana voters deserve a candidate who's transparent about where campaign money comes from.
Right now, Mark Thane is relying on a fundraising platform that federal investigators say may be funneling foreign money into American elections, and whose leadership won't answer basic questions about it.
Lyn Hellegaard isn't the one with a platform under federal investigation.
Mark Thane is the one who needs to explain why he continues to use it.
