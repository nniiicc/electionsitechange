What We're Hearing… "Your signs are everywhere." "You're working so hard." I hear this a lot lately, and it means more than visibility.
When someone says it's good to see a sign at a house that hasn't had one before, that tells me something is shifting.
When someone says it helps to know which houses are safe, that tells me people are looking for connection — not just politics.
And yes, we are working hard.
But that work is about more than one campaign.
It reflects the kind of dedication, consistency, and neighbor-to-neighbor advocacy I'm committed to bringing to the Montana Senate.
As I wrap up follow-up from the thousands of doors knocked this cycle, one thing stands out more than anything else: people have a lot going on.
Health issues.
Aging parents.
Job changes.
Kids.
Bills.
Uncertainty.
Real life.
It's made me reflect on why I stepped up to run in the first place.
I did it because I can, because I have the support of my family, and because I believe in public service.
But I'm not separate from the challenges people are facing — I'm living them too, as a working parent trying to hold a lot together at once.
This campaign has also been touched by hard things close to home.
A longtime friend and member of our team was in a terrible accident .
Moments like that remind you quickly what matters.
One of the biggest frustrations people have with politics is the feeling that politicians aren't real people.
I get that.
I'm committed to serving and staying connected to the realities families are carrying every day.
After the failed levies , I heard both heartbreak and frustration: people who wished they could do more, and people who know our teachers deserve better.
We cannot keep setting schools up to fail by asking more and more of local property owners.
2027 needs to be a big year for education, and I’ll be a bold advocate for more state support and for Montana’s constitutional guarantee of free, quality public education.
(IR Photo) The neighborhood is buzzing about the activity off Mchugh , bordering SD 41.
For $1,750 monthly rent in a four plex to be ‘affordable,’ tenants would need to make $70,000 annually.
The State legislature can provide resources and incentivize building affordable homes, but strong wages are just as important as we address affordability here in Helena and across the State.
