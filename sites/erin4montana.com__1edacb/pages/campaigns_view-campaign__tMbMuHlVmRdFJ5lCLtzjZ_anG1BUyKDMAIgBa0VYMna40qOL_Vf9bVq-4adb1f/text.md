What I’m Hearing Around SD41 There is a clear pattern emerging across SD41.
The Missing Middle People are squeezed between rising housing costs, health care bills, and wage gaps.
Families are working hard and still feeling behind.
Independent voters are sharing stories about loved ones who cannot accept promotions without losing housing vouchers or health coverage.
These are not data points.
These are our neighbors.
Housing from Every Angle During a recent Hometown Helena, Emily McVey of United Way shared that Helena is one of the least affordable housing communities in the country.
Nationally, many early-career to midcareer workers, purchasing a home at today's prices and mortgage rates remains financially out of reach because housing supply and affordability are not keeping up.
Construction costs are rising as well.
Even $1,500 in additional builder costs can push a home out of reach for hundreds of buyers .
At the same time, we remain thousands of units below demand.
I am grateful for leaders like Mark Runkle.
We both agree that building in town near services makes housing more affordable and greener.
Health Care Costs A promising approach may be stronger consumer protections.

What I’ve Been Working On Affordability is complex.
It will take a series of real solutions working together.
Last week I sat down with Jakob Kuntz, Executive Director of Helena Habitat for Humanity .
I learned: ➡ A household earning $85,000 can afford roughly a $200,000 home.
The median home price in Helena is more than double that. ➡ The market is largely building for the highest earners, while working families are squeezed out. ➡ Affordable housing is not separate from the property tax conversation.
Smart density and more homes can broaden the tax base and stabilize long-term costs.
What can be done: ➡ Fully implement 2025 state-backed housing bond legislation. ➡ Advance a state housing tax credit. ➡ Incentivize infill and missing middle housing. ➡ Explore financing tools that lower barriers for working families, not just high-income buyers..
Innovative financing, like the Rose Hills model in East Helena, can help make more attainable homes possible.

What I’m Cooking Lately One of my favorite Helena jobs was working at The Filling Station, now The Crepery, back when it sat on the corner of Euclid and Benton at the edge of SD41.
I learned a few lessons there that still guide the campaign today: keep it simple, prepare well, and use the right tools.
At home, we don’t have a fancy crepe machine.
Just a skillet, a spreader, and a spatula.
The tools are simple, but the preparation makes all the difference.
While ALL of the topping options are so delicious, powdered sugar with a squeeze of lemon, can hit the spot.
Campaigns can feel complicated.
But we are staying grounded in those same principles: preparation, simplicity, and a steady hand.
Official crepe quality control with my son, Tupper.
Verdict: powdered sugar and lemon still wins.
