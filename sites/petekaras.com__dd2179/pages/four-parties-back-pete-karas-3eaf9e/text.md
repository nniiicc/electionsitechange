News Contact Donate Four Parties Back Pete Karas for Wisconsin Secretary of State Pete Karas has earned the endorsement of the Independence Party, making it the fourth political party to back his campaign for Wisconsin Secretary of State.
The Independence Party joins the Wisconsin Green Party, the Transhumanist Party, and the Pirate Party in supporting Karas.
These four parties do not agree on everything.
In fact, they have very different ideas about what the future should look like.
But they agree on one thing: Wisconsin voters deserve more than two choices.
For decades, voters have been told that supporting an independent or third-party candidate means wasting their vote or helping the candidate they like least.
In the race for Wisconsin Secretary of State, voters have a rare chance to reject that argument.
The office lost most of its policy-making authority decades ago.
That means voters can support Karas without worrying about changing the balance of power on major policy issues.
There is no spoiler risk.
There is no wasted vote.
There is simply an opportunity to send a message to Madison and Washington that the two-party system is not working for everyone.
“Green, Transhumanist, Pirate and Independence parties have different visions for our future, but they agree our political system has been broken by limiting our choices at the polls to two options,” Karas said.
“The people are frustrated, and this office is one place they can act on it without any downside.
No spoiler.
No wasted vote.
Just a real signal.” Richard Whitney, chairperson of the Independence Party, said the party is proud to support Karas.
“Those two parties have failed us over and over again and will continue to put their party’s interests over those of the people if we allow them to do so,” Whitney said.
Karas will face Democrat JoCasta Zamarripa and the winner of the four-way Republican primary in the general election.
His campaign takes no corporate money.
Karas is a lifelong Wisconsinite who was born and raised in Racine and now lives in Muskego.
He served three terms on the Racine City Council, earned a degree in economics from UW–La Crosse, and previously owned a bar and music venue.
As Secretary of State, Karas would advocate for making the office nonpartisan, giving it responsibility for operations at the State Capitol, and continuing to modernize Wisconsin’s state records.
Four parties have now backed his campaign.
They may not agree on every issue, but they agree that voters deserve another choice.
