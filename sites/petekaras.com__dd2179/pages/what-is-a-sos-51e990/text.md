What is a Secretary of State?
The Wisconsin Secretary of State is a statewide elected office with limited, mostly administrative responsibilities.
Unlike in many other states, the Wisconsin Secretary of State does not control elections or set policy.
The Secretary of State’s Responsibilities Include
- Authenticates official documents
- Certifies public records for legal and international use, as well as issuing apostilles.
- Custodian of the Great Seal of Wisconsin
- Authorizes its use on official state documents.
- Publishes state laws and records
- Oversees publication of legislative acts, resolutions, and the Wisconsin Blue Book.
- Maintains official state records
- Keeps gubernatorial proclamations, commissions, and other formal records.
Chairperson of the Board of Public Lands
- The Secretary of State serves as the Chairperson of the Board of Commissioners of Public Lands.
- This board manages the State Trust Fund, which supports public education.
- Responsibilities of the board include overseeing:
- Loans to local governments
- Stewardship of state trust lands
- This role is about oversight and administration, and not day-to-day land management.
What the Office Does Not Do
- Run or oversee elections
- Elections are administered by the Wisconsin Elections Commission.
- Regulate corporations or nonprofits
- Create or enforce public policy
- Control state agencies or the state budget
