Pete on No Peasants: The Rundown
Pete spent about an hour on the No Peasants panel this week, the independent show Kelly hosts for candidates who don't fit the two-party mold.
He was on alongside Doug Marsh, the Green Party's U.S.
Senate candidate in Michigan, and Michael Harbaugh out of Ohio.
We watched the whole thing.
Here's the rundown for anyone who missed it live.
Pete spent most of the hour doing what he does best: naming who's getting taken care of in Madison and who's stuck with the bill.
He reminded the panel up front that he's running with no corporate PAC money and no party backing, funded by Wisconsinites and nobody else, the same way he served three terms on the Racine City Council.
That's the lens for everything else he got into.
On the data center giveaway
Pete pointed to a bill the legislature passed last year, Republicans and Democrats together, that handed data centers a sales tax exemption on their purchases.
He said it adds up to billions in subsidies once you total it across the projects, and that the same package made it easier to fold data centers into TIF and TID districts, which lands on local property taxes.
He didn't let the governor off, either.
Evers holds the line-item veto, the Vanna White veto where he can strike individual letters until a bill says what he wants.
He could have killed this.
He didn't.
Pete's read: that's what "bipartisan" usually means in Wisconsin, both parties looking out for the same people.
He's got a name for that, the Badger Grift and Spend Award, and this is a textbook case.
On the fight to stay on the ballot
Pete walked the panel through the challenge to his nomination papers.
A Democratic-aligned challenger moved to throw out every signature the campaign collected.
Not a fraud allegation, he was clear about that.
A paperwork argument, built on a form the state was slow to update and an out-of-state circulator rule that dropped about two weeks before collection even started.
Every circulator was a Wisconsin resident, Pete noted, with their addresses printed right on the form.
The challenge got withdrawn just before the Elections Commission was set to meet.
Pete made the point that outlasts the headline: the campaign won, and it's still holding a $13,000 legal bill for defending signatures nobody ever called fake.
As he put it on the show, they can't beat us at the ballot box, so they try to drain us before we get there.
If you want to help close that gap, petekaras.com.
On election transparency
Pete worked the 2016 Wisconsin recount, and he brought the receipts.
He was careful to say he is not claiming any election was stolen.
What he said is that the recount turned up 17,800 errors in just half the state, that Wisconsin runs the most decentralized election system in the country, with local clerks plugging in memory sticks they can't independently verify, and that the voting machine code the state was supposed to release publicly still hasn't come out, even after the campaign won a lawsuit over it.
His point wasn't a conspiracy.
It was transparency.
Give people something real to check, he argued, and you take the oxygen away from anyone trying to invent a story.
It's the rare election conversation that isn't carrying water for one party or the other.
On why this race
Pete's pitch to the panel was short.
This is a statewide race a third party can actually win, which makes it the most useful vote on the ballot, not a spoiler.
A strong showing, even 20 percent, shakes the system and tells every other independent candidate the door is open.
Win it outright and the Green Party earns minor party status in Wisconsin: automatic ballot placement in 2028 and no more signature gauntlet.
He pointed to the coalition already behind him, the Greens, the Pirate Party, the Transhumanists, with more in the works.
They don't agree on everything.
They agree the duopoly has to crack first.
Not a protest vote, as Pete framed it.
A proof-of-concept.
Before the hour wrapped, he teased what's coming on the ad front, including the Karas Files spoof and one still in the works called Electile Dysfunction.
He says they're funny.
He's biased.
