Why The Secretary of State Race Matters in Wisconsin
The Office Few Talk About—But All Voters Should Understand
When people think of high-stakes political races, they often focus on governors, senators, or presidents.
But in Wisconsin, one of the most overlooked yet critical offices is the Secretary of State.
While it may not generate headlines, this position plays a vital role in defending democracy, increasing government transparency, and ensuring voters have a voice.
What Does the Wisconsin Secretary of State Actually Do?
Historically, the Wisconsin Secretary of State was responsible for a range of administrative and election-related duties.
But over the years, the state legislature stripped the office of much of its authority, handing key responsibilities to partisan appointees and diminishing its oversight role.
However, the office still holds real potential:
Custodian of State Records: The Secretary of State maintains legislative and executive documents, administrative rules, and other critical state records.
Business and Apostille Services: The office manages important business certifications and international document verifications.
Potential for Reform: With strong leadership and public pressure, the office could be restored as a watchdog over elections, ethics, and public transparency.
In other states, Secretaries of State often oversee elections and safeguard voting infrastructure.
While Wisconsin has redistributed those duties, the opportunity remains to advocate from the office and work toward meaningful, voter-centered reform.
Why the Office was Weakened
The weakening of the Secretary of State’s office wasn’t an accident.
It was a calculated decision by those in power to reduce accountability and consolidate control elsewhere.
Taking away oversight from an elected, publicly accountable office and placing it in the hands of partisan commissions undermines transparency and voter trust.
When key democratic functions are managed behind closed doors or by unelected appointees, the system starts to lose legitimacy.
The Secretary of State’s office was once a central guardian of democratic processes.
It can be that again—with the right leadership.
