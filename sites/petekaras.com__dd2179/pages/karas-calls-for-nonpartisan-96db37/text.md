Karas Calls for Nonpartisan Wisconsin Secretary of State Office
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
MUSKEGO, Wis. — Green Party candidate for Secretary of State Pete Karas is calling for the office to be made nonpartisan, arguing that a role with no real policymaking power has no business carrying a party label on the ballot.
The Wisconsin Secretary of State has been stripped of nearly all its statutory authority, starting in 1946.
Today the office runs on two full-time staff and functions mainly as a records custodian.
It doesn't write policy, doesn't vote on legislation, and doesn't run Wisconsin's elections, a job that belongs to the Wisconsin Elections Commission.
"Slapping a 'D' or an 'R' on this office doesn't tell voters anything useful," Karas said.
"It just turns a records-keeping job into another partisan fight, and it tricks people into skipping a race where their vote could actually break the two-party lock."
Karas pointed to Wisconsin's judicial races and the Superintendent of Public Instruction as existing precedent for nonpartisan statewide offices.
He argues the Secretary of State belongs on that list.
Karas is running on the Green Party ballot line, with endorsements from the Transhumanist Party, Pirate Party, and Independence Party.
He says the ballot line is a necessity of the current system, not a preference.
"I need a party line to get on the ballot," Karas said.
"But nobody after me should have to.
If this office has almost no power, a party label on it is just decoration.
Making it nonpartisan is honesty in ballot design."
Karas's campaign has centered on the argument that the Secretary of State's office is the weakest elected office of its kind in the country, making it the lowest-risk place for frustrated voters to cast a protest vote against the two-party system.
