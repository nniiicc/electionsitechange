Karas Pledges to Keep Data Center Power Lines Out of BCPL Forests
Green Party candidate for Secretary of State would use his BCPL seat to block easements serving data centers
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
August 19, 2026
Contact: vote@petekaras.com | PeteKaras.com
MUSKEGO, Wis. — Pete Karas, the Wisconsin Green Party candidate for Secretary of State, pledged today to vote against any easement that would run a new power line through Board of Commissioners of Public Lands forest or property to serve a data center.
He said he'd go further if the office let him.
“If this office had the power to ban data centers in Wisconsin, I'd ban them, full stop,” Karas said.
“We're giving away our electricity and our water and our farmland to server farms that answer to shareholders in some other state.
The office can't do that, and I'm not going to pretend it can."
The Secretary of State holds one of three seats on the BCPL, with the State Treasurer and the Attorney General.
The board manages about 75,000 acres of public trust land, most of it forest up north, held in trust for the state's public schools.
Those three commissioners decide who gets to run roads, pipelines, and power lines across it.
Wisconsin is in the middle of a data center building boom.
Meta is building in Beaver Dam, a hyperscale campus is planned for Port Washington, and Microsoft is building in Mount Pleasant, in Karas's home county of Racine.
The pressure has reached the northeast too.
Around Green Bay and the Fox Valley, data center proposals have run into steady pushback, and places like Kaukauna and Brown County have moved to limit or pause them.
A single large facility can pull as much electricity as a small city, and utilities have proposed billions of dollars in new transmission lines to feed the buildout.
Much of that lands on ordinary customers' bills.
The facilities also draw heavily on local water for cooling and swallow large tracts of land, while the servers work for companies based elsewhere.
There's one place the office can actually take action.
“They stripped most of this office down back in 1946.
What's left is a seat on the Board of Commissioners of Public Lands,” Karas said.
“If I win, and the other two commissioners make me chair, I'll vote no on any easement that puts a new data center power line through that forest.
I get one of three votes either way.
As chair, I'd set the agenda too, so I'd use both.”
He pointed to who owns the land.
“That forest was set aside for Wisconsin's schoolkids,” Karas said.
“You don't cut a power line corridor through it so a server farm somewhere can run.
That's the opposite of growing the trust for the schools.
It's one of the few real things this office can do, so I'll do it.”
Most of the trust forest sits across northern and north-central Wisconsin, from the Wausau area up through Rhinelander, with parcels that run toward Green Bay.
Data center pressure has now climbed into that same part of the state.
No transmission project before regulators crosses BCPL forest today.
Karas said his pledge covers any future one that would.
“The data centers are moving into the same stretch of the state where we hold a lot of trust forest,” Karas said.
“If this happens, I pledge to do all I can to keep data centers out of Wisconsin.”
