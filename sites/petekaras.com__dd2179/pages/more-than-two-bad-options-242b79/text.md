News Contact Donate You Deserve More Than Two Bad Options Why It’s Time to Think Beyond the Two Parties Stuck Choosing Between the Lesser of Two Evils?
Every election, we’re told the same thing: vote for the lesser of two evils.
But what if we stopped accepting evil as our only option?
For decades, the two-party system has forced Americans to choose between candidates who don’t speak to their needs, values, or communities.
In Wisconsin and across the country, millions of voters feel politically homeless—frustrated by a system that prioritizes party loyalty over real solutions.
In a time when polarization dominates and common-sense solutions are lost in the noise, many voters are left wondering: who’s actually fighting for me?
The Two-Party System Isn't Working The Democratic and Republican parties dominate every level of government.
But their grip on power hasn’t delivered the progress most voters want.
Instead, we get: Endless gridlock while problems like healthcare, education, and climate go unresolved.
Corporate influence shaping policies to benefit donors, not constituents.
Performative politics that divide communities and ignore real people.
Suppressed alternatives through ballot access laws, debate exclusions, and media blackouts.
This system doesn’t serve the people, it protects itself.
It reinforces a binary narrative, leaving no room for nuance, innovation, or voices that speak for the millions who fall outside party lines.
There’s Another Way: The Power of Independent and Third-Party Voices History shows us that real political change often comes from outside the establishment.
Movements like abolition, women’s suffrage, civil rights, and labor protections weren’t born in party headquarters—they were built by people who challenged the status quo.
Third-party and independent candidates bring new ideas, challenge groupthink, and represent the many voters who are tired of politics as usual.
They aren’t tied to party machines.
They answer to the people.
And in an age when trust in government is at a historic low, that independence is more important than ever.
Independent candidates also serve as catalysts.
They force hard conversations, elevate ignored issues, and pressure the dominant parties to evolve.
Even when they don’t win outright, they shape policy by shifting the political dialogue and inspiring new coalitions.
Pete Karas: A Different Kind of Candidate Pete Karas isn’t running to be part of a broken system.
He’s running to fix it.
As an independent candidate for Wisconsin Secretary of State, Pete isn’t beholden to either party.
He’s focused on restoring transparency, protecting democracy, and giving voters back their voice.
Pete’s record of public service, including three terms on the Kenosha Common Council, proves he’s willing to stand up to powerful interests and do what’s right—not what’s politically convenient.
His independence isn’t a talking point—it’s a track record.
From pushing for open government to fighting for everyday Wisconsinites, Pete has shown that real leadership doesn’t follow a party script.
Why Independent Leadership Matters Now More Than Ever At a time when polarization is deepening and trust in institutions is crumbling, independent leaders offer something essential: accountability to people, not parties.
They listen, respond, and govern based on values—not party platforms.
In Wisconsin, voters deserve more than two stale options every election cycle.
They deserve candidates like Pete who are bold enough to speak the truth and pragmatic enough to get things done.
As Secretary of State, Pete would focus on increasing government transparency, ensuring election integrity, and making state resources more accessible to the public—not playing political games.
Don't Let Your Vote be Taken for Granted Major parties rely on the idea that you have nowhere else to go.
