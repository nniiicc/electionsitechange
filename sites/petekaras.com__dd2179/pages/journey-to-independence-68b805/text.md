News Contact Donate Why I’m Not a Democrat or a Republican: Pete Karas’ Journey to Independence A Personal Stand Against a Broken System When people ask why I’m running for Wisconsin Secretary of State as an independent, I tell them it’s simple: I’ve spent my life working for the people, not for party interests.
I’m not a Democrat.
I’m not a Republican.
I’m someone who believes we deserve better than the false choice between two parties that have both failed to represent everyday Americans.
A Working-Class Upbringing Rooted in Community I’ve spent years rooted in Wisconsin as a public servant and advocate for everyday people.
My path has never been defined by political labels, but by my commitment to fairness, transparency, and civic responsibility.
My work on the Kenosha Common Council and in the community reflects my values: accountability, integrity, and service.
From Activist to Public Servant My political journey didn’t start in party headquarters.
It began with local activism and a commitment to grassroots change.
Over the years, I’ve worked to advance transparency, advocate for working people, and push for reforms that reflect the needs of everyday Wisconsinites.
I’ve always believed that real change happens from the ground up—when people come together to challenge entrenched power.
That same passion drove me to public office.
I served three terms on the Kenosha Common Council, where I was known for asking tough questions, pushing for transparency, and standing up to powerful interests.
I was never afraid to rock the boat if it meant doing what was right.
Why I Left the Party Labels Behind Over time, I saw how both major parties began to mirror each other’s failures.
The Democrats talked a good game about workers and the marginalized, but repeatedly prioritized big donors and establishment politics.
The Republicans claimed to stand for freedom, but often worked to restrict rights and centralize power.
Neither side was willing to fix the rigged system they both benefited from.
Neither welcomed independent thinkers.
And neither offered real solutions to the problems everyday Wisconsinites face: stagnant wages, voter suppression, government secrecy, and corporate overreach.
I realized that meaningful reform wouldn’t come from within either party.
So I left them both behind.
Why I'm Running for Secretary of State The Wisconsin Secretary of State may not be a glamorous position, but it’s a powerful one.
It should be a watchdog office—a check on power, not a cog in a political machine.
Right now, both major parties see this office as a tool for political positioning.
I see it as a platform to protect democracy, improve transparency, and restore trust in state government.
As an independent, I’m not bound by party platforms or talking points.
I’m accountable only to the people of Wisconsin.
That’s what public service should be.
Breaking the Duopoly, One Vote at a Time People often tell me they feel politically homeless.
They’re tired of voting for the "lesser of two evils." They want leaders who listen, act with integrity, and speak hard truths.
That’s what this campaign is about.
We don’t have corporate PACs or national party support.
What we do have is people—Wisconsinites who are ready to shake up the system and demand better.
