News Contact Donate Karas: Drop the School Fund Appeal and Put the Money Back FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE September 24, 2026 Contact: vote@petekaras.com | PeteKaras.com MUSKEGO, Wis. — Green Party candidate for Secretary of State Pete Karas is calling on Milwaukee County and the State of Wisconsin not to appeal the court ruling that struck down the Milwaukee County school-fund carve-out, and he's blaming both major parties for letting it happen in the first place.
Earlier this month, a Waukesha County judge ruled the provision unconstitutional and voided it.
It had let Milwaukee County keep money from certain vehicle fines that otherwise goes to the Common School Fund, which pays for books, technology, and other materials in Wisconsin's public-school libraries.
The amount at stake was about $2.2 million a year.
"The court got it right.
Don't waste taxpayer money appealing it, put the money back, and be done," Karas said.
"And end it now.
This was a circuit-court ruling, so until an appeals court settles it, any county can try the same move.
Drag this out and every school library in Wisconsin is the next target." Karas said the bigger question is how the provision got this far.
"Republicans put it in the budget.
A Democratic governor signed it.
Milwaukee County took the money.
Then a board of two Democrats and a Republican decided to ask the Legislature to fix the problem instead of going to court," Karas said.
"Everybody had a chance to stop this, and nobody did." The Board of Commissioners of Public Lands, which oversees the Common School Fund, includes Secretary of State Sarah Godlewski, State Treasurer John Leiber, and Attorney General Josh Kaul.
In September 2025, Godlewski and Leiber approved a resolution calling for a legislative solution before litigation.
Kaul abstained.
"The Legislature created the problem, and the board's answer was to ask the Legislature to fix it," Karas said.
"Then a private law firm and a taxpayer had to go to court and do what the people responsible for the fund wouldn't." Karas said the episode is why he's running as a Green.
The Secretary of State holds limited policy power but sits on the board that guards the Common School Fund, a place he says a third-party voice can do real good without handing either major party more power.
"If you're a trustee, protect the trust.
Don't wait for the party that wrote the problem, and don't wait for the party that signed it.
Do the job," Karas said.
"Neither party is going to break a system that's working for both of them.
