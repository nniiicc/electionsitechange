Before most people figure out what they want to do with their lives, I was dispatching police and fire calls for Carroll County Sheriff’s Office and Wolfeboro Police Department.
I moved on to NH E911 and Lewiston-Auburn 911, fielding emergencies and learning — fast — what it looks like when government works and when it doesn’t.
Starting that job at 18 matured him quickly.
I saw the best and worst of what public institutions can do for people.
Building a career the hard way.
Transitioning into information technology around 2011, I earned my Bachelor’s in IT Management from Granite State College, and worked my way up to IT Director at the Cliff House in Cape Neddick, Maine.
I didn’t stop there — while working full time I earned his Master’s degree in Cybersecurity from Southern New Hampshire University, graduating with a 3.76 GPA, now working in IT for a global firm.
The through-line of my career is solving problems that affect real people — systems that either work or fail the humans depending on them.
Rochester is home.
I moved to Rochester in 2016.
I’m married with three kids who keep me busy every day.
I deal with the same property tax bills, the same energy costs, the same school decisions every other Rochester family faces.
That’s not a talking point — it’s Tuesday.
Why he’s running.
The NHGOP has spent years passing laws that create confusion and hardship for ordinary Granite Staters — from the botched vehicle inspection repeal, to voucher schemes that drain public school funding, to energy policies written by utility lobbyists.
I’m not a career politician.
I’m someone who has spent my life in service roles — dispatch, IT infrastructure, cybersecurity — understanding that systems either serve the people who depend on them, or they don’t.
I’m running to make Strafford District 19’s representation work for everyone in it: left, right, and center.
The shared human experience is the one thing we all have in common.
