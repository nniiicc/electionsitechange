My focus is on practical, measurable solutions that improve affordability, strengthen our communities, and make government more accountable.
Below are the priorities I will bring to the State House.
FISCAL RESPONSIBILITY Fiscal Responsibility That Works for Residents Fiscal Responsibility That Works for Resident New Hampshire’s revenues are strong, but rising costs show that budget performance alone isn’t enough.
The focus should be on whether policy decisions are improving affordability for residents—not just balancing the state budget.
My approach: Prioritize spending that delivers measurable outcomes Increase transparency and accountability in how funds are used Avoid policies that shift long-term costs to municipalities or taxpayers What this means for you: State policy should reduce financial pressure—not shift it elsewhere.
EDUCATION Protecting Public Education Public education is a core part of every community in New Hampshire.
Recent expansion of Education Freedom Accounts (RSA 194-F) has redirected public education funding into private and alternative education settings, including households without demonstrated financial need.
The priority should remain strengthening the system that serves the majority of students.
My approach: Maintain a strong and stable funding base for public schools Ensure transparency and accountability in how education funds are used Evaluate education policy based on outcomes and long-term impact What this means for you: A public education system that remains strong, reliable, and accessible to every family.
PROPERTY TAXES Property Tax Stability New Hampshire’s reliance on property taxes creates increasing and unpredictable pressure on homeowners, particularly when property valuations increase rapidly.
Adjustments to local spending controls do not fully address the unpredictability residents experience year to year.
My approach: Limit sharp increases in property assessments Improve predictability in year-to-year tax impact Protect homeowners, especially those on fixed incomes What this means for you: Greater stability in housing costs and less risk of being priced out of your home.
ENERGY Energy Cost Accountability Energy bills are rising across New Hampshire, and recent changes to rate-setting structures have not consistently reduced costs for ratepayers.
Ensuring affordability requires stronger accountability and clearer alignment between utility performance and customer outcomes.
My approach: Strengthen oversight of utility rate-setting processes Ensure performance-based models prioritize affordability and reliability Increase transparency in how energy costs are determined Simple principle: If your bill goes up, executive compensation should reflect that—not ignore it.
CANNABIS Cannabis Reform New Hampshire is currently the only state in New England without a regulated cannabis market.
As a result, revenue is leaving the state while neighboring systems continue to expand.
My approach: Legalize and regulate cannabis responsibly Keep revenue within New Hampshire Establish clear rules that prioritize public safety and economic benefit What this means: A controlled system that reflects current realities and keeps economic activity in-state.
GOVERNANCE Clear, Effective Lawmaking Legislation should be understandable, practical, and focused on clear, measurable results Complex or rapidly changing policies can create uncertainty for residents, municipalities, and businesses.
My approach: Prioritize clear and enforceable legislation Focus on outcomes rather than intent alone Reduce unnecessary complexity in state policy What this means: Government that is easier to understand, predict, and rely on.
WORKFORCE Obtaining a better balance New Hampshire’s economy depends on both strong businesses and residents being able to afford to live here.
Current policy often treats these as separate goals, but they are directly connected.
If businesses are growing while workers still struggle with affordability, the system is not aligned.
My Approach If we expect businesses to contribute to the state, we should also provide clear and measurable ways for them to reduce that burden by investing in New Hampshire workers.
This includes: incentives for businesses that support employee student loan repayment tax credits tied to workforce training and certification programs policies that encourage hiring and retaining workers within the state These incentives should be targeted, measurable, and directly tied to workforce outcomes—not broad and disconnected tax reductions.
What This Means for You Businesses would still have a path to remain competitive, but those benefits would be linked to real investment in people.
Workers would have more opportunities for training, advancement, and long-term stability, including support for education-related debt.
Over time, this creates a more balanced system where economic growth is more closely tied to affordability and stability for the people living in New Hampshire.
