Data centers are not just warehouses for computers they are becoming part of a growing surveillance infrastructure that concentrates unprecedented amounts of personal data and power in the hands of government agencies and large corporations.
These facilities consume massive amounts of electricity and water, contribute to environmental concerns through energy use and electronic waste, and are increasingly being built on productive farmland that should remain available for food production and local communities.
Too often, developers receive special tax incentives while residents face higher utility costs, strained infrastructure, and reduced local control.
Ohioans should not have their movements routinely tracked, photographed, and stored simply for driving on public roads.
While technology can assist law enforcement, public safety should never come at the expense of privacy, due process, and protection from warrantless surveillance.
As Governor, Don would work with the General Assembly to restrict or prohibit government use of automated license-plate surveillance systems, establish strong limits on the collection and sharing of vehicle-location data, and prevent state resources from funding mass-surveillance networks.
Ohioans should be treated like free people not suspects whose daily movements belong in a government-accessible database.
