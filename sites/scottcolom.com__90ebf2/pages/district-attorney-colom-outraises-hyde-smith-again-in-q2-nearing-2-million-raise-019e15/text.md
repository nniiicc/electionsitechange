As Colom rolls out his “Corruption Costs Mississippi” plan, the contrast is clear: his campaign is funded by Mississippians, hers by the Washington special interests she answers to
District Attorney Colom: “Cindy Hyde-Smith answers to the special interests writing her checks.
I answer to the people of Mississippi.
That’s why this quarter, once again, they out-gave the corruption machine funding her”
Colom outraised Hyde-Smith outright in direct contributions, and his campaign committee ($659,376) even outraised hers ($431,280).
Every dollar Colom raised came from donors who chose to give — not from money shuffled in through Washington committee transfers.
Hyde-Smith, meanwhile, again spent almost as much as she raised.
“Mississippians are betting on this campaign because they know the difference between a senator who works for herself and for Washington, and a prosecutor who will work for the people,” said District Attorney Colom.
“Cindy Hyde-Smith answers to the special interests writing her checks.
I answer to the people of Mississippi.
That’s why this quarter, once again, they out-gave the corruption machine funding her.”
Colom’s haul was driven by 3,769 individual donors in Q2 giving an average contribution of $138 — with nearly a third of those donors, some 1,205 Mississippians, giving from inside the state.
Across the campaign, 11,492 individuals have now contributed since launch, the kind of broad, small-dollar base that reflects a genuine grassroots movement rather than a Washington rolodex.
The numbers land in the middle of Colom’s Corruption Costs Mississippiagenda rollout, unveiled Tuesday outside the Lowndes County Courthouse where he has served as district attorney for a decade.
There, Colom laid out his plan to root out the corruption in Washington and made a prosecutor’s case that Hyde-Smith has spent her time in office working for herself and her donors instead of the people who sent her.
The fundraising tells the same story from the other direction: while Hyde-Smith’s money comes from the special interests she protects, Colom’s comes from the Mississippians he’s fighting for.
“Every dollar in this campaign is a Mississippian saying they’re ready for a senator who actually shows up for them,” said District Attorney Colom.
“We’re going to keep earning that trust, one neighbor at a time, all the way to November, because Mississippi Matters.”
