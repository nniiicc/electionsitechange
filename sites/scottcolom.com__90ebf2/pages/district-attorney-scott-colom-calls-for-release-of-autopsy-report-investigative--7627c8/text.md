Jackson, MS — Today, U.S.
Senate candidate and District Attorney Scott Colom released the following statement regarding developments in the Nolan Wells case:
“Nolan Wells was 18 years old, and the grand jury’s decision not to bring any charges does not answer the parents’ questions about what happened to their son.
Twenty-three Jackson County residents sat on that grand jury, heard from 43 witnesses, and took the work seriously.
But a process that ends with a cause of death listed as undetermined is not the same thing as an answer, and no family should be asked to accept one in place of the other without an opportunity to review the evidence for themselves.
“What has driven this case from the beginning is not a shortage of theories, but a shortage of trust.
When people do not believe a system will tell them the truth, speculation fills the space where facts should be.
Full transparency is the first step toward rebuilding that trust.
Within the parameters allowed by law, the state should publicly release the State Medical Examiner’s full autopsy report and the sheriff’s complete investigative file, so people can read the record, including phone records and eyewitness statements, and judge it for themselves.
I have done this before: when a case in my own district ended in a way my community could not accept, I put the investigative file on my website for anyone to read.
“I am not the prosecutor on this case, and I will not speculate about evidence I have not seen.
But I have spent ten years working to build trust between Mississippi communities and the people who enforce the law, and it gets built exactly one way: by showing people the work instead of asking them to take it on faith.
That work does not end with this report.
Families can survive a hard answer.
What destroys them is silence, and our state has lived with too much of it.
“My heart goes out to the entire Wells family and those who knew Nolan.
Nadia and I will continue to keep the family in our thoughts and prayers.”
