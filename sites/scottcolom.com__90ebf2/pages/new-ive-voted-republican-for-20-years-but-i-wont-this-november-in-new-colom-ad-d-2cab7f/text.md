Senate nominee Scott Colom launched “ Lost Her Way ,” a new ad running statewide across digital and connected television.
In it, Mark Vines, a Southaven businessman who has voted Republican for 20 years, explains in his own words why he won’t vote for Cindy Hyde-Smith this November: her vote to block the release of the Epstein files.
On Sept.
10, 2025, the Senate voted 51 to 49 to kill a measure that would have forced the Justice Department to release the files.
Two Republicans broke with their party to vote for release — Cindy Hyde-Smith voted to kill it .
A year later, Vines hosted Colom in Hernando so a group of DeSoto County Republicans could sit across the table from him and ask about it directly.
And the fight over those files isn’t over: in August, the Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as Attorney General by a single vote, 50 to 49, at 4:17 in the morning, and Cindy Hyde-Smith voted yes .
Now, Blanche’s Justice Department is asking a federal appeals court to pause a judge’s orders on the Epstein records.
“If Cindy Hyde-Smith will not stand up against child abuse, she won’t stand up for Mississippi,” Vines says in the ad.

Senate) The ad arrives as Hyde-Smith’s campaign turns to attack ads against Colom rather than answering for her own record.
“Cindy Hyde-Smith is lying about me because she can’t defend her own record.
I’ve spent my career putting sex offenders and violent criminals in prison, no matter their party or their background.
But when Cindy had the chance to hold predators accountable by forcing the Epstein files into the open, she voted to keep them sealed,” said District Attorney Colom.
“Now Republicans who’ve voted for her for years are telling Mississippi the truth about her.
