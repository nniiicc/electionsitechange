transparency in the General Assembly and why the Healing Through History Act, a bipartisan bill to help adoptive families access important parts of a child’s foster care history, was left behind.
After hearing Melissa’s story, Senator Hills filed SB 2895 to ensure families have access to the information they need to better understand a child’s history and support their healing.
Melissa later came to Springfield to testify in support of the legislation.
The bill passed the Senate Child Welfare Committee and the Illinois Senate unanimously.
But despite unanimous bipartisan support, it was assigned to the House Rules Committee and never received a hearing or vote.
When legislation that helps children and families can be stopped without ever being considered, it raises serious questions about transparency and accountability in Springfield.
Senator Hills remains committed to fighting for children, families, and a legislative process that puts people before politics.
