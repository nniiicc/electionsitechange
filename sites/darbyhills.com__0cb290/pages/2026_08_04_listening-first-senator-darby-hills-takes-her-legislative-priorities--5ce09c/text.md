While the General Assembly may be out of session, Senator Darby Hills is spending her summer where she believes it matters most, walking neighborhoods, knocking on doors, and hearing directly from the people she represents.
Joined by Team Darby volunteers, Hills has been visiting communities across the 26th Senate District to have face-to-face conversations with residents about the challenges they are experiencing and how state government can better serve them.
“Our best ideas don’t come from Springfield, they come from the people living these challenges every day,” Hills said.
“There is simply no substitute for sitting down with a family on their front porch and hearing their story firsthand.”
One issue has risen above nearly every other conversation: the growing burden of property taxes.
Throughout the district, Hills has met homeowners who say rising property taxes are threatening their ability to remain in their homes.
Some residents have shared that they were forced to take out reverse mortgages simply to keep up with their tax bills, while others worry they may eventually be priced out of the communities they’ve called home for decades.
“These conversations are heartbreaking,” Hills said.
“Families who have worked hard their entire lives should not have to wonder whether they’ll be able to afford to stay in their own homes.
Illinois has an affordability crisis, and property taxes are a major part of it.”
Those conversations reinforce Hills’ commitment to advancing legislation that provides meaningful property tax relief and strengthens long-term housing affordability.
During the 104th General Assembly, Hills has sponsored and co-sponsored numerous measures aimed at reducing the tax burden on homeowners, including:
- SB 2745 – Property Tax Relief for Disabled Persons
- SB 3844 – PTELL Aggregate Extension Reform
- SB 0216 – PTELL Reform
- SB 1644 – Homeowner Property Tax Relief
- SB 2246 – Property Tax Assessment Limit
- SB 3401 – Condo Reserve Study Requirements
- SB 3540 – Property Tax Growth Limits
- SB 3781 – Refundable Property Tax Credit
- SB 3782 – Fairness in Property Tax Foreclosure Task Force
- SB 3848 – Homeowner Insurance Premium Deduction
- SB 3849 – Homestead Exemption CPI Adjustment
- SB 3868 – Property Tax Credit Expansion
Residents have also expressed growing concerns about the rapid expansion of large-scale data centers near residential neighborhoods.
Many voiced frustrations over limited public notice and the lack of meaningful opportunities for surrounding communities to weigh in before major development decisions are made.
In response to those concerns, Hills filed Senate Bill 1050, legislation designed to strengthen public notice requirements and require written consent from neighboring municipalities before a data center could be constructed within three miles of their boundaries.
The proposal is intended to improve transparency and ensure nearby communities have a meaningful voice in the planning process.
“Responsible development and community input should go hand in hand,” Hills said.
“Families deserve transparency, meaningful public engagement, and a seat at the table before decisions of this magnitude are made.”
Another topic frequently raised at the doorstep has been neighborhood safety, particularly the increasing use of electric bicycles and other micromobility devices by children and teenagers.
Hills co-sponsored SB 3484, bipartisan legislation establishing common-sense statewide safety standards for e-bikes and other micromobility devices.
The bill passed both chambers of the Illinois General Assembly and is currently awaiting action by Governor JB Pritzker.
“As a mom, legislator, and community member, I’ve heard from parents, local officials, and law enforcement about the challenges created by rapidly evolving technology and a lack of clear rules,” Hills said.
“E-bikes and scooters can be a great way for families to get outside and stay active, but our laws need to keep pace with how these devices are being used.
SB 3484 creates a consistent statewide framework, provides clearer expectations for riders, and gives law enforcement the tools they need to address unsafe operation.”
Beyond property taxes and public safety, Hills says nearly every conversation comes back to one common theme: the rising cost of living.
To help make Illinois more affordable for working families, Hills has sponsored and co-sponsored legislation that would reduce everyday costs, including:
- SB 2687 – Home Utility Relief Act, creating utility rebates for families struggling with rising utility bills.
- SB 2763 – ACA Premium Tax Deduction, providing income tax relief for qualifying health insurance premiums and medical expenses.
- SB 3792 – Income Tax Deduction for Tipped Wages.
- SB 3851 – Prescription Drug Tax Elimination, removing the state sales tax on prescription drugs and cancer treatments while protecting local government revenue.
- SB 3869 – Diaper Tax Exemption, eliminating the state sales tax on diapers for infants, children, and adults.
- SB 4205 – Temporary Motor Fuel Tax Reduction to provide relief at the gas pump.
For Hills, the purpose of the summer listening tour extends beyond gathering feedback.
She says every conversation helps shape the priorities she takes back to Springfield.
“Whether someone is worried about property taxes, public safety, development in their community, or simply making ends meet, I want them to know their voice matters,” Hills said.
“I’ll continue meeting with as many residents as possible because the best way to represent our district is to listen first and then fight for solutions.”
Hills and Team Darby will continue walking neighborhoods throughout the 26th Senate District in the coming weeks as part of her ongoing commitment to remain accessible and responsive to the people she serves.
