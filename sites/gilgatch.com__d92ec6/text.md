Gil Gatch is a fighter for truth, liberty, and the rule of law in the courtroom, in the State House, and in the community.

Both jobs come down to the same thing: showing up for people when it matters.
He brings that same conviction to the South Carolina House, where he represents District 94 and the families of Summerville.
His approach is straightforward: read the bill, ask the hard question, and vote the way he told folks back home he would.

Gil's priorities
Here are the priorities Gil continues to fight for to benefit House District 94.
Power our future
South Carolina's growth is outpacing its energy generation.
Build on the Energy Security Act with proactive planning that keeps power reliable, saves families money, and prepares our grid for our children and grandchildren.
Hold insurers accountable
Pass a South Carolina Insurance Transparency Act requiring insurers to show ratepayers how they set prices and justify rising costs before asking lawmakers to make it easier to deny claims.
Axe the income tax
Eliminate South Carolina's income tax and let earnings stay with the families and businesses that produced them.
Defend parental rights
Reform the family court system so parents keep their say in how their own children are raised.
Support small businesses
As hemp regulations move forward, protect South Carolina's small businesses while keeping these products out of children's hands.
Invest in safer roads
Dedicate funding to road projects that alleviate traffic, improve commutes, and increase road safety for families across House District 94.
Fuel the fight
Support principled leadership in the South Carolina House and help build a stronger community for everyone.

When Columbia had a surplus, the easy vote was to spend it.
Gil voted to send it back to the people who earned it, cutting the income tax faster than the law required, year after year.

- 7% → 6%.
The top rate has come down every year since 2022, reaching the state's 6% goal two years early.

Gil backed speeding up the cuts instead of waiting on Columbia's timeline.
