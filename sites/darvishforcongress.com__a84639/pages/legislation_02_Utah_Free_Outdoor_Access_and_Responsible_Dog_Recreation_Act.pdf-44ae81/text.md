A BILL To preserve free ordinary access to public outdoor recreation, create responsible dog-access pilots in designated watershed areas, and coordinate State, local, and Federal land-management policy.
Be it enacted by the Legislature of the State of Utah: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'Utah Free Outdoor Access and Responsible Dog Recreation Act'.
SEC.
2.
POLICY It is the policy of the State to keep ordinary hiking, trail, and day-use recreation broadly accessible while protecting drinking-water quality, wildlife, public safety, and sensitive natural resources.
SEC.
3.
FREE ORDINARY ACCESS A State agency or participating local government may not impose a general admission, hiking, or day-use fee solely for ordinary pedestrian access to a public trail, overlook, canyon, or similar recreation area unless the fee is specifically authorized by statute for a documented maintenance, capacity, or resource-protection need.
The State shall prioritize replacement funding so that parking-management costs do not become a de facto general trail-access fee.
SEC.
4.
PRIORITY ACCESS AREAS The State shall coordinate with Salt Lake, Summit, and Wasatch Counties and relevant municipalities concerning Guardsman Pass, Bloods Lake, Bonanza Flat, Empire Pass, Donut Falls, Big Cottonwood Canyon, Little Cottonwood Canyon, and similarly situated high-use areas.
SEC.
5.
FEDERAL-LAND COORDINATION The Governor and Utah congressional delegation are requested to pursue cooperative agreements, appropriations, or Federal legislation to reduce or replace ordinary access fees on Federal lands where State law cannot directly control the fee.

6.
RESPONSIBLE DOG RECREATION PILOT The Division of Outdoor Recreation, in consultation with drinking-water and land-management authorities, shall establish limited dog-access pilots on selected trails where water-quality and wildlife conditions can be protected.
Pilot rules may require a leash not exceeding 6 feet, designated trails, no entry into streams or reservoirs, immediate waste removal, seasonal restrictions, permits or education where justified, and targeted closure of locations where monitoring demonstrates unacceptable risk.
SEC.
7.
WATERSHED SAFEGUARDS Dog access shall not be authorized in a manner that violates applicable drinking-water law.
Agencies shall use monitoring and scientific review to distinguish site-specific risks from blanket exclusions and may suspend a pilot when objective water-quality criteria are exceeded.
SEC.
8.
UTAH OUTDOOR ACCESS FUND There is created a restricted account known as the Utah Outdoor Access Fund.
Money may be appropriated for trail maintenance, sanitation, parking operations, shuttle service, water-quality monitoring, waste stations, and public education to reduce reliance on ordinary access fees.
SEC.
9.
ENFORCEMENT A violation of leash, waste, water-entry, or designated-trail rules may be enforced by warning, civil citation, permit suspension, or other proportionate administrative measures established by rule.
SEC.
10.
REPORTING Participating agencies shall report annually on visitor use, fee collections, maintenance costs, dog-related incidents, water-quality results, and recommended continuation, expansion, modification, or closure of pilot programs.
SEC.
11.
LOCAL AND FEDERAL AUTHORITY Nothing in this Act compels a Federal agency to take an action beyond State jurisdiction or prevents a local government from adopting more protective site-specific rules supported by publicsafety, watershed, or wildlife findings.
SEC.
12.
SEVERABILITY If any provision of this Act is held invalid, the remainder shall not be affected.
SEC.
13.
EFFECTIVE DATE This Act takes effect on May 7 following enactment, except that rulemaking may begin immediately.
