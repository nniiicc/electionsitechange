DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED Bobby Darvish - Working Legislative Draft DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED UTAH FAMILY PROSPERITY, HOMEOWNERSHIP, AND ECONOMIC FREEDOM ACT OF 2026 A BILL To increase the ability of Utah families to build wealth, expand the supply of attainable housing, reduce unnecessary taxes and regulatory costs, protect private property, encourage entrepreneurship and investment, and promote a competitive free-market economy.
Be it enacted by the Legislature of the State of Utah: SECTION 1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the "Utah Family Prosperity, Homeownership, and Economic Freedom Act of 2026." SEC.
2.
PURPOSE The purposes of this Act are to (1) dramatically expand Utah's housing supply; (2) reduce the government-imposed cost of constructing and purchasing homes; (3) make starter homes economically viable again; (4) protect private property rights; (5) increase disposable household income; (6) encourage saving, investment, entrepreneurship, and ownership; (7) reduce regulatory barriers faced by Utah businesses; (8) prevent government policies that artificially restrict housing supply; (9) encourage Utah residents to acquire appreciating assets and business ownership; and (10) create conditions favorable to long-term household wealth creation.
SEC.
3.
FINDINGS The Legislature finds that (1) Homeownership can provide families with an important mechanism for accumulating household equity and intergenerational wealth.
(2) Utah faces housing-affordability constraints and housing-supply pressures that affect households in many communities.
(3) Increasing housing supply is an important component of reducing upward pressure on housing costs.
(4) Land prices, regulatory delays, permit costs, impact fees, construction costs, financing costs, and restrictions on housing density can increase the final cost of housing.
(5) State housing policy should emphasize increasing lawful housing production and removing unnecessary barriers rather than imposing general price controls.
(6) Sustainable prosperity is promoted by private property, competitive markets, predictable regulation, entrepreneurship, savings, investment, and responsible government finance. www.darvishforcongress.com Page 1 DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED Bobby Darvish - Working Legislative Draft SEC.
4.
UTAH HOUSING FREEDOM ZONES Municipalities and counties shall designate appropriate areas in which residential construction shall receive expedited approval. (a) BY-RIGHT HOUSING.
Residential construction complying with objective health, safety, infrastructure, and building standards shall be permitted by right.
Approval may not be conditioned upon subjective architectural, aesthetic, political, or neighborhood-preference standards. (b) STARTER HOMES.
Local governments shall permit construction of smaller detached starter homes on appropriately serviced residential parcels.
A municipality may establish reasonable health and safety standards but may not establish minimum house sizes substantially exceeding applicable building-code requirements solely for the purpose of restricting smaller homes. (c) TOWNHOUSES AND SMALL MULTIFAMILY HOUSING.
Local governments shall provide reasonable areas in which duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, townhomes, courtyard housing, cottage developments, and similar ownership-oriented housing types may be constructed. (d) ACCESSORY DWELLING UNITS.
An owner of a qualifying residential property may construct at least one accessory dwelling unit subject to reasonable health, safety, parking, utility, and building standards.
SEC.
5.
THIRTY-DAY RESIDENTIAL PERMIT GUARANTEE A local government receiving a complete application for construction of a code-compliant single-family home, townhouse, duplex, or accessory dwelling unit shall approve or deny the application within 30 calendar days.
If an application is denied, the government shall provide the applicant with a written description of each deficiency.
Once all identified deficiencies are corrected, the application shall receive expedited reconsideration.
A government may not restart the statutory review period through repetitive requests for information that is immaterial to determining compliance.
SEC.
6.
ONE-STOP PERMITTING Every municipality and county issuing residential development permits shall establish a unified permitting process permitting an applicant to coordinate zoning, building review, engineering, utilities, fire review, transportation review, and other required local approvals through a single application portal.
Government agencies shall coordinate internally rather than requiring applicants to repeatedly transmit identical information between government departments.
SEC.
7.
IMPACT-FEE REFORM (a) Impact fees shall be limited to the reasonable proportionate cost of infrastructure directly attributable to new development. (b) Impact fees may not be used as a general revenue source. (c) Every impact fee shall be publicly itemized. (d) A municipality imposing an impact fee shall publish the methodology used to calculate the fee. (e) Fees collected for a specific infrastructure purpose shall be expended for that purpose within a reasonable statutory period or refunded as provided by law.
SEC.
8.
STARTER-HOME IMPACT-FEE RELIEF Local governments shall be authorized and encouraged to reduce or defer impact fees applicable to owner-occupied starter homes.
Any deferred fee shall be structured so that the cost does not become an immediate barrier to construction or homeownership.
Preference shall be given to permanent reductions in underlying government costs rather than taxpayer-funded subsidies.
SEC.
9.
PARKING FLEXIBILITY FOR QUALIFYING HOUSING A local government may not require excessive off-street parking as a condition of approving residential development when adequate street or shared parking exists; the property is located near significant transit; the property owner voluntarily proposes fewer parking spaces; or the requirement would materially prevent otherwise lawful residential construction. www.darvishforcongress.com Page 2 DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED Bobby Darvish - Working Legislative Draft Private developers remain free to provide as much parking as market demand supports.
SEC.
10.
PROPERTY-RIGHT PROTECTION No local government may impose a land-use restriction that deprives an owner of substantially all economically reasonable residential use of property without providing the process and compensation required by the Utah Constitution and the United States Constitution.
Nothing in this section eliminates reasonable regulations protecting public health, safety, infrastructure, water resources, or property rights of neighboring owners.
SEC.
11.
NO STATEWIDE RENT CONTROL Neither the State nor a political subdivision may establish government price controls on lawful residential rent except as expressly authorized by subsequent enactment of the Legislature.
Nothing in this section limits enforcement of leases, fraud laws, habitability requirements, contractual obligations, or generally applicable consumer-protection laws.
SEC.
12.
REGULATORY COST DISCLOSURE Any State agency proposing a regulation reasonably expected to increase housing construction costs shall publish the estimated cost per affected housing unit; the statutory authority supporting the regulation; reasonably available lower-cost alternatives; and an explanation of why the regulation is necessary.
SEC.
13.
REGULATORY OFFSET REQUIREMENT Except for emergency, public-health, public-safety, or federally required regulations, a State agency proposing a regulation imposing substantial new private-sector compliance costs shall identify existing regulatory burdens that may be eliminated, simplified, or reduced.
The Legislature intends that unnecessary regulatory burdens upon Utah households and businesses decline over time.
SEC.
14.
UTAH FIRST-HOME SAVINGS ACCOUNTS Utah shall establish tax-advantaged First-Home Savings Accounts.
A Utah resident may contribute money to a qualifying account for a home down payment; closing costs; construction of a first home; purchase of residential land for a principal residence; or substantially improving a principal residence.
Investment earnings retained and used for qualifying purposes shall receive the maximum State tax preference permitted by law.
Accounts may invest in lawful diversified financial assets rather than being restricted to ordinary bank deposits.
SEC.
15.
FAMILY INVESTMENT ACCOUNTS The State shall establish an optional tax-preferred investment framework permitting Utah families to save and invest for homeownership, education, retirement, business formation, and long-term household wealth.
The program shall emphasize private investment and individual ownership rather than government management of investment assets.
SEC.
16.
CAPITAL FORMATION AND SMALL-BUSINESS OWNERSHIP Utah shall simplify State securities, registration, and business requirements to the maximum extent consistent with fraud prevention and investor protection to facilitate lawful investment in Utah startups, small businesses, employee-owned businesses, family businesses, and community enterprises.
SEC.
17.
OCCUPATIONAL LICENSING FREEDOM No occupational license shall be required unless the State demonstrates that licensing is reasonably necessary to protect the public from substantial health, safety, or financial harm. www.darvishforcongress.com Page 3 DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED Bobby Darvish - Working Legislative Draft Where adequate consumer protection can be achieved through registration, bonding, insurance, certification, inspection, or ordinary civil and criminal law, the State shall use the less restrictive alternative.
SEC.
18.
UNIVERSAL LICENSING RECIPROCITY A person holding a substantially equivalent occupational license in good standing in another State shall be eligible for expedited Utah recognition unless Utah demonstrates a material public-safety reason for additional requirements.
Military spouses and veterans shall receive expedited processing.
SEC.
19.
HOME-CONSTRUCTION WORKFORCE FREEDOM State agencies shall review licensing and certification requirements affecting residential construction for unnecessary barriers to entry.
The review shall include electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, contractors, engineers, architects, inspectors, and skilled construction trades.
Health and safety standards shall remain enforceable.
SEC.
20.
SALES-TAX RELIEF FOR NEW STARTER-HOME CONSTRUCTION The Legislature shall establish targeted relief from State-imposed sales taxes on qualifying materials incorporated into newly constructed owner-occupied starter homes.
The benefit shall be conditioned upon reasonable mechanisms designed to ensure that savings flow toward reducing the cost of the qualifying home.
Luxury residential construction shall not qualify.
SEC.
21.
PROPERTY-TAX TRANSPARENCY Every Utah property-tax notice shall clearly state the previous year's tax; the proposed tax; the percentage increase; each taxing entity responsible for the increase; and the date and location of any hearing concerning the increase.
No taxing authority may characterize a tax increase resulting from increased property valuations as automatic or unavoidable when the authority possesses legal discretion to adjust its rate.
SEC.
22.
PRIMARY-RESIDENCE PROPERTY-TAX PROTECTION The Legislature shall establish additional protections against sudden increases in the property-tax burden on owner-occupied primary residences while maintaining constitutionally required uniformity and adequate funding of essential local services.
Any mechanism adopted under this section shall avoid shifting disproportionate costs onto first-time homebuyers.
SEC.
23.
GOVERNMENT LAND INVENTORY The State, counties, municipalities, school districts, special districts, and other political subdivisions shall identify government-owned parcels that are no longer reasonably necessary for a public purpose and may be suitable for residential or mixed-use development.
Such property may be offered through transparent competitive disposition procedures.
Preference may be established for proposals producing owner-occupied starter housing, provided competition and taxpayer value are preserved.
SEC.
24.
FEDERAL LAND COORDINATION The Governor and Attorney General are authorized to petition the Federal Government for appropriate transfer, exchange, or development of federally controlled parcels adjoining developed Utah communities when development would not materially impair designated national parks, wilderness areas, monuments, or environmentally sensitive land; adequate water and infrastructure exist or can reasonably be provided; the affected local government supports the project; and the transaction provides appropriate value to taxpayers.
SEC.
25.
INFRASTRUCTURE FOLLOWS GROWTH Transportation, water, sewer, electricity, and other infrastructure funding shall prioritize areas in which local governments have adopted policies allowing substantial housing construction. www.darvishforcongress.com Page 4 DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED Bobby Darvish - Working Legislative Draft The State shall not subsidize infrastructure restrictions whose principal purpose is preventing otherwise lawful residential development.
SEC.
26.
WATER-SMART HOUSING Nothing in this Act requires approval of development lacking a lawful and sustainable water supply.
Local governments may require reasonable water-conservation measures, but such requirements shall be technologically neutral whenever practicable and shall not be used as a pretext for unnecessarily prohibiting housing.
SEC.
27.
SMALL-BUSINESS REGULATORY BILL OF RIGHTS A Utah small business is entitled to clear notice of applicable regulations; reasonable opportunity to correct non-dangerous first-time violations; timely permit decisions; written explanations for government denials; access to administrative appeal; protection from duplicative regulation; and equal treatment regardless of political affiliation or viewpoint.
SEC.
28.
NO CORPORATE WELFARE WITHOUT PUBLIC DISCLOSURE No State economic-development incentive benefiting an individual corporation shall be granted without public disclosure of the value of the benefit; performance obligations; projected employment; applicable clawback provisions; and the government entity approving the transaction.
The policy of the State should favor broad-based tax treatment over selective governmental favoritism.
SEC.
29.
TAXPAYER SURPLUS DIVIDEND When unrestricted recurring State revenue materially exceeds the amount necessary to fund enacted appropriations, constitutionally required reserves, and prudent emergency reserves, the Legislature shall consider returning excess revenue through broad-based tax reductions or taxpayer rebates rather than automatically establishing new permanent spending programs.
SEC.
30.
STATE SPENDING DISCIPLINE Growth in recurring State spending should not exceed sustainable long-term growth in Utah's population and economy except when the Legislature expressly determines that an emergency or overriding public need warrants additional spending.
One-time revenue should ordinarily be used for debt reduction, infrastructure, reserves, one-time tax relief, or other one-time obligations rather than creating unsupported recurring expenditures.
SEC.
31.
DEBT REDUCTION AND INTEREST SAVINGS The State shall prioritize prudent reduction of high-cost public debt when doing so provides taxpayers with greater long-term value than maintaining the obligation.
Savings generated through debt reduction should, where practicable, be reflected in future tax or spending decisions.
SEC.
32.
PROHIBITION ON UNFUNDED MANDATES The State shall not impose a substantial new financial obligation upon a municipality or county without identifying the anticipated cost; identifying a funding source; or allowing reasonable local flexibility in implementation.
SEC.
33.
COMPETITIVE ENERGY POLICY Utah policy shall encourage reliable, abundant, competitively priced energy from lawful sources.
State energy policy should evaluate affordability, reliability, security, land impact, environmental requirements, and long-term system cost rather than favoring a particular technology solely through political preference.
SEC.
34.

35.
HOUSING-COST SCORECARD The Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity shall publish an annual Utah Housing Cost Scorecard showing, to the extent reliable data are available, median home price; median rent; residential permits; time required for permit approval; estimated housing shortage or surplus; average impact fees; construction costs; homeownership rates; first-time-homebuyer affordability; property-tax burden; and housing production by municipality.
The report shall identify government-imposed costs separately where reasonably measurable.
SEC.
36.
MUNICIPAL FREEDOM AND ACCOUNTABILITY REPORT The State shall publish objective information allowing Utah residents to compare municipalities based upon housing permits; permit-processing times; taxes; impact fees; business-license costs; housing production; infrastructure performance; and regulatory burdens.
No municipality shall receive an ideological rating or political score under this section.
SEC.
37.
PROTECTION AGAINST SPECULATIVE VACANCY TAXES The State and its political subdivisions may not impose a special tax merely because a lawful privately owned residential property is temporarily vacant.
Ordinary property taxation, nuisance law, building codes, and generally applicable laws remain enforceable.
SEC.
38.
FREEDOM TO BUILD FOR FAMILY MEMBERS Subject to reasonable health, safety, utility, and building standards, residential property owners may construct lawful accessory housing intended for parents, grandparents, adult children, caregivers, disabled family members, or other lawful occupants.
Government shall not require proof of familial relationship as a condition of ordinary residential occupancy where such a restriction would otherwise violate law.
SEC.
39.
HOME-BASED BUSINESS FREEDOM A resident may operate a nonhazardous home-based business that does not create substantial traffic, noise, pollution, safety hazards, or material interference with neighboring property.
A local government may adopt reasonable regulations addressing actual external impacts but may not prohibit a home occupation merely because commercial activity occurs in a residence.
SEC.
40.
NO GENERAL WEALTH TAX Neither the State nor a political subdivision may impose an annual tax measured solely by a person's net wealth or unrealized appreciation unless subsequently authorized through express legislation satisfying applicable constitutional requirements.
SEC.
41.
NO TAX ON UNREALIZED GAINS Utah shall not impose State individual income tax on an unrealized capital gain merely because the market value of an asset increased while the taxpayer continued to own the asset.
SEC.
42.
FAMILY BUSINESS SUCCESSION The Legislature shall minimize State tax and administrative barriers that unnecessarily force the liquidation of farms, ranches, family businesses, or other productive assets solely because ownership transfers between generations.
Existing creditor rights and lawful tax obligations remain enforceable.
SEC.
43.

44.
SEVERABILITY If any provision of this Act or application thereof is held invalid, the remainder of the Act shall not be affected.
SEC.
45.
EFFECTIVE DATE This Act shall take effect July 1, 2027, except that agencies and political subdivisions may begin rulemaking and administrative preparations immediately upon enactment.
