DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED Bobby Darvish - Working Legislative Draft DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE FREEDOM, DECENTRALIZATION, AND COMPETITION ACT OF 2026 A BILL To preserve a decentralized and competitive artificial intelligence ecosystem in the United States; to prevent excessive concentration of artificial intelligence infrastructure, models, information, and decision-making authority; to prohibit the establishment of a centralized governmental authority with general control over artificial intelligence; to protect freedom of speech, privacy, innovation, interoperability, and consumer choice; and for other purposes.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SECTION 1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the "Artificial Intelligence Freedom, Decentralization, and Competition Act of 2026" or the "AI Freedom and Decentralization Act." SEC.
2.
FINDINGS Congress finds the following: (1) Artificial intelligence has the potential to become foundational infrastructure for communications, commerce, national security, scientific research, education, medicine, transportation, entertainment, and the exchange of information.
(2) Concentration of artificial intelligence capabilities in a single government agency, corporation, consortium, computing provider, model provider, or small number of gatekeepers may create systemic dependencies and single points of failure.
(3) A competitive ecosystem consisting of independent developers, open-source projects, universities, small businesses, nonprofit organizations, researchers, cloud providers, hardware manufacturers, and competing artificial intelligence companies strengthens resilience and innovation.
(4) No person, corporation, or governmental entity should possess the practical ability to determine, by virtue of exclusive control over artificial intelligence infrastructure, what lawful information Americans may access, produce, communicate, research, or create.
(5) Artificial intelligence policy should protect freedom of speech, privacy, due process, competition, technological innovation, consumer choice, intellectual property, cybersecurity, and national security.
(6) The Federal Government should not establish a centralized artificial intelligence authority possessing general licensing, censorship, or operational control over the development and lawful use of artificial intelligence throughout the United States.
(7) Artificial intelligence infrastructure should, where technically and economically feasible, support interoperability, portability, competition, multiple providers, and the ability of users and developers to change providers without unreasonable restrictions.
(8) Existing antitrust and competition authorities should be vigorously available to address unlawful monopolization, collusion, exclusionary practices, anticompetitive tying, and other unlawful restraints of competition involving artificial intelligence.
(9) Public policy should regulate unlawful conduct and demonstrable harms rather than establish governmental control over the underlying technology or lawful computation itself.

SEC.
4.
DEFINITIONS (a) ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE.
The term "artificial intelligence" means a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, generates predictions, recommendations, classifications, decisions, content, or other outputs from received inputs. (b) GENERAL-PURPOSE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE.
The term "general-purpose artificial intelligence" means an artificial intelligence model or system capable of performing a broad range of tasks and capable of being incorporated into multiple applications. (c) ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE INFRASTRUCTURE.
The term "artificial intelligence infrastructure" includes computing resources, cloud services, specialized processors, model-hosting services, model distribution platforms, application programming interfaces, training infrastructure, and other technologies substantially necessary for the development or deployment of artificial intelligence. (d) COVERED AI GATEKEEPER.
The term "covered AI gatekeeper" means a person or entity possessing substantial market power over one or more essential layers of artificial intelligence infrastructure such that the entity has the practical ability to materially restrict competitors' access to customers, computing capacity, model distribution, or other essential inputs.
Market power shall be determined under established principles of Federal antitrust law and shall not be inferred solely from the size or success of an enterprise. (e) INTEROPERABILITY.
The term "interoperability" means the ability of a system, where technically feasible, to exchange information or operate with another system through documented and commercially reasonable interfaces or standards. (f) OPEN-SOURCE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE.
The term "open-source artificial intelligence" means artificial intelligence for which model components, software, source code, weights, documentation, or other significant components are made publicly available under terms permitting substantial inspection, modification, research, or redistribution.
SEC.
5.
PROHIBITION ON A CENTRALIZED FEDERAL ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AUTHORITY (a) GENERAL PROHIBITION.
No department, agency, commission, office, corporation, or instrumentality of the Federal Government may establish or operate a governmental authority having general jurisdiction to license, approve, deny, control, or prohibit the development or deployment of general-purpose artificial intelligence solely because such technology constitutes artificial intelligence. (b) NO UNIVERSAL AI LICENSE.
The Federal Government may not require a person to obtain a general Federal artificial intelligence license merely to (1) develop an artificial intelligence model; (2) train an artificial intelligence model; (3) possess model weights; (4) research artificial intelligence; (5) publish lawful artificial intelligence research; (6) distribute an artificial intelligence model; (7) operate lawful artificial intelligence software; or (8) provide lawful artificial intelligence services. (c) NO COMPUTE PERMISSION SYSTEM.
No Federal department or agency may establish a general system requiring prior governmental permission merely to acquire or use computing resources for lawful artificial intelligence development. www.darvishforcongress.com Page 2 DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED Bobby Darvish - Working Legislative Draft (d) RULE OF CONSTRUCTION.
Nothing in this section prevents enforcement of generally applicable laws concerning criminal activity, fraud, cybersecurity, privacy, intellectual property, export controls, national security, consumer protection, civil rights, child exploitation, or regulated professional activities.
SEC.
6.
PROTECTION OF OPEN-SOURCE AND INDEPENDENT AI DEVELOPMENT No Federal agency may prohibit or materially discriminate against an artificial intelligence system solely because the system is open-source, independently developed, locally operated, or not controlled by a major artificial intelligence provider.
Nothing in this section shall require the Federal Government to purchase, deploy, or approve a system that fails applicable cybersecurity, national-security, reliability, or procurement requirements.
SEC.
7.
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE COMPETITION PROTECTIONS (a) UNLAWFUL EXCLUSIONARY CONDUCT.
A covered AI gatekeeper may not use substantial market power to unlawfully (1) prevent customers from using competing artificial intelligence models or providers; (2) condition access to essential artificial intelligence infrastructure upon an agreement not to use a competitor; (3) impose contractual restrictions whose primary purpose or effect is to prevent lawful switching between competing providers; (4) retaliate against customers solely for using competing artificial intelligence services; (5) use control of one essential layer of artificial intelligence infrastructure to unlawfully foreclose competition in another layer; (6) enter into agreements to divide artificial intelligence markets, customers, technologies, or territories in violation of Federal antitrust law; or (7) coordinate with competitors to unlawfully suppress competing artificial intelligence technologies. (b) EXISTING ANTITRUST LAW.
Nothing in this Act shall weaken or supersede the Sherman Act, the Clayton Act, the Federal Trade Commission Act, or any other Federal antitrust law.
SEC.
8.
INTEROPERABILITY AND PORTABILITY A covered AI gatekeeper shall provide commercially reasonable means, where technically feasible and consistent with cybersecurity and intellectual-property protections, for customers to (1) export customer-provided data; (2) retrieve customer-created prompts and outputs where retained by the provider; (3) transfer commonly used machine-readable data to competing services; and (4) connect competing products through documented interfaces when such access is ordinarily made available to the gatekeeper's own substantially similar products.
Nothing in this section requires disclosure of proprietary model weights, trade secrets, security credentials, classified information, or intellectual property unrelated to the customer's own information.
SEC.
9.
FEDERAL PROCUREMENT AND MULTI-VENDOR RESILIENCE (a) POLICY.
It shall be the policy of the Federal Government to avoid unnecessary dependence upon a single commercial artificial intelligence provider. (b) COMPETITIVE PROCUREMENT.
For significant Federal artificial intelligence deployments, agencies shall, to the maximum extent practicable (1) use competitive procurement; (2) evaluate products from multiple independent providers; (3) avoid unnecessary vendor lock-in; (4) maintain the technical ability to replace a provider when commercially and operationally reasonable; (5) favor documented interoperability standards; and (6) develop continuity plans addressing the failure, compromise, or withdrawal of a major artificial intelligence provider. (c) EXCEPTIONS.
An agency may depart from subsection (b) when required by national security, classified operations, cybersecurity, an emergency, unique technical requirements, or a documented determination that multiple-provider architecture is technically infeasible or would impose disproportionate cost.

PROHIBITION ON GOVERNMENT AI CENSORSHIP (a) PROTECTED SPEECH.
No Federal agency may use artificial intelligence, or direct a private artificial intelligence provider to use artificial intelligence, for the purpose of suppressing lawful speech protected by the First Amendment. (b) INDIRECT ACTION.
A Federal agency may not evade subsection (a) by coercing, threatening, directing, or materially pressuring a private entity to suppress constitutionally protected speech that the Federal Government could not lawfully suppress directly. (c) LAWFUL GOVERNMENT FUNCTIONS.
Nothing in this section prevents enforcement of criminal law; protection of classified information; cybersecurity operations; moderation of speech on government-controlled systems consistent with the Constitution; counterintelligence activities authorized by law; or communications in which a governmental entity merely identifies potentially unlawful activity without coercing suppression of lawful speech.
SEC.
11.
PROTECTION AGAINST CENTRALIZED AI SURVEILLANCE No Federal agency may establish a general artificial intelligence system for persistent surveillance, profiling, or tracking of Americans in violation of the Constitution or applicable Federal law.
Artificial intelligence shall not be construed as creating any new governmental surveillance authority.
Any governmental use of artificial intelligence for surveillance shall remain subject to applicable constitutional requirements, statutory authority, warrant requirements, judicial process, and congressional oversight.
SEC.
12.
HUMAN ACCOUNTABILITY No Federal officer or employee may avoid legal responsibility for an official governmental decision by asserting that an artificial intelligence system made or recommended the decision.
When Federal law requires a decision to be made by an authorized official, responsibility shall remain with the authorized official.
SEC.
13.
NO ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SOVEREIGNTY No artificial intelligence system operated by or on behalf of the Federal Government may exercise sovereign governmental authority independently of human officials legally accountable under the Constitution and laws of the United States.
No artificial intelligence system may independently (1) enact a law; (2) issue a binding regulation; (3) impose a criminal sentence; (4) authorize the use of military force; (5) issue a final judicial judgment; (6) levy a Federal tax; or (7) exercise another sovereign power constitutionally assigned to an elected, appointed, or judicial officer.
SEC.
14.
OPEN STANDARDS AND DECENTRALIZED ARCHITECTURE The Secretary of Commerce, acting through the National Institute of Standards and Technology, may facilitate voluntary, consensus-based technical standards supporting interoperability, portability, decentralized artificial intelligence architectures, cybersecurity, resilience, authentication, privacy-enhancing technology, open technical protocols, and the ability to operate artificial intelligence systems locally or across multiple providers.
Standards developed pursuant to this section shall be voluntary unless separately required under authority expressly granted by an Act of Congress.
SEC.
15.
PROTECTION OF SMALL DEVELOPERS, RESEARCHERS, AND STARTUPS Federal artificial intelligence policy shall, where practicable, consider the compliance burden placed upon individuals, academic researchers, nonprofit organizations, open-source developers, small businesses, and startup companies.

16.
NO GOVERNMENT-MANDATED IDEOLOGICAL OUTPUT The Federal Government may not require a privately developed general-purpose artificial intelligence system to express, suppress, endorse, or oppose a political, religious, philosophical, or ideological viewpoint as a condition of lawful development or general availability, except to the extent necessary to comply with otherwise applicable law.
Nothing in this section shall restrict the right of a private developer to determine the lawful design, values, behavior, or content policies of its own product.
SEC.
17.
NO FEDERAL AI SOCIAL-CREDIT SYSTEM The Federal Government may not establish an artificial intelligence-driven social-credit system that assigns individuals a generalized government score based upon lawful speech, lawful associations, political beliefs, religious beliefs, or other constitutionally protected activities for the purpose of determining access to unrelated government rights, benefits, or privileges.
SEC.
18.
ENFORCEMENT (a) DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE.
The Attorney General, acting through the Antitrust Division and other appropriate components of the Department of Justice, may bring a civil action to enforce sections of this Act within the Department's jurisdiction. (b) FEDERAL TRADE COMMISSION.
The Federal Trade Commission may enforce applicable commercial provisions of this Act under its existing statutory authority. (c) NO NEW CENTRALIZED AI REGULATOR.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to establish an independent Federal artificial intelligence commission, ministry, licensing board, or centralized artificial intelligence regulator. (d) CIVIL REMEDIES.
A court of competent jurisdiction may award appropriate equitable relief, including injunctions, orders requiring cessation of unlawful conduct, and other remedies authorized by law.
SEC.
19.
WHISTLEBLOWER PROTECTION An employee or contractor of the Federal Government may not be retaliated against for making a lawful disclosure to Congress, an Inspector General, or other authorized oversight body concerning unlawful AI-enabled surveillance; unlawful AI-enabled censorship; concealment of significant AI system failures; violations of this Act; or efforts to unlawfully circumvent the decentralization requirements of this Act.
Nothing in this section authorizes disclosure of classified information except through channels authorized by law.
SEC.
20.
STATE AUTHORITY Nothing in this Act shall prevent a State from enforcing generally applicable laws concerning fraud, privacy, consumer protection, civil rights, criminal conduct, professional services, or other matters traditionally within State authority.
A State may not establish a general licensing requirement that effectively prohibits the interstate development or distribution of general-purpose artificial intelligence solely because a person develops or distributes artificial intelligence, except where expressly authorized by Federal law.
SEC.
21.
NATIONAL SECURITY Nothing in this Act shall require disclosure of classified information; limit lawful export controls; prohibit restrictions on artificial intelligence technologies provided to foreign adversaries; impair lawful intelligence or counterintelligence activities; prohibit cybersecurity requirements for Federal systems; or restrict military artificial intelligence systems otherwise authorized by law.

RULE OF CONSTRUCTION Nothing in this Act shall be construed to create immunity for conduct that would otherwise constitute fraud, theft, extortion, stalking, unlawful discrimination, exploitation of children, intellectual-property infringement, unauthorized access to computer systems, unlawful surveillance, conspiracy, terrorism, trafficking, violation of export-control laws, or any other criminal or civil violation under otherwise applicable law.
The principle established by this Act is that lawful artificial intelligence technology itself shall not be subjected to centralized governmental control merely because it is artificial intelligence.
SEC.
23.
SENSE OF CONGRESS It is the sense of Congress that Artificial intelligence should remain an ecosystem of competing technologies rather than evolve into a single system controlled by one government, corporation, or small group of gatekeepers.
America should seek resilience through decentralization, competition through open markets, security through diversity of systems, and liberty through the continued distribution of technological power.
No institution should possess unilateral control over what lawful ideas Americans may explore, what lawful information they may access, what lawful software they may build, or what lawful artificial intelligence technologies they may use.
The future of artificial intelligence should remain distributed among the American people, innovators, researchers, businesses, universities, nonprofit organizations, and competing technologies rather than concentrated in a single center of power.
SEC.
24.
SEVERABILITY If any provision of this Act, or the application of such provision to any person or circumstance, is held unconstitutional or otherwise invalid, the remainder of this Act and the application of its provisions to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected.
SEC.
