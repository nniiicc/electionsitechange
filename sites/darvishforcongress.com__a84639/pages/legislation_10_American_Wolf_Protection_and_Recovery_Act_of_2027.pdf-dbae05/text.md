DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED AMERICAN WOLF PROTECTION AND RECOVERY ACT OF 2027 Consolidated draft - supersedes three prior wolf-protection drafts LEGISLATIVE DRAFT - NOT INTRODUCED.
This document consolidates prior working drafts prepared in earlier conversations.
It is provided for discussion and drafting purposes and has not been verified by legislative counsel.
A BILL To prohibit recreational and trophy killing of wolves independent of Endangered Species Act classification, establish recovery and delisting safeguards, support nonlethal conflict prevention and rancher compensation, and maintain habitat connectivity and genetic diversity.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'American Wolf Protection and Recovery Act of 2027'.
SEC.
2.
FINDINGS AND PURPOSE Congress finds that wolves are ecologically significant native predators whose long-term conservation depends on connected populations, genetic diversity, science-based monitoring, conflict prevention, and predictable rules.
The purpose of this Act is to establish a durable national prohibition on recreational and trophy killing while preserving narrowly tailored authority for human safety, veterinary care, conservation, and verified livestock conflict.
SEC.
3.
NATIONAL RECREATIONAL AND TROPHY KILLING BAN No person may intentionally hunt, trap, snare, poison, bait, pursue, or kill a wolf for recreation, sport, trophy, contest, bounty, or fur harvest in a manner subject to Federal jurisdiction, except as expressly authorized under section 7.
SEC.
4.
PROTECTION INDEPENDENT OF ENDANGERED SPECIES ACT CLASSIFICATION The prohibition in section 3 applies regardless of whether a wolf population is listed, delisted, or reclassified under the Endangered Species Act of 1973.
A change in Federal listing status does not authorize recreational or trophy killing prohibited by this Act.
SEC.
5.
FEDERAL PROTECTION AND DELISTING SAFEGUARDS The Secretary of the Interior shall use the best available science in administering Federal protections for gray wolves and other listed wolf populations.
A wolf population may not lose Federal protection merely because a State authorizes recreational killing.
Any listing, reclassification, or delisting decision shall consider recovery criteria, population resilience, habitat connectivity, and genetic exchange as required by applicable law.

6.
NATIONAL RECOVERY AND CONSERVATION PLAN The Secretary shall maintain or develop science-based recovery and conservation planning addressing population monitoring, habitat, prey, migration corridors, genetic connectivity, population exchange, tribal consultation, cross-border conservation, coexistence with rural communities, and voluntary conservation agreements with willing landowners.
SEC.
7.
LIMITED EXCEPTIONS (a) Human safety.
A person may act where reasonably necessary to stop an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury. (b) Veterinary care.
A licensed veterinarian or authorized wildlife officer may humanely euthanize a severely injured, diseased, or suffering wolf. (c) Research and conservation.
The Secretary may authorize capture, handling, relocation, translocation, or take for legitimate scientific, recovery, or conservation purposes. (d) Verified livestock or domestic-animal conflict.
Authorized wildlife officials may relocate or, when reasonable nonlethal options have failed or are impracticable, lethally remove an individually identified wolf responsible for verified repeated depredation.
SEC.
8.
NONLETHAL CONFLICT PREVENTION AND RANCHER COMPENSATION The Secretary of Agriculture and the Secretary of the Interior shall provide grants, technical assistance, or cooperative support for range riders, fladry, guard animals, fencing, husbandry changes, carcass management, relocation, and comparable nonlethal methods.
Federal programs shall provide prompt and fair compensation for verified livestock losses caused by wolves.
SEC.
9.
HABITAT, CORRIDORS, AND GENETIC DIVERSITY Federal land and wildlife programs shall consider habitat connectivity, migration corridors, population exchange, genetic diversity, and population resilience.
Nothing in this section authorizes acquisition of private property without the owner's consent except under otherwise applicable law.
SEC.
10.
STATE AND TRIBAL LAW A State or tribe may provide greater protection for wolves.
A less protective State rule does not authorize conduct prohibited by this Act where Federal jurisdiction exists.
Nothing in this Act diminishes lawful tribal treaty rights.
SEC.
11.
PENALTIES AND ENFORCEMENT A knowing violation of section 3 is subject to civil penalties established by regulation, restitution for unlawfully taken wildlife, forfeiture where otherwise authorized by law, and any additional penalties available under applicable Federal wildlife law.

12.
RULE OF CONSTRUCTION Nothing in this Act diminishes existing protections for the Mexican gray wolf or another listed canid, limits legitimate defense of human life, requires lethal management where effective nonlethal management is available, or prevents emergency wildlife action otherwise authorized by law.
SEC.
13.
SEVERABILITY If any provision of this Act is held invalid, the remainder shall not be affected.
SEC.
14.
EFFECTIVE DATE This Act takes effect 30 days after enactment.
