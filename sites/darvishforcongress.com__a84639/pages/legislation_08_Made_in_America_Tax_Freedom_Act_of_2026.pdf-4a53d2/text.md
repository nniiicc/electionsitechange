A BILL To encourage domestic manufacturing through a Federal point-of-sale rebate tied to qualifying State and local purchase taxes on fully American-made and American-assembled products.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'Made in America Tax Freedom Act of 2026'.
SEC.
2.
PURPOSES The purposes are to reward domestic manufacturing and supply chains, provide consumers a transparent price incentive for qualifying products, prevent deceptive origin claims, and respect State taxing authority through a voluntary Federal rebate rather than a Federal sales tax mandate.
SEC.
3.
DEFINITIONS “Fully American-Made Product” means a product qualifying under Federal Trade Commission “Made in USA” standards as all or virtually all made in the United States and meeting any additional domestic-content criteria established under this Act.
“American-Assembled Product” means a product substantially assembled in the United States from domestic and foreign components but not qualifying as Fully American-Made.
“Imported Product” means a product not qualifying for either category.
SEC.
101.
FULL REBATE FOR FULLY AMERICAN-MADE PRODUCTS At the point of sale, an eligible purchaser of a Fully American-Made Product shall receive a Federal rebate equal to 100 percent of the covered State and local sales, use, or comparable transaction tax otherwise imposed on that purchase.
SEC.
102.
PARTIAL REBATE FOR AMERICAN-ASSEMBLED PRODUCTS An eligible purchaser of an American-Assembled Product shall receive a Federal rebate equal to 50 percent of the covered State and local sales, use, or comparable transaction tax otherwise imposed on that purchase.
SEC.
103.
IMPORTED PRODUCTS No rebate under this Act is available for an Imported Product.
The ordinary State and local tax rules remain unchanged.

104.
POINT-OF-SALE IMPLEMENTATION Treasury shall establish a system permitting participating retailers and payment processors to apply the rebate instantly at purchase or, where necessary, through a rapid post-purchase credit.
Receipts shall separately identify the underlying tax and Federal rebate.
SEC.
105.
RETAILER REIMBURSEMENT Treasury shall reimburse participating retailers for valid rebates, including reasonable administrative costs established by regulation, while using anti-fraud controls and audit authority.
SEC.
201.
VOLUNTARY STATE PARTNERSHIP States may voluntarily share tax-rate and product-category data or enter cooperative agreements to simplify administration.
A State is not required to change its tax base, rate, collection mechanism, or exemption structure.
SEC.
202.
ANTI-COMMANDEERING RULE Nothing in this Act commands a State or local government to impose, repeal, reduce, increase, or administer any tax.
The rebate is a Federal expenditure program.
SEC.
301.
CERTIFICATION The Federal Trade Commission, Department of the Treasury, and U.S.
Customs and Border Protection shall establish interoperable origin-certification procedures using existing “Made in USA” standards where practicable.
SEC.
302.
MARKINGS AND CONSUMER DISCLOSURE A seller claiming eligibility shall provide a clear qualifying designation in the product listing or point-of-sale system and retain documentation supporting the designation.
SEC.
303.
ONLINE AND MARKETPLACE SALES Online retailers and marketplace facilitators may participate and shall provide the same origin disclosure, rebate calculation, and recordkeeping required for physical retailers.
SEC.
401.
FALSE CLAIMS It is unlawful to knowingly misrepresent a product’s origin or assembly status to obtain a rebate.
Treasury and the Federal Trade Commission may recover improper payments and impose civil penalties.
SEC.
402.
PENALTIES A person who knowingly engages in a material scheme to obtain rebates through false origin certifications is subject to repayment, a civil penalty of up to three times the improper rebate, exclusion from the program, and other penalties available under Federal fraud law.

403.
RECORDKEEPING Participating retailers and certifying manufacturers shall keep supporting records for not less than 5 years, subject to reasonable small-business safe harbors established by regulation.
SEC.
501.
ANTI-EVASION No person may divide, relabel, minimally process, or route an imported product through the United States principally to evade the domestic-origin requirements of this Act.
SEC.
502.
SUBSTANTIAL ASSEMBLY Treasury and the Federal Trade Commission shall define substantial assembly using factors such as transformation, labor, manufacturing steps, component value, and the location of final production, while preventing trivial assembly from qualifying.
SEC.
601.
REPORTS Treasury shall report annually to Congress on rebate expenditures, consumer use, domesticcontent patterns, geographic distribution, fraud findings, administrative costs, and any measurable effects on domestic manufacturing investment.
SEC.
701.
RULEMAKING Not later than 12 months after enactment, Treasury, the Federal Trade Commission, and U.S.
Customs and Border Protection shall issue implementing regulations.
SEC.
702.
SEVERABILITY If any provision of this Act is held invalid, the remainder shall not be affected.
SEC.
703.
EFFECTIVE DATE The rebate program shall begin on the first January 1 occurring at least 12 months after final implementing regulations are issued.
