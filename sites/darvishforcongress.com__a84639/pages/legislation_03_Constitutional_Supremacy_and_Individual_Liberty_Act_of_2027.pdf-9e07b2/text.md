A BILL To reaffirm constitutional supremacy, protect lawful criticism of religion, prohibit coercive religious punishments, and protect individual choices involving religion, food, alcohol, and companion animals.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'Constitutional Supremacy and Individual Liberty Act of 2027'.
SEC.
2.
FINDINGS AND PURPOSE The Constitution is the supreme law governing governmental coercion in the United States.
Religious belief and voluntary religious practice are protected, but no religious, customary, or private rule may be given governmental effect in a manner that violates constitutional rights, criminal law, due process, or equal protection.
SEC.
3.
PROTECTION OF SPEECH CONCERNING RELIGION The government may not punish or disadvantage a person merely for peaceful criticism, rejection, satire, or condemnation of a religion, religious doctrine, or religious political ideology, including speech characterized by others as Islamophobic, anti-Christian, anti-Jewish, anti-religious, or similar, provided that otherwise applicable laws concerning true threats, incitement, harassment, defamation, violence, or crime remain enforceable.
SEC.
4.
CONSTITUTIONAL SUPREMACY OVER RELIGIOUS LAW No Federal, State, local, tribal, religious, arbitral, or private decision may be enforced by a governmental actor to the extent enforcement would deny a nonwaivable constitutional or statutory right.
Voluntary religious arbitration remains lawful where ordinary contract and public-policy safeguards are satisfied.
SEC.
5.
PROHIBITION ON COERCIVE HUDUD AND SIMILAR PUNISHMENTS No person may impose or threaten stoning, amputation, flogging, execution for apostasy or blasphemy, forced marriage, or comparable corporal or criminal punishment under color of religious, customary, or private authority.
Conduct already criminal under Federal or State law remains punishable under that law.

6.
FREEDOM OF FOOD CHOICE No governmental entity may prohibit a competent adult from purchasing, possessing, serving, or consuming lawful food, including pork, solely to enforce a religious dietary rule.
Voluntary observance and private religious dietary practices remain protected.
SEC.
7.
FREEDOM OF LAWFUL ALCOHOL CHOICE No governmental entity may prohibit otherwise lawful purchase, possession, or consumption of alcohol solely to enforce a religious prohibition.
This section does not limit generally applicable alcohol, age, licensing, impaired-driving, health, or public-safety laws.
SEC.
8.
COMPANION DOGS AND FAMILY PROTECTION No person may injure, seize, poison, or unlawfully exclude a lawfully kept companion dog solely because a religious doctrine regards the animal as impure or undesirable.
Generally applicable animal-control, housing, disability, sanitation, and public-safety laws remain in force.
SEC.
9.
RIGHT TO LEAVE OR CHANGE RELIGION Every person has the right to adopt, reject, leave, or change religion without governmental penalty and without violence, confinement, coercion, or deprivation of civil rights by another person.
SEC.
10.
NO RELIGIOUS CRIMINAL JURISDICTION No private or religious body may exercise criminal jurisdiction, impose criminal sentences, or operate a coercive penal tribunal within the United States.
Nothing in this section prohibits internal religious discipline that is voluntary, nonviolent, and without purported governmental force.
SEC.
11.
EQUAL APPLICATION This Act applies without regard to the religion, denomination, ideology, ethnicity, national origin, sex, or identity of any person or organization.
SEC.
12.
CIVIL REMEDY A person subjected to governmental enforcement in violation of this Act may seek declaratory or injunctive relief and any remedy otherwise available under Federal civil-rights law.
This Act does not create damages against private religious belief or protected speech.
SEC.
13.
RULE OF CONSTRUCTION REGARDING RELIGIOUS FREEDOM Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prohibit peaceful worship, religious clothing, prayer, dietary observance, voluntary arbitration, religious education, or other conduct protected by the First Amendment or Federal religious-liberty statutes.

14.
SEVERABILITY If any provision of this Act or its application is held invalid, the remainder shall not be affected.
SEC.
15.
EFFECTIVE DATE This Act takes effect 180 days after enactment.
