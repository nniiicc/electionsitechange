DRAFT LEGISLATION - NOT INTRODUCED EVIDENCE, DUE PROCESS, AND FALSE ALLEGATION ACCOUNTABILITY ACT OF 2027 Consolidated draft - supersedes the earlier False Accusation Accountability and Due Process Act LEGISLATIVE DRAFT - NOT INTRODUCED.
This document consolidates prior working drafts prepared in earlier conversations.
It is provided for discussion and drafting purposes and has not been verified by legislative counsel.
A BILL To protect the right to report crime, require evidence-based and sex-neutral government procedures, create accountability for proven deliberate fabrication, and provide restitution, record correction, sealing procedures, and due-process safeguards.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'Evidence, Due Process, and False Allegation Accountability Act of 2027'.
SEC.
2.
PURPOSE This Act protects genuine victims and good-faith reporters while establishing safeguards against deliberate fabrication, biased investigation, and unjust governmental deprivation based solely on accusation.
It consolidates the prior False Accusation Accountability and Due Process Act into this Act.
SEC.
3.
FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE An accusation is evidence to be investigated, but it is not by itself proof of guilt.
Government action shall remain consistent with probable cause, due process, the presumption of innocence, and the applicable burden of proof.
SEC.
4.
DEFINITIONS 'Serious criminal allegation' means an allegation of rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, stalking, child abuse, kidnapping, or another violent felony.
'Knowingly fabricate' means intentionally make a material factual assertion known by the speaker to be false; it does not include mistake, traumarelated inconsistency, lack of corroboration, dismissal, declination of prosecution, recantation standing alone, or acquittal.
SEC.
5.
RIGHT TO REPORT CRIME Nothing in this Act shall discourage, delay, or penalize a person for making a good-faith report, seeking a protective order, obtaining medical care, or cooperating with law enforcement.

6.
FEDERAL OFFENSE OF KNOWINGLY FABRICATING A SERIOUS CRIMINAL ALLEGATION A person who knowingly and materially fabricates a serious criminal allegation to a Federal officer or in a matter within Federal jurisdiction, with intent to cause a wrongful arrest, prosecution, incarceration, loss of custody, loss of employment or housing, or comparable legal deprivation, may be prosecuted under this section upon proof beyond a reasonable doubt.
SEC.
7.
FABRICATION OR MANIPULATION OF EVIDENCE It is an aggravated violation to knowingly create, alter, destroy, plant, or manipulate material physical or digital evidence, recruit or induce false testimony, falsify communications, commit perjury, or engage in a repeated scheme of fabrication in furtherance of a fabricated serious allegation.
SEC.
8.
EVIDENTIARY SAFEGUARDS FOR A FABRICATION PROSECUTION A prosecution under sections 6 or 7 may not be based solely on acquittal, dismissal, declination of prosecution, inconsistent statements, recantation, lack of corroboration, or failure to prove the underlying allegation.
The Government must establish knowing fabrication through independent evidence beyond a reasonable doubt.
The Attorney General shall require supervisory prosecutorial approval before an indictment under sections 6 or 7.
SEC.
9.
PENALTIES Penalties shall be proportionate to the fabrication conduct, the defendant's intent, actual harm, obstruction, use of fabricated evidence, witness manipulation, and repeated conduct.
No enhanced penalty may be based merely on the seriousness of an unproven underlying allegation; enhancement requires proof of the fabrication conduct itself.
SEC.
10.
RESTITUTION AND EXISTING CIVIL REMEDIES A sentencing court may order restitution for direct economic losses caused by proven deliberate fabrication, including reasonable legal expenses, lost wages, documented treatment costs, property loss, and costs of correcting official records, to the extent permitted by Federal restitution law.
Nothing in this Act limits an otherwise available civil remedy.
SEC.
11.
PROBABLE CAUSE AND INDIVIDUALIZED INVESTIGATION Covered Federal investigative agencies shall base arrest and charging decisions on individualized facts and applicable probable-cause standards.
Agencies shall preserve material exculpatory and inculpatory evidence consistent with law.
SEC.
12.
NO PRESUMPTION OF GUILT BASED UPON SEX OR GENDER A Federal agency receiving funds under this Act may not adopt a policy that treats a complainant as credible or an accused person as culpable solely because of sex, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, or relationship status.

13.
PRESUMPTION OF INNOCENCE Public communications by covered Federal agencies shall, where practicable, distinguish allegations, charges, and convictions and shall not describe an unconvicted person as guilty as an official finding.
SEC.
14.
GOVERNMENT EMPLOYMENT AND SECURITY DECISIONS An unresolved allegation may be considered only under otherwise lawful individualized risk standards.
Agencies shall provide notice and a meaningful opportunity to correct materially inaccurate criminal-history information used in an adverse employment, clearance, licensing, or contracting decision, subject to national-security exceptions.
SEC.
15.
PROTECTIVE ORDERS AND DUE PROCESS Nothing in this Act prevents emergency protective orders where authorized by law.
Longer-term orders shall provide constitutionally sufficient notice, an opportunity to be heard, findings supported by the governing burden of proof, and procedures to correct demonstrably false material information.
SEC.
16.
PROTECTION OF GENUINE VICTIMS AND GOOD-FAITH REPORTERS A person may not be prosecuted, subjected to a new Federal civil cause of action, or denied a benefit merely because a report is uncorroborated, a prosecution is declined, a case is dismissed, or an accused person is acquitted.
Retaliation against a good-faith reporter remains prohibited where otherwise unlawful.
SEC.
17.
FEDERAL GRANT CONDITIONS The Attorney General may condition designated discretionary criminal-justice grants on written policies for evidence preservation, sex-neutral investigation, timely disposition reporting, correction of demonstrably erroneous records, and procedures consistent with this Act.
No grant condition may require non-prosecution of a good-faith complainant.
SEC.
18.
RECORD CORRECTION, ANNOTATION, AND SEALING A person whose government record contains a material factual error, a misattributed allegation, a disposition omitted after dismissal or acquittal, or a record materially produced by proven intentional fabrication shall have a timely process to request correction or annotation.
Where otherwise authorized by law, the person shall receive an expedited process to seek sealing of a record materially produced by proven fabrication.
SEC.
19.
NOTICE FOLLOWING DISMISSAL OR ACQUITTAL Where practicable, a Federal agency maintaining an arrest or charge record shall update the record with a final dismissal or acquittal and provide the affected person information concerning correction and sealing procedures.

20.
PROTECTION AGAINST RETALIATORY USE OF THE JUSTICE SYSTEM Federal investigators and prosecutors shall not knowingly use criminal process for the purpose of punishing lawful speech, litigation, reporting, or the exercise of constitutional rights.
SEC.
21.
PROHIBITION ON QUOTA-BASED ENFORCEMENT No Federal grant under this Act may require an agency to achieve a predetermined number or percentage of arrests, charges, protective orders, or convictions for a covered offense.
SEC.
22.
DATA AND TRANSPARENCY The Attorney General shall publish annual aggregate data, to the extent feasible, on reports, arrests, charging outcomes, dismissals, convictions, acquittals, record-correction requests, and prosecutions for proven fabrication, without identifying victims or uncharged persons.
SEC.
23.
STATE ADOPTION Congress encourages States, including Utah, to enact parallel sex-neutral offenses, evidentiary safeguards, record-correction procedures, and protections for good-faith reporters consistent with this Act.
Nothing in this section requires a State to adopt a particular sentence.
SEC.
24.
SEX-NEUTRAL APPLICATION This Act applies equally regardless of the sex, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, or relationship status of the complainant or accused person.
SEC.
25.
NO INTERFERENCE WITH LEGITIMATE INVESTIGATIONS Nothing in this Act requires premature disclosure of investigative information, restricts lawful evidence collection, or prevents authorities from taking emergency action supported by law.
SEC.
26.
RULE OF CONSTRUCTION AND SEVERABILITY Nothing in this Act alters the elements of an underlying criminal offense, creates a presumption that an unproven report was false, or reduces protections for victims.
If any provision is held invalid, the remainder shall not be affected.
SEC.
27.
EFFECTIVE DATE This Act takes effect 180 days after enactment and applies prospectively, except that recordcorrection procedures may be used for existing records where administratively feasible.
