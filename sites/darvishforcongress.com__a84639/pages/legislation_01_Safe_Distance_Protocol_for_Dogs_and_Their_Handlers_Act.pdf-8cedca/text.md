A BILL To establish minimum-distance rules around dog-handler teams in public spaces, recognize warning signage, and create graduated civil enforcement and public education.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'Safe Distance Protocol for Dogs and Their Handlers Act'.
SEC.
2.
PURPOSE AND FINDINGS Congress finds that avoidable close-contact interactions with dogs can create risks to people, animals, and handlers, including defensive reactions, bites, panic, or conflict over responsibility.
The purpose of this Act is to establish clear spatial-respect rules, reduce preventable confrontations, and educate the public concerning safe conduct around dogs regardless of serviceanimal status.
SEC.
3.
MINIMUM DISTANCE REQUIREMENTS (a) Indoor public spaces.
Except as provided in subsection (c), a person shall maintain at least 6 feet of distance from a dog and its handler in an indoor public space. (b) Outdoor public spaces.
Except as provided in subsection (c), a person shall maintain at least 10 feet of distance from a dog and its handler in an outdoor public space. (c) Consent.
A person may approach within the distances established by this section when the handler gives clear verbal or written consent or when ordinary passage makes temporary proximity unavoidable and the person proceeds reasonably.
SEC.
4.
RECOGNITION OF WARNING SIGNAGE A clearly visible sign, patch, leash sleeve, vest, or other notice stating “Do Not Pet,” “Do Not Approach,” “Anxious Dog,” or substantially similar language constitutes notice that the handler does not consent to unnecessary approach or contact.
Knowing disregard of such notice may be considered in determining whether a violation of this Act occurred.

5.
ENFORCEMENT AND PENALTIES (a) First offense.
A verbal or written warning and educational material concerning responsible conduct around dogs. (b) Second offense.
A civil penalty not to exceed $100. (c) Subsequent offenses.
A civil penalty not to exceed $500.
A separate criminal offense is not created solely by proximity; however, conduct resulting in injury remains subject to otherwise applicable law.
SEC.
6.
EDUCATION The Secretary of Health and Human Services, in consultation with relevant animal-control and public-safety agencies, may support public education through local governments, schools, veterinary and pet-related businesses, and public media concerning respectful interaction with dogs and handlers.
SEC.
7.
EXEMPTIONS This Act does not restrict emergency medical personnel, law-enforcement officers, firefighters, certified animal-control officers, veterinarians, or other authorized personnel acting within the scope of official duties or responding to an emergency.
SEC.
8.
RULE OF CONSTRUCTION Nothing in this Act diminishes rights or obligations under the Americans with Disabilities Act, State animal-control laws, premises-safety rules, or laws governing dangerous or vicious animals.
SEC.
9.
EFFECTIVE DATE This Act takes effect 180 days after enactment.
