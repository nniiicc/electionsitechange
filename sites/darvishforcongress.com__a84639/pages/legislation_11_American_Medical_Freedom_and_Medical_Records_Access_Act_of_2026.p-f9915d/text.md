A BILL To establish a regulated pharmacist-access pathway for certain antibiotics, promote price competition, and guarantee free electronic access and correction rights for personal medical records.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'American Medical Freedom and Medical Records Access Act of 2026'.
SEC.
2.
FINDINGS Congress finds that timely access to basic care and personal medical records can reduce unnecessary delay and expense; that antibiotic resistance and patient safety require structured safeguards; and that individuals should be able to see and seek correction of core medical records used to make decisions about their care.
SEC.
3.
PHARMACIST-ACCESS ANTIBIOTICS FOR DEFINED BACTERIAL INFECTIONS The Secretary of Health and Human Services, acting through the Food and Drug Administration and in consultation with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and State pharmacy boards, shall create a designation allowing pharmacists to dispense specified oral antibiotics without an individual physician prescription for defined low-risk bacterial conditions under standardized protocols.
Eligible drugs may include amoxicillin, penicillin V, cephalexin, nitrofurantoin, trimethoprimsulfamethoxazole, and doxycycline only to the extent the Food and Drug Administration determines a protocol can safely address indication, allergy, pregnancy, interaction, renal or hepatic risk, resistance, required testing, red flags, and referral.
A pharmacist shall screen for contraindications, exclude likely viral illness, require point-of-care testing when designated, provide counseling, document the encounter, and refer the patient for medical evaluation when the protocol identifies a danger sign, diagnostic uncertainty, treatment failure, or recurrent infection.
A pharmacist acting in good faith within an approved protocol receives the same professionalliability protections and duties applicable to comparable pharmacist clinical services under State law.

4.
AFFORDABILITY AND COMPETITION Nothing in this Act authorizes price fixing.
Federal agencies shall promote generic competition, price transparency, and the ability of patients to compare the cash price and insured price of covered medications and pharmacist services.
SEC.
5.
FREE ELECTRONIC MEDICAL-RECORD ACCESS AND CORRECTION A covered health-care provider or health-information network shall provide an individual, without charge, secure electronic access to the individual's designated record set in a commonly usable format, subject to privacy and safety exceptions already recognized by Federal law.
Individuals shall have a clear electronic process to request correction or amendment of materially inaccurate information and to receive the provider's response.
This section does not require deletion of clinically relevant information lawfully maintained as part of the record.
SEC.
6.
MEDICAL RECORDS BILL OF RIGHTS An individual has the right to know what personal medical records are maintained by covered entities, obtain a usable electronic copy without an access fee, learn how to seek correction or amendment, receive notice when a correction request is denied in whole or in part, and obtain information explaining how to submit an appropriate statement of disagreement where Federal law provides that option.
SEC.
7.
RELATIONSHIP TO STATE LAW A State may provide greater medical-record access, privacy, correction rights, or pharmacist authority.
This Act preempts only a State rule that directly prohibits the minimum Federal medicalrecord access rights created by sections 5 and 6.
Pharmacist practice remains subject to State licensure except to the limited extent necessary to implement a Federal drug designation under section 3.
SEC.
8.
PUBLIC EDUCATION The Secretary of Health and Human Services shall publish plain-language materials explaining appropriate antibiotic use, antimicrobial resistance, pharmacist protocols, medical-record access rights, and medical-record correction procedures.
SEC.
9.
IMPLEMENTATION Not later than 12 months after enactment, the responsible agencies shall issue implementing rules, technical standards, and model patient disclosures.
Agencies shall consult clinicians, pharmacists, patient advocates, antimicrobial-stewardship experts, privacy experts, and health-information networks.

10.
COORDINATION WITH CRIMINAL-RECORD LEGISLATION Nothing in this Act alters criminal-history record access or correction rights.
Criminal-history transparency and correction are addressed separately by the Know Your Record Act of 2027 and other applicable law.
SEC.
11.
SEVERABILITY If any provision of this Act is held invalid, the remainder shall not be affected.
SEC.
12.
EFFECTIVE DATE This Act takes effect 12 months after enactment, except that rulemaking may begin immediately.
