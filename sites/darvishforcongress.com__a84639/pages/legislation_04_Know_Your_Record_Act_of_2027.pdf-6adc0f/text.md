A BILL To establish free nationwide personal access to criminal-history records, explain what records are visible to employers and government, create access logs and correction procedures, and improve interstate accuracy.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled: SEC.
1.
SHORT TITLE This Act may be cited as the 'Know Your Record Act of 2027' or the 'National Criminal Record Transparency and Accuracy Act'.
SEC.
2.
PURPOSE The purpose of this Act is to give every person a free, secure, understandable way to see criminal-history information associated with that person and to correct inaccurate or incomplete records before those records cause avoidable harm.
SEC.
3.
DEFINITIONS In this Act: “criminal-history record” includes arrest, charge, disposition, conviction, sentence, warrant, supervision, and related identity information maintained for criminal-justice purposes; “portal” means the national access system created by this Act; and “covered agency” includes the Department of Justice and participating State or local repositories.
SEC.
4.
NATIONAL PERSONAL CRIMINAL-RECORD PORTAL The Attorney General shall establish a secure national portal through which an individual may authenticate identity and obtain, without charge, the criminal-history records maintained or reported about that individual by participating repositories.
SEC.
5.
FREE ACCESS No fee may be charged for personal access under this Act.
Reasonable identity-verification measures may be required, but such measures may not be designed to discourage access.
SEC.
6.
RECORD DETAIL AND SOURCE IDENTIFICATION Each displayed record shall identify, to the extent available, the originating agency, case number, event type, date, disposition, and whether the record is pending, dismissed, sealed, expunged, corrected, or otherwise restricted.

7.
EMPLOYMENT VISIBILITY REPORT The portal shall provide a plain-language report identifying which portions of the person’s record may ordinarily appear in common employment, licensing, housing, volunteer, or governmental background checks, subject to Federal and State restrictions.
SEC.
8.
GOVERNMENT AND EMPLOYER DISCLOSURE TRANSPARENCY Where technically feasible, the portal shall explain the categories of authorized users that may receive a record and the principal legal authority for disclosure.
SEC.
9.
ACCESS LOG A person shall be able to view a log of non-law-enforcement background-check accesses to the person’s record when disclosure of the access is not prohibited by law or a court order.
The log shall identify the accessing entity, date, and stated purpose when available.
SEC.
10.
DISPUTE AND CORRECTION PROCESS A person may dispute inaccurate, incomplete, duplicative, or misattributed information through the portal.
The disputed item shall be marked as disputed within 2 business days and ordinarily resolved within 30 days, with written notice of the result.
SEC.
11.
DOWNSTREAM CORRECTION NOTICES When a covered agency materially corrects a record, it shall, where practicable and lawful, notify repositories and background-screening systems to which the erroneous record was transmitted so they can update their records.
SEC.
12.
SEALED, EXPUNGED, JUVENILE, AND PROTECTED INFORMATION The portal may display protected information to the individual to whom it pertains where lawful, but shall not expand third-party access to sealed, expunged, juvenile, victim, witness, intelligence, or other protected information.
SEC.
13.
SECURITY AND PRIVACY The Attorney General shall require strong authentication, encryption, audit logging, breach notification, data minimization, and prohibitions on commercial resale of portal credentials or personal portal reports.
SEC.
14.
STATE INTEROPERABILITY States and territories are encouraged to connect criminal-history repositories to the portal through common data standards that preserve State sealing, expungement, and disclosure rules.

15.
MODERNIZATION AND IMPROVEMENT GRANTS The Attorney General may award grants to States, tribes, and localities to digitize dispositions, reconcile identity records, eliminate duplicate entries, improve court-to-repository reporting, and support portal interoperability.
SEC.
16.
ACCURACY STANDARDS Covered agencies shall adopt reasonable procedures to ensure maximum feasible accuracy and to match arrests with final dispositions.
SEC.
17.
ADVERSE-ACTION NOTICE When Federal law permits an employer, agency, or other covered decisionmaker to take adverse action based materially on criminal-history information, the person shall receive the record relied upon or a clear method to obtain it and information about available dispute rights.
SEC.
18.
ENFORCEMENT The Attorney General may enforce compliance by Federal agencies and through grant conditions for participating repositories.
Existing rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act and civil-rights laws are preserved.
SEC.
19.
PUBLIC EDUCATION The Department of Justice shall publish plain-language materials explaining personal record access, correction rights, sealing and expungement limits, and the difference between arrest, charge, dismissal, and conviction.
SEC.
20.
RULEMAKING Not later than 1 year after enactment, the Attorney General shall issue implementing regulations after consultation with State repositories, courts, privacy experts, civil-rights organizations, employers, and criminal-justice agencies.
SEC.
21.
IMPLEMENTATION DEADLINES Existing FBI personal-access and dispute processes shall comply with the free-access requirements within 1 year after enactment.
The national portal shall be operational not later than 2 years after enactment.
SEC.
22.
RULE OF CONSTRUCTION Nothing in this Act authorizes disclosure to a third party that is otherwise prohibited by law, alters the presumption of innocence, or limits a State from providing greater privacy, access, correction, sealing, or expungement rights.
