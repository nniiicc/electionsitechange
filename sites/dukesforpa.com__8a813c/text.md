He faces the same opponent again this fall for the full term. -- DAYS TO NOV 3 About Meet Brandon Meet Brandon Dukes, husband, father, and candidate for PA State House District 12.
Brandon is running because the district is changing—growing in population, evolving in demographics, and ready for new leadership.
He believes it’s time for a representative who reflects that change and is focused on the future.
A current resident of Cranberry Township, Brandon grew up in the city of Pittsburgh as the youngest of four children in a middle-class family.
Those early experiences shaped his values—hard work, responsibility, and understanding the everyday challenges families face.
He went on to attend the University of Pittsburgh, where he earned a degree in political science, building a foundation for his commitment to public service.
Professionally, Brandon has spent the past 16 years in the financial industry, most recently working in the mortgage and commercial lending sector.
Through this work, he has seen firsthand the challenges families and small businesses face when it comes to affordability, access to capital, and long-term financial stability.
Outside of work and the campaign trail, Brandon is an avid Star Wars fan and enjoys 3D printing—though nothing comes before spending time with his wife and daughter.
“Believe in Better” is his campaign slogan because Brandon believes our community deserves more—and that together, we can choose leaders who will fight for a stronger, more affordable, and more inclusive future in Harrisburg.

Scott Timko Property Taxes His tax plan shifts the bill onto your house Scott Timko says he will cut taxes, but his plan does the opposite.
Cutting state money for schools doesn't make the cost disappear; it just shifts it onto you.
When Harrisburg sends less money to your school district, your school district raises property taxes to fill the gap.
Butler County homeowners have watched school taxes go up year after year.
Timko's plan would likely make that worse, not better.
Public Schools He would drain Seneca Valley and Mars Area to pay private tuition Scott Timko supports school voucher programs that take taxpayer dollars out of Butler County public schools to pay for private school tuition.
That means less money for Seneca Valley, Mars Area, and other local schools your family depends on.
Butler County families have spent decades building schools worth being proud of.
Timko's plan would drain those schools to fund someone else's private tuition bill.
Health Care He said he favors defunding Medicare and Medicaid A conservative voter guide asked candidates to choose among four health care positions.
Option D read that taxpayer funded health care should be abolished in all forms and that Medicaid and Medicare should be defunded.
Timko answered in his own words: I favor option D - but I don't think that is a realistic achievement.
He added that he could live with option C, keeping the two programs.
In the same questionnaire he listed eliminating as many social programs as possible among his top priorities, and said he opposes the minimum wage and any increase to it.
Source: Timko candidate questionnaire, iVoterGuide, 2026 Career Politician Six years of running, nothing delivered Scott Timko has spent the last six years chasing power for himself.
Since 2020, he's lost the same State House seat twice, reached for a seat in Congress, and this spring barely survived his own primary.
In all this time, he has not delivered anything for the families who live here.
Butler County voters deserve someone who wants to serve this community, not someone who has spent six years chasing a political career.
Source: Cranberry Eagle, Aug.
5, 2026 Taxes He ran on lowering taxes while his own company owed the state Timko made lower taxes and fiscal responsibility the center of his campaign.
Pennsylvania Department of Revenue records show three outstanding liens against Got Gumbo LLC, the company tied to him: two from 2021 totaling $29,430.14, and one filed in January 2026 for $13,157.65.
That is more than $42,000 in unpaid state taxes, some of it five years old.
Timko disputes the liens, and as of the week of the special election he had not publicly addressed them.
Source: PoliticsPA, Aug.
16, 2026 · Butler Eagle, Aug.
16, 2026 Money He called himself self-funded, then took party money At a candidate forum Timko presented self-funding as proof of independence: Since I’m a self-funded candidate, I answer only to the voters.
Reports showed roughly $76,000 of his own money going to consultants, signage and mailers.
In early August, days before the special election, he reported support from the state Republican Party.
