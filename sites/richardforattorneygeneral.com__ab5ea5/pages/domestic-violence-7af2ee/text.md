THIS MUST END.
In 2023, 51 South Carolina women were killed by men.
In more than 95 percent of those cases, the woman knew her killer.
South Carolina's rate of women killed by men — 1.8 per 100,000 — remained well above the national average.
And over the quarter-century the data was tracked (1996–2020), South Carolina ranked among the ten deadliest states for women 23 times, holding the worst spot in the nation four times.
These are not random acts of violence.
In case after case there was a prior history — prior 911 calls, prior incidents, prior charges that were plea-bargained away or dropped.
The system saw these women before they died.
Too often, it failed them.
Domestic violence is still treated as a lesser offense in too many places across this state — a "family matter," a "he-said-she-said," a case treated as easier to dismiss than to prosecute.
The result is a death toll that has kept South Carolina near the top of the nation's worst rankings for a generation.
The failure is not the law.
South Carolina strengthened its domestic-violence laws in 2015.
The failure is the will to use them.
