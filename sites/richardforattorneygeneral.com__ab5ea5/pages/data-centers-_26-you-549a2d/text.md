You turn on the tap and you don't think twice.
The water's there—for your family, your kids' baths, your garden, your morning coffee.
But data centers are arriving across South Carolina at extraordinary scale.
They can consume enormous amounts of water and electricity, require massive new infrastructure, and affect the communities where they're built.
One mid-size facility can use 300,000 gallons of water a day—as much as 1,000 households.
And too often, South Carolinians are having to go to court just to make their government follow the rules and let them see what's happening.
In Spartanburg County, citizens are now in court alleging that an almost $3 billion data center was classified as a “minor” development, avoiding the public Planning Commission review required for major developments.
Another proceeding challenges whether construction of its proposed 450-megawatt power plant began without the public review and approval required by state law.
In Dorchester County, a citizen had to sue seeking information about how much water a data center would use after the information was claimed to be a trade secret.
THAT IS YOUR WATER—NOT THEIRS.
Why should ordinary South Carolinians have to hire lawyers and sue their own government just to make public government public?
