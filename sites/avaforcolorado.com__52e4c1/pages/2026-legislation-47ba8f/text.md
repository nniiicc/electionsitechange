This gives Colorado Springs Utilities a little more time to pursue clean energy responsibly without driving up costs or risking blackouts.
SB26-149: Pathways for Individuals with Mental Health Disorder – Keeps dangerous individuals who are incompetent to stand trial from being released back into the community by creating pathways for treatment, supervision, and long-term placement when necessary to protect public safety.
SB26-137: Measures to Reduce Administrative Burdens – Requires departments to review their rules every five years to identify outdated or burdensome regulations and allows legislative committees to recommend further review, improving accountability and efficiency.
SB26-015: Commercial Sexual Activity with a Child Offenses – Strengthens protections for children by increasing penalties for exploitation and internet luring, helping hold predators accountable and protect our most vulnerable.
HB26-1298: Background Checks for Child Welfare Placements – Gives counties the authority and resources to conduct background checks on individuals involved in childcare.
This is a straightforward step to ensure that children in the welfare system are kept safe.
HB26-1290: Criminal Offense of Assault – Strengthens accountability by implementing stricter penalties for those who assault providers.
This helps keep medical professionals safe while they’re on the job and addresses repeat violent behavior.
HB26-1138: Retail Theft Prevention Program – Creates a statewide Retail Theft Prevention Advisory Board designed to combat organized felony-level retail theft and gift card fraud by coordinating efforts and providing funding to support investigations and prosecutions.
HB26-1105: Discuss Adoption Information with Pregnant Persons – Required providers to inform women seeking an abortion about adoption options unless they declined, ensuring they are aware of all choices.
SB26-186: Update Workers’ Compensation Statutes Allow Electronic Filing – Cleans up the current workers’ compensation system by updating outdated statutes.
By modernizing the process, it reduces unnecessary paperwork and creates a simplified system.
HB26-1212: Constitutional Carry of Handgun – Allowed individuals 18 or older to carry concealed handguns without a permit.
Coloradans deserve to exercise their self-defense rights without administrative hoops HB26-1024: Raising Age of Voluntary Relinquishment of Child – Extends voluntary child relinquishment from # hours to # days and creates a path for parents to reunite with their child, offering more flexibility and time during a difficult decision.
HB26-1334: Modify Standards of Wildfire Resiliency Code Board – Extended the timeline and improved local coordination for wildfire protection in construction.
Many communities, including areas in Colorado Springs, struggled to meet state deadlines.
HB26-1129: Gas Utility Service – Would have removed residential natural gas emissions from utilities’ clean heat goal calculations.
With many Coloradans facing high energy costs, it aimed to reduce regulatory burdens and protect consumers from additional cost increases.
HB26-1432: Health-Care Payment Programs – Creates the Hospital Quality Incentive Program, rewarding hospitals for high-quality care, lower costs, and better patient outcomes.
It reduces administrative burdens to help providers focus on patient care.
HB26-1325: Natural Medicine – Creates an ibogaine research pilot program, funds research, and sets a framework for future ibogaine use.
Ibogaine shows promise for treating various mental health conditions, giving Coloradans access to innovative treatments.
SB26-095: Measures to Support Victim-Survivors of Crimes – Improves access to forensic exam results for sexual assault victims and adds training to help law enforcement better respond.
Victims deserve to stay informed and receive the best care and support.
HB26-1260: Updates to Child Care Assistance Programs – Delays upcoming child care program changes to give state and local governments more time to prepare.
By improving oversight and ensuring financial stability, the bill aims to support families and providers long-term.
HB26-1110: Vulnerable Adult Financial Exploitation Banking – Protects vulnerable adults from financial exploitation by letting banks delay suspicious transactions when abuse is suspected, helping shield seniors and other vulnerable adults from scams.
SB26-173: Barre & Pilates Teacher Training Regulatory Exemptions – Deregulates pilates and barre teacher training courses by removing certain state requirements.
As one of the most heavily regulated states, seeing regulations removed was a great step forward.
