The Leelanau Early Childhood Development Commission announced it received the funding to increase the reach of the Infant and Toddler Child Care Start-Up Expansion program, supported by state Rep.
Betsy Coffia, D-Traverse City, and state Sen.
John DaMoose, R-Harbor Springs.
The program, an effort between the state’s Department of Labor and Economic Opportunity and the LECDC, aims to establish new in-home child care businesses serving working families with children, ages spanning from infancy to 6 years old.
The program also applies to any existing in-home child care business interested in growing their capacity within those counties.
“We have received a lot of interest so far,” Mary Ann Behm, program coordinator, said Tuesday, highlighting 13 total interest forms received through the LECDC website.
Behm said, while three of those forms were out of the two-county program area, she’s followed up with 10 applicants so far who are interested in applying for the program.
Eligible adults must follow a step-by-step application process to receive the grant funding.
“It’s really a wrap-around program for somebody who wants to start a new (child care) program or expand their program,” Behm said.
In 2022, Leelanau County was one of five communities in the state to receive a grant from the Early Childhood Investment Corporation, a nonprofit based in Lansing.
Fundraising at federal and state levels, including private financial supporters, helped create five new child care businesses in Leelanau County that year.
Behm said in a release announcing the grant that Leelanau County will be the model for Grand Traverse and Benzie counties.
The LECDC hopes to see several more child care businesses open and thrive in the two counties.
“We’re estimating eight to 10 (businesses) between the two counties,” Behm said.
