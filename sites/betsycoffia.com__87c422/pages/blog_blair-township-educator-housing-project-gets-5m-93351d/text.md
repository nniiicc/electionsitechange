Representatives from Traverse City Area Public Schools, Michigan Education Association and state political offices gathered Tuesday at Blair Elementary School to announce a grant aimed at initial phases to build housing for school staff, and possibly first responders.
“Teachers and first responders have simply been priced out of communities like Traverse City,” said Michigan Gov.
Gretchen Whitmer.
Currently, 60 percent of workers spend more than 30 percent of their income on housing, Whitmer said, adding that the low housing supply contributes to higher costs, and the area needs 1,400 new rentals and 1,800 new homes in the next five years.
Ed Potas, real estate development manager at Cinnaire, a nonprofit community development financial organization working on the project, said the plan is to build 72 one- and two- bedroom units near Blair Elementary.
One-bedroom units will be 650 sq/ft minimum and the two bedrooms will be 850 sq/ft minimum, and the project expects to utilize city water and septic systems, Potas said.
Phase One renters will be considered if their income is at or below 80 percent of the area median income.
The AMI is calculated annually by the Department of Housing and Urban Development (and published online) and reflects the midpoint of the area’s income range.
A family of four in Grand Traverse County would qualify for an 80 percent AMI if the household makes $79,050 or less.
Phase Two will mix of households incomes with the 80 percent and with higher incomes, in the 100-120 percent range, by design.
“People in that entire range (of incomes) are genuinely struggling and they are not actually able to find and afford housing in this region,” said State Representative Betsy Coffia (D-Traverse City), quoting average home prices between $400,000 and $500,000 and $1,800 for average monthly rent.
“For me, that rings alarm bells for the sustainability of some of our most important institutions, like our schools,” Coffia said.
Leaders from TCAPS, Grand Traverse Area Catholic Schools, Interlochen Center for the Arts, and Northwest Education Services have been working together for two years to find employee housing solutions, said TCAPS Superintendent John VanWagoner.
They approached Coffia for help last November, and she, and State Senator John Damoose (R-Harbor Springs) secured bipartisan legislative support for the $5 million Office of Rural Prosperity grant.
The next step in the funding process happens this October when the group submits a Michigan State Housing Authority application for the balance of the $23 million estimated building cost.
Once the funding is secure, it could be another 18-24 months before renters can move in.
VanWagoner said that units could open to first responders if the school groups have a tenant shortage.
Whitmer called educators and first responders “anchors of the community” who deserve secure housing.
For schools, the housing relief can’t come too soon.
VanWagoner said the cost of housing impact the district’s ability to hire new teachers.
“Six or seven (applicants) in the past two years took the job that we offered and within three weeks called us back and said ‘I just can’t make it work’,” said VanWagoner.
Moreover, he said, schools face another issue on the horizon.
“We have an aging population of teachers.
If you look at our staff, we have probably 30-40 percent in the next five years who are all able to retire.” Kathryn DePauw covers Indigenous Affairs in partnership with Report for America.
