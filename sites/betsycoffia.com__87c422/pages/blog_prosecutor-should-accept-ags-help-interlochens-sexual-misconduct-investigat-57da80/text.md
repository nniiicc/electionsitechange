State Rep.
Betsy Coffia, D-Traverse City, this week published an open letter to Prosecuting Attorney Noelle Moeggenberg, urging her office to take up Nessel’s team on their offer for assistance.
Coffia cited the prosecutor’s remarks last year about her office being understaffed and struggling with a large criminal case load.
Interlochen Center for the Arts released in report last month that detailed accounts from 70 alumni who say they had been sexually assaulted while they were students or campers at the academy located south of Traverse City.
Compiled during a two-year external investigation that was requested by the academy, the report outlined alleged incidents that took place from the 1960s to the 2010s and involved 47 alleged abusers, including Jeffrey Epstein.
After it was released, the Grand Traverse County Prosecutor said her staff were combing through the 97-page report to determine if any cases could result in criminal charges.
None of the people accused in the report are currently employed by the arts institution and about one-third are deceased, according to a letter from Interlochen President Trey Devey and Board Chairman Barrett Rollins .
Last month, Moeggenberg said her office would move to prosecute any case in which the victim wanted to go forward, and that was still allowable under the statute of limitations.
She said involving the AG’s team would depend on the number of actionable criminal cases that came from the report.
In making her letter to the prosecutor public, Coffia on Wednesday said she is pushing for transparency “on a matter of grave public concern.” “Right now it is imperative to secure timely justice as swiftly and comprehensively as possible for the 70 people who have come forward with allegations against 47 Interlochen staff, donors, trustees, visitors and other adults accused of sexual misconduct toward them as children,” Coffia wrote in her letter.
“With such an expansive influx of new cases originating here in Grand Traverse County, now more than ever expert state assistance is needed in order to handle these cases thoroughly, appropriately and in a timely manner.” She said the community as a whole as well as abuse survivors deserve an “all hands on deck approach” to making sure any living suspects are held accountable.
Coffia said Nessel’s team has a strong track record of investigating large-scale sex abuse cases.
She pointed to the AG’s cases against Catholic clergy, Boy Scouts of America personnel and Larry Nassar’s conviction.
“I respectfully ask and strongly urge you to move decisively to accept her team’s offer and fully leverage the capacity, skill and track record it brings for the benefit of our community, survivors and their families,” Coffia said.

Epstein, who was a regular visitor to Interlochen, contributed nearly a half million dollars from 1990 to 2003.
He and Maxwell often stayed in the Jeffrey E.
Epstein Scholarship Lodge, which he had paid to have constructed.
The two were also known to have paid for scholarships of several children.
Interlochen cut ties with Epstein in 2008 when he was convicted in Florida of soliciting a minor, and all donor recognition was removed at that time.
Epstein’s building, renamed the Green Lake Lodge, was demolished this June .
The investigative report released last month included information about two additional sexual assaults involving Epstein.
Interlochen President Trey Devey and Board Chairman Barrett Rollins addressed the findings of the report last month.
“The abuse described in the report is heartbreaking and is contrary to everything Interlochen stands for,” the two stated in the letter.
