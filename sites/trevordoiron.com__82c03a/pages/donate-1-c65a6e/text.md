Invest in People, Not Politics.
I’m not accepting campaign contributions.
Instead, if you’re feeling generous, I’d encourage you to consider supporting one of the local charitable organizations listed below.
The strength of our community depends on neighbors helping neighbors — volunteers showing up, and essential services being there when families need them most.
There’s already too much money in politics, and I firmly believe those dollars are better spent reinvesting directly in the people and programs that make Jay, Livermore, and Livermore Falls such special places to call home.
Spruce Mountain Ski Slope
Spruce Mountain Ski Slope holds a special place in my heart — it’s where I learned to ski, where generations of local kids first felt winter’s magic under their feet, and one of the few places in our area where families can get outdoors together without screens.
Today, community volunteers are working hard to revitalize the slope and improve infrastructure so Spruce Mountain can continue to be a year-round hub for recreation, gathering, and fun for all ages.
Supporting this effort helps ensure that future kids, families, and neighbors have the same opportunities to enjoy the mountain and make lasting memories that so many of us cherish.
Seniors Plus - Meals on Wheels
I’ve seen firsthand how much this program matters because I volunteer as a Meals on Wheels driver here in Jay.
Each delivery is more than just a meal — it’s a friendly face, a quick check-in, and a reminder that our seniors aren’t alone.
SeniorsPlus helps older adults and neighbors with disabilities stay healthy, independent, and connected by providing nutritious home-delivered meals and essential support services.
Programs like this run on volunteers and community generosity, and they make a real difference in helping people remain safely in their own homes.
Supporting SeniorsPlus is one of the simplest, most meaningful ways we can take care of one another.
Food Cupboard of Jay, Livermore & Livermore Falls
I volunteer at the local food cupboard because I believe no family should wonder where their next meal comes from.
One moment that truly moved me — and partly inspired me to launch this campaign — was learning that more Thanksgiving food baskets were distributed in 2025 than during the height of the Great Recession in 2008.
That staggering reality shows how many of our neighbors are struggling right now.
The food cupboard relies on dedicated volunteers and community support to provide groceries and dignity to families in need, and every donation helps keep this essential safety net strong.
