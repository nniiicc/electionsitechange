TISHA BENOIT BALANCE THE DAMN BUDGET!
Because Washington’s debt eventually lands on your kitchen table.
#1 AFFORDABILITY: Why does everything feel so expensive? → Washington’s borrowing has consequences.
Think of it this way: Washington borrows more → the national debt grows → interest costs rise → government needs even more money just to service the debt.
#2 HOUSING : Can’t afford the house you could have afforded a few years ago? → When Washington borrows a lot of money, it can help push interest rates higher.
Higher rates make home loans more expensive, so the same house can cost you hundreds more every month.
The house may not have changed.
The cost of borrowing did.
Affordability has to become a national priority.
That means getting federal spending under control, lowering borrowing pressure, increasing housing supply, and making it possible for working Americans to afford a home again.
#3 CREDIT CARDS: Your family pays interest.
So does Washington. → Let’s compare household interest with federal interest expense.
When your family borrows money, you have to pay back the loan plus interest .
Washington works the same way.
The more money the government borrows, the more interest taxpayers have to pay.
Today, the federal government spends about $1 trillion a year just on interest .
That is money we cannot use for roads, schools, Social Security, veterans, or lowering taxes.
The more we owe, the less money we have for the things that actually help American families.
#4 SOCIAL SECURITY : We promised it to you.
We need to make sure we can pay for it. → Fiscal sustainability.
Programs like Social Security and Medicare are promises Americans have planned their lives around.
Fiscal sustainability simply means making sure the government has enough money to keep those promises not just today, but years from now.
If we keep spending more than we bring in, eventually something has to give.
The goal is simple: protect the promises, fix the math, and make sure the money is still there when you need it.
#5 CHILDREN: Imagine your child’s future → “She didn’t borrow $40 trillion.
Why are we leaving her the bill?” Our kids did not create this debt, but they will be the ones paying for it if we keep borrowing.
We fix it by spending smarter, cutting waste, growing the economy, protecting Social Security and Medicare, and stopping Washington from creating new programs without a way to pay for them.
The goal is not to fix everything overnight.
The goal is to stop making the problem bigger, balance the budget over time, and leave the next generation more opportunity than debt.
