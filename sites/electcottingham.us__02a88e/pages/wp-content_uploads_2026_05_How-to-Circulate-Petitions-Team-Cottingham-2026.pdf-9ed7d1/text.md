The Importance of Notaries When it comes to petitioning in Illinois, the notary public is the most important role one can take in a local petition initiative.
Like in many other states, Illinois’s Secretary of State requires that all petition forms be properly notarized prior to their final submission.
Roles of the Notary Public • While many assume that the notary’s job is to notarize all of the signatures on the petition, that is false. • The role of the notary is to ensure that the circulator/affiant has properly signed the circulator’s affidavit while in their presence. • The notary also certifies that the information found within the petition is accurate.

For a signature to be considered complete, it must contain the following: 1.
Signature of the petitioner that matches the signature on their driver’s license & voter registration card.
2.
The petitioner’s name in print.
3.
The full registered voting address of the petitioner, which must include their street number and street name.
4.
The city/town/village of the petitioner.
5.
The county of the petitioner.
Here is an example of what a complete signature should look like.

Page |7 How to Fix & Prevent Mistakes • If a mistake is made by the petitioner while filling out their information on the petition, simply strike through the entire line and move on to the line below it to start over. • Stray Mark Rule: DO NOT cross out any text or allow any rips or tears to be made to the petition form, for allowing such will result in the INVALIDATION OF THE ENTIRE PAGE.
How to Prevent Fraud • • • • As the circulator of the petition, you are not permitted to correct mistakes made by the petitioner, for this is considered fraud and will INVALIDATE THE ENTIRE PETITION.
As the circulator of the petition, you cannot allow someone to sign for another person, for this is also considered fraud and will INVALIDATE THE ENTIRE PETITION.
As the circulator of the petition, you cannot allow one person to sign for members of their family, for this is called Family Fill-In, which is also considered fraud and will INVALIDATE THE ENTIRE PETITION.
As the circulator of the petition, you must keep those who are signing your petition within your line of sight at all times, which will prevent fraud and other fatal mistakes from being made.
These examples of petition fraud by hand-of-another could be detrimental to the success of our petition drives, which is why it is of great importance that we train our circulators to avoid these fatal mistakes.
