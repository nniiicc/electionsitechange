David takes a moderate view on agricultural policy, believing it should reflect the realities faced by local farmers rather than partisan extremes.
Policies should support land stewardship, water quality, and long-term sustainability while also respecting property rights and the economic pressures of farming in a growing region.
He believes the government's role should be practical and supportive—investing in infrastructure, research, and risk management—without overregulation that could threaten family farms or long-standing agricultural traditions.
A balanced approach is essential to preserve both the productivity and character of House District 56 for future generations.
Education David’s political view on public education in Kentucky recognizes the importance of strong, well-funded public schools while also valuing fiscal responsibility and local control.
Public education plays a critical role in preparing Kentucky’s workforce, supporting rural communities, and ensuring every child has a fair opportunity to succeed, regardless of zip code.
At the same time, a balanced approach supports accountability, efficient use of taxpayer dollars, and collaboration with teachers, parents, and local school boards rather than one-size-fits-all mandates.
David believes that we need to invest in teacher retention, career and technical education, and student support services—while remaining open to innovation and improvement without undermining the public school system that serves the majority of Kentucky students.
Public education is the future of our district.
The students we have today are our leaders in the future.
We need to help public education to become more fully funded, including transportation, SEEK funding, and other aspects involved with our educational system.
The Second Amendment Gun ownership in Kentucky holds significance for many views because it is closely tied to traditions of hunting, farming, and personal responsibility, as well as constitutional rights.
For rural communities in particular, firearms are often practical tools used for sport, wildlife management, and protection of property, not just political symbols.
At the same time, David recognizes that the right to own a gun comes with the responsibility to handle and store firearms safely, follow the law, and respect public safety concerns.
Balancing individual freedoms with sensible regulations and education allows Kentucky to honor its cultural heritage while also working to reduce accidents and misuse.
This topic should reflect values shared across the political spectrum.
Our First Responders With a degree in Police Administration and time spent as the Deputy Jailer, David approaches interactions with first responders from a place of respect, practicality, and balance, recognizing both the challenges they face and the accountability they carry.
He values the vital role that local police, firefighters, and EMS play in keeping the community safe, especially in a close-knit district where people tend to know one another.
At the same time, David appreciates calm communication, professionalism, and fairness, preferring cooperation over confrontation and dialogue over ideology.
It shouldn't be about party lines, but about shared community values, trust, responsibility, and a mutual desire to see our 56th district remain a safe and stable place to live.
David has lots of friends who are first responders of some kind or another.
He talks with them about issues in their jobs and tries to find a way to solve any problems they may encounter.
First responders need to be taken care of because they take care of us in our most vulnerable moments.
A strong mental health system in Kentucky is essential for the well-being of individuals, families, and communities across the state.
Access to timely, affordable mental health care helps people stay healthy, work consistently, and support their families, while also reducing strain on emergency rooms, schools, and the criminal justice system.
Kentuckians face unique challenges, including rural access gaps, workforce shortages, and higher-than-average rates of substance use and depression, which makes a reliable and coordinated mental health system especially important.
Taking a balanced approach—one that encourages responsible funding, community-based services, and collaboration between public and private providers—can improve outcomes without unnecessary political division.
Strengthening mental health care is not about partisanship; it is about ensuring that Kentuckians have the support they need to lead stable, productive lives.
