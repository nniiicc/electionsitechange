Today we celebrate Labor Day, which for many Vermonters is a day to relax, host a BBQ, maybe sleep in or catch up with friends.
This day of rest at the end of summer was created to celebrate the successes of the labor movement in the United States and the contributions of workers to our country.
The American labor movement has worked to advance higher wages & decent benefits, safer working conditions, and more equitable workplaces for millions of people over the past 100+ years.
These successes, however, have not been guaranteed, as powerful forces continually try to prevent workers from organizing or labor unions from bargaining with employers.
To ensure that workers in Vermont are guaranteed the right to organize and collectively bargain, the Legislature has advanced Proposal 3, which I co-sponsored.
This amendment to the Vermont Constitution “to provide that the citizens of the State have the right to collectively bargain” was introduced in 2023 and passed by two consecutive legislatures, as required for any proposal to amend the Vermont Constitution.
Now the Collective Bargaining Amendment will appear on the ballot and you will have the opportunity to vote on it this fall (read the full text of the amendment here).
I urge you to vote “yes” on this important measure so workers in Vermont can be assured that no matter the political headwinds, they will always be able to organize and bargain for their wages and conditions of employment.
I am a long-time supporter of organized labor, and am honored to be endorsed by Vermont AFL-CIO and Vermont-NEA.
In the true spirit of Labor Day, please join me making a plan to vote for Proposal 3.
Back to School.
Vermont schools are back in session!
I hope it’s been a smooth start for everyone, despite a few bumps, such as a shortage of school bus drivers that has many districts scrambling.
The new school year also means that cell phones are no longer allowed for students in schools, as 2025 Act 72 goes into effect, requiring schools to enforce a no cell phone policy.
School boards are also required, under 2026 Act 125, to develop or update immigration protocols to protect all students by next school year and the Attorney General’s Office has released an Immigration Resource Guide for Students.
Finally, school boards are working to meet the requirements of 2026 Act 170, which requires them form both cooperative education service agencies to share services and study committees this fall to formulate plans for merging school districts.
For more about Act 170, read my post on consensus education reform.
College students are also coming and going, as Middlebury College students return to campus and local students scatter to colleges far and wide.
This summer, two wonderful students worked with me on the campaign trail and have now headed off to college or a semester abroad.
Their energy and insights were so wonderful as we talked with constituents across the district.
Read Quincy Sabick’s piece about interning with my campaign: A Remarkable Young Woman’s Perspective.
I also recommend this week’s cover article in Seven Days, “College Try,” about the Community College of Vermont, an oft forgotten higher education gem in Vermont.
I hope college students, staff, and professors everywhere have a great semester!
