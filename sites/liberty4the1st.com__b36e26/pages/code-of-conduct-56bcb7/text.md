CHAD FOR CONGRESS The Mission The Logic The Combat Vet Voter FAQ Code of Conduct Contact Candidate Code of Conduct The Rules I Will Govern Myself By I believe voters deserve to know the principles by which a candidate intends to govern before they vote.
My principles will not change because of polls, donors, political pressure, or the 24-hour news cycle.
1 .
The Constitution Comes First I will defend the Constitution and the rights it protects.
Government exists to protect life, liberty, and property —not to manage every aspect of our lives.
TL;DR: Government exists to protect life, liberty, and property.
2 .
My Principles Are Not for Sale I will vote according to the principles of the Libertarian Party Platform , whether those positions are popular or unpopular.
You may disagree with me.
You should never have to wonder where I stand.
I will not change my principles to obtain votes, money, endorsements, or political power.
TL;DR: You should never have to wonder where I stand.
3 .
No Party Loyalty Above the Law I will not defend corruption, criminal conduct, or violations of the law simply because the person responsible is a member of my party or shares my political beliefs.
The standard applies to my side too .
If evidence of corruption or criminal conduct exists, I will not ignore it because it is politically inconvenient.
The law must apply equally—regardless of party, position, wealth, or power.
TL;DR: The standard applies to my side too.
4 .
No Identity Politics or Political Theater I will not use race, sex, or identity as political weapons, or engage in other forms of political theater designed to divide Americans, manufacture outrage, or generate votes and clicks.
TL;DR: No political theater designed to divide Americans.
5 .
Politics Without Hate I will challenge bad ideas, bad legislation, political parties, and public officials.
I will not manufacture hatred toward Americans simply because it is politically useful.
I wish my opponents well.
I do not want to bear the burden of hate or resentment.
I can fight their ideas without hating the people who hold them.
TL;DR: I can fight their ideas without hating the people who hold them.
6 .
Power Is Not the Prize Plato warned about those who seek power for its own sake.
I believe there is wisdom in that warning: the desire to wield power is not proof that someone should wield it.
Public office should be a responsibility—not a prize.
I want to defend the Constitution, reduce the power of the federal government, protect individual liberty, and then get out of the way.
I want to go back to watching the Broncos, Nuggets, and Avs , and playing my guitar.
That is the point: I don't want to spend my life in Washington.
I want to make government small enough that people can get back to living theirs.
TL;DR: Make government small enough that people can get back to living theirs.
The Standard Is the Standard To the voters out there: we have been blessed to live in a free country where freedom can grow and where our differences can be accepted.
Our history is far from perfect.
We should never shut the door on it or pretend the past did not happen.
We should learn from it, grow from it, and leave something better for those who come after us.
We do not have to agree with one another to respect one another's rights.
We do not have to hate our political opponents.
And we do not have to surrender our principles to win an election.
The Standard Is the Standard.
That applies to me, my party, my allies, and my opponents.
No exceptions. — A phrase famously associated with Pittsburgh Steelers coach Mike Tomlin.
