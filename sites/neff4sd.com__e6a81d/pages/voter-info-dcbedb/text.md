Find My County Auditor 2026 General Election KEY ITEMS ON YOUR BALLOT THIS NOVEMBER 3 South Dakota voters will consider four proposed amendments to the State Constitution in the November 3, 2026 General Election.
The summaries below are provided for voter education; Elliott's campaign perspective is labeled separately.
I AMENDMENT I — MEDICAID EXPANSION FUNDING South Dakota's Constitution currently requires the state to provide expanded Medicaid coverage under the federal Affordable Care Act.
Amendment I would allow the state to discontinue that expansion if the federal government pays less than 90% of the costs required under the program.
Elliott's Read Gives protection to taxpayers if the federal funding share falls below 90%.
Elliott notes that the current cost of this expansion is roughly $340 million.
YES: Changes the Constitution so South Dakota would no longer be required to maintain expanded Medicaid if the federal funding share falls below 90%.
NO: Leaves the current constitutional requirement for Medicaid expansion in place regardless of whether the federal funding share falls below 90%.
J AMENDMENT J — U.S.
CITIZENSHIP REQUIRED TO VOTE Amendment J would add language to the South Dakota Constitution expressly stating that only citizens of the United States are eligible to vote in South Dakota elections.
Elliott's Read Should non-U.S. citizens be permitted to vote in South Dakota elections?
YES: Adds the U.S. citizenship requirement explicitly to the South Dakota Constitution.
NO: Leaves the Constitution unchanged without adding this new language.
K AMENDMENT K — UNCLAIMED PROPERTY TRUST FUND South Dakota receives substantial amounts of unclaimed property each year.
That money remains claimable by its rightful owners even after it comes into state custody.
Amendment K would constitutionally establish an invested trust fund for unclaimed property, helping preserve it for future claims while generating investment income for the state.
Elliott's Read Should your unclaimed property flow into the general fund, or be kept for you in trust so only its investment earnings may support the state's general fund?
YES: Establishes the unclaimed-property trust in the State Constitution, allowing funds to be invested for the long term while preserving them for claims and providing investment income to the state.
NO: Leaves the Constitution unchanged; the Legislature would continue governing the handling of unclaimed property under state statute.
L AMENDMENT L — 60% VOTE REQUIRED FOR CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS Currently, an amendment to the South Dakota Constitution generally passes when it receives a majority of the votes cast on the measure.
Amendment L would raise that threshold so future constitutional amendments must receive at least 60% of the votes cast to become part of the Constitution.
Elliott's Read There appears to be a significant amount of campaign mail creating confusion around this issue.
At its core, Amendment L would raise the bar for changing our foundational state constitution.
Elliott also finds it noteworthy that out-of-state, Washington, D.C.-based money is being spent to oppose this amendment.
It raises a question worth asking: Why are outside special interests investing in keeping South Dakota's threshold for changing our Constitution at a simple majority?
YES: Changes the Constitution to require 60% voter approval for future constitutional amendments.
NO: Keeps the current approval threshold, under which a simple majority of votes cast is sufficient.
