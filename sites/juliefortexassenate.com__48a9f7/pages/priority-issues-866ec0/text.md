Returning Texas Public Education to Its Purpose
Article VII, Section 1 of the Texas Constitution says:
“A general diffusion of knowledge being essential to the preservation of the liberties and rights of the people, it shall be the duty of the Legislature of the State to establish and make suitable provision for the support and maintenance of an efficient system of public free schools.”
We have allowed our education system to become increasingly complicated, bureaucratic and burdened with responsibilities that extend far beyond the fundamental mission of educating children.
I believe a strong general education should give every child the foundation necessary to become a capable, independent adult.
That includes:
- Reading and writing
- Mathematics
- Science
- History
- Civics, citizenship and government
- Financial literacy and money management
- Physical fitness
- Practical life skills
- The arts
These are the building blocks.
Once a student has mastered these fundamentals, they have the tools to continue learning throughout life - whether they pursue college, a skilled trade, entrepreneurship, military service, or another path.
Put More of the Education Dollar Where It Matters Most
This brings us to funding.
Texas has increased funding for public education, including a significant investment in public schools in 2025.
More money does not automatically produce better education.
The results should concern every Texan.
Our children's ability to read, reason, understand history and government, manage money, and acquire the knowledge necessary to become capable, independent citizens should be the measure that matters most.
Texas ranked 44th in the nation in 8th grade reading, having declined the last two years.
I want to see education dollars prioritized toward the things that directly affect a child's ability to learn: Teachers.
Curriculum.
Books.
Educational tools.
Classroom materials.
School safety.
And effective instruction.
I would rather see an additional dollar go toward the teacher standing in front of a classroom than another dollar added to an unnecessary layer of bureaucracy,.
Teacher compensation should be a major priority and I support a 20% increase in teacher base pay at all levels.
Teaching is not simply another job.
It is a profession requiring knowledge, skill, patience, judgment and an ability to shape the next generation.
Teachers deserve compensation that respects both the profession and the God-given gifts many bring to it.
At the same time, we should simultaneously examine administrative compensation and staffing so that our salary structure is better aligned with our actual mission.
Protect Children's Privacy
Our children should not become sources of data simply because they attend public school.
I believe Texas should prohibit unnecessary data mining and surveying of children in schools.
A child's personal information - and information about a child's family - should be protected.
Parents should know what information is being collected, why it is being collected, who has access to it and how it will be used.
Government and schools should not assume that because a child is in a classroom, every piece of information about that child is theirs to collect.
Parents are the primary guardians of their children - not the government.
Parents Have a Right to Choose
I also believe school choice should be truly universal.
Parents should have meaningful choices about where and how their children are educated.
That includes traditional public schools, public charter schools, private schools, homeschooling and other educational models.
Parents Who Homeschool Should Be Left Alone
Not every child needs to be educated inside a government school.
Texas has long recognized the right of parents to homeschool their children, and I believe that right should be protected and strengthened.
A School Cannot Be an Effective Place to Learn if It Is Not Safe
Children cannot learn effectively in an environment where classrooms are repeatedly disrupted by violence or serious misconduct.
Teachers and staff should not be expected to sacrifice their own safety - or the safety of other students - in order to maintain an environment that is fundamentally unmanageable.
We need clear discipline standards and effective procedures for removing violent students when necessary, while preserving appropriate due process and educational opportunities.
A safe classroom is a prerequisite to a productive classroom.

A Philosophy of Stewardship
Every taxpayer dollar represents the hard work and sacrifice of a Texas family.
Government is not the owner of those resources; it is their steward.
Good stewardship requires asking not only whether something is a good idea, but whether it is the proper responsibility of government.
Our goal should not be a bigger government or a smaller government.
Our goal should be the right government - one that faithfully fulfills its constitutional duties, empowers citizens, strengthens communities, and respects the trust placed in it by the people.
That is how we restore confidence in government.
That is how we strengthen Texas.
That is how we restore the partnership between government and the people it serves.
Government Leads
These are the constitutional and fundamental responsibilities of government.
They include protecting life and liberty, administering justice, securing our elections, defending our borders, maintaining essential public infrastructure, ensuring public safety, and faithfully executing every duty assigned by the Constitution.
These responsibilities should always receive first claim on taxpayer dollars because they are the very reason government exists.
If government cannot fully perform its constitutional obligations, it should not expand into responsibilities beyond them.
Government Partners
Some initiatives strengthen Texas and create greater opportunity, but they do not always require government to be the primary provider.
Whenever practical, these efforts should encourage:
- Individual responsibility
- Family involvement
- Private enterprise
- Local leadership
- Nonprofit organizations
- Public-private partnerships
- Personal voluntary charitable investment
Taxpayer funding may sometimes be appropriate, but it should complement - not replace - the initiative, generosity, innovation, and commitment of Texas citizens.
Government should empower communities, not become a substitute for them.
COMMUNITY OPPORTUNITIES
Communities Lead
Many worthwhile causes enrich our neighborhoods and improve lives, but they are often best accomplished outside government.
These efforts should primarily be supported through personal voluntary charitable investment by individuals, families, churches, civic organizations, nonprofits, businesses, and local communities.
When people freely choose to invest their time, talents, and resources in causes they value, they build stronger communities while experiencing the meaning, purpose, and fulfillment that come from serving others.
Compassion is most powerful when it is voluntary.
Government should never crowd out the generosity and service of free citizens.
