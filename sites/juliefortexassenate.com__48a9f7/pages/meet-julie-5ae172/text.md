Every life is shaped by certain convictions.
Mine has been shaped by a belief that leadership is not about power - it is about faithfully caring for what has been entrusted to us.
Over the years, I've come to believe that stewardship extends far beyond finances or property.
It is reflected in how we value people, care for relationships, honor truth, protect freedom, and faithfully use every opportunity God places in our hands.
Whether raising a family, operating a business, serving in a ministry, leading a nonprofit, or holding public office, I believe the same standard applies: Leave people stronger.
Leave organizations healthier.
Leave communities better than you found them.
That belief didn't begin in politics.
It was shaped through a lifetime of learning, serving, growing, and seeking God's direction.
I was born in Kentucky, raised in Florida, and moved to Texas in 1991 simply because I wanted to be closer to my mom.
I wasn't pursuing politics or a particular career path.
I simply wanted to build a life rooted in faith, family, meaningful work, and service wherever God opened the next door.
Looking back now, I can see that every one of those doors was preparing me for the next.
I had the privilege of serving in the United States Army, where I learned discipline, responsibility, teamwork, and the importance of quietly doing your job well.
Afterward, much of my professional career centered around nonprofit leadership and community service.
I served organizations supporting military families, individuals experiencing homelessness, adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities, and men and women rebuilding their lives after addiction.
I also spent time in executive business leadership.
As Corporate Administrator for a company employing more than 300 people, I was responsible for human resources, payroll, compliance, executive operations, and organizational management.
That experience reinforced something I have seen throughout my life: Good leadership requires both competence and character.
One of the greatest privileges of my career came while serving as Program and Events Director for the USO in San Antonio.
Working with military members and their families changed me.
Among the programs I developed was one of the area's first retreats specifically for Gold Star families - parents, spouses, and children grieving the loss of loved ones who gave their lives in military service.
One evening, we gathered around a campfire.
Family members quietly wrote down the words they never had the opportunity to say before saying goodbye.
As each note disappeared into the fire, I watched complete strangers become a community.
I'll never forget that night.
It reminded me that leadership isn't always about having answers.
Sometimes it's simply about creating a place where hope can begin to grow again.
Years later, while serving as Executive Director of a nonprofit helping individuals recover from addiction and homelessness, another moment permanently changed the way I lead.
One of our residents affectionately referred to me as her "benefactor." Instead of feeling honored, my heart sank.
I realized she believed her future depended upon someone else's approval rather than recognizing the gifts and abilities God had already placed within her.
That day changed me.
From then on, I became intentional about helping people regain confidence in themselves rather than dependence upon others.
Instead of doing everything for them, I encouraged them to make the phone calls, solve the problems, obtain the documents, navigate the obstacles, and discover something many had forgotten: " I can do this ." I still believe one of the greatest gifts a leader can give another person is confidence.
That conviction grew even stronger while serving through church's food pantry ministry.
During volunteer training based on the principles of When Helping Hurts , one lesson stayed with me.
Never define people by what they lack.
Every person has value.
Every person has gifts.
Every person has something meaningful to contribute.
That simple idea transformed the way I looked at service.
Instead of asking only, "What does this person need?" I began asking, "What gifts has God already placed within this person?" Whether someone was standing in a food pantry line or leading a successful business, I wanted them to know they had something valuable to offer.
I believe one of leadership's greatest responsibilities is helping people recognize the value they already possess.
That philosophy has shaped my family as well.
My husband, Shane, and I married in 2007, blending our family of four children into one.
Today, one of our greatest joys is being grandparents.
Years ago, after encouraging Shane for more than a decade to start his own glass company, I decided to stop encouraging him and start believing for him.
One Christmas morning, I handed him a box.
Inside were business cards, licensing paperwork, marketing materials, a completed website, and everything needed to launch the business he had dreamed about for years.
That Christmas gift became MYCO Glass & Mirror .
Today, Shane serves customers throughout South Texas while I manage the business operations, marketing, and administration.
Every time I think about that Christmas morning, I still smile.
Following the tragic church shooting near our home in 2017, our family responded and attended the next week's service, just to be able to help however we could.
We stayed and joined the church, beginning our entire family on a path to a closer relationship with God.
We also had the privilege of helping establish and organize our church's armed security ministry.
For years, I coordinated volunteer training, helped secure grant funding to strengthen campus security, led our church food pantry, mentored women, served as a trustee, and volunteered wherever I was needed, including mowing the church grounds.
Another unexpected chapter came when I was invited to teach a women's retreat breakout session on biblical communication.
Preparing that lesson eventually became my book, Boldly Meek .
Its message is simple: Healthy families, healthy churches, healthy organizations, healthy communities, and even healthy government all depend upon people who are willing to speak truth with humility, extend grace, resolve conflict directly, and pursue reconciliation instead of division.
One verse in scripture has become especially meaningful throughout my life: "As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another." Proverbs 27:17 I often say that leadership should sharpen people, not diminish them.
It should build confidence, strengthen character, encourage responsibility, and leave others better equipped than before.
Ironically, I never set out to become a politician.
My journey into public service began simply by helping someone else run for office.
As I immersed myself in legislative hearings, committee testimony, and conversations throughout South Texas, I became convinced that government matters far more than most people realize.
I also became convinced that public office is not a position of privilege or prestige.
It is a public trust.
After encouraging others to seek this office, I eventually realized I needed to answer that same call myself.
Today I am running for the Texas Senate because I believe government exists to protect the God-given freedoms and liberties of the people, provide for public safety, maintain essential infrastructure, and faithfully steward the public trust.
When I'm not traveling the district or studying legislation, you'll probably find me reading, singing, mentoring a nonprofit or small business, mowing the lawn, or sitting beside my six-year-old granddaughter trying to explain the latest legislative debate in words she can understand.
We sometimes laugh about what we jokingly call the political "blah-blahs," but I treasure those conversations because they remind me why the future is worth investing in.
As I look back on my journey, I don't see a collection of jobs or titles.
I see opportunities.
Opportunities to serve.
Opportunities to learn.
Opportunities to grow.
And opportunities to faithfully steward whatever God placed in my care.
Whether that has been a family, a ministry, a business, a nonprofit organization, or now the public trust, my goal has remained the same: Leave people stronger.
Leave organizations healthier.
Leave communities better than you found them.
That is the standard I have tried to live by.
And it is the standard I will bring to the Texas Senate.
