In addition to help with promotion and other assistance, ministries and organizations participating in the Community Ministry Support Project may be considered by an independent panel of community leaders to receive any remaining campaign funds after the election, consistent with applicable law.
The selection will be based on the panel's evaluation of community impact and need, not on political activity or support.
Eligibility Any ministry or nonprofit organization that provides charitable care or services to people in Dimmit, Duval, Caldwell, Jim Hogg, Karnes, La Salle, Live Oak, McMullen, Starr, Webb, Wilson & Zapata counties, and portions of Bexar, Hays, Guadalupe & Travis counties ( see map ) are eligible to be nominated and participate in this project.
501(c)(3) exempt organization determination is not required in order to participate.
Online completion of the nomination form below is required.
Political Neutrality The Community Ministry Support Project exists to strengthen ministries and community organization, not to seek political endorsements.
Participating organizations will never be asked to endorse my campaign, support my candidacy, or encourage others to vote for me.
In fact, I respectfully ask participating ministries and organizations not to endorse my campaign.
My hope is that every participating organization can confidently take part knowing this project exists solely to celebrate and strengthen the work they do in our communities.
WHY THIS PROJECT MATTERS Healthy communities are built by people who know their neighbors, see the needs around them, and choose to serve.
Across our district, countless ministries, nonprofits, churches, and volunteers quietly change lives every day.
Yet many of these organizations operate with limited resources and little public recognition.
This project exists to help strengthen those already doing the work by sharing their stories, connecting them with supporters, and helping them expand their impact, all at no cost to the ministry or organization.
Redirecting Support Where It Changes Lives Political campaigns often raise and spend extraordinary amounts of money.
While campaigns have legitimate expenses, I've long believed that communities also deserve direct investment in the people and organizations serving them every day.
When appropriate, I will encourage my supporters who are able, to consider giving directly to local ministries and community organizations alongside their support for my campaign.
Every dollar invested in a food pantry, recovery ministry, mentoring program, pregnancy resource center, or local outreach has the potential to strengthen families and change lives long after an election has ended.
One or more participating ministries or organizations will be selected by an independent panel to receive any remaining campaign funds after the election, consistent with applicable law.
Every Community Has Leaders One of the most common things I've heard while visiting communities is that many people feel they don't have the credentials, education, or influence to have a meaningful voice.
Yet these same individuals are often the ones feeding hungry families, mentoring young people, caring for the elderly, or walking alongside those facing addiction or hardship.
I believe every person has unique, God-given talents, experiences, and gifts that bring value to their community.
Leadership isn't defined by a title, a degree, or elected office.
It is found in ordinary people who faithfully use their abilities to serve others with compassion, integrity, and perseverance.
Some of the greatest leaders in our communities are those whose names rarely make the headlines, but whose daily acts of service quietly change lives.
This project exists to honor those individuals, amplify their voices, celebrate the unique value they bring, and encourage others to discover and use their own gifts in service to their neighbors.
When people recognize that their contributions matter, communities become stronger, more connected, and more resilient.
Strengthening Those Who Serve Many ministries and nonprofits have incredible passion, but limited access to the tools that help organizations grow.
Training in nonprofit development, fundraising, board governance, compliance, volunteer management, storytelling, and community partnerships is often difficult to find, especially in rural communities.
With my background in nonprofit development, I hope to help connect organizations with practical resources, educational opportunities, experienced mentors, and one another so they can continue serving effectively for years to come.
Building a Network, Not Just a Program Communities become stronger when organizations know one another instead of working in isolation.
By connecting ministries, nonprofits, churches, volunteers, business leaders, and local supporters, we can share ideas, solve common challenges together, and build partnerships that multiply the impact of everyone involved.
The goal isn't to create another organization.
The goal is to strengthen the ones that already exist.
Investing in People Who Invest in Others One of my deepest convictions is that lasting change happens when people invest in other people.
Whether in faith, leadership, or community service, healthy organizations grow by developing others who can continue the work.
I want this project to encourage mentorship, apprenticeship, and leadership development so knowledge, experience, and compassion are intentionally passed from one generation to the next.
Strong communities don't depend on one person. they flourish when people are equipped and encouraged to serve, teach, and lead others.
A Commitment Beyond the Election This initiative isn't intended to end on Election Day.
Whether I am elected or not, I intend to continue visiting organizations, telling their stories, connecting people with opportunities to serve, and helping ministries and nonprofits strengthen their work.
Because healthy communities don't only need good public policy.
They need neighbors who know one another.
Churches that open their doors.
Volunteers who answer the call.
Nonprofits that meet needs with compassion.
And, leaders who believe the strongest communities are built from the ground up.
WHY NOW?
People have asked me why I'm launching the Community Ministry Support Project during a campaign.
The answer is simple.
Campaigns naturally bring attention to candidates.
While I have that attention, I want to use it to shine a light on the people who quietly strengthen our communities every day.
If this campaign gives me a platform, I want to steward that platform well.
I want people to know about the church feeding families every week.
The recovery ministry helping people rebuild their lives.
The volunteers mentoring children.
The nonprofit serving veterans.
The neighbors who faithfully care for others without seeking recognition.
Those are the stories our communities need to hear.
Whether I'm elected or not, those organizations will still be serving, and they deserve to be known, celebrated, and strengthened.
That's why this project exists.
Not to draw more attention to me.
But to redirect whatever attention I have toward the people who are already making a difference.
