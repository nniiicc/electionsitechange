Court Motion PDF The Rule Behind Donor Masking: 11 CFR 110.6 One federal regulation explains why a candidate's own FEC filings can look spotless even while party accounts hold the money.
This is also why surface-level database searches of a candidate can be evaded: the routing itself is the escape hatch.
Code of Federal Regulations · Title 11, Section 110.6 Earmarked Contributions and the Party-Routing Gap When a donor writes a check directly to a candidate's principal campaign committee, 11 CFR 110.6 requires that contribution to be itemized on Schedule A of the candidate's quarterly FEC report with the donor's name, employer, and address.
When the check is made out to a national party committee instead (the DCCC, the NRCC, or a leadership PAC), the donor's name appears only on the party committee's own monthly filing.
The candidate's personal disclosure remains completely clean.
A searcher who stops at the candidate's filings, or at any single database built on those filings, finds nothing: the evasion is built into the routing, and that is precisely the lesson.
