Donate Kathy Dawes has called Idaho home for over fifty years.
When Kathy and her husband first arrived, they were welcomed with open arms and found a place to truly put down roots.
Since then, she has given back in every way possible—through education, the arts, parks, science, and civic engagement—because she knows firsthand that strong communities are built by people who show up, work together, and invest in each other.
Inspired by her father, who was a professor at the University of Oklahoma Medical School, Kathy attended Carleton College and then taught high school science in Wisconsin.
She and her husband, Dana, set out on an adventure living on a remote island in northern Ontario, Canada, cutting holes in the ice for water and chopping wood to keep warm.
Encouraged by a friend to head west, they packed everything they owned into a VW Bug and drove to Idaho to attend the University of Idaho, where both Kathy and Dana earned their master’s degrees.
In Idaho, Kathy worked as a lab technician and graphic artist for scientific publications, which deepened her appreciation for science and education.
As a stay-at-home mom, she cherished the years she spent raising their two young children.
When they started school, she returned to teaching, first in Special Education at Moscow High School, where she found real fulfillment helping students discover their strengths and connect with job opportunities in the community.
She later taught science at Moscow Junior High School, encouraging curiosity and hands-on learning until retiring in 2008.
A dedicated community builder, Kathy helped found the Moscow Community Theater, Arts Commission, Friends of Phillips Farm, and Palouse Discovery Science Center.
She also dedicated 13 years to the Latah County Parks Board to protect and improve local parks.
Driven by a commitment to civil discourse and investing in programs that bring people together, Kathy became a moderator for Braver Angels in 2018, leading workshops designed to bridge political divides.
Her dedication to fostering community and human rights earned her the 2024 Latah County Human Rights Task Force Rosa Parks Award.
From raising her family to teaching generations of local students, Idaho has given Kathy countless opportunities to serve by protecting parks, supporting the arts and sciences, promoting human rights, or helping neighbors bridge political differences.
She is grateful to call this place home and is running for Idaho House to keep our communities strong, welcoming, and connected.

In the Idaho Legislature, she will use her skills and experience to make living in Idaho as affordable and welcoming as possible for all by focusing on real solutions instead of culture-war issues.
She is committed to providing quality public education, workforce training, and higher education opportunities for all.
Kathy believes prevention is key.
She will take a fiscally conservative approach by prioritizing prevention programs to mitigate costlier issues in the future.
She supports preserving our public lands as well as recreational and environmental resources for future generations.
Key Priorities and Values: I VALUE AN ECONOMY THAT WORKS FOR ALL HARD-WORKING IDAHOANS AND FAMILIES Cut the tax on groceries, saving families 6% at the supermarket.
Expanding the homeowners tax exemption.
Affordable housing, healthcare, and childcare.
Prevention programs that promote community health and well-being are designed to mitigate more costly issues in the future.
I VALUE OUR SCHOOLS Public funds used for quality public schools, teachers, and early education programs, not private schools.
Supporting universities, colleges, and trade schools is important to keep talent and the next generation of leaders in Idaho.
I VALUE INDIVIDUAL FREEDOM AND LOCAL CONTROL Protecting individual liberty and freedom for ALL Idahoans to live our best lives, free from discrimination of any kind, and be able to make our own healthcare decisions.
Advocating for local control and the rights of local governments and boards to make decisions for their communities.
I VALUE CIVIL DISCOURSE AND ACTIVE PROBLEM SOLVING Current political divisiveness often keeps us from talking civilly with each other, with the respect and dignity we all deserve.
Using my skills as an educator and trained moderator, I will “bring Idahoans together.” Provide opportunities for productive, respectful dialogue among Idahoans, including among our legislators on both sides of the aisle.
Focus on finding common ground and determining workable solutions to meet the needs of Idahoans and our communities.
