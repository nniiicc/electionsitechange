Aug 18 2 min read Yes on 3: Allow Congressional Redistricting in Maryland Earlier this month, the Maryland General Assembly held a special session to consider a constitutional amendment on how congressional redistricting should be handled.
The purpose of this constitutional amendment is to ensure that Maryland has the same ability as other states to pass a congressional redistricting plan under applicable federal laws.
What It Does (and Doesn’t) Do The proposed constitutional amendment does not contain a congressional map.
It does not draw a single district line or make any changes ahead of the 2026 general election.
It makes three straightforward changes to allow Maryland to meet the moment as states across the country engage in mid-decade redistricting.
1.
It clarifies that the term “legislative district” in Article III of the Maryland Constitution refers to districts for the Maryland Senate and House of Delegates.
This resolves ambiguity and gives courts clear guidance.
2.
It clarifies that the Maryland Constitution and Declaration of Rights do not apply to congressional redistricting.
In the event of a future lawsuit, courts would judge the constitutionality of the map based on applicable federal law, making it consistent with other states.
3.
It gives the General Assembly the option to send a congressional redistricting case directly to the Supreme Court of Maryland when time is critical.
This does not change the process for every case.
It simply places another tool in the toolbox so disputes can be resolved quickly and efficiently when necessary.
Ultimately, Marylanders will have the final say in November when they vote on whether to accept or reject these changes.
