Creating More Food Justice
I have worked for many years to build a more just, sustainable, and resilient food system.
My approach is to make systemic change through new policies and legislation.
At the same time, I also promote and support hands-on help to people in need.
Recently, I have been focusing on ways to create more effective edible food waste recovery and diversion programs.
Dealing with Waste in Our Food System
(Graphic credit: Isra Qadri, University of Maryland School of Public Policy)
In the United States, it is estimated we waste between 30-40% of our total food supply each year.
In Maryland, this represents 1.7 billion pounds of food and 19% of the state’s total waste, even though most of it is edible and delicious.
Most of the waste is ending up in our landfills or is incinerated.
This has huge implications for our environment.
At the same time, we know too many households are experiencing food insecurity.
This has long been unacceptable to me.
In 2021, I introduced and successfully passed a bill requiring any company that produces more than one ton of organic waste per week to either donate eligible food or compost it.
Next session I plan to propose legislation to expand who must donate or compost food waste and to improve the quality and amount of food that is diverted for human consumption.
