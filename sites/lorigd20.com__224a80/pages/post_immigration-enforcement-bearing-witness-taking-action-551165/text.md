Immigration Enforcement: Bearing Witness, Taking Action
Some of the many photos featured on the GoFundMe pages of people living in Montgomery County who have had family members detained by federal Immigration and Customs Enforcement actions.
While the shock and awe of the early days of President Trump’s immigration crackdown have died down, the cruel reality of mass detentions and deportation continues.
The Immigrants Rights Collective recently declared, “What we are witnessing is not simply an immigration enforcement operation.
It is an assault on the safety, dignity, and stability of our communities.
Every detention leaves behind children, parents, loved ones, coworkers, neighbors, and entire communities forced to pick up the pieces.”
The sheer scale of the terror is mind boggling.
For example, last month ICE reported a record-breaking number of arrests: more than 1,300 people in the Maryland and Virginia suburbs; 51,000 individuals nationwide.
The vast, vast majority of those arrested had no criminal record.
For the individuals who are detained, this involves:
- The devastation of being kidnapped: ICE arrests are rife with instances of mistreatment, physical violence, and violation of an individual’s rights.
- The horrors of detention: People who are arrested are often placed in cramped holding cells, frequently without access to adequate nutrition, needed medication, contact with family members, or legal representation.
They are being moved further away from Maryland and more frequently than ever before.
They often have no information about where they are being sent or when.
If one fights deportation, they face the threat of extended time in detention.
- The uncertainty of being deported: Sometimes people are deported back to their own country, where they may or may not have family and supportive communities.
For some, they may not have lived there for decades or since they were children.
In some cases, those countries are very dangerous places to return to.
Some people are being sent to countries they have no connection to at all.
These arrests—and the fear they create—also affect family and community members left behind.
There is the trauma of walking to school and seeing parents being snatched away from their children.
There is the physical and emotional stress of living in constant fear of being picked up, whether one has legal documentation or not.
How might that impact how you get to work, go to the grocery store, access healthcare, take kids to school, or attend religious services or other community gatherings?
How might all of these things affect your health?
Recent studies have documented a decrease in the number of Hispanic children attending school and fewer immigrant children receiving healthcare or putting off going to a doctor until a problem gets much worse.
Food insecurity is at a five-year high, but many food providers report immigrants are reluctant to reach out for assistance over fears of targeted enforcement actions.
Consider all the devastation, horror, and uncertainty and multiply it by the 51,000 people who were arrested just last month.
It is hard to contemplate and it is devastating—especially if you believe, as I do, that the pain is the point of this cruel MAGA strategy.
Taking Action: What I Am Doing (at the State level)
Last session I introduced and passed several pieces of legislation to address some of the crises caused by increased and indiscriminate immigration enforcement, including:
- the No Kings Act, which creates a legal recourse when someone’s civil rights have been violated by Federal agents, and
- protecting sensitive personal data kept by the state and prohibiting data brokers from selling Marylanders’ data to ICE through my data privacy bill.
I worked with colleagues to support the ban on 287G agreements and the Community Trust Act to end Maryland law enforcement cooperation with ICE.
I also introduced the Aviation Safety Act, to add safeguards and transparency to ICE flights through Maryland airports.
This bill did not pass, but I plan to bring it back again next Session.
I am currently engaged with individuals affected by ICE actions and with immigration advocacy organizations.
I am developing legislation for next Session to continue doing everything within the power of the State to protect Maryland’s immigrant communities.
