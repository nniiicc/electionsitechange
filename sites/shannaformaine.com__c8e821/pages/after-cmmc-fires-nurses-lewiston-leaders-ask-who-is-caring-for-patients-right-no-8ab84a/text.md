CMMC confirmed Friday that 15 nurses have been fired, saying that “employment actions are based on documented violations of established hospital policies and standards.” Local leaders from across the political spectrum said the firings appeared to be related to recent union activities, which would be in violation of state and federal law, and demanded the nurses be reinstated. “(This) is just bad business and bad for our community,” Lewiston Mayor Carl Sheline said, later calling the actions “shortsighted and damaging.” Nurses at CMMC filed for an election late last month to join the Maine State Nurses Association, which represents 4,000 nurses and caregivers across the state.
During their campaign, nurses have advocated for improved patient care, safer staffing, better equipment and personnel retention.
They will hold a vote Sept.
24.
On Tuesday, nurses and supporters held a demonstration outside the hospital.
In the time since the union filing, city leaders and union officials said, the nurses were suspended, with many of them later terminated.
Several groups and individuals, including the Maine AFL-CIO, have characterized the mass suspension as retaliation by Prime Healthcare , the parent company of the foundation that owns CMMC, against unionizing workers and have demanded the reinstatement of the nurses.
A CMMC spokesperson said Friday that no employee has been terminated due to union activity, which, they said, does not exempt employees from responsibilities “to uphold basic patient-care, safety, attendance and workplace hospital policies that apply to everyone.” Violations for fired nurses include “patient abandonment; timekeeping violations; disruption of patient-care; and other conduct that interferes with patient care or patient safety,” the spokesperson said.
Matters related to five additional employees “remain pending due to violations of longstanding hospital policies and standards, including those needed to ensure patient care and safety,” the spokesperson said.
The hospital remains focused on providing safe, high-quality care, the spokesperson said.
“We respect the rights of every nurse to engage in legally protected activity and to make their own individual decision about union representation, including the right to support a union or to remain in a direct relationship with the hospital,” the spokesperson said.
“We remain committed to working constructively with our nurses and addressing concerns in the best interests of our patients and community.” LOCAL LEADERS REACT Local elected officials and candidates of both political parties spoke out in support of the nurses.
