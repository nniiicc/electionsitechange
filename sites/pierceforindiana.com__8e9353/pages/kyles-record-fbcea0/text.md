Kyle’s Record:
Getting Results for Hoosiers
Representative Kyle Pierce is focused on a simple goal at the Statehouse: solving problems, protecting individual freedom, and making Indiana an even better place to live, work, raise a family, and do business.
Representing House District 36, Kyle has worked to turn conservative principles into meaningful public policy.
From protecting Hoosiers' most personal data to preparing Indiana for the future of digital finance, his legislation focuses on real issues facing Hoosiers today and challenges coming tomorrow.
Protecting Hoosiers' Genetic Privacy
House Bill 1521 — Consumer Genetic Testing
Your DNA may be the most personal information you have.
Hoosiers should have a say in what happens to it.
Kyle authored HB 1521 to strengthen protections for consumers who use at-home genetic testing services.
The law requires greater disclosure about how genetic information is handled, establishes consent requirements for certain uses of genetic data and biological samples, and gives consumers greater control over their information.
It also protects Hoosiers from certain forms of discrimination based on their use of consumer genetic testing or their genetic testing results.
The principle is simple: Your DNA belongs to you.
HB 1521 received overwhelming bipartisan support and was signed into law in 2025.
Making Indiana a Leader in Digital Assets
House Bill 1042 — Cryptocurrency & Financial Innovation
Technology moves quickly.
Government shouldn't stand in the way of innovation — but the law should provide clear rules for the people and businesses building the future.
Kyle authored HB 1042 to establish greater clarity for digital assets and cryptocurrency in Indiana.
The legislation protects Hoosiers' ability to use and take custody of digital assets, addresses the treatment of noncustodial technologies, and expands cryptocurrency investment options within certain public retirement and defined-contribution programs.
The goal is to ensure Indiana remains a state where financial innovation can thrive while Hoosiers maintain control over their own assets.
Indiana should welcome innovation, protect individual choice, and give entrepreneurs room to build.
HB 1042 was signed into law in 2026.
Promoting Accountability in Financial Decision-Making
House Bill 1273 — Proxy Advisors
When financial decisions are made on behalf of investors, those decisions should be transparent and accountable.
Kyle authored HB 1273 to establish requirements surrounding proxy advisory services and help ensure that organizations influencing major investment decisions operate under clearer standards.
The legislation reflects Kyle's broader commitment to protecting investors, strengthening accountability, and ensuring financial decisions are made with beneficiaries' interests in mind.
HB 1273 passed both chambers of the General Assembly and was signed into law in 2026.
Keeping Indiana's Laws Clear and Effective
House Bill 1088 — Technical Corrections
Good government also means making sure Indiana's laws are clear, consistent, and work as intended.
Kyle authored HB 1088, Indiana's technical corrections legislation, to address errors, inconsistencies, and clerical issues throughout the Indiana Code without making substantive policy changes.
It may not generate headlines, but this kind of work matters.
Effective government requires lawmakers who are willing to dig into the details and make sure the law works for the people it serves.
HB 1088 passed the General Assembly and became law in 2026.
Giving Hoosiers Greater Clarity in Banking
House Bill 1284 — Deposit Account Agreements
Kyle authored HB 1284 to modernize Indiana law governing deposit account agreements between financial institutions and their customers.
The legislation addressed how banks and credit unions may provide customers notice of changes to their account agreements and how continued use of an account may constitute acceptance of those updated terms.
The measure was part of Kyle's work on financial policy and his efforts to ensure that Indiana law keeps pace with how Hoosiers and financial institutions conduct business.
