MY STORY

My parents couldn't read or write, but they raised me to value hard work and integrity.
When I arrived in America, I didn't speak much English—but I spoke the language of perseverance.
I've been speaking it ever since.
In 2017, I was elected to the Douglas County School Board.
We hadn't passed a school bond in over a decade.
But in 2018, we passed a $250 million bond and mill levy override that brought new schools, better tools, and renewed trust.
Later, I joined the South Metro Fire Rescue board—where I helped oversee a $200 million budget to support over 570,000 residents.
I've never worn the uniform, but I've always had their back.
But service is more than numbers.
It's about people.
It's about how we show up —especially when it's hard.
At a school board forum, someone said I didn't belong—because of how I speak.
That moment hurt.
But I didn't shout.
I didn't quit.
I filed a report, took a breath—and kept serving.
Because leadership isn't about how you sound.
It's about how you serve.
Last year, when a respected Japanese American physician was wrongly accused of bringing communism into a local campaign, I stood up.
We gathered 1,200 signatures, with mayors, senators, and neighbors standing shoulder to shoulder.
Because injustice doesn't scare me—silence does.
That's why I'm running for Colorado State Senate.
I believe in real solutions—smart growth, excellent education, and care that honors our seniors.
I want to represent those who've been told they're not enough.

THE WORK BEHIND THE WORDS
Why I stepped up to serve
Family, business, and public service have shaped the way I solve problems.
01/PUBLIC EDUCATION
The Teacher Conference That Changed Everything
I’ll never forget the parent–teacher conference that changed everything for me.
My daughter’s teacher—an extraordinary educator shaping young minds—was spending her own grocery money on classroom supplies.
Someone needs to fight for these kids, I thought.
That’s why I ran for the Douglas County School Board—not for a title, but as a parent who believed our teachers deserved better.
In my four years on the board, we secured an additional $40 million annually to raise teacher pay, expanded Career and Technical Education programs, and passed a $250 million bond to repair our aging schools.
Every dollar went where it belonged—to our teachers and students.

For Working Families

Every classroom should be fully funded, every teacher fairly paid.

As someone who watched my parents sacrifice everything for their children's future, I understand the importance of intergenerational care.
