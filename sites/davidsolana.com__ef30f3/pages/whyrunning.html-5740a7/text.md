When I was a kid in the 1980s, we would gather around a TV in school to watch Space Shuttle launches.
We knew that America was the country defending individual rights and democracy in the world.
We knew the American Dream meant hard work would enable us to do better in life than our parents did.
In the 1990s, with the end of the Cold War, the world was our oyster.
America was free to lead the way to a brighter democratic future.
While domestic policy squabbles were already hinting that we were not on a path toward healing from the worst parts of American history, we could at least point to the burgeoning World Wide Web, federal budget surpluses, and a booming economy as signs that we were on the right path.
Then in 2001 we suffered a national tragedy, rallied around a president who led us into decades of impossible-to-win wars that created their own spin-off wars and surging budget deficits.
We wasted American lives, trillions of dollars that could have been spent solving problems at home, and we killed hundreds of thousands of non-Americans, making many new enemies in the process.
The pandemic should have been a time to reset and to rally around a full society effort to defeat an enemy we could agree was bad for all of us.
Unfortunately the pandemic was used as a political tool to further divide us.
We need to unite once more as a nation to solve our biggest problems, and to regain our pride in becoming the beacon of democracy in a fast-changing world.
We need to solve our biggest problems at home: DC’s Right to Home Rule Unaffordable and rising costs Health care Education Unsustainable national debt and inequality Aging infrastructure We need to rally toward an America-led future where we invest in ourselves to invent, develop, and produce the technologies that will define the future.
We have the ability to make smart choices about where we want to be, and how to get there through smart spending choices, and equitable taxation.
WHAT AM I GOING TO DO AS DELEGATE?
I know, I know, you’re thinking that the Delegate to the House of Representatives is a meaningless position because the Delegate cannot vote on the House floor.
I can’t tell you how many times I’ve heard people call it a cushy position for DC politicians to sit back and collect a paycheck.
That can’t be farther from the truth.
The Delegate’s role is to advocate tirelessly for your rights as DC Citizens .
As a non-voting member, it’s imperative that the Delegate be constantly working with other members of Congress to write, co-sponsor, and introduce legislation.
Most importantly of all, I will be encouraging Congress to recognize the best way forward for DC is as a state .
Years of working the halls of Congress with a Democrat as our representative have not worked.
It’s time to try something new, let’s send an Independent to Congress to represent us, and not allow either major political party to stop us from speaking up for our rights, or to say we don’t deserve to have the same rights as other US Citizens just because of how we vote.
Our two-party system has led us to a national debt greater than the national production, to health-care-driven bankruptcies, and to a socially unsustainable wealth gap.
As an Independent, I am not beholden to any donor, to any political party.
I will work for the people of DC.
It’s time to send an Independent to Congress.
