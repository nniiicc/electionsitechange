Laken Riley Act
One Year After the Laken Riley Act, Leadership and Law Enforcement Are Saving Lives
One year ago, I stood in the White House as President Donald Trump signed the Laken Riley Act into law.
Laken was a 22-year-old nursing student whose life was brutally cut short by an illegal alien in Athens.
Her murder shook our community, a place I’ve lived my entire life, and our state – and showed the nation the real-world consequences of failed immigration enforcement.
After four years of Joe Biden’s failure to enforce the law, President Trump has focused on securing the border and deporting those who’ve illegally come to our country.
At its core, the Laken Riley Act, sponsored by Congressman Mike Collins, reaffirmed that public safety requires cooperation.
When illegal aliens commit crimes, local, state, and federal authorities must work together.
Georgia was already headed in that direction.
In the wake of Laken’s murder, I led the effort in the Georgia House to pass House Bill 1105, legislation that mandates cooperation between local and state law enforcement and federal immigration authorities.
It includes criminal penalties for any local officials who fail to do so.
The goal was simple: ensure that criminals who are in this country illegally are identified, reported, and removed.
Because Georgia acted, we have not seen the same issues playing out in states like Minnesota, where local refusal to cooperate with federal authorities has resulted in tragic outcomes.
In Georgia, the law is clear: law enforcement must cooperate.
Period.
And the results are undeniable.
The differences in outcomes between Georgia and Minnesota are undeniable.
Last year, ICE arrests in Georgia increased by nearly 400 percent, and our state recorded the fourth-highest number of ICE arrests in the entire country.
That didn’t happen by accident—it happened because Georgia chose enforcement over excuses.
Recent operations underscore the impact.
During Operation Angel’s Honor, named for Laken Riley, ICE arrested more than a thousand individuals nationwide, including violent offenders charged with crimes such as sexual assault, attempted murder, and crimes against children.
These were not hypothetical threats—they were real individuals removed from our communities before more lives could be destroyed.
None of this progress would be possible without the brave men and women of U.S.
Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
These agents show up every day, often under political and physical attack, to do a difficult and dangerous job.
They deserve our thanks—not scorn—for enforcing the law and protecting American families.
I am deeply grateful to President Trump for his leadership in securing the border, restoring accountability, and honoring Laken Riley’s memory with action instead of empty rhetoric.
One year after he signed the Laken Riley Act, the results are clear: enforcement works, cooperation saves lives, and leadership matters.
We must remain unwavering in our commitment to public safety, the rule of law, and the men and women who stand on the front lines every day to protect us.
