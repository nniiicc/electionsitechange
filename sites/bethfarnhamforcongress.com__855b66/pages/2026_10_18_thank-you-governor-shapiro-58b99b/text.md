Russia’s war of aggression against Ukraine: EU agrees largest-ever package of listings targeting almost 1600 individuals and entities in Russia’s military-industrial complex Other source that I can’t link to: Elon Musk points finger at Trump over Ukrainian satellite restrictions There is bipartisan concern that Ukraine’s defense against Russian attacks has been hampered by the Trump administration ratcheting down the speed of its SpaceX-developed satellite system.
Trump attacks Zelensky as he receives major pushback on Russian diesel deal October 12, 2026 / 08:35 By Julia Jester Oct.
14, 2026, 7:54 PM EDT Ukraine was blindsided by new limits placed on satellite connectivity inside the country that handicapped its ability to use military drones and defend against Russian attacks.
After bipartisan backlash on Wednesday, Elon Musk said it was the U.S., not his company SpaceX that provides the systems, that made the call.
The limits affected both the Starlink satellite connections, used for civilian and defensive activities, as well as Starshield, the version designed for government and military use for both defensive and offensive capabilities.
On Tuesday night, Ukraine discovered that neither system could connect to data from objects — like satellite-controlled defensive and offensive drones — traveling more than 100 miles per hour, a source familiar with the actions told MS NOW on the condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military operations.
Serhii Beskrestnov, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s defense technology adviser, confirmed overnight that the speed limit for use of Starshield services “resulted in the disruption of several missions.” “It is clear that this action is not coincidental and is related to the current political situation,” Beskrestnov wrote.
The Starshield connections are “critical for Ukraine’s defense,” Ukrainian Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov wrote Wednesday on X , saying the high-speed use was being restored.
Musk said the Starshield satellite connections the Ukraine military relies on are ultimately controlled by President Donald Trump.
“Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the US government,” Musk wrote on X .
“Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS.” The White House, Pentagon and SpaceX did not respond to MS NOW’s requests for comment.
SpaceX provided no prior warning to the Ukrainian government about the limitation, the source said.
Ukrainian forces learned about the Starshield speed restrictions from customer support after filing a complaint about operational issues.
SpaceX informed Ukrainian officials that the restriction applies to drones and aircraft flying at speeds of 100 mph or higher, but, the source said, military units also reported issues with performance at lower drone speeds.
“This makes the operation of UAVs, airplanes and helicopters at high speeds impossible, which will also impact efforts to counter Shahed drones within Ukraine,” the source said, adding the speed restriction affects all defense operations and logistics, giving Russians “a real leverage on the frontline” at a time when Ukraine has effectively stopped their advances.
Even Republicans had strong words for both Musk and the Trump administration.
“I’m confident this does not reflect a change in SpaceX policy because if so, intentionally degrading Ukraine’s ability to defend itself might be construed as aiding and abetting a mass murderer,” Sen.
Thom Tillis, R-S.C., wrote to Musk on X , asking him to clarify SpaceX’s position.
The timing of the Starshield changes coincides with Ukrainian officials saying the U.S. and allied partners have not been giving them the same amount of early warnings about incoming Russian ballistic missile attacks.
Rep.
Don Bacon, R-Neb., said Trump’s decision to reduce intelligence sharing with Ukraine and access to Starlink equated to “aiding the invader & the war criminal who bombs cities,” and threatens to reduce congressional support for the Trump administration.
“Their agenda is dead unless they change paths,” Bacon wrote on X .
Ukraine Caucus Co-Chairs Reps.
Marcy Kaptur, D-Ohio; Brian Fitzpatrick, R-Pa.; Mike Quigley, D-Ill.; and Joe Wilson, R-S.C., wrote in a bipartisan statement they were “alarmed” by reports of the U.S. withholding both intelligence and Starlink support, “putting innocent civilians and American citizens in Ukraine at the mercy of Russian attacks.” “U.S. allies pay for Ukraine’s access to Starlink,” the lawmakers wrote.
“Restrictions that put civilians in harm’s way are unconscionable and will not be accepted by Congress.” The source familiar with Ukrainian forces’ use of Starshield confirmed to MS NOW that the signal was improving as of Wednesday afternoon, but only slightly.
“The situation is better than before, but it is still not good enough, and the service has not returned to its previous level,” the source said.
Ukraine is seeking to restore its unlimited access regardless of speed, which was part of the current service agreement contracted through the U.S. government.
The agreement has been in place since November 2025 and is expected to remain in effect through May 2027.
As SpaceX reduced Starshield connectivity for Ukraine overnight, Russia targeted critical energy infrastructure in the Kyiv and Kharkiv regions in a deadly ballistic missile strike.
Earlier this week, Trump denied withholding intelligence essential for Ukraine’s defenses after multiple threats against Ukraine if it failed to halt attacks on Russian oil refineries — even as Russia has flouted the so-called energy ceasefire announced by the president.
Rep.
Jim Himes, the top Democrat on the House Intelligence Committee, said in a statement that the committee “has received no notification of any change in posture with respect to intelligence support to Ukraine.” Zelenskyy said in an interview Wednesday that he felt betrayed by Trump’s diesel deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin, announced last week, and that his immediate response was that Ukraine is “losing a strong partner” in America.
“We can’t be weak, otherwise we can’t survive,” he told Piers Morgan.
“That’s why it’s painful when President Trump chooses one side — not our side.” Zelenskyy criticized people around Trump for giving the president “wrong” advice.
“I think his advisers are wrong.
I don’t know who — who is pushing him not to the right side,” Zelenskyy said.
“This is not the right side of history.” Julia Jester Julia Jester is a politics reporter for MS NOW.
