In this episode of The Rich Giardino Show: Where the Constitution, Crime, and Politics Meet, Rich welcomes Allen Caruso, Republican candidate for New York State Assembly District 113, for a conversation about affordability, small business, district representation, and the experiences that shaped his decision to run.
Caruso is a Saratoga Springs small-business owner with experience in restaurants, hospitality, real estate, and real estate development.
Born in Plattsburgh and raised in Saratoga, he earned a business degree from SUNY Plattsburgh and describes a path from poverty and foster care to building businesses and creating jobs.
THE CONVERSATION EXPLORES: • AFFORDABILITY & UTILITY COSTS: Caruso says one restaurant’s utility bill rose from roughly $1,100 in 2018 to nearly $2,900 this past January, and explains why he sees affordability as the central issue in the race. • GAS TAX & UPSTATE COMMUTES: Rich and Allen discuss suspending or repealing New York’s gas tax and the burden fuel costs place on residents who must drive long distances to work, shop, and access healthcare. • SMALL BUSINESS & CREDIT-CARD FEES: Caruso proposes giving businesses a state credit for the credit-card processing fees they pay when collecting sales tax on New York’s behalf. • REDISTRICTING & REPRESENTATION: The conversation examines a district spanning parts of Saratoga, Warren, and Washington counties and Allen’s concerns about how legislative boundaries are drawn. • FROM FOSTER CARE TO BUSINESS OWNERSHIP: Caruso shares his childhood experience with poverty, instability, and foster care—and how that background shaped his outlook on opportunity and public service.
