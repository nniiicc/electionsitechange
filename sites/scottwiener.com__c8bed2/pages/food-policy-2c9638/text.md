Even families who aren't going hungry have felt grocery prices climb sharply over the past few years, squeezing household budgets across the city and country.
Scott has spent his time in Sacramento authoring law after law to strengthen the food safety net.
Examples of Scott’s legislative work include: SB 900, creating a rebate program for CalFresh recipients who buy fresh California produce, thus making fresh fruits and vegetables more affordable for low income people; SB 278 and SB 282, protecting CalFresh recipients from being penalized for government errors and expanding access to prepared meals for homeless, elderly, and disabled residents; and SB 285, streamlining CalFresh enrollment by shortening the application form and requiring the ability to sign up for CalFresh by phone.
Scott kept fighting on enrollment access even after setbacks: after his 2020 bill to simplify the CalFresh application for seniors and people with disabilities stalled in committee, he came back the following year and got the same reforms incorporated directly into the state budget.
As Chair of the Senate Budget Committee, he's also backed billions of dollars for California's universal school meals program and support for food banks, and as a member of the Senate Health and Judiciary Committees, he's supported protecting shoppers from surveillance pricing and making medically supportive food a Medicaid benefit.
Congressional Republicans have spent the past year cutting people off SNAP benefits and shrinking what's left for those still enrolled, stripping climate and conservation guardrails out of Farm Bill funding meant to help family farmers adapt, even as grocery consolidation lets a handful of chains distort prices and squeeze independent stores.
Scott believes Congress should be strengthening the food safety net, not gutting it, and restoring real competition to a grocery industry that increasingly serves shareholders over shoppers.
