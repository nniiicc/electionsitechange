The Contrast This race is not about who Supervisor Chan is.
It's about what she has blocked and failed to deliver for San Franciscans.
KQED’s senior political correspondent summed her up in December as “mostly known for opposing things.” (KQED: “ A San Francisco Political Showdown: Who Will Take Pelosi’s Seat? ” — in the embedded radio segment, at 1:50.) Supervisor Chan cast the third-highest share of No votes of the eleven San Francisco supervisors in 2025.
The “On her phone” clip (Media item 1) shows what that looks like in the chamber.
Senator Wiener has passed more than 100 laws.
Housing Supervisor Chan voted to kill 469 Stevenson Street — a 495-unit development beside a BART station, including 119 below-market-rate homes, to be built with union labor, on what was a parking lot.
(San Francisco Board of Supervisors, October 26, 2021.) (SF Standard: “ Supervisors under fire: Vote against proposed SoMa apartment building sparks furor, may violate state law ”) Supervisor Chan voted against Mayor Lurie’s Family Zoning Plan — the citywide rezoning for 36,000 more homes that the state required San Francisco to adopt — voting No at both readings, one of four No votes as the plan passed 7–4, after trying to exempt every residential neighborhood.
(Board of Supervisors, December 2 and December 9, 2025.) (SF Standard: “ Time to build: SF supervisors approve controversial ‘Family Zoning’ plan ”) Supervisor Chan authored the Board resolution opposing SB 79 — Senator Wiener’s law allowing more homes within a half mile of major transit stops, signed in October 2025 — unless it was rewritten to preserve local control.
Her resolution said the law “undercuts the years-long community process” and the Board rejected it 7–4.
(Board of Supervisors File 250727, July 22, 2025.) (SF Examiner: “ How California SB 79 may increase SF housing height limits ”) Supervisor Chan tried to get the city to sue the State of California to stop SB 423 — Senator Wiener’s law that fast-tracks housing permits in San Francisco.
In a December 26, 2023 letter with Supervisor Aaron Peskin, she asked the City Attorney for the city’s legal options, “including but not limited to litigation against the State,” calling the law an “unfair legislative mandate.” The City Attorney did not sue.
She was also one of only two votes against the city’s own December 2023 ordinance to comply with the law.
(SF Standard: “ San Francisco housing fight: 2 lawmakers urge suing state ”) Senator Wiener wrote and passed the laws that have built thousands of affordable homes in San Francisco — and tens of thousands more statewide — built under the laws Supervisor Chan tried to block.
In Congress, his plan builds 8 million homes.
(SF Standard: “ Scott Wiener Doubles Down on Bill That Fast-Tracks New Housing ”; “ Sen.
Scott Wiener wants to give cities $10K for each new home they build ”) Transit Supervisor Chan was the sole vote, 10–1, against SB 532 — Senator Wiener's bill to fund Bay Area public transit from bridge revenue — the bill that kept BART and Muni from severe service cuts.
(Board of Supervisors, July 25, 2023.) (SF Examiner: “ What’s driving Connie Chan?
Richmond legislator’s transit stances spark debate ”) Supervisor Chan opposed Measure RR, the 2020 Caltrain funding measure — the first dedicated funding source in Caltrain’s history, passed as the pandemic wiped out 95 percent of the railroad’s ridership — and ran for supervisor against it.
Thousands of San Franciscans ride Caltrain every day.
Voters approved it anyway: 70 percent across the three counties, 74 percent in San Francisco.
(KQED: “ Caltrain’s ‘Lifeline’ Sales Tax Appears to Be on Track for Approval ”) Parks, public space, and nightlife Supervisor Chan opposed the car-free JFK Promenade in Golden Gate Park and opposed Sunset Dunes — both passed — and was the only supervisor to oppose expanding concerts in Golden Gate Park and free concerts in Union Square, Civic Center, and along the Embarcadero.
She actively worked to sabotage Outside Lands and opposed allowing the Castro Theater to become a live music venue.
(KQED: “ SF Supervisors Vote to Keep Cars Off JFK Drive in Golden Gate Park ”; KRON4: “ Ticketed concerts approved in Golden Gate Park ”) Senator Wiener wrote the law creating entertainment zones and California’s new liquor license for live music, and supported both Car-Free JFK and Sunset Dunes.
Wiener strongly supports live music in San Francisco.
(SFist: “ SF Now Has 30 Boozy ‘Entertainment Zones,’ Which Are Boosting Local Businesses ”) Supervisor Chan supports Prop G on the November ballot to close Sunset Dunes Park and re-open it to cars.
Sunset Dunes is the 3rd most visited park in San Francisco.
Senator Wiener strongly opposes Prop G.
(Mission Local: "Sunset Dunes becomes proxy battle in San Francisco congressional race") Schools Supervisor Chan opposed the 2022 school board recall — recalled by roughly three-quarters of San Francisco voters — and stood by the recalled member who wrote racist posts attacking Asian Americans.
She opposed merit-based admission at Lowell and eighth-grade algebra.
(SF Standard: “ Recall fallout: Local PAC targets Mar, Chan over school board stances ”) Senator Wiener supported the school board recall, supports merit-based admission at Lowell, and has long supported eighth-grade algebra.
(SFGATE: “ SF Board of Education recall receives endorsement from Scott Wiener ”) Public safety At an August 2020 forum, Supervisor Chan said it was "definitely" time to " dismantle and restructure " the San Francisco Police Department.
In a 2020 candidate debate she was the only candidate to answer no when asked whether she would support hiring more police.
(SF Standard: “ Richmond voters have crime on their minds.
That’s a problem for Connie Chan ”) Supervisor Chan voted against ending open-air drug markets (Board file250197, 3/25/25).
Senator Wiener wrote the law making car break-ins easier to prosecute, wrote the law allowing police to deal with sale of stolen goods on our streets, and supports the tools to shut down open-air drug markets.
