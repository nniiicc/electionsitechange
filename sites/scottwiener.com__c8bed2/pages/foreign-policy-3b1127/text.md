China & Taiwan China is America’s most consequential geopolitical challenge and a critical partner on so many of the globe’s pressing issues.
The economic, cultural, and economic ties between California and China run deep, and we must foster and respect those ties.
We can have disagreements with China — and protect American interests — without unnecessarily escalating to the point of conflict.
Indeed, the U.S. and China have a solemn responsibility to collaborate on the major challenges confronting our planet, including climate change, AI risk, pandemics, economic stability, and avoiding catastrophic wars.
Navigating this relationship requires clear eyes and a coherent strategy.
Scott supports existing U.S. policy toward Taiwan and opposes any forceful attempt to change the status quo across the Taiwan Strait.
War with China would be catastrophic for the entire world, which means deterrence must be backed by serious diplomacy.
The U.S. should compete with China the right way: by leading.
That means investing in domestic semiconductor manufacturing, AI infrastructure, and clean energy so we’re not dependent on countries overseas for critical supply chains.

Foreign Aid & USAID When Trump gutted USAID, he handed a strategic gift to our adversaries and brought immediate suffering to millions around the globe.
Foreign aid prevents wars, disease, and famines.
It builds stable governments, keeps pandemics from becoming global crises, and creates the kind of goodwill that military budgets can’t buy.
Programs like PEPFAR have saved over 25 million lives and built real U.S. credibility across Africa and Asia.
For every dollar we spend on development assistance, we save many more in military spending down the road.
When America walks away from this work, China and Russia fill the vacuum.
The human cost has been staggering.
In a single year, global health aid from the federal government dropped 67%, and independent researchers estimate that 781,000 additional people died in 2025 alone as a result, the majority of them children.
What aid remains is now being conditioned on mining concessions and corporate favors rather than country need, a corrupt inversion of everything foreign assistance is supposed to stand for.
Meanwhile, the career public health professionals who made these programs work have been politically purged, replaced by consultants operating under the unwritten rule that no former USAID staff need apply.
America’s global leadership and the responsibility that comes with it starts with our values.
Scott Wiener will fight to fully restore USAID and rebuild America’s development capacity, and ensure that aid reaches the people who need it through institutions with the expertise to deliver it.
In Congress, Scott will work to: Fully restore USAID and reverse the Trump administration’s dismantling of U.S. foreign assistance.
Protect life-saving programs like PEPFAR and expand full-spectrum global health funding, including reproductive health, child nutrition, vaccine access, and health systems capacity.
Route aid through trusted multilateral institutions like GAVI, the Global Fund, WHO, UNICEF, and the World Bank, which have the expertise the gutted State Department no longer does.
Reinstate U.S. contributions to GAVI, which raised global vaccination rates from #% to #% and saved more than 17 million lives.
Rebuild the State Department’s diplomatic capacity and restore U.S. leadership in multilateral development institutions.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​ Supporting Human Rights Against Authoritarian Regimes Scott is deeply committed to global human rights.
Not selectively, not when it's convenient, and not only when the abuser is an official adversary.

Protecting PEPFAR & America's Life-Saving Investments Abroad Scott Wiener came of age as a gay teen during the height of the AIDS crisis, watching the epidemic tear through his community as the Reagan Administration turned the other way.
That experience has driven his life's work — authoring landmark California legislation to modernize HIV criminalization laws, expand access to PrEP, and destigmatize testing and treatment — and it grounds his belief that the fight against HIV/AIDS cannot stop at America's borders.
The U.S.
President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR) has been one of America's greatest foreign policy achievements precisely because it has embodied that conviction: over two decades, it has saved more than 25 million lives, supported treatment for 21 million people, and prevented millions of mother-to-child transmissions.
Scott will fight to preserve and strengthen PEPFAR as a cornerstone of American global leadership.
The Trump Administration has betrayed PEPFAR's mission, converting a humanitarian program into a vehicle for extracting corporate concessions, conditioning life-saving HIV funding on preferential pharmaceutical market access, data-sharing agreements of dubious legality, and exploitative mineral extraction deals wholly unrelated to public health.
Countries like Zambia, which had nearly 90% of verified HIV cases under treatment, now face total funding cutoffs for refusing to comply.
Compacts have been hidden from the public in violation of transparency laws, fiscal commitments have been imposed on debt-distressed nations without parliamentary consultation, and the community-based NGO networks that reach LGBTQ people and other marginalized populations have been dismantled.
Scott will push Congress to restore PEPFAR's integrity: mandating full public disclosure of all compacts, prohibiting conditionality that ties health funding to commercial or mineral agreements, and ensuring any transition to country-led health systems reflects actual capacity rather than an arbitrary political deadline.
America’s Global Alliances One of America’s greatest strategic foreign policy assets is our alliances.
The relationships, treaties, and institutions we’ve built since World War II give the U.S. a coalition of democracies that no adversary can match.
Trump spent his terms treating allies like adversaries, threatening to abandon security commitments, and pulling back from the institutions that make collective action possible to solve the world’s biggest challenges.
That damage is real, and it needs to be undone.
Scott will work to rebuild trust with democratic partners across the globe.
No country solves climate change alone.
No country counters authoritarian aggression alone.
In Congress, Scott will work to: Rebuild trust and collaboration with democratic allies.
Reaffirm U.S. commitment to collective defense and security agreements, including NATO.
Restore U.S. participation in and leadership of multilateral institutions, including the UN, WTO, WHO, and international agreements on climate and other key issues.
Work with partners to develop shared strategies on China, Russia, climate, and global health threats.
