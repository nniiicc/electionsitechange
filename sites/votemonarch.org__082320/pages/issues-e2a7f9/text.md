DATA CENTERS & AI I asked Shelbyville to pause new data centers until we have rules in place, and I'll bring that fight right into Frankfort.
Any data center built should require at least three public hearings (with proper public notice) and local approval, be free of NDAs between government officials and companies and they should pay for their own power and grid costs with an independent & sustainable energy source.
I'll fight to remove the tax giveaways, implement requirements for Kentucky based businesses to be used for construction and place restrictions on water usage while exploring the exclusive use of closed loop cooling systems.
I'll advocate for bond postings as a requirement so taxpayers aren't stuck with the cleanup when the technology evolves or Data centers shut down.
I'll push to make sure AI doesn't impact how people get elected and advocate for keeping AI completely out of our election systems and voter rolls.
I'll push for small business tax incentives for increasing online security, investments in our statewide cybersecurity infrastructure and push for education, training programs, and universal worker protections. ​ HOUSING & AFFORDABILITY Kentucky is short on homes, and prices locally have gone up over 43% in just 5 years.

PROTECTING OUR ENVIRONMENT I'll fight to restore what Kentucky lost with SB 89 and push for investment in replacing lead pipes and aging water lines, along with testing for "forever chemicals" (PFAS) in our drinking water.

AGRICULTURE & JOB CREATION We need to focus less on attracting established businesses and more on building them from scratch.
Kentucky is primed to lead industries that create good paying careers in industries that don't need AI to be successful.
With rising fuel costs and a diminishing oil supply there's a growing demand for biofuel processing in aviation and Kentucky Soybean & Corn growers could play a key role.
HB 869 was an OK start, but as someone who works in Aviation, I know what the industry really needs is processing and qualified labor.
Raw material alone can't support the engineers we need for engine overhauls.
With investments in STEM programs, and targeted university development programs, we have a window to be the central hub in a completely new supply chain.
Additionally, Twenty-five years ago, the Kentucky Innovation Act helped homegrown companies turn university research and development into Kentucky businesses.
Since then, surrounding states have made themselves more attractive by out investing us 2:1 in student created startup grants.
I'll fight to reignite our statewide grant program and with the University of Kentucky, the University of Louisville and Northern Kentucky's growing life sciences hub I'll work to make us a national leader in medical research.
Especially when it comes to the cancers that hit Kentucky families harder than anywhere else in the country.
