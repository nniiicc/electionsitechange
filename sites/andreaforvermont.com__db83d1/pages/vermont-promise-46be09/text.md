Provide Tax Relief for Vermonters Provide $250M in income tax relief in 2027, targeted at low and middle-income Vermonters, and Social Security and retired military income recipients.
Move the Education Property Tax Credit to the General Fund, reducing homestead property taxes by $134M and a rate decrease of $0.35/100, $1,400 on a $400,000 home.
Vermont's total annual spending has increased 62% since 2019, while inflation has increased 31%.
If Vermont had increased its budget according to inflation, we would now be spending $7.6B — almost $2B less than Vermont currently spends.
With a majority, we will pass a budget that provides $250M of income tax relief targeted at low and middle-income Vermonters, and Social Security recipients and military retirees in the 2027 session.
This is a necessary first step toward lowering Vermont's crushing tax burden and making Vermont more affordable.
Vermont currently has 900 unfilled positions that are budgeted for — #M; the Green Mountain Care Board now exceeds #M, and the Cannabis Control Board exceeds #M.
These are just a few examples of where savings can be found.
We will also pass a bill that moves the Education Property Tax Credit (income sensitivity) to the General Fund and reduces homestead property taxes by $134 million, while only costing the General Fund $75M.
The General Fund is contributing $105M to property tax relief in FY27.
In other words, we can save property taxpayers $134M and save the General Fund $30M, and continue to protect Vermonters who pay their education taxes based on household income.
Most tax relief requires a commensurate spending decision.
Not in this case; Vermont's Education Property Tax Credit system is the poster child for a Rube Goldberg machine.
The Property Tax Credit increases every homeowner's property tax rate by $0.35/100, $1,400 on a $400k home.
40% of homeowners who currently receive a credit would be better off if Vermont didn't provide a credit.
Those who do benefit will remain protected and continue to pay based on household income.
Affordable Healthcare and Insurance Give health insurers greater flexibility to offer more affordable, accessible coverage options, and increase the number of health insurers in Vermont.
Reduce the burden of rising health insurance premiums so employers can attract workers, businesses can grow, and families can access the care they need.
Every Vermonter deserves access to high-quality, affordable healthcare close to home.
Yet rising insurance premiums, deductibles, and out-of-pocket costs have become one of the greatest affordability challenges facing Vermonters.
Families, employers, and small businesses are paying more, while rural communities face additional challenges from provider shortages and limited access to care.
Recently, two birthing centers were forced to close.
In 2010, President Obama signed the Affordable Care Act (ACA) with the goal of making health insurance more affordable and accessible while protecting patients.
Since then, 48 states across the country have found ways to make the ACA work for their residents.
Vermont, unfortunately, chose a different path.
In 2011, Vermont Democrats passed Act 48, creating the Green Mountain Care Board and setting the state on a path toward a government-directed, universal healthcare system.
Thankfully, Governor Peter Shumlin abandoned the plan in 2014 when its enormous costs became clear.
But the policies adopted under Act 48 had already taken a toll.
Competition diminished, regulatory burdens increased, and insurers became less willing to serve Vermont's small and challenging market.
Vermont now suffers from the highest healthcare and health insurance costs in the country.
We need a different approach.
We will pursue policies that increase competition, expand consumer choice, and lower the cost of health insurance without sacrificing quality or the patient protections provided by the ACA.
Affordable healthcare is also essential to Vermont's economic future.
Businesses consistently identify healthcare costs as a major barrier to growth and workforce recruitment.
Lower insurance costs will help attract new residents, retain young workers, support small businesses, and make it easier for Vermont employers to compete.
Healthcare decisions should be made as close to patients as possible.
We will work to reduce unnecessary administrative burdens, strengthen healthcare workforce recruitment and retention, and improve the long-term financial stability of community hospitals, rural health centers, and independent medical practices.
Make Housing More Affordable Drive construction costs down by fully implementing 802 Homes and the Community and Housing Infrastructure Program (CHIP), remove bureaucratic barriers to housing, and allow more homes per parcel.
Reduce the cost to renovate existing housing stock and increase Vermont's housing supply.
40 years of overregulation have badly hurt Vermont's housing industry and the number and quality of available homes that Vermonters can afford.
In a nutshell, in much of Vermont, the cost of building is greater than the value of the home.
Only Vermonters with large amounts of cash can build in this economic environment.
If we can turn this relationship around, then our housing problem will be solved.
We will increase Vermont's housing supply and lower the cost of homeownership by fully implementing the 802 Homes initiative and the Community and Housing Infrastructure Program (CHIP).
We will modernize laws that govern local zoning and permitting to allow more home construction.
Reducing unnecessary regulatory barriers, attracting private investment, making it easier for builders to meet market demand, and increasing the supply of housing is the most effective long-term strategy to improve affordability.
At the same time, we will incentivize improvement and preservation of the housing we already have.
We will continue Vermont's Housing Improvement Program (VHIP) to bring vacant and underutilized homes back onto the market, and expand the Mobile Home Infill Program (MHIP) to strengthen one of Vermont's most affordable housing options.
By combining new construction with the rehabilitation of existing homes, we will create more housing opportunities, reduce costs, revitalize neighborhoods, and help communities across rural and urban Vermont grow in a responsible and sustainable way.
Revitalize Rural Vermont Restore common sense to land-use decisions by empowering landowners and licensed professionals, reducing duplication, improving predictability, and lowering permitting costs and delays.
Help farmers, food processors, and the forest economy diversify, modernize, and expand.
Vermont's farms, forests, and working lands are the foundation of Vermont's rural communities, economy, and heritage.
Farmers, foresters, and landowners have long been responsible stewards of our natural resources while producing the food, fiber, forest products, and renewable materials that sustain Vermont.
Yet these industries face growing financial pressures, workforce shortages, rising regulatory costs, and burdensome permitting.
Government should be a partner in their success, not an obstacle.
We will strengthen Vermont's agricultural, food processing, and forest economy by reducing unnecessary regulations, restarting Vermont's machinery and investment tax credit, encouraging innovation, and expanding economic opportunity.
We will support farms, food processors, and forest products businesses as they modernize operations, adopt new technologies, diversify, and reach new markets.
Vermont should be a premier destination for agritourism.
Our proximity to population centers makes this goal achievable, and it will serve as a catalyst for increased visits to Vermont that will improve our economy, brand, and ability to market our agricultural and craft products more broadly.
We will recognize land enrolled in Vermont's Current Use Program as contributing toward the state's Act 59 conservation goals, acknowledging the program's vital role in protecting working lands and preventing unnecessary development.
We will also modernize Vermont's permitting process by restoring greater decision-making authority to landowners and qualified licensed professionals.
Finally, decisions that impact rural Vermont will be made using an open and transparent process with rural Vermont at the table.
Vermonters who work the land know their land the best.
We will listen to them.
Fully Fund Roads and Bridges Fully fund the Transportation Fund and increase annual state highway paving from 56 miles in FY27 to a goal of 300 miles.
With 2,700 miles of state highways, Vermont must invest in maintaining the infrastructure that connects our communities and economy.
It costs less to maintain a system of roads and bridges that are kept in good/very good condition than it is to maintain the same system in poor/very poor condition.
We will fully fund the Transportation Fund and make the long-term fiscal decisions necessary to improve the condition of Vermont's roads and bridges.
Vermont has approximately 2,700 miles of state highways that require regular maintenance, resurfacing, and reconstruction.
Yet under current funding levels, the state plans to pave only 56 miles of state highway in FY27 — an inadequate pace that allows roads to continue deteriorating faster than they can be repaired.
We will establish an annual target of 300 miles of state highway paving.
Vermont's roads and bridges are a foundation of our economy, connecting families to jobs, businesses to customers, farmers to markets, and communities to essential services.
For too long, Vermont has shortchanged the maintenance of our transportation infrastructure, allowing roads to deteriorate and increasing automotive repair costs for taxpayers.
A reliable transportation system is especially critical for rural Vermont, where residents often travel longer distances for work, healthcare, education, and everyday needs.
Strong transportation infrastructure is essential for economic growth.
Businesses consider road quality, accessibility, and reliability when deciding where to locate and expand.
Eliminate Costly Energy Mandates and Reduce Carbon Emissions Support voluntary adoption of cleaner heating and transportation technologies through efficiency programs and electrification incentives — not costly mandates.
Keep electricity affordable to reduce energy costs, encourage electrification, and lower emissions.
Young Vermonters want a future where they can afford to live, work, and build a life while protecting the environment.
We believe Vermont can reduce carbon emissions without driving up the cost of housing, transportation, heating, and electricity through expensive government mandates.
The best climate policy is one people can afford.
When clean technologies like heat pumps, electric vehicles, weatherization, and renewable electricity are practical and affordable, Vermonters choose them.
Government should encourage innovation, expand consumer choice, and help lower costs — not rely on costly mandates, penalties, and lawsuits.
For too long, Vermont has pursued a strategic policy of reducing carbon emissions by imposing unnecessary mandates.
We will repeal the Global Warming Solutions Act, the Clean Heat Standard, and the 2024 Renewable Energy Standard because we believe these policies increase costs, create uncertainty, and make it more difficult for Vermonters to afford everyday life.
Instead, we will pursue practical energy policies focused on affordability, reliability, innovation, and measurable environmental results.
We will continue to support energy efficiency, weatherization, advanced heating technologies, the transition to electric and hybrid vehicles, and modern electric infrastructure while encouraging private-sector innovation that makes clean energy more accessible to all Vermonters.
Vermont can protect its natural environment and build a stronger economy at the same time.
By making clean energy affordable, instead of mandatory, Vermont can reduce emissions, lower costs, create opportunity, and ensure our state remains a place where the next generation can afford to live, work, and thrive.
Make Communities Safe and Increase Accountability Advance a constitutional amendment in 2027 to reform Vermont's bail system and keep repeat and violent offenders from endangering communities while awaiting trial.
Repeal Vermont's Raise the Age laws.
Keeping Vermonters safe is government's most fundamental responsibility.
Every family, business, and community deserves to feel secure in their homes, schools, workplaces, and downtowns.
Our criminal justice system must protect the rights of the accused while also ensuring the safety of victims and the public.
We will advance a constitutional amendment in 2027 to reform Vermont's bail system so judges have greater authority to detain individuals awaiting trial who pose a significant threat to public safety or are serial repeat offenders.
Vermont's current constitutional limitations almost always require the release of repeat and violent offenders who present an ongoing danger to their communities.
Our proposal will give judges the flexibility to consider an individual's risk to public safety when making pretrial release decisions, helping prevent additional violent crimes while protecting the constitutional rights of those accused.
We will also repeal Vermont's Raise the Age laws.
While young people should have opportunities for rehabilitation, serious criminal offenses require meaningful accountability and consequences.
Public safety must remain the first priority, particularly for violent crimes that threaten victims and communities.
Our goal is a justice system that is fair, accountable, and focused on protecting Vermont communities.
By strengthening bail laws, holding violent offenders accountable, and giving law enforcement and the courts the tools they need, we will improve public safety while restoring confidence in Vermont's criminal justice system.
