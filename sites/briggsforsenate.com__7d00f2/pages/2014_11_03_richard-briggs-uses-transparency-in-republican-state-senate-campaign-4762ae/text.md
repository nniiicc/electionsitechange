Home About Issues Endorsements News Volunteer Donate Richard Briggs uses transparency in republican state Senate campaign November 3, 2014 Briggs never planned on getting involved in politics.
It was as a practicing heart surgeon in Knoxville in 2007, frustrated by what he described as dishonest and corrupt government, that he first decided to step up.
He was encouraged by fellow members of Leadership Knoxville, a servant-leadership program, who believed it was time for community members with no political ambitions to take a stand.
That spirit of reluctant but principled service is precisely what makes Dr.
Briggs so compelling.
He went on to represent District 5 in Knox County, was elected in 2008, and re-elected for a six-year term in 2010.
He built a record of steady, trustworthy leadership before ever setting his sights on the state Senate.
What sets Dr.
Briggs apart is the freedom that comes from a life well-built outside of politics.
As he put it himself: “I’m not a career politician.
I don’t need the job… When you’re not obligated to anyone, you have this tremendous freedom to do what you think is right.” That independence has been the hallmark of his career ever since.
His professional background is remarkable, a successful heart surgeon, a U.S.
Army colonel who commanded combat hospitals in Afghanistan and Baghdad, and a combat surgeon during Desert Storm.
He brought all of that real-world experience to his vision for Tennessee, centered on the belief that the most important thing in a person’s life is a well-paying job with good benefits, so they and their family can enjoy a solid, middle-class American life.
Perhaps most telling of all is how Dr.
Briggs campaigned: he personally knocked on the doors of 50,000 Knoxville residents to get to know the people he would represent and understand their individual concerns.
That kind of shoe-leather dedication speaks to a man who doesn’t just talk about serving his community, but he shows up for it, one door at a time.
