Home About Issues Endorsements News Volunteer Donate Richard Briggs wants to change book-ban law June 10, 2026 Senator Richard Briggs is once again demonstrating the principled, independent leadership that defines his career, but this time taking on Tennessee’s library book review law to ensure that historically significant and award-winning works remain accessible to students.
After the Pulitzer Prize-winning book Roots was pulled from Knox County school library shelves, Dr.
Briggs spoke out, saying he was “appalled” by the removal.
Drawing on his deep roots in the Knoxville community and his own military experience, including a tour in Afghanistan, he noted that the removal of The Kite Runner , set in a country where he served, also troubled him personally.
Rather than simply criticizing the law, Dr.
Briggs is doing something about it.
He has said he is willing to sponsor legislation to reform the appeals process, arguing that final decisions on book removal should rest with elected school board members who are accountable to the public, not with non-elected committees operating without public transparency.
His approach is measured and thoughtful, he is not opposed to initial reviews of books, but believes the current process lacks proper checks and democratic accountability.
In a rare show of bipartisan cooperation, Democratic Rep.
Sam McKenzie of Knoxville has said he wants to join Dr.
Briggs in sponsoring legislation to reform the law.
Together, they are working to ensure that books are evaluated in their full historical and literary context, with a fair and transparent process for communities and families.
This effort reflects Dr.
Briggs at his best, a principled lawmaker willing to cross party lines, stand up for his constituents, and bring common sense reform to policies that have gone too far.
