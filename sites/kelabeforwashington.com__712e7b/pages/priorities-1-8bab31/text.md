Priorities we will fight for:
- As a first-generation college student, I understand firsthand that access to quality education can change the entire trajectory of a life.
We need to fully fund our transition to kindergarten programs, early literacy supports, and behavioral health resources like social workers in schools to meet the needs of our students.
- Every child deserves a strong start.
That means fully funding transition to kindergarten programs and early literacy supports, because reading resources shouldn't be a privilege reserved for families who can afford the support.
And I will work to establish the youth development fund account to increase access to positive youth development programs.
- I will work to bring back funding for the education legacy trust fund, which includes resources for K-12 education, childcare, class size reductions, and support for teachers.
Ensuring better outcomes for our kids.
- We need to provide pathways to employment for our youth.
That means funding pathways to 2 year and 4 year degrees, as well as workforce training.
- My mother went bankrupt because of the mountain of medical debt left behind when my grandmother passed away after a long battle with lymphoma.
Families shouldn’t be punished for seeking medical care.
- I will work to create limits on interest incurred on medical debt.
We shouldn’t punish families that are seeking care.
- Healthcare is a human right.
Washington state should take steps to establish Universal Healthcare.
I will be a champion in Olympia for healthcare for all.
- I will support the establishment of the Washington Health Trust funded by progressive revenue.
The Washington Health Trust could amount to saving the state an estimated $5-13 billion annually in total healthcare spending by eliminating administrative waste, fraud, and cost-inflation incentivized within the for-profit insurance industry.
- As a kid, I moved around all over Seattle because no matter how hard my mother worked as a CNA, a single mother’s wages could not meet the high costs of rent, food, and healthcare.
And as an adult, the rising cost of living and the housing landscape the dream of owning a home has become out of reach.
We need to prioritize pathways to homeownership for those historically left out.
- I will fight for legislation that protects renters.
We need a ban on junk fees and regulations on Ratio Utility Billing Systems (RUBS), which inflate basic utilities on many renters.
These protections would ensure people do not end up overpaying for the basics while living in one of the most expensive cities in the country.
- The racist history of redlining in Washington state has made it difficult for marginalized communities to own their own homes.
We must prioritize pathways towards homeownership for those historically left out of the process.
Through partnerships from the state, counties, cities, and local community land trusts.
- I will be an advocate for investing in social housing developers all across the state.
The social housing model is a self-sustaining and publicly-funded affordable housing development that allows families to stay in place.
- I have friends that were recruited to join gangs in middle school, and if they didn’t have programs like Rainier Scholars, they would have gone down that path.
Keeping young people engaged in learning isn't just an education issue, it's a public safety solution.
- Invest in a multi-pronged approach to violence prevention.
Providing mental health services to youth, case management that keeps kids in school, and provides training for youth to develop and build job skills.
- Fund activations in our communities along targeted blocks and corridors.
This means infrastructure investments like lighting and safe sidewalks, as well as legislation that incentivizes small businesses to open in empty store fronts.
This not only makes our neighborhoods feel safer, but community bonds are built between community members.
- Expand investments in non-violent services like the CAREs team across the state.
We need to create legislation that allows them to do their jobs in a safe and effective manner.
