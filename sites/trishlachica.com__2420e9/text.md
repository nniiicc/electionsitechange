Trish La Chica is a trusted leader who prioritizes her constituents, ensures all voices are heard, and works tirelessly so that our community remains the best place to live and raise our families.
Trish La Chica is the State Representative for District 37, proudly serving Mililani Town, Mililani Mauka, Waipio Gentry, and Koa Ridge.
As representative, Trish has advocated for better public transportation options, successfully pushed for the regulation of e-cigarettes to protect our youth, and brought funding to improve schools and address homelessness.
Prior to joining the legislature, Trish spent over 15 years in leadership roles working for non-profit organizations, as well as state and county government in areas such as health, Medicaid, housing, and homelessness.
Her passion for advocacy began as an investigative journalist in Manila, shining light on the daily struggles of Filipino families.

Trish is in the trenches at the Capitol, advocating for public policies we need and creating solutions for our community, working families, and keiki.
When DOE canceled school bus routes for two consecutive school years, Trish organized community meetings, rode TheBus to document the impact on students, and worked with Governor Green to restore emergency bus service both times.
Trish brought HPD Chief Joe Logan and other officials directly to residents to address local crime and safety concerns, helped launch new neighborhood security watches, and organized a wildfire safety workshop after the Mililani Mauka wildfire.
Trish helped pass SB975, now Act 62, creating comprehensive e-cigarette regulations so vape products are no longer left as the only unregulated tobacco product in Hawaii and keiki have stronger protections from youth nicotine use.
After recurring outages disrupted the community, Trish organized a public meeting with HECO so residents could get direct answers, understand planned grid changes, and prepare for what was coming next.
Trish partnered with local organizations to clean up a large homeless encampment in Kipapa Gulch, removing more than 38,000 pounds of debris, and secured $5 million in state funding to continue similar cleanup efforts statewide.

2025-05-30
HB 862: Addressing School Bus Shortages - Multiple school bus routes were suspended this past academic year due to a nationwide bus driver shortage.
This bill reverses the suspensions and allows for alternative vehicles, such as small buses, vans and more, to pick up students and bring them to school.
“Hawaiʻi continues to face a school bus crisis, and we’ve heard from countless parents, families and educators about the urgent need to expand transportation options, while keeping safety front and center,” said House Committee on Education Vice Chair Trish La Chica.
“This new law paves the way for our students to thrive, by expanding the department’s options to secure reliable transportation and ensuring that transportation barriers don’t stand in the way of our keiki and their opportunities to succeed.”
