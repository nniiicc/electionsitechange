2025–2026 Legislative Session
I continue to work hard for my fellow North Carolinians during this 2025–2026 session of the General Assembly.
I am guided by the values and priorities of the people I represent—those being fairness, opportunity, accountability, and respect for all.
This session, much of my work has focused on defending our civil liberties and strengthening protections for communities that have too often been marginalized or excluded.
I cannot condone warrantless searches and confinements of our immigrant neighbors and I will not stand idly by while the LGBTQ community is vilified by our leaders in Raleigh.
Our constitution DEMANDS equal protection under the law for all people and that is a fight I will never shy away from.
I have also continued my long-standing work on environmental and public health issues, particularly those that impact us here in Wilmington as well as all the other ¨downstream communities.¨ I continue to put forward legislation to address plastic pollution and PFAS “forever chemicals,” to restore wetlands protections, and to hold polluters accountable.
Protecting our water, our air, and our natural resources is essential—not only for our health, but for the economic future of our state.
Similarly, protecting families and individuals remains central to my legislative work.
Drawing on my background as an attorney, I have worked with my colleagues to put forward bills that would modernize domestic violence protections, prevent economic abuse, and strengthen legal tools for survivors.
I believe our laws should serve as a safeguard for people in moments of vulnerability and crisis.
Public education continues to be a priority for me.
The current leadership in Raleigh has virtually bankrupted public education placing the financial burden on locals rather than funding our constitutional mandate to provide a sound and basic education for every child.
The net result of this is skyrocketing property taxes locally to bridge the gap.
I support healthy and high-performing schools and I do not believe that the voucher program is serving all students.
It is funding private education for the children of more wealthy people.
If you want your child to go to a private school, that´s fine.
But,.
Is should not be paid for by the taxpayers.
A strong public education system has historically been a source of pride for us here in NC.
Regrettably, that is no longer the case with teacher pay and per pupil spending at the bottom of the barrel.
I have also supported legislation that honors service and strengthens communities, including measures benefiting veterans, first responders, and working families.
Throughout this session, my approach has remained consistent: practical problem-solving rooted in fairness, accountability, and opportunity.
I am proud of the work I am doing on behalf of House District 18 and all North Carolinians.
I remain committed to showing up, speaking out, and fighting for policies that move our state forward—together.
Deb.
