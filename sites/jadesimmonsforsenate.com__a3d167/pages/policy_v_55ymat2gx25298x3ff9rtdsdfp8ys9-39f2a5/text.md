It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by the Thurgood Marshall College Fund, Thurgood Marshall College, the Marshall family or estate, or any other institution bearing Justice Marshall's name.
Open Every Door.
Let Talent Rise.
America cannot change its history.
But we can change what that history costs people today.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan is a proposed generational investment in opportunity, capacity, and economic mobility that is designed to remove measurable barriers that continue to prevent talented Americans from reaching their full potential and accessing everything that should be available to them.
This is not a plan to decide what Black Americans should become.
It is a plan to make sure that talent, ambition, hard work, and determination have a fair opportunity to become whatever it wills.
A doctor, scientist, engineer, teacher, entrepreneur, skilled professional, homeowner, business owner, researcher, or leader.
Open every door.
Let talent rise.
WHY A THURGOOD MARSHALL PLAN?
Thurgood Marshall understood that equality under the law is foundational, but that the promise of equality means little if people remain blocked from exercising the opportunities that equality is supposed to protect.
The original Marshall Plan represented another powerful American idea: when circumstances have damaged the foundations of opportunity, the nation can make a large-scale investment in rebuilding capacity, institutions, infrastructure, and economic strength.
America did it for Europe to great effect and now it is time for it to make an even more consequential investment on its own soil and for its own people.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan brings those ideas together for a new generation.
This is not about DEI initiatives or even about leveling the playing field.
Instead, it is about America living up to its original promise of equality by doing some retroactive work.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan is here to remove barriers, build capacity, expand opportunity, and measure the results of those efforts.
This is a new Marshall Plan in spirit and scale named in the spirit of a man who stood for excellence, equality, and access to opportunity.
THE PRINCIPLE Government should not pick the destination, but it should make sure the road is open.
We should not lower the ceiling, but we should remove the obstacles between talent and the ceiling.
The objective is never to create permanent dependence on government as that has created more harm than good and has crushed more will than it has raised hope.
This plan aims to create the conditions in which people, families, businesses, institutions, and communities can build lasting capacity and independence.
THE PLAN 1.
GIVE EVERY CHILD ACCESS TO AN EXCELLENT EDUCATION Opportunity begins long before college.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan would pursue a stronger educational pipeline from early childhood through adulthood.
That means expanding access to: High-quality early childhood education Affordable childcare Literacy and mathematics Advanced coursework AP, IB, and dual-credit opportunities Gifted and talented programs Tutoring and academic intervention Arts and humanities Career and technical education College counseling Summer enrichment Mentorship and career exposure A child's zip code should not determine how high that child is allowed to aim.
2.
OPEN EVERY DOOR TO HIGHER EDUCATION The path to opportunity is not one-size-fits-all.
The plan would strengthen pathways into: Four-year universities HBCUs Community and technical colleges Medical, dental, veterinary, and pharmacy schools Law and business schools Engineering and technology programs Graduate and doctoral education Professional certifications Research fellowships Internships and apprenticeships International educational opportunities And it would make further, more strategic national investments in HBCU excellence.
That means modern research facilities, laboratories, technology, faculty development, graduate programs, broadband, equipment, and partnerships with industry, national laboratories, healthcare institutions, and universities.
HBCUs are not simply institutions to preserve.
They are institutions to build up and to expand.
Current HBCU policy proposals are already moving in this direction.
The bipartisan IGNITE HBCU Excellence Act introduced in 2026, for example, proposes investments in facilities, research infrastructure, broadband, equipment, and institutional capacity.
The Thurgood Marshall College Fund has identified institutional capacity, student success, workforce pipelines, and research as major priorities.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan would build on that foundation with a broader national strategy.
3.
BUILD EVERY PATH TO PROSPERITY There should not be one definition of success.
There should be many pathways upward.
The professional pathway Expand access to: Medicine Law Engineering Science Technology Finance Education Research Corporate leadership Executive development Graduate and doctoral education The entrepreneurial pathway Expand access to: Startup capital Growth capital Venture investment Accounting and legal support Government contracting Commercial real estate Acquisition financing Export opportunities Patents and commercialization Business succession planning The skilled and technical pathway Expand access to careers in: Advanced manufacturing Aerospace Energy Semiconductors Construction Robotics Information technology Transportation Healthcare technology Skilled trades Every pathway should contain a pathway upward.
A person should be able to begin with an entry-level opportunity and see a credible route toward greater skill, greater responsibility, greater income, and ultimately ownership.
4.
MAKE HOMEOWNERSHIP A GATEWAY TO WEALTH Homeownership can be one of the most important pathways from income to long-term wealth.
The plan would explore stronger federal support for: Down payments Closing costs Credit building Responsible mortgage access Financial education Appraisal and inspection assistance Home rehabilitation Protection against predatory lending Preservation of inherited property First-generation homeownership But ownership should not stop at the family home.
The plan would also seek to expand opportunities for Black Americans to become: Builders Developers Commercial property owners Landlords Investors Real-estate entrepreneurs The goal is not simply to help people buy an asset.
The goal is to help families build real wealth.
5.
HELP BLACK BUSINESSES SCALE Starting a business is not the same as building a company.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan would focus not only on entrepreneurship, but on growth.
A business that scales creates jobs, wealth, tax revenue, suppliers, innovation, and opportunity for others.
That means improving access to: Commercial lending Growth capital Venture capital Government contracts Accounting and legal services Technology Export markets Commercial real estate Executive and Business mentorship Acquisition financing Succession planning 6.
TREAT CHILDCARE AS ECONOMIC INFRASTRUCTURE Childcare is not merely a private family expense and it is not a luxury.
It is part of the infrastructure that allows parents to work, businesses to retain employees, and children to enter school ready to learn.
The plan would explore policies that increase: Childcare availability Affordability Provider capacity Early childhood quality Workforce participation Employer partnerships Access for working families If a parent cannot work because childcare does not exist or is unaffordable, that is an economic barrier and we should treat it accordingly.
7.
OPEN THE PROFESSIONAL NETWORKS THAT OPPORTUNITY REQUIRES Talent is not enough if opportunity is invisible.
The plan would strengthen connections among students and professionals and: Hospitals Law firms Engineering firms Universities Research laboratories Technology companies Financial institutions Energy companies Aerospace companies Major corporations Government agencies Entrepreneurs and investors The objective is not to create race-based jobs.
It is to make sure that access to opportunity is not pre-determined by family wealth, geography, or historical exclusion.
Talent should be able to meet opportunity wherever it lies.
8.
INVEST IN BLACK SCIENTIFIC, MEDICAL, AND INTELLECTUAL LEADERSHIP America's future competitiveness depends on the people who discover what comes next.
Black people have always been a part of the greatest discoveries in our nation and this new Marshall plan aims to both preserve that space at the forefront and create more of it.
This is not only about access now, but about making sure that breakthroughs account for the differentiated needs of African-Americans, especially in scientific discovery and healthcare.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan would strengthen pathways into: Biomedical research Medicine Engineering Artificial intelligence Biotechnology Energy Aerospace Computer science Materials science Environmental research The objective is not simply to produce more degrees.
It is to produce more discoveries, cures, companies, patents, technologies, and American leadership.
9.
REBUILD COMMUNITIES WHERE DISINVESTMENT HAS COMPOUNDED Opportunity requires a place in which it can actually be inspired and take root.
Too often, in American cities, the line of demarcation between the ‘haves’ and the ‘have nots’ is painfully obvious to the eye, the disparity too great to ignore.
The plan would support strategies addressing: Safe streets Modern schools Parks and recreation Broadband Transportation Grocery and essential services Housing rehabilitation Business corridors Environmental cleanup Healthcare access Libraries Community spaces 10.
MAKE BLACK MATERNAL HEALTH A NATIONAL PRIORITY A mother-to-be should not face a greater risk of dying simply because of her race, where she lives, or the ingrained discrimination that has lead to the under treatment of black women in emergent situations.
The plan would expand research and accountability around: Maternal mortality Severe maternal morbidity Prenatal care Postpartum care Rural healthcare access Emergency response Hospital outcomes Preventable deaths Maternal health disparities And it would specifically examine the health experiences of women veterans who have been disproportionately at risk.
11.
CONNECT MILITARY SERVICE TO THE OPPORTUNITY PIPELINE For many young Americans, military service is one of the first major institutions they encounter after high school.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan would strengthen the transition from military service into: Higher education Professional careers Entrepreneurship Homeownership Healthcare Research Skilled trades Leadership Service should open doors.
It should never become an invisible barrier to the next chapter of a person's life.
12.
PUBLIC SAFETY AND EQUAL JUSTICE Opportunity cannot flourish where people are afraid to walk down their own street and those streets also don’t feel safe when law enforcement is feared instead of relied upon.
This divide must be addressed, not dismissed or inflamed.
The plan supports effective public safety alongside accountability.
That means: Professional policing Better training Investigative technology Violent-crime task forces Independent investigation of serious misconduct Transparent use-of-force reporting Improved crime-clearance rates Witness and victim protection Violence-intervention strategies Justice reform should make the system more accurate, more equitable, and more proportionate, not merely more lenient.
This should also consider encouragement for pathways into law enforcement to increase representation and trust.
That includes examining: Appropriate sentencing for nonviolent offenses Diversion Indigent defense Prosecutorial misconduct Police misconduct Evidence and forensic standards Reentry Employment after incarceration Expungement and record sealing Correctional education Recidivism reduction Public safety and civil rights are not opposing goals.
A just system must be capable of delivering both.
13.
ENFORCE EQUAL RIGHTS WITHOUT CREATING A NEW SYSTEM OF RACIAL PREFERENCE The Thurgood Marshall Plan is grounded in equal treatment under law.
That means equal access, equal accountability, and equal opportunity.
Maybe most importantly, this means vigorous enforcement when discrimination is proven because discrimination is costly.
Federal civil-rights protections should be enforced in areas including: Employment Housing Lending Education Public accommodations Contracting Licensing 14.
PROTECT BLACK AMERICANS FROM HATE CRIMES AND TARGETED VIOLENCE America has a long history of racial violence and the current climate has seen an increase in racial tension elevated by an overabundance of hate-filled rhetoric.
The federal government should ensure that hate crimes and racially motivated violence are: Properly reported Properly investigated Properly prosecuted Properly documented Victims should receive support.
Law enforcement should have the training and resources necessary to recognize and investigate credible evidence of bias-motivated violence.
And when circumstances surrounding a death or violent act raise legitimate questions because of America's history of racial violence, the answer should be neither rumor nor automatic dismissal.
The answer should be truth.
We should reject complacency without investigation.
THE TEXAS OPPORTUNITY DIVIDEND Texas should not measure this plan simply by how much government spends.
Instead, we should measure what Texas gains.
The Thurgood Marshall Plan proposes a Texas Opportunity Dividend, a public scorecard measuring whether investments are producing measurable results.

Every major initiative should have: A measurable objective and a defined implementation plan.
A responsible agency or institution should be assigned with an approved timeline.
There must be public reporting and independent evaluation where appropriate.
The plan must operate on a clear standard for expansion, modification, or termination.
The goal is not to preserve a program, it is to remove the barrier.
HOW THE THURGOOD MARSHALL PLAN WOULD WORK The Thurgood Marshall Plan is not a single government program.
It is a framework of federal investments and policy reforms designed to systematically remove specific barriers to opportunity.
Different barriers require different solutions.
Some investments would flow through existing federal programs.
Others would require new legislation.
Some would take the form of competitive grants, institutional investments, tax incentives, research funding, loan guarantees, public-private partnerships, or direct assistance to eligible families and businesses.
The federal government should not simply spend money.
It should invest it through mechanisms designed to produce measurable outcomes.
Every major initiative should answer five questions: Who is eligible?
Who administers it?
Where does the money come from?
How is success measured?
When does the intervention change, expand, or end?
We are not promising that every outcome will be equal.
But, we should fight to make opportunity real.
And when opportunity becomes real, people will do what Americans have always done when given a genuine chance: They will build, create, innovate, lead…and they will rise.
OPEN EVERY DOOR.
LET TALENT RISE.
