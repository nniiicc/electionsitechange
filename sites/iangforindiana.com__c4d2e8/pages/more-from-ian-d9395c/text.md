A Man is not a mountain: Eugene’s allies Eugene Debs wasn’t standing alone.
Beside him was a coalition of brilliant organizers, radicals, and reformers who refused to let corporate power dictate the terms of human life.
When we talk about Debs Day, we are honoring the entire front line: Mary Harris “Mother” Jones: also known as “the most dangerous woman in America,” Mother Jones was a tireless organizer for striking miners and industrial workers.
She walked right into dangerous strike zones, organized miners’ wives, and famously led the 1903 “March of the Mill Children” to fight child labor.
Co-founding the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW) alongside Debs in 1905, her mandate was simple: “Pray for the dead and fight like hell for the living.” Lucy Parsons: Having been born into slavery, Parsons grew into one of the most powerful orators and radical organizers in American history.
She co-founded the IWW, edited labor publications, and spent decades advocating for the rights of the industrial workers, women, and Black Americans who built this nation.
Her relentless focus was on systemic economic equality, warning that liberty without economic independence was a delusion.
A.
Philip Randolph: A titan of both the labor and civil rights movements, Randolph carried Debs’ vision of industrial unionism forward.
He organized the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters—the first predominant African American labor union—and successfully forced federal action against employment discrimination.
Randolph proved that true working-class solidarity must dismantle racial barriers in order to succeed.
Victor L.
Berger: Debs’ longtime strategic ally, Berger was a key builder of democratic socialist political institutions and the editor of the Social Democratic Herald .
In 1910, he became the first Socialist elected to the U.S.
House of Representatives, proving that working-class advocacy could break into state and federal legislative halls.
Working together, these organizers transformed isolated workplace grievances into a nationwide demand for fundamental rights.
The cost of ignorance: History repeats Absolutely none of the rights we enjoy today were handed to us as gifts from a benevolent corporate boardroom, or political elites.
Someone fought for them, bled for them, and won through sheer working-class solidarity.
If you fail to learn from history, you are doomed to repeat it.
When we forget how the 8-hour workday, safe working conditions, and fair wages were won, we start to take them for granted.
The moment working class people forget their history, the powers-that-be start chipping away at those hard-won victories: Eroding Union Rights: Undermining collective bargaining and weakening the leverage of local workers.
Rolling Back Child Labor Laws: Seeing attempts across the country to loosen safety standards and put kids back on hazardous job sites.
Stagnant Wages vs.
Corporate Profits: Watching as corporate profits skyrocket and our families struggle to cover rent, healthcare, and basic groceries.
(They’re picking your pockets.) Eugene Debs and his allies handed us the blueprints.
They demonstrated that real power doesn’t come from money—it comes from people standing together in solidarity.
As a candidate for the statehouse, I’m not just asking you to remember history; I’m asking you to make it with me.
To stand as a Debs Democrat means refusing to watch as our rights are eroded piece by piece.
It means fighting for a government that puts working families first, protects labor, and ensures every Hoosier gets a fair return on their hard work.
Please, enjoy your day off.
Celebrate with your family.
Just please remember the fight that made today possible in the first place—and resolve to keep that fight alive.
Happy Debs Day!
