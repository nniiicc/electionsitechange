Georgia is seeing rapid growth in data center development, raising important questions about what that growth means for communities, utility costs, water, infrastructure, natural resources, and taxpayers.
Dumont Walker supports a statewide moratorium on new data center development while Georgia examines the impact of these projects, reviews the incentives being offered, and determines what protections should be in place.
He believes communities should also have a meaningful voice in decisions that could affect them for decades.
Pause New Development While Georgia Gets the Answers Data centers are major infrastructure projects, and decisions about where they are built and how they operate can have lasting effects on surrounding communities.
Dumont believes Georgia should take the time to understand those effects before approving more projects.
A statewide moratorium would provide time to examine energy and water demands, infrastructure needs, environmental impacts, land use, tax incentives, and potential effects on surrounding communities.
Taking the time to answer those questions allows us to make better informed decisions.
Protect Families From Higher Costs Rapid data center growth creates new demands on Georgia's infrastructure and energy system.
Dumont believes residents deserve to know who will ultimately pay for the infrastructure required to support that growth and what protections are in place for residential customers.
Families should not be expected to shoulder additional costs created by large corporate developments.
Dumont supports policies that fairly allocate the costs associated with data center development and protect Georgia households.
Protect Georgia's Water and Natural Resources Before approving additional data center development, Dumont believes Georgia needs a clearer understanding of how proposed facilities will use water, energy, and land and what those demands could mean for surrounding communities and the environment.
Different facilities can have different impacts.
Georgia needs reliable information and appropriate standards to evaluate those projects responsibly.
Economic development and environmental responsibility do not have to be competing goals.
Georgia can pursue growth while protecting the resources communities depend on.
Review Corporate Tax Incentives Georgia should not hand out generous tax incentives to data center corporations without a clear and measurable benefit to the people who are footing the bill.
Dumont supports rolling back the tax incentives Georgia offers to data center development.
Economic development incentives should serve a clear public purpose, create meaningful benefits for communities, and deliver a return that justifies the cost to taxpayers.
When they do not, the state should not continue writing a blank check.
Data centers can place significant demands on infrastructure and community resources, and Dumont believes corporations should bear their fair share of those costs.
Georgia should require greater transparency and accountability around these projects and redirect public resources toward investments that more directly strengthen families and communities.
Taxpayer dollars should work for Georgians first.
Give Communities a Real Voice The people who will live alongside these projects should have a meaningful role in decisions about them.
Dumont believes residents deserve clear information about proposed developments, including their expected resource and infrastructure needs, incentives, and potential effects on the surrounding community.
Communities should have that information and an opportunity to participate before decisions are made, not after.
