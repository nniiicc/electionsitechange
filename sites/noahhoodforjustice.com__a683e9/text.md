Justice Noah Hood is a lifelong Detroiter, public servant, and jurist committed to facts, fairness, integrity, and the rule of law.
Born in 1986 and raised on Detroit’s east side, he grew up in a family deeply rooted in public service, faith, and community leadership.
His father served on the Detroit City Council and as senior minister at Plymouth United Church of Christ, while his mother has served for decades as a federal judge after being appointed to the U.S.
District Court by President Bill Clinton.
His grandfather was a founding member of SCLC and contemporary of Andrew Young.
Justice Hood graduated as valedictorian of Cass Technical High School before attending Yale University, where he studied anthropology and languages.
He went on to earn his law degree from Harvard Law School and began his career practicing commercial litigation in Detroit.
Driven by a commitment to public service, Hood joined the U.S.
Attorney’s Office in Toledo, where he prosecuted complex federal cases including fraud, identity theft, money laundering, and crimes against children.
His work earned recognition from federal law enforcement partners for his leadership and dedication to justice.
In 2019, Hood was appointed to the Third Judicial Circuit Court in Detroit, one of the busiest trial courts in the country, where he presided over high-volume criminal dockets and handled serious felony trials.
During his tenure, he also presided over the Veterans Treatment Court, a problem-solving court for justice-involved veterans.
His experience on the bench shaped his belief that justice must be both efficient and fair, ensuring that every case receives careful attention.
In 2022, Hood was appointed to the Michigan Court of Appeals.
There, he authored numerous opinions on nearly every aspect of Michigan law, including search-and-seizure, the Confrontation Clause, juvenile justice, family law, zoning law, construction law, and no-fault.
The decisions demonstrated a commitment to high-confidence facts and our rules-based order.
Today, Justice Hood continues his service on Michigan’s highest court, bringing a deep respect for the law, a commitment to thoughtful judicial decision-making, and a lifelong dedication to the people of Michigan.
