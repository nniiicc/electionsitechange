PA District 3 is not one place.
It's dozens of distinct neighborhoods, each with its own history, its own businesses, its own way of looking out for its own.
This campaign is built block by block. · Central 01 Center City A downtown that has to work for the whole city.
From Rittenhouse to Logan Square to Washington Square West, Center City's hospitals, universities, and employers anchor the entire region.
But Center City only flourishes when the neighborhoods around it do too.
The campaign sees the whole map, not just the skyline. · Lower North 02 Fairmount & Brewerytown Row houses, breweries, and a neighborhood that knows its own name.
Between the Art Museum steps and Girard Avenue, Fairmount and Brewerytown have always been working neighborhoods — anchored by legacy homeowners, corner businesses, and the small shops that line Fairmount Ave and Girard.
Growth has to leave room for the people who built the block. · North (West of Broad) 03 Strawberry Mansion & Sharswood Historic Black neighborhoods on the edge of the park.
Bordering Fairmount Park, Strawberry Mansion and Sharswood carry generations of Black homeownership, church life, and community organizing.
The work here is reinvestment without displacement — and making sure the families who stayed through the hard years see the benefits of what comes next. · Upper North (West of Broad) 04 Nicetown–Tioga Industrial roots, neighborhood grit.
Built around the rail lines and the Wayne Junction corridor, Nicetown–Tioga has carried Philadelphia's industrial backbone for over a century.
Today the fight is housing stability, air quality, and good jobs that don't require leaving the neighborhood to find. · West 05 West Philadelphia Where Black wealth was built — and must be built again.
From Mantua to Powelton to Cobbs Creek, West Philly is a community of legacy homeowners, small-business builders, and faith communities.
Tangled titles, predatory lending, and disinvestment have taken wealth from generations of families.
The work is to help claim it back. · Southwest 06 Southwest Philadelphia The neighborhoods the airport flies over.
Kingsessing, Elmwood, and Eastwick are home to multi-generational families, West African and Caribbean communities, and small businesses lining Woodland and Elmwood Avenues.
The story here is environmental justice — flooding, air quality, and the airport's footprint — alongside the work of keeping homes affordable. · South 07 South Philly Where every storefront is somebody's life's work.
The Italian Market, East Passyunk, Point Breeze, Grays Ferry, and the row houses down to the stadiums — South Philly is a city of independent businesses run by people who know every customer's name.
Small business policy is family policy here. · Northwest (River Wards) 08 East Falls, Manayunk & Roxborough The river wards on the Schuylkill.
Strung along the Schuylkill and Ridge Avenue, East Falls, Manayunk, and Roxborough mix working families, Main Street small business, and some of the city's best green space along the Wissahickon.
The work is keeping the neighborhoods affordable for the people who grew up here. · Northwest 09 Germantown Historic streets, modern grit.
Germantown has been a model of multi-racial, multi-generational neighborhood organizing for decades.
The work now is keeping legacy residents in place and giving young people a reason to build their futures here. · Far Northwest 10 Mt.
Airy & Chestnut Hill Integration as a lived practice — and a fight to keep it.
Mt.
Airy and Chestnut Hill share a civic culture built around the Avenue, the train line, and decades of intentional integration.
The pressure now is affordability — making sure the teachers, nurses, and longtime families who made these neighborhoods what they are can still live in them.
