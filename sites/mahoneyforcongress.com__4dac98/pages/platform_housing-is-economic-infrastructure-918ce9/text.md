02 · The Problem When workers can't afford to live near work, the city falls behind.
Philadelphia is home to some of the country's most resilient neighborhoods — and some of its most unaffordable rents relative to wages.
Teachers, nurses, transit operators, and trades workers are being priced out of the very communities they serve.
Housing isn't a separate problem.
It's the load-bearing wall of every other policy: workforce, education, public safety, family stability. · Dennis's Plan Treat housing like the infrastructure it is.
Expand federal investment in workforce housing near transit and job centers.
Protect naturally affordable neighborhoods from speculative displacement.
Make first-time homeownership reachable through down-payment assistance, tangled-title reform, and credit pathways.
Direct federal HUD and Treasury programs to reward cities that build — not those that block.
Dennis will fight to: Expand affordable and attainable housing Preserve naturally affordable neighborhoods Support first-time homeownership Increase housing near transit and job centers Strengthen anti-displacement strategies Stable housing creates stable families and a stronger economy. · How we pay for it Existing federal tools, used at scale.
Modernize the Low-Income Housing Tax Credit and pair it with existing tax-exempt bond authority to leverage private capital.
