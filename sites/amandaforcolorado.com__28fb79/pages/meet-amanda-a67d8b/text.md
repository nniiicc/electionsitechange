Amanda has also shaped the laws Colorado's elections run on.
As Executive Director of Colorado Common Cause, and later as Clerk, she helped draft:
Independent redistricting
Took map-drawing away from politicians and gave it to independent commissions.
Colorado voters passed both.
Automatic voter registration
Registered more than 250,000 new voters in its first year.
An end to prison gerrymandering
Counts incarcerated people as residents of their home communities instead of the prison's.
Multilingual ballots
Expanded translated ballots from 6 counties to 20 and created a statewide translator hotline.
Jail voting
Made Colorado the first state to require in-person voting in every county jail.

When more people can vote, more people belong.
Amanda has spent her career making sure the people who are most often left out of elections, of government, of the systems that are supposed to serve them, get included.
When she became Clerk, she found that of roughly 850 eligible voters in the Jefferson County jail, only three had voted in 2022.
They had the right to vote and no real way to exercise it.
People told her voting from jail couldn't be done.
Amanda worked with people who had lived it and with community partners to write SB24-072, and Colorado became the first state to require in-person voting in every county jail.
Jefferson County jail went from 3 voters to nearly 350 in its first year.
Statewide, more than 2,300 Coloradans voted from jail, nearly ten times as many as in 2022.
On the first day of voting, one voter told Amanda it was one of the top five experiences of his life.
He said it made him feel like he mattered.
