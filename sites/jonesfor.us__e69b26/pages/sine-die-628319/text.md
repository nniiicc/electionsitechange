Todd Jones • October 12, 2026 It's a wrap!
Putting a bow on the 2026 session...
Sine Die has come and gone.
The 2026 legislative session is officially in the books, and I'm headed back to District 25 with a lot to show for it.
This was one of the most productive sessions since you’ve elected me to represent our community.
We cut taxes, secured major wins for working Georgians, passed legislation that puts our district ahead of the curve on the issues that matter most — and we did it while the national media was busy looking everywhere else.
Here's the full picture. ✅ BILLS I SPONSORED HB 987 — The Voluntary Portable Benefit Plan Act This is the bill I'm most proud of this session.
Hundreds of thousands of Georgians work as independent contractors, gig workers, and freelancers — and for too long, they've had no path to health insurance, retirement savings, or paid time off.
HB 987 fixes that.
It creates portable benefit accounts that workers own and control, where companies can voluntarily contribute to their coverage.
No government mandate.
No bureaucratic strings.
Just a free-market solution that puts working Georgians in charge of their own future.
It passed with strong bipartisan support and is headed to the Governor's desk.
HB 1272 — The Georgia Payment Stablecoin Act Georgia is now one of the first states in the nation to establish a real regulatory framework for digital currency.
This bill creates a licensing process for stablecoin issuers, requires dollar-for-dollar reserves backing every coin in circulation, and holds issuers to the same anti-money laundering standards as traditional banks.
No government ban on crypto.
No wild west.
Just smart, free-market rules that protect consumers and position Georgia as a leader in the new economy.
Proud to carry this alongside my fellow Forsyth County legislator, Senator Greg Dolezal, in the Senate.
HB 1230 — Prison Drone Security Criminals have been using drones to smuggle drugs, phones, and weapons into Georgia prisons.
This bill gives law enforcement the tools to detect, intercept, disable, and seize those drones.
Anyone caught using a drone to smuggle contraband into a prison is now looking at a felony with serious prison time.
Common sense.
Long overdue.
HB 986 — Personal Delivery Device Safety Sidewalk delivery robots are already on Georgia's streets — and they're only going to become more common.
This bill sets sensible speed limits and requires robots to emit an audible warning when approaching pedestrians, wheelchairs, or vehicles.
The kind of forward-thinking legislation that keeps Georgia ahead of the curve on emerging technology.
HB 414 — Protecting Georgia Elections from Outside Interference Out-of-state donors, consultants, and political organizations have been able to meddle in Georgia elections with virtually no accountability — until now.
This bill empowers the Georgia Ethics Commission to investigate and subpoena outside entities trying to buy influence in our elections.
It passed with strong bipartisan support.
Georgia's elections should be decided by Georgians.
HB 1206 (via SB 396) — Total Cost of Ownership for School Tech Purchases When school districts buy technology, they often focus only on the sticker price — ignoring maintenance, training, licensing, and replacement costs down the road.
This bill requires school districts to evaluate the total cost of ownership on technology purchases over $100.
Smarter spending, better outcomes for students.
HR 1007 — Arts-Based Mental Health & Veterans I sponsored and passed a resolution recognizing the proven impact of arts-based programs in treating PTSD, depression, and other mental health conditions — particularly for veterans and service members.
Georgia's heroes deserve every tool available. 🏛️ BIG WINS FOR EVERY GEORGIAN Gas Tax Suspended — 33 Cents a Gallon Back in Your Pocket For 60 days, Georgia's gas tax is suspended.
That's real money back in your wallet every time you fill up — no red tape, no means testing, no bureaucracy.
Just immediate relief at the pump for every Georgian who drives to work.
HB 463 — Income Tax Cut Georgia continued its march toward being one of the most tax-competitive states in the nation.
HB 463 reduces the flat income tax rate from 4.99% to 3.99% over time.
It also raises the standard deduction to $36,000 for married couples and $18,000 for single filers, increases the dependent exemption, exempts the first $1,750 in tips and the first $1,750 in overtime pay, and raises the income exclusion for retirees to $70,000.
If you work, save, or retire in Georgia, this bill puts money back in your pocket.
HB 1193 — Georgia Early Literacy Act Every public school with kindergarten through third grade students will now have access to a school-based literacy coach.
This landmark bill builds a statewide framework to ensure Georgia's youngest learners develop strong reading skills from day one.
As a parent and an educator, this one hits close to home.
HB 974 — $38.5 Billion State Budget The House passed a responsible budget that fully funds our schools at $14.9 billion, increases Medicaid reimbursement rates for doctors and hospitals, adds funding for foster care services, invests in graduate medical education, and supports local infrastructure across the state.
Georgia is growing, and this budget reflects that growth responsibly.
HB 1118 — Paid Maternal Leave for State Employees New mothers who work for the state of Georgia will now receive 120 hours of paid maternal leave in the first three weeks following birth.
Families matter.
This is a long overdue recognition of that.
HB 350 — Baby Boxes Fire stations, police stations, and medical facilities can now install newborn safety devices — secured, monitored receptacles that allow mothers in crisis to safely surrender a newborn without prosecution.
This bill saves infant lives.
Simple as that. 🔄 FIGHTS WORTH FINISHING Not every good bill makes it across the finish line in 40 days.
Here are a few I'm bringing back next session.
HB 991 — Healthcare Worker Flexibility This bill would have given nurses and other healthcare professionals the freedom to pick up contract shifts at other facilities on their own terms.
The need is real, the bill is ready, and I'll be reintroducing it next session.
HR 1023 — Parental Bill of Rights Constitutional Amendment Parents have a fundamental right to direct the upbringing and education of their children.
This resolution would enshrine that right in Georgia's Constitution — not just statute, where it can be weakened or reversed.
It didn't make it across the finish line this session, but this fight is too important to walk away from.
HB 1396 — Homeless Services Coordination This bill passed the House but stalled in the Senate.
It would require organizations operating shelters and outreach programs near schools and parks to participate in Georgia's tracking system — better coordination, less waste, real accountability.
Back next session.

Why do we need an Ai Elections Bill?
By Todd Jones • February 12, 2024 Election integrity is an incredibly hot-button issue in American politics, particularly since the 2020 and 2016 elections.
In fact, I think it’s been so hot-button, many people have developed “election integrity fatigue”.
Frankly, I get it.
I’m tired of watching grifters from across the political spectrum–like Jill Stein or Sydney Powell–use wild conspiracies to create doubt about our elections.
That doubt creates political instability that can bleed into economic and social instability, negatively impacting our quality of life.
That’s why I’ve worked hard to strengthen voter confidence in our elections.
Before we passed election reforms in 2021, only 56% of Georgia voters were confident in the results of our elections.
After the 2022 election, that number increased to 73% .
The reforms we worked tirelessly to pass are working, and Georgia has since been ranked as the top state in the country for election integrity.
At the same time, an incredible 99% of Georgia voters said they had zero issues casting their ballot.
I would love to use this moment to take a victory lap and move on to other issues, but our election law has to keep pace with advances in technology.
Like the widespread use of digital advertising in the 2016 election, recent advances in Ai technology could completely change the way campaigns work in 2024.
Ai is more than just a chatbot now, it has the ability to create photos, audio, and video.
Just a few weeks ago, Ai was used to create a fake robocall from President Joe Biden encouraging Democrats to stay home on election day.
While this particular attempt may run afoul of New Hampshire’s law against voter suppression, there’s little that can be done under current law to prevent future Ai “deep fakes”.
Our election laws simply couldn’t foresee this technology.
That’s why I’m co-sponsoring legislation to prevent Ai “deep fakes”.
The legislation would not prevent the use of Ai for campaign purposes, but would make it illegal to create fake content portraying another candidate or elected official.
If this practice remains legal, campaigns and consultants will eventually add the Ai “deep fake” to their election strategy.
That will further erode trust in our election, and create tremendous instability.
I believe this bill should enjoy bipartisan support and will help put guardrails on Ai to ensure it’s a tool used to create economic growth, not a weapon for political turmoil.
I am also working to prevent the use of Ai to create pornographic content, which can be used to bully and embarrass with fake depictions of obscene acts.
I should be able to provide more information about these efforts soon.
Until then, I hope you will support HB 986.

In your service, Todd Jones Georgia State Representative Meet You in the Next Industrial Revolution By Todd Jones • May 11, 2018 By Todd Jones Georgia House of Representatives, District 25 May 2, 2018 Georgia sits at a crossroads.
Stay the current course and bet on the tried and true or jump into unchartered waters.
Our economy is growing, job rates are at an all-time high and our children attend the best schools in the state.
The challenge is where do we go next.
We are on the precipice of the next industrial revolution.
Augmented intelligence, 3-D printing and autonomous vehicles will fundamentally change our business and personal pursuits and passions.
Individually or combined, these three tectonic technology advancements will forever change the way we live.
The examples are endless, but here are a few: Healthcare – an orthopedic surgeon printing an exact replica of the patient’s knee nearly assuring the perfect fit and less recovery time, IBM Watson, via AI, making a diagnosis of a rare bone cancer because it had “consumed” years of medical journals to “learn” what humans can’t and providing mobility to the elderly and home-bound patients via AV, liberating a whole segment of our population.
Education – empowering teachers to teach and removing the administrative burden by leveraging AI success coaches to handle the mundane and unlock the educator’s passion to change children’s lives.
Providing real-time, 3-D modeling to high school robotics teams and higher education engineering students.
As a community and state, it is an imperative that we embrace AI, 3-D and AV.
The intersection of these burgeoning technologies and industries like transportation, education, healthcare and of course manufacturing sets up Georgia’s new “north star”.
We must rally private enterprise, education and government toward creating a workforce with the right skills, a business ecosystem with limited regulation and cash liquidity and R&D bolstered through deep partnerships between our higher education institutions and businesses of all sizes.
We have seen how the Internet can be the great equalizer.
Uber versus taxis, Facebook versus traditional publishing and on and on.
Imagine a manufacturing process comprised primarily of three phases – dream it, design it and print it.
An entrepreneur could have a customized product on the company web site within days, not weeks or months.
Each customer getting it in the color they choose along with their monogram.
Compare that to today.
Today’s manufacturing processes are laborious, inefficient and typically do not create a positive return on investment for quarters and in many cases years.
Let’s not pick on manufacturing, this can be said about nearly every industry of today.
The core processes existing in the USA are rooted in post-World War II thinking.
That mantra drove a massive economic expansion in the 50’s and early 60’s, but like the abacus and corded phones we need to be willing to walk away from what is comfortable.
Setting us up for success means a commitment from each of us.
We are all stakeholders in this revolution.
Note revolution, not evolution.
Evolving has been what we have been doing for decades.
Today is our chance to grab tomorrow.
The question is, do we have the resolve?
