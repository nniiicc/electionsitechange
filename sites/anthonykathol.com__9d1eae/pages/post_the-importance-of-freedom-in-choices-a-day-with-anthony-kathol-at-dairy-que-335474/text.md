The Importance of Freedom in Choices: A Day with Anthony Kathol at Dairy Queen
Freedom to choose shapes many parts of our daily lives, from the food we eat to the stores where we shop.
Recently, District 27 State Senate candidate Anthony Kathol visited a local Dairy Queen Grill and Chill to talk with voters about what freedom means in business and government.
His simple trip for a free Dilly Bar sparked a larger conversation about who should decide what customers get and why free enterprise matters.
Why a Free Dilly Bar Matters
At first glance, a free Dilly Bar might seem like just a small treat.
But Anthony Kathol used this moment to ask voters a bigger question: Is Dairy Queen required to give away free Dilly Bars?
The answer is no.
This offer is a choice made by the business, not a government rule.
This example shows how free enterprises decide what benefits to offer customers based on what makes sense for their business.
Dairy Queen chooses to give away free Dilly Bars to attract customers, build goodwill, and encourage repeat visits.
These decisions come from understanding customers and the market, not from government mandates.
Free Enterprise Knows Customers Best
State Senate Candidate Anthony Kathol emphasized that businesses, not governments, understand what customers want and need.
When companies compete freely, they innovate and improve their products and services to win customer loyalty.
This competition benefits consumers with better choices and prices.
In contrast, government-run services often lack the same incentives to respond quickly or creatively to customer demands.
When the government controls a service, it may focus more on rules and budgets than on customer satisfaction.
The Opponent’s Vision: Government-Run Grocery Stores
During his conversation, Kathol pointed out that his opponent supports Democratic Socialist policies, including government-run grocery stores.
This idea means the government would control where and how people buy groceries, limiting options and competition.
Government-run stores could reduce customers' freedom to choose products, prices, and shopping experiences.
It could also stifle innovation and lead to inefficiencies, as government agencies may not operate with the same flexibility and customer focus as private businesses.
Why This Election Is About Freedom
The choice in this election goes beyond just who will hold office.
It is about whether voters want to protect the freedom to choose or accept more government control over everyday decisions.
State Senate Candidate Kathol’s message is clear: electing him means supporting free enterprise and the individual's right to make their own choices.
It means trusting businesses to serve customers well and keeping government out of areas where it does not belong.
What Voters Can Do
Voters in District 27 have a clear decision to make on November 3, 2026.
They can choose a candidate who believes in freedom, choice, and the power of free markets.
Or they can choose a candidate who supports expanding government control over businesses and consumer options.
Here are some ways voters can engage:
- Visit local businesses and ask how free enterprise benefits the community.
- Talk with candidates about their views on government involvement in the economy.
- Consider how policies affect everyday choices, from grocery shopping to dining out.
- Vote for candidates who prioritize freedom and customer choice.
The Bigger Picture: Freedom in Everyday Life
The conversation at Dairy Queen is a reminder that freedom to choose is not just a political slogan.
It affects real people and real decisions every day.
Whether it is a free Dilly Bar or the grocery store down the street, the ability to choose matters.
When businesses compete freely, customers win.
They get better products, better service, and more options.
When government controls too much, choices shrink, and people lose the power to decide what works best for them.
Final Thoughts
Anthony Kathol’s visit to Dairy Queen showed how a simple moment can spark important discussions about freedom and choice.
This election is about more than politics; it is about protecting the individual's right to make their own decisions.
On November 3, 2026, voters in District 27 can support a future where free enterprise thrives, and government stays within its proper role.
Elect Anthony Kathol for State Senate to keep the freedom to choose alive in your community.
