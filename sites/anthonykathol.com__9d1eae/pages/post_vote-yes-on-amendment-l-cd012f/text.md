Lately, voters have been asking me how to vote on Constitutional Amendment L.
The title of the Amendment to the South Dakota Constitution is that a proposed constitutional amendment receives an affirmative vote of 60% of the votes cast.
Source: https://sdsos.gov/elections-voting/upcoming-elections/general-information/2026%20Election%20Information/2026%20Ballot%20Question%20Information/2026%20Ballot%20Question%20Files/2026%20BQ/2026GeneralBQPamphletFINAL.pdf Let’s look at what happened two years ago, when South Dakotans voted on Amendment G, funded by out-of-state money.
Amendment G appeared on the November 5, 2024, ballot as an initiated constitutional amendment led by Rick Weiland and the Dakotans for Health Political Action Committee (PAC).
It sought to add a new section to Article VI of the South Dakota Constitution establishing a right to abortion and defining how the state could regulate abortion across pregnancy trimesters.
The measure was defeated, preserving South Dakota’s near‑total abortion ban.
Illinois Democratic Governor JB Pritzker’s Thinking Big America PAC donated $790,000 to Weiland’s Dakotans for Health Political Action Committee.
See here: https://sdcfr.sdsos.gov/Document.aspx?DocumentID=6379&type=img Moreover, you can see how many out-of-state people donated to the Yes on G Campaign here: https://sdcfr.sdsos.gov/Document.aspx?DocumentID=6441&type=img Thankfully, South Dakotans voted down Amendment G.
See the screenshot below of the 2024 Election Night unofficial results posted on the South Dakota Secretary of State’s website: Source: https://sdsos.gov/elections-voting/election-resources/election-history/2024_Election_History.aspx During the 2024 General Election, there were 625,192 registered voters in the State of South Dakota.
Only 69.82% of the voters chose to participate in the 2024 election, which equates to 436,478 ballots cast.
In other words, only two out of every three voters participated in the general election.
Based on the numbers identified above, for Amendment G to have passed, it would have required a simple majority (50% + 1 vote) to enshrine abortion into the South Dakota State Constitution.
To be successful in their campaign, Dakotans for Health needed 213,377 YES votes to pass Amendment G.
Now, if we look at the number 213,377 versus the number that would vote NO in this hypothetical: 213,376, and then add on the number of voters that chose not to participate in the general election (188,714 voters) plus those that did vote in the general election but chose not to cast a vote on this specific initiated Constitutional Amendment G (9,725 voters), then the total number is 411,815 voters (213,376 + 188,714 + 9,725).
Hence, as one can see, the 213,377 YES votes cast to enshrine abortion into our State Constitution are less than the 411,815 voters who chose to stay at home or vote NO.
In other words, 34% of registered voters in South Dakota decide that abortion should be enshrined in our State Constitution under this hypothetical example.
Is this what we want in South Dakota?
Rule by a minority?
See summary of the simple majority hypothetical on Amendment G below, along with the corresponding pie graph: Amendment G ( Simple Majority Hypothetical) Votes Yes Votes Cast, 50% + 1 (Simple Majority) 213,377 No Votes Cast 213,376 Total Votes Cast 426,753 Number of Voters not Casting a Vote on Amendment G 9,725 Registered Voters not participating in the 2024 Election 188,714 Total Number of Eligible Registered Voters Eligible to Vote in 2024 625,192 By voting YES on Proposed Amendment L, the bar is raised from #% +1 (simple majority) to #% of all votes cast to amend the State Constitution.
In my hypothetical example demonstrated above, the new numbers are as follows, with 60% of voters passing Amendment G to enshrine abortion in the State Constitution.
Amendment G (60% of Votes Cast Hypothetical) Votes Yes Votes Cast, 60% of votes cast 256,052 No Votes Cast 170,701 Total Votes Cast 426,753 Number of Voters not Casting a Vote on Amendment G 9,725 Registered Voters not participating in the 2024 Election 188,714 Total Number of Eligible Registered Voters Eligible to Vote in 2024 625,192 As one can see, under Proposed Amendment L, a higher bar is set at 60% - no longer the simple majority.
Therefore, it took 41% of registered voters (256,052) to change the South Dakota Constitution, not 34% (213,377) in this hypothetical.
As one can see, 41% of voters is still less than the majority of all registered voters.
Because some voters will always sit out a given election cycle, the electorate as a whole must protect our State Constitution from being amended by out-of-state special interest groups.
These groups, such as Dakotans for Health PAC, want nothing more than to amend our State Constitution because it is relatively easy and cheap to persuade voters in South Dakota since all that is required to change our current State Constitution is a simple majority of votes cast (50% plus 1).

Constitution is protected from being amended.
Our U.S.
Constitution has only been changed or amended 27 times.
The 27th Amendment was added to the U.S.
Constitution and was accepted as a validly ratified constitutional amendment on May 20, 1992.
Before that, the 26th Amendment to the U.S.
Constitution was ratified and signed into law in July 1971, lowering the legal voting age in the United States from 21 to 18.
Changes to the U.S.
Constitution require a 2/3 vote by both the U.S.
House of Representatives and the Senate, followed by ratification by ¾ of the state legislatures (38 states) – a much higher bar than 60% of the votes cast to amend the South Dakota State Constitution.
