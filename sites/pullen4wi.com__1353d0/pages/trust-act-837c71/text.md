KEEP Act · NEST Act · HOME Act · PAVE Act · PRICE Act STAY Act · SMALL Act · CHANCE Act · TRIM Act · PRUNE Act SHIELD Act · RISE Act · STOP Act · EQUAL Act · ROOTS Act BARE Act · TRUST Act · DRAW Act THE TRUST ACT The TRUST Act — Transparent, Reliable, Unified Standards for Election Turnout Free, Fair & Accessible Elections Wisconsin already gets a lot right on election administration — paper ballots statewide, public pre-election equipment testing, and a post-election audit required by law.
The TRUST Act builds on that foundation with three targeted upgrades, not a rewrite of what's already working.
1.
Smarter, Risk-Based Audits Wisconsin's current post-election audit checks a flat 10% of reporting units and a handful of contests, regardless of how close any given race was.
The TRUST Act moves to a risk-limiting audit model: Sample size scales to the margin of victory — closer races get audited more thoroughly, safe wins get a lighter check.
This is more statistically rigorous than a flat percentage, not less.
Audit coverage expands beyond the current handful of top-of-ticket contests to include down-ballot and local races, so no race is too small to verify.
2.
Ballot-Count Reconciliation at Close Before results leave any polling place, the numbers should visibly match: Each polling place reconciles and publicly posts total ballot counts at close — ballots issued, ballots cast, and ballots processed by the tabulator.
This is basic bookkeeping, not a recount: it confirms the quantity is right before anyone looks at who won what.
3.
A Clear Rule for When Something's Actually Wrong Today, if an audit finds a discrepancy, the law leaves the next step vague.
The TRUST Act closes that gap: If a risk-limiting audit finds a discrepancy beyond the expected statistical margin, that polling place's ballots for the affected race are hand-counted in full before certification.
This targets a hand count at the specific place a problem actually showed up, instead of requiring one everywhere by default.
The Bottom Line Wisconsin's clerks and poll workers already do this work well.
The TRUST Act gives them a sharper, more statistically sound audit process, a simple public numbers-check at every polling place, and a clear rule for what happens if something doesn't add up — so confidence in the result doesn't depend on taking anyone's word for it.
