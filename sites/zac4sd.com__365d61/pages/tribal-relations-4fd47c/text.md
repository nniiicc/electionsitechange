Rapid City's Native community lives in this district: North Rapid, downtown, and the neighborhoods around Oglala Lakota College's He Sapa Center on Knollwood Drive.
This page is what I will carry to Pierre for you, who I will carry it with, and how to grade me on it.
The commitments Invite me to your table Where I stand Eight years ago a family court case brought my son to Rapid City, and I followed him here.
I learned what it is to face a system that was never built for the family in front of it.
Many Native families learned that generations before I did.
The Rural Ethnic Institute put District 32's candidates in front of Native voters at Oglala Lakota College's He Sapa Center in May and again on October 16, 2026.
I was at that table both times.
The commitments below are the ones I made there, in writing, so they can be checked.
I will not promise what a state legislator cannot deliver.
What a state representative can do is show up before the session, carry the bills that keep dying in committee, and come back to be graded.
That is what this page is.
I am not here to speak for you.
I am here to make sure Pierre stops speaking over you.
Zac Martin · He Sapa Center, Rapid City What I will carry to Pierre Seven commitments.
Each one with a receipt.
Nothing here is a slogan.
Every item names a bill, a vote or a date, so you can check it against the record and against me.

The bills I file in January will have your fingerprints on them.
That is a schedule, not a speech, and you can hold me to the dates.
Receipt The 102nd South Dakota Legislature convenes in January 2027.
02 2 of 7 A state and tribal consultation commission.
In 2026 Rep.
Eric Emery asked the House to create a South Dakota and tribal consultation commission: the Governor, the Secretary of Tribal Relations, one representative from each of the nine tribes and one lawmaker from each chamber, meeting at least every three months, with the Governor reporting every state initiative that touches the tribes before it lands.
HB 1190 had seven sponsors and died in House State Affairs, 8 to 4, without a floor vote.
I will co-sponsor it and work the committee, not just sign the sheet.
Receipt HB 1190 (2026).
Deferred to the 41st legislative day in House State Affairs, February 9, 2026.
03 3 of 7 A yearly report to tribes on the taxes collected on their land.
The state collects sales and other taxes on reservation land under agreements with the tribes, and the tribes cannot see the numbers.
Twice in two sessions a bill asked the Department of Revenue to send each tribe a yearly estimate of what was collected in its agreement area, split between remote and in-person sellers, with a back report for the prior six years.
HB 1018, with fifteen sponsors from both parties, died in House Taxation in 2025, 12 to 1.
SB 241, carried by Sen.
Tamara Grove, died in Senate Taxation in 2026, 4 to 3.
Neither got a floor vote.
I will co-sponsor it a third time.
Receipt HB 1018 (2025), House Taxation, 12 to 1.
SB 241 (2026), Senate Taxation, 4 to 3.
04 4 of 7 ICWA, written into state law.
The Indian Child Welfare Act is federal law, and Pennington County is where South Dakota was taken to federal court over it.
A federal judge in Rapid City found in 2015 that the county's emergency custody hearings violated the law.
That ruling was later set aside on procedural grounds, not because the facts changed.
The next Legislature should write state law that puts South Dakota ahead of the federal floor, not behind it.
I will write that bill with tribal child welfare leaders, not for them.
Receipt Oglala Sioux Tribe v.
Van Hunnik, D.S.D.
2015; vacated by the Eighth Circuit in 2018 on abstention grounds.
The family court reform stack 05 5 of 7 Reentry that starts on day one, and treatment people can reach.
Native people are 39 percent of South Dakota's prison population and Native women return to prison at a rate of 66 percent, which is why reentry planning has to start the day a sentence begins and treatment has to be reachable from the reservation and the rural county.
That is not a statistic about a people.
It is a report card on a state.
The state's own reentry report said the same thing in August: nobody should leave prison without an ID, a Medicaid card and a place to sleep.
The women's prison in Rapid City has 96 treatment beds and a mother and infant unit.
The state built the model here.
I will hold the $650 million men's prison to it.
Receipt South Dakota Searchlight, December 17, 2025, Department of Corrections figures.
CSG Justice Center reentry assessment to the Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force, Rapid City, August 20, 2026.
Tough on violent crime.
Smart on the rest.
06 6 of 7 Tax fairness for the people who rent.
More than four in ten households in District 32 rent.
In 2026 Pierre cut property taxes for homeowners and will pay for it by letting the sales tax go back up next July.
A renter pays the sales tax at the register and the property tax inside the rent check, and gets none of the relief.
I will carry a renter's rebate so the people paying the tax finally see it, and a rule that the property tax share of a unit is disclosed to the tenant.
Receipt US Census ACS 5-year, table B25003: 42.2 percent of District 32 households rent.
SB 245 (2026), sections 2 and 6.
The affordability agenda 07 7 of 7 Every legal vote counts.
Every legal voter gets a straight answer.
I support proof of citizenship to register, and the 2026 law already counts a tribal ID as that proof.
I want a tribal ID accepted everywhere a driver's license is.
Then the part the state is failing at: nearly six thousand South Dakotans are now on a federal-only list that lets them vote for Congress and nothing else, and the state's own voter portal does not tell them.
Nobody should lose a state ballot without a letter that says why and how to fix it.
I will carry that fix.
Receipt SB 175 (2026), House 64 to 3, signed March 30, 2026.
South Dakota Searchlight, October 13, 2026: 5,735 federal-only registrants.
