About the Candidate A Saginaw Guy — Fighting for Us Jason Tunney is running for Michigan State Senate in District 35 — representing the people of Saginaw, Bay, and Midland Counties.
He has never held elected office before.
He is running because he believes good government demands honesty, competence, and someone willing to show up.
"I'm paying taxes and I'm not being represented.
This is un-American.
This is no taxation without representation — and I will speak up about it." — Jason Tunney His Story Jason was born on February 16, 1972 — his mother's 18th birthday — at Saginaw General Hospital.
His parents brought him home to a small duplex on Erna Drive in Saginaw.
His mother worked at the McDonald's on Bay Road.
His father got a job as a millwright at General Motors.
1980 was a tough year: his parents divorced, and his father was laid off from GM for the entire year.
His father told him: "Son, you need to study hard, because these jobs aren't going to be here when you graduate from high school." They weren't.
Jason studied hard.
He graduated, went to the University of Michigan, then law school.
He came straight back to Saginaw and joined the Saginaw County Prosecutor's Office as an assistant prosecuting attorney — trying violent crimes, auto theft cases, and crimes against women and children.
It was there he met his wife, Pamela, who was also an assistant prosecutor.
Together, they have over twelve years of prosecuting experience between them.
After four years in the prosecutor's office, Jason joined his family's business, Duro-Last — a roofing manufacturing company his grandfather, John Burt, founded right here in the Great Lakes Bay Region.
Jason spent two years literally on rooftops around the country, learning the product from the ground up.
He moved through quality assurance, then legal, eventually becoming General Counsel, Executive Vice President, and a member of the Board of Directors.
He worked there for over twenty years.
Jason and Pamela have three sons — twin boys who are 22 and a younger son who is 18.
He still lives in the Saginaw area.
He has never run for any elected office.

Why He's Running One evening in April 2025, sitting at home, Jason realized he was paying state taxes for a State Senator he didn't have.
The seat had been vacant for months with no special election called.
He decided to run because he believes in good government — and because 270,000 people in this district deserved a voice.
