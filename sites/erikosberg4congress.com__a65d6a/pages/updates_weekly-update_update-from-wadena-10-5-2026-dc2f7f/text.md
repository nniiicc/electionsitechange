What we don’t know is which agency’s appropriation is paying for these ads.

A chainsaw approach, when a scalpel would be best…
I recently met with leadership & staff at Productive Alternatives in Perham, MN.
This is a non-profit social services agency.
They provide employment, disability, mental-health & community-based human services in rural Minnesota.
Their headquarters are in Fergus Falls, and they have locations in Moorhead, Little Falls, Alexandria, Brainerd, Fosston & Hoffman.
They provide services to roughly 1,000 per day and employ hundreds of people.
I was impressed by the Perham facility and the environment they have created.
I met with some of their service recipients and employees too.
I believe these are good people, doing the right things for the right reasons.
They aren’t doing this to get rich.
As we combat fraud, we need to remember there are service providers doing it right.
They shouldn’t be punished.
Neither should the people receiving services.
I’ve suggested we should adopt a “Public Trust Score” so not all providers are lumped together when it comes to oversight.
This is what I mean when I say we need to use a scalpel, not a chainsaw.
Productive Alternatives is in a particular bind because transportation is one of the services they provide to the rural communities they operate in.
I heard them say they put on close to a million miles a year helping those with disabilities access services.
Rising operating expenses, plus cuts in funding, don’t exactly lead to better outcomes.
Services, and ultimately clients, suffer.
