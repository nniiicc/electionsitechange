Meet Ida: A Proven Leader Who Knows How to Get Things Done
Ida Keeley has spent more than 25 years working inside the public systems Washington families rely on and leading the people and organizations responsible for making those systems work.
She has successfully managed complex public budgets, supervised hundreds of employees, led difficult programs, served on and chaired boards, worked across multiple levels of government, and brought together people with very different perspectives to solve multiple-agency issues.
That experience taught Ida something fundamental: good government requires both compassion and competence.
It isn’t enough to fund a program and hope for the best.
Leaders have a responsibility to know whether it is working, hold people accountable for results, be transparent about how public dollars are spent, and change course when something isn’t working.
That’s the kind of leadership Ida will bring to Olympia.
From Lived Experience to Public Service
Ida’s path to public service is deeply personal.
Growing up in an unstable home environment and moving between relatives, she experienced firsthand the challenges vulnerable children and families face.
Rather than allowing those experiences to define her future, Ida built a career trying to make those systems work better for the next family.
She began as a social worker and rose through leadership positions in child welfare and juvenile justice at the county and state levels.
Over the course of her career, she has been responsible not simply for advocating for change, but for responsibility implementing it.
Managing People, Budgets and Results
As a public-sector administrator, Ida has managed multimillion-dollar budgets and large, complex programs responsible for serving Washington’s most vulnerable residents.
She understands that every public dollar comes from people who worked hard to earn it.
She believes government has an obligation to spend those dollars carefully, transparently, and effectively.
As a leader, Ida believes in setting clear expectations, supporting employees in doing their best work, and holding people accountable for results.
She has seen firsthand that strong public services depend on strong workforces, people who are valued, properly trained and compensated, adequately staffed, and given the tools they need to succeed.
Bringing People Together
Some of Ida’s most important work has required bringing together people from institutions that don’t always agree.
She has built partnerships across state and local government, courts, law enforcement, community organizations, schools, Tribes, advocates, and families.
She has worked government-to-government with Tribal nations, including helping develop trainings and Memoranda of Understanding.
She partnered with Navy programs to support military families returning from deployment.
And through the Washington Association of Juvenile Court Administrators, she worked directly on legislation affecting Washington’s juvenile justice system.
Ida doesn’t believe collaboration means avoiding difficult conversations.
It means getting the right people around the table, listening, establishing shared goals, and staying focused on results.
Building Strong Organizations
Ida was part of the founding team of Dawson’s Place Child Advocacy Center, helping build an organization that serves children who have experienced abuse.
She has also served in leadership roles on numerous community and statewide boards, including Children’s Campaign Fund and Children’s Campaign Fund Action, the YMCA, Citrine Women’s Health and Wellness, the Washington Association of Juvenile Court Administrators, and Washington Association of Child Advocacy Programs, and the Arlington Community Resource Center after the Oso Slides.
Those experiences have given Ida another perspective she’ll bring to the Legislature: how to build organizations, manage competing priorities, oversee resource allocation, and turn a mission into measurable action.
Rooted in Our Community
Ida and her husband, a longtime Arlington teacher, have called Lake Stevens home for twenty years and raised their family here.
Ida is running because she believes the 39th District deserves a representative who understands our communities and also has the experience to make government work.
She knows how to bring people together.
She knows how to manage.
And she knows that ultimately, leadership has to produce results.
