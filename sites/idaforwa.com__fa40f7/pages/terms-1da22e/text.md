But I want to say clearly where I stand.
I've spent my career in child welfare, juvenile justice, courts, government budgeting, and nonprofits, and I know how these systems connect (or don't).
When we cut ... community supports, the cost doesn't disappear.
It shifts to ERs, first responders, and the housing and homelessness crisis.
Families lose stability, and kids lose the consistency, routines, and medications they depend on.
When children and family systems lose that support, they are no longer low risk.
They can escalate into higher cost systems like foster care, intensive residential services, and institutional care.
We have a culture where we are trained to respond to crisis instead of investing in prevention, and prevention costs less than crisis response.
In the end, taxpayers, hospitals, first responders, social workers, and behavioral health providers pay the price of these cuts.
