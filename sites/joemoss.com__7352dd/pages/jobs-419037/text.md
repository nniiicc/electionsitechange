Joe Moss is seeking candidates for the House District 89 office team that will serve West Michigan in Lansing beginning January 2027.
The people who join this office will help deliver on a simple standard: bold leadership, courage, commitment to excellence, and protecting individual rights.
Legislative Aide / Constituent Services This is the first point of contact for the office.
Keep operations running, make sure residents of HD-89 can reach their representative, and help resolve problems.
You will: Serve as first point of contact for phone, mail, and district email.
Manage the representative’s calendar, travel between Lansing and the district, and daily workflow.
Track and support constituent casework.
Draft correspondence, ceremonial letters, and routine replies.
Help plan district meetings, coffee hours, and in-district events.
Keep files, contacts, and follow-ups organized.
Write social posts and help monitor what is happening in the district.
Support session, committee, and meeting prep as needed.
You should bring: Professional, resourceful administrative and customer-service skills.
Excellent writing, phone manner, and judgment under deadline.
Microsoft Office proficiency and the ability to learn House systems quickly.
A willingness to learn the legislative process and how state agencies actually work.
Preferred: knowledge of HD-89 communities, prior office or government experience, and a bachelor’s degree.
Neither a degree nor prior Lansing experience is required if the skills are there.
Compensation: House FY27 scale — Constituent Services I: $35,000–$54,848; Constituent Services II: $43,546–$60,055, plus House employee benefits.
Level set by experience.
Location: District 89 and Lansing office, with regular travel.
Some evenings and district events are part of the job.
Legislative Aide / Policy Services This is the legislative engine of the office.
Help turn Joe’s agenda into bills, amendments, testimony, and votes.
You will: Research, draft, and track legislation tied to the office priorities.
Prepare materials for session, committees, and meetings.
Staff committee meetings and attend meetings on the representative’s behalf.
Work with bill drafters, policy advisors, stakeholders, and caucus staff.
Answer constituent questions that require a policy or legislative response.
Plan district policy meetings and keep the office current on issues that matter locally.
Help manage the legislative calendar and flag votes, hearings, and deadlines.
Mentor interns and keep the policy workflow disciplined.
You should bring: A bachelor’s degree and/or legislative-related experience (two years for Policy I; five years preferred for Policy II).
Strong research, writing, and analytical skills.
You will be judged on the quality of the memo, not the length.
Familiarity with the Michigan legislative process and state government operations, or the ability to learn it fast.
Comfort representing the office with stakeholders, agencies, and other legislative staff.
Preferred: experience in policy, local government, energy/land use, budgeting, or conservative policy shops.
Knowledge of West Michigan is a plus.
Compensation: House FY27 scale — Policy Services I: $47,683–$65,760; Policy Services II: $59,321–$80,000, plus House employee benefits.
Level set by experience.
Location: District 89 and Lansing office, with travel to the district for meetings and events.
Session, committee weeks, and end-of-session periods require extra hours.
Paid Internships The office will hire paid legislative interns.
Interns support constituent service, communications, office operations, and legislative research.
Strong interns earn responsibility early and a close look at how a results-driven House office functions.
Typical work includes: Answering phones, sorting mail, and helping resolve constituent issues.
Drafting emails and letters.
Filing, organizing, and keeping the office tight.
Monitoring, researching, and summarizing legislation.
Supporting district events and other duties as assigned.
Schedule and pay: Part-time and at-will.
Paid intern rate: $13.73–$20.00 per hour on the House FY27 scale.
When the House is in session, intern hours generally fall Tuesday–Thursday.
Schedules are flexible.
Some travel may be required.
Students in a formal internship program must keep the office informed of academic deadlines and credit requirements.
Who should apply: Students, veterans, or participants in a job-entry program.
A positive attitude, clear communication, Microsoft Office skills, passion for service.
A willingness to learn the legislative process and the discipline to make other people’s work effective.
Students and HD-89 residents are strongly encouraged to apply.
College students at GVSU, Hope, Cornerstone, and nearby campuses are a natural fit.
Cedarville is a plus.
Positions These are official Michigan House of Representatives positions, paid by the House, with office hours in Lansing, and district hours in West Michigan.
These jobs are separate from campaign volunteer roles.
If you want to help elect Joe, visit the Volunteer page.
The Michigan House allows each representative two full-time staff members.
Placement on the House pay scale (Level I or Level II) to match experience.
Positions are at-will, partisan positions.
How to apply Applications are being collected now so the office can hit the ground running in January 2027.
Hiring is contingent on Joe taking office.
Email the following as PDFs to jobs@joemoss.com with the subject line: HD-89 – [Role] – [Your Name] One-page cover letter.
Say which role you want and why this office.
Optional: writing samples, portfolio items, memos, constituent letters, or published pieces.
A short note answering: What should a House office in District 89 get right in year one?
Qualified applicants will be contacted for a conversation.
Please do not call the campaign office to check the status.
After Joe is sworn in, formal onboarding will also run through Michigan House Human Resources, which administers payroll and benefits for all member-office staff.
Good to know These are at-will, partisan positions serving a conservative Republican.
Titles are official House titles; the work is broader than the title and standard description.
Session weeks, late nights, and district weekends are a reality.
If you need a 9-to-5 with no surprises, this is the wrong job.
Start date target: January 2027, following the convening of the new legislative session.
