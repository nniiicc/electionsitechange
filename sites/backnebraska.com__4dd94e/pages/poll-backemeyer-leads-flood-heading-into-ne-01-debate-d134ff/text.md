The survey of 600 likely voters, conducted August 31–September 3, carries a margin of error of +/- 4%.
The result comes as NE-01 remains squarely on the national radar: the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) named the district to its “Districts in Play” list earlier this cycle, the first time it’s appeared on that list in 20 years.
“Mike Flood has supported Trump’s every move to make gas, groceries, and healthcare more expensive.
He’s shown us time and time again that we can’t trust him to step out of Trump’s shadow to do what’s best for Nebraskans,” said Backemeyer .
“In this debate, voters will see a clear choice: more of the same failed policies from a career politician in Mike Flood, or someone who actually fights for Nebraska’s families and farmers.” Backemeyer’s campaign has also built a broad coalition of support heading into the debate.
He has recently been endorsed by the Nebraska Farmers Union, which for the first time in 20 years has endorsed a Democrat alone, as well as the Nebraska State Education Association (NSEA).
A full list of endorsements can be found here .
A Career Politician, Same Failed Playbook Backemeyer’s campaign says the momentum reflects mounting frustration with Flood’s record in Washington — a record defined by policies that have directly hurt Nebraska families and farmers: Tariffs and the Trade War: Flood has voted six times in support of President Trump’s reckless blanket tariff policy which has raised costs and cut into markets for Nebraska’s farmers and small businesses, and has driven up prices for Nebraskans.
The War in Iran: Flood has voted six times to back the war of choice in Iran, resulting in skyrocketing gas and diesel prices and adding $1 billion a day to the national debt.
Healthcare Cuts: Flood voted for the so-called “On Big Beautiful Bill” that cut $1 trillion from Medicaid and hurt rural hospitals, and voted to raise premiums for Nebraskans on the Affordable Care Act (ACA).
The debate is scheduled for Wednesday, September 23 at 7 pm, hosted by Nebraska Public Media, the Omaha World Herald, and KRVN Rural Radio.
