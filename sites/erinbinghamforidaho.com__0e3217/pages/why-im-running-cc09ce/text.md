Education and Training for Our Kids Every child in District 32 deserves a real shot at a great future.
That means strong public schools, well-supported teachers, and pathways that reflect the real world our kids are entering.

She knows what teachers need, what parents expect, and what students deserve.

Housing that was affordable a few years ago is out of reach for many young families today.
Healthcare costs continue to climb.
Groceries, utilities, and everyday expenses are stretching budgets that are already tight.
Erin hears it at every door: families in District 32 are feeling the squeeze.
She is in Boise working on real solutions right now.
She believes in removing government barriers, encouraging private sector solutions, and trusting Idahoans to make the best decisions for their own families.
Erin will apply those principles to the real affordability challenges facing Bonneville County.
What she is working on: Removing regulatory barriers that slow down responsible housing development and drive up costs Ensuring Idaho families can afford to live where they were raised Protecting Medicaid for the Idahoans who depend on it while pursuing fiscally sustainable long-term solutions Keeping government out of decisions that belong to families and their doctors Agriculture and Water Rights Southeast Idaho is farm country.
The land, the water, and the people who work both are central to the identity and economy of this region.
Erin and her family live on working land south of Idaho Falls and understand that agriculture is not just an industry here.
It is a way of life.
Water rights are the foundation of Idaho agriculture, and protecting them is non-negotiable.
As the federal government continues to assert pressure over land and water management, Idaho must stand firm in defense of the rights that farmers, ranchers, and irrigators depend on.
What she is working on: Protecting Idaho's water rights from federal overreach Supporting Idaho's farmers, ranchers, and the agricultural economy that anchors southeast Idaho Ensuring that state land and water policy is set in Idaho, not in Washington D.C.
