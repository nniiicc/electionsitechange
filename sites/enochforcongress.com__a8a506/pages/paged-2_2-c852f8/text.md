Vanessa Enoch is running to make life more affordable for families who are doing everything right but still feel squeezed.
For her, affordability means getting the basics back in sync—when costs rise faster than wages, families fall behind.
That's why she's focused on updating the rules that drive up everyday costs, including the cost of health care and the cost of housing, and on expanding the availability of good places to rent or buy so people can live near their jobs and put down roots.
That same principle guides her approach to Ohio's data center boom: economic development should pay its own way.
As these facilities expand across the state, the companies that profit from them—not working families—should cover the cost of the power lines and grid upgrades they require, so no one's electric bill goes up to subsidize a corporation.
Paying their own way means protecting the communities around them, too: Vanessa will fight the water, air, and noise pollution these facilities can bring, so no neighborhood has to trade its health or quality of life for a corporation's bottom line.
And she'll make sure our communities aren't overrun in the process—that sprawling data centers don't replace our farmland or exhaust the resources our families and future depend on.
Recognized for leadership in business and civic life, Vanessa brings practical experience and a solutions-first approach to public service.
In Congress, she will fight for accountable government that works for people, not special interests.
