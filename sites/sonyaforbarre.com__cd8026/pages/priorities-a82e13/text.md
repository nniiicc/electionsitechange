HOUSING & THE UNHOUSED
Housing is a basic need.
There is a housing shortage, and we have prioritized profit over people.
We need to address the impact that private equity and short-term rentals are having on our housing market and, in turn, on our neighbors who are facing a financial crisis that will have an impact on their housing.
I support building affordable and subsidized housing, modest single-family homes, senior housing, and creating or funding programs that will provide resources to keep people housed.
A divorce, a medical issue, or a car that needs a $500 part to keep a job should not be the reason someone loses their home.
I want to work to build more housing, while also keeping people in the homes they already have.
Other issues directly related to people struggling with housing include stagnant wages, lack of benefits, income disparity, including an inequitable tax structure, astronomical health insurance costs, and the rising costs of food, gas, and heating oil.
We must start somewhere.
The Housing First model will meet everyone’s basic need for shelter, making all the other issues more manageable.
AFFORDABILITY
The cost of everything is on the rise.
The cost of food, gas, heating oil, housing, and property taxes have all risen.
And then there’s health insurance.
Health insurance in Vermont is the most expensive in the US.
When we look at the other side of the coin, workers’ wages have not kept up with the rising cost of everything, and there are huge tax inequities that mean not everyone is paying their fair share.
One way to address these issues is to hold major corporations accountable.
If my small local business can provide a livable wage and benefits, why can’t WalMart or Amazon?
When major corporations shirk their responsibilities as employers, we all pay the price through corporate welfare.
The cost of subsidizing health insurance and SNAP benefits falls on all of us because large corporations are trying to line their shareholders’ pockets.
We are all paying because some people are gaming the system to make more money.
And it’s not the people at the bottom; they are just trying to survive.
It’s the people at the top who are taking advantage of every opportunity to make more money.
Let’s address the elephant in the room and hold corporations accountable for taking care of their employees.
With tax inequities, we need everyone to contribute their fair share so we can all thrive.
People should not be suffering while others have millions, billions, or trillions of dollars.
Another issue that is contributing to the high cost of living in Vermont is our aging and declining population, which means fewer people and fewer resources to share the cost of running our state.
We need to provide opportunities so that our own children want to stay in Vermont, students who have come to study here want to stay, and people want to move here because they can afford a house, pay taxes, and have the safety net of good benefits.
OUR FUTURE
Our future is a very broad issue and includes our children, our students, our schools, and our planet.
We need to find ways to provide safe, affordable childcare for every family.
We need to give students a good educational foundation and the tools to keep learning and growing.
We need to help our schools with the impossible task of providing mental health services while educating our children.
We need to consider the impact of climate change on our small towns and cities.
How can we look upstream to make sure we are providing a place where our children and their families can thrive?
We need to continue to build on the childcare foundation we have, to make sure parents can go to work and know their young children are learning and thriving.
As kids enter school, we need to have more afterschool programs and summer programs to take care of our students, and provide enrichment and opportunities outside of the classroom to learn.
We need more mental health services and wraparound services to help families who are struggling with generational poverty and generational trauma.
We need to find a way to collaborate across our region and our state on flood mitigation efforts, since flooding does not stop at the town line.
We all need to pitch in to do our part to make sure that the future is bright for everyone.
