home Facts About Electricity Rates in Georgia Fact #1.
The Georgia General Assembly does not set electricity rates.
The Official Code of Georgia Annotated § 46-2-23 regarding the PSC specifically provides that the commission shall have exclusive power to determine what are just and reasonable rates and charges to be made by any person, firm, or corporation subject to its jurisdiction.
Its jurisdiction for electricity providers is limited to investor-owned electricity providers.
Fact #2.
Georgia Power is Georgia’s only investor-owned electricity provider, and therefore its rates are set by the PSC.
Electric membership cooperatives (EMCs) and municipal electric utilities are not subject to PSC retail rate regulation.
EMCs are membership owned and therefore their rates are set by membership elected boards.
Municipal providers' rates are set locally.
Fact #3.
Georgia Power’s base rates are established through PSC proceedings involving public hearings, sworn testimony, review and recommendations by PSC staff, and a final vote by the commissioners.
Rates may increase or decrease, and certain charges are adjusted through separate proceedings.
A rate decrease was approved by the PSC on May 28, 2026.
Fact #4.
Electricity rates reflect numerous costs, including fuel, generation, transmission, environmental compliance, and infrastructure investments.
Some costs are recovered through separately identified charges on customers’ bills.
Fact #5.
The General Assembly establishes energy policy - not rates - through legislation.
As Chairman of the House Energy, Utilities and Telecommunications Committee, I work to support legislation that puts downward pressure on electricity rates and oppose measures that would increase recoverable costs or force some ratepayers to subsidize others; an example is legislation that would cause one set of ratepayers to subsidize another set by providing special pricing of electricity purchased or electricity sold back to the utility. home
