Skip to content Time For A Change Home April 4, 2024 Uncategorized Rights and Responsibilities Guns are an important part of a rural lifestyle, from hunting to predator control to possibly putting down an injured animal.
What they certainly are NOT is an all-purpose dispute resolution tool.
When we see guns pulled out during an argument over a parking spot, or a flea market purchase, for heaven’s sake, we are seeing symptoms of a very sick culture.
When someone can walk in and gun down dozens of total strangers for no sane reason, red warning signals are flashing.
The well-regulated militia of the Second Amendment harks back to the yeoman archers of medieval England.
Yeomen were required to maintain their own bows and supply of arrows (these are sized for the user, not standardized like modern weapons), and to practice under supervision every week, so that they were ready to be called up to war if the king needed them.
Our founders recognized the value of being able to call up citizen soldiers who were already adept with their weapons, and wrote that into the constitution.
They did NOT envision those citizens rebelling against the government, and a few early rebellions were put down quite firmly.
They also did not envision a country where toddlers frequently shoot their siblings or themselves because their parents didn’t have the COMMON SENSE to keep their guns secured.
I stand for the rights of responsible gun owners, and ONLY responsible gun owners.
There are no rights without responsibilities.
Not in nature, not in law.
The owner of a gun needs to be responsible for how it is used, just as the driver of a car is responsible if he causes injury to another.
If a toddler can get his mom’s loaded gun and shoot her or his siblings, that is not an accident.
That is negligence.
Would you give a toddler the car keys?
The legislature can and should keep guns out of the hands of those who cannot use them responsibly, the same as we take away the license of a serial drunk driver.
The needed change in mindset and culture, though, needs to come from the bottom up.
