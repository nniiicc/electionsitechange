But the biggest reasons for citizens opposing the data centers that power AI are the effects of the centers themselves.
POWER A data center in your town, or indeed anywhere in your state, will raise your power bill.
It’s the basic logic of supply and demand.
They will also strain the grid and make blackouts more likely.
They are projected to use between 6 and 12% of total US electricity demand in 2028.
But the use is not evenly distributed.
Data center electricity consumption isn’t evenly distributed.
Northern Virginia, the world’s largest data center market, has approximately 4,000 MW of capacity .
In Ireland, data centers now consume 22% of the country’s total electricity —a stark example of how concentrated development can strain local power grids. (link above) And they are driving the use of fossil fuel generation higher just when we desperately need to be cutting it back.
Utilities are having to buy gas-powered electricity or build it out , to meet demand.
The largest hyperscale data centers consume as much as 650 MW, the entire output of an average power plant, and enough to run a medium size city.
WATER It takes an awful lot of water to cool all those servers running flat out 24/7.
Large data centers can use five million gallons of water a day!
And the water they discharge is often badly polluted.
Even a center built with a closed loop system managed to release a dangerous load of bacteria when it flushed the system.
The closed systems also require more energy.
And most of the planned centers are to be built on land already in drought conditions.
Wells are running dry, rivers are in crisis, and we are giving this priceless resource to tech bros instead of human beings?
Citizens are being told to conserve water and let their gardens die, while the data center down the road just keeps guzzling water and turning out slop.
The overall water impact of AI is far larger than datacenters themselves, however.
A January study found that datacenters will be responsible for just 4% of the 30tn gallons of extra water that will be needed, globally, for AI expansion by the mid-point of this century.
Power generation and semiconductor fabrication for AI will suck up much more water than the datacenters themselves, the report states.
(Guardian link above) NOISE The servers themselves emit a loud steady humming tone.
Workers inside are exposed to as much as 96 decibels , enough to damage hearing.
Data center interior noise commonly reaches 80-96 dBA, requiring hearing protection for workers.
Exterior cooling equipment typically produces 55-100 dBA depending on the source.
Low-frequency hum from fans can remain noticeable up to 2.5 miles away in some cases.
Most community noise regulations set daytime limits of 50-60 dBA but often fail to capture low-frequency data center tones.
Noise barriers, acoustic louvers and slower large fans can reduce community exposure significantly.
If the center employs gas-fired generators to get its power, as many of Musk’s do, for instance, that adds a roar akin to a jet engine.
Neighbor noise complaints include health effects of the constant noise, disrupted sleep, stress, anxiety, headaches and inability to concentrate.
AIR POLLUTION Running gas generators to power a center puts industrial levels of pollutants into the surrounding air.
A center connected to the grid may still run diesel backup generators , which are particularly bad polluters.
This causes public health damages and lost productivity.
Being next to a gas-powered center is comparable to having a diesel truck in your house, for gas and particulates.
EMF FIELDS The EMF fields around the constantly running centers are reported to be disrupting bee colonies nearby.
Bees are sensitive to EMF fields even just under power lines.
Their navigation and behavior is affected.
What effects these fields have on humans is still hotly debated.
HEAT ISLANDS If you have to cool the servers, where does all that heat go?
Into the surrounding area, of course.
An average data center raises the temperature nearby about 2 degrees Celsius (about 3.5 degrees Fahrenheit).
A hyperscale center can raise it as much as 9º C (16º F).
The effect can be felt for up to six miles.
That is enough to push a summer heatwave from miserable to potentially deadly. all of which leads to: FALLING PROPERTY VALUES A mayor was reported to have told constituents complaining of the effects of the data center he had greenlighted, “just sell your house, then.” To whom?
