Half of all US renters pay more than the recommended maximum of 30% of their income, just for rent.
Half of that number (so, a quarter of all renters) pay more than 50% of income.
Just for rent.
This is not sustainable.
You wonder why there are so many homeless in America?
Look at housing affordability!
Solving this takes a whole array of actions and ideas.
It does little good to let developers put up acres of large luxurious homes that the people of the district cannot afford to buy.
We need a mix of housing types, for young people just starting out, for smaller families, for older people who need to downsize, and for midlife families who need a place to keep an eye on Granddad close by, while he can stay in his house.
This takes zoning changes, which are usually local, but the state could set some standards to guide the process.
It also does no good to build a lot of houses and then let corporations scoop them all up and turn them into rentals for large profits, denying home buyers any chance to have their offer considered.
Giant private equity firms own a large percentage of our houses now, and raise rents for maximum profit, with no consideration of how the community is affected.
This is something a legislature can act on, if they will.
There is a bill in the US senate to limit this.
