Skip to content Time For A Change Home April 4, 2024 Uncategorized Our Schools Idaho has had the worst funded schools in the US for years.
Combined with the legislature’s reluctance to spend any money on the public good, we also have the requirement for a supermajority (60%) to pass any local school bond.
This means that many small school districts have been unable to fund repairs or replacements for crumbling buildings.
In 2022 Reclaim Idaho got an initiative on the ballot to fund schools with a small tax on businesses.
The governor called the legislature back into SPECIAL SESSION for the express purpose of kneecapping the initiative if it passed (which it did).
That session made promises of better funding, without any specified funding stream.
Those promises were promptly broken.
In 2023 session, a change to the funding formula for schools resulted in them getting LESS, by about 25%, than they were promised.
In 2024 the shortfall approached a third.
It took an investigation by ProPublica, in partnership with the Idaho Statesman, making the shame of our schools go national, to get Governor Brad Little to urge the legislature to make funds available for school buildings.
So Mike Moyle wrote a bill.
A bill so intent on presenting itself as tax relief instead of school funding, that it fails to provide enough for any of the necessary repairs or replacement buildings in small school districts, and makes the inequality of urban and rural districts worse.
Look, it is just as expensive to maintain a school building for 100 students as it is for 200.
Not half as much.
Distributing funding for buildings per student is no good.
It should be distributed per building, and weighted by the age of the buildings in question.
A district that has just built a brand new school does not need a new roof to keep it from collapsing on the kids, or new plumbing so the kids can actually use the bathrooms in their schools.
