Newsweek, July 12, 2026 Not how it works The truth Senators don’t negotiate their own seats.

See the receipts Asked whether an independent who refuses to caucus can be effective, Seth Bodnar said he plans to negotiate for committee assignments the same way every other senator does.
That isn't how the Senate works, and the difference matters enormously for Montana.
Senators don't negotiate for committee seats individually.
Each party conference allocates its own slots, Democrats through their Steering Committee, Republicans through their Committee on Committees.
At the start of each Congress, the two leaders negotiate an organizing resolution setting the ratios, and the Senate ratifies the conference lists.
A senator's committee assignment comes through his conference.
There is no third door.
This is precisely why Bernie Sanders and Angus King caucus with Democrats.
Both won as independents.
Both caucus anyway, because that is the price of a committee seat.
The same was true of Joe Lieberman and of Harry Byrd Jr. before him.
No senator in modern history has served on committees without caucusing with a party.
So Mr.
Bodnar has two options.
He can caucus, abandoning the central promise of his campaign.
Or he can ask one party to surrender a seat from its own allocation as a favor, which is dependence dressed up as independence.
For Montana, this isn't abstract.
Montana has never had influence proportional to its population; it has had influence proportional to its committee seats.
Max Baucus chaired Senate Finance.
Jon Tester chaired Veterans' Affairs and sat on Appropriations.
Agriculture and Interior Appropriations are where farm policy, rural hospital funding, and public lands decisions are actually made.
A senator outside both conferences is a senator outside those rooms.
Mr.
Bodnar deserves a fair hearing on this.
He may believe a party would seat him voluntarily.
He may be right that in a narrowly divided chamber, a swing vote carries leverage.
But Montanans are entitled to hear the answer, not simply the assurance.
Which committees does he expect to serve on, and through which conference's allocation?
Every senator who has faced that question has answered it the same way, by joining a caucus.
