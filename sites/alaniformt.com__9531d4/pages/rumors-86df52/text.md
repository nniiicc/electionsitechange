So let’s take the rumors you may have heard, one at a time.
Ask yourself why.
It’s easy for people to believe a white male candidate was a Green Beret, with no scrutiny, no proof, and no questions about his mission or how long he held that role.
Seth Bodnar has released one DD-214, from when he left active duty in 2011. [1] Since then he served in the Army Reserve, which has no Green Berets, and then in a Montana National Guard headquarters assignment that the Guard itself called “not a highly deployable asset.” [2] [3] Almost no one has asked what his military role has been since.
His service is honorable, and that isn’t the point.
The point is the double standard: when a strong woman of color runs for office, suddenly she couldn’t possibly have had an impressive military and law-enforcement career.
Alani has published her service record in full: both DD-214s, her decoration citations, and letters from the people she worked alongside. [4] [5] [6] When Sen.
Tammy Duckworth’s war record came under attack in her 2016 race, she released years of her own medical records, with a doctor’s letter declaring her fit to serve. [7] The claim Her military record is “unverifiable.” It’s just her word.
The record Her DD-214s and decoration citations are posted in full at alaniformt.com/service . [4] [5] A DD-214 is issued by the military, not the service member. [8] Each citation carries an order number and the signature of the awarding officer, and her Meritorious Service Medal was signed by the general who now commands the entire Air Force Office of Special Investigations. [5] [9] The claim Other veterans’ DD-214s count.
Hers doesn’t.
The record A DD-214 is the military’s own record of service, whoever it belongs to. [8] Alani has posted both of hers, plus her citations. [4] [5] It’s the same document every veteran relies on, and it deserves the same standard.
The claim If she had nothing to hide, she’d just sign a FOIA request.
The record A public records request returns a short list of basics: dates of service, rank, assignments, and which awards someone was eligible for.
It doesn’t return the citations, evaluations or orders. [10] Alani has already published more than a records request would ever produce. [4] [5] The claim “I know people who were at the same base,” and they say she exaggerates.
The record Sharing a base tells you nothing about someone else’s job.
Soldiers in a different service and an unrelated unit have no window into a compartmented Air Force counterintelligence officer’s work.
And no one has ever said what, specifically, they contradicted.

The record She commanded an Air Force Office of Special Investigations unit in the Gulf and served in Iraq, and she described that work in a 2020 interview, years before she ran.
