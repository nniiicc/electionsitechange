For immediate release · Contact: press@alanibankheadforsenate.com Helena, Mont.
(Oct.
12, 2026) · Alani Bankhead, the Democratic nominee for U.S.
Senate in Montana, responded Monday to Shannon O’Brien’s resignation as chair of the Montana Democratic Party and her endorsement of independent Seth Bodnar, published by MS NOW .
“Shannon didn’t switch sides today.
She’s been working against the Democratic nominee since the summer,” Bankhead said.
“Montana Democrats chose their nominee in an open primary, and a party chair’s job is to stand with that choice.
This summer she went and got a legal opinion, without a vote of her own board, on whether the party could refuse to replace me if I quit.
Her own board members wanted her performance reviewed for it.
Now she’s using her exit to campaign for a man who never ran in our primary.” “She says I won’t take advice.
The only ‘advice’ the party’s old guard ever gave me was to quit.
That was a pressure campaign, not advice.
I spoke at our state convention.
I’ve kept showing up at county central committees and kitchen tables across Montana.
And Montanans are voting right now.” Her claims, and the record “She refuses to listen.” The only thing Bankhead refused was the demand to withdraw.
That was the advice, on the public record and behind closed doors.
Seventeen legislators signed a July 9 letter urging one of the two candidates out.
Twenty-two signed an Aug.
7 letter naming her .
A leaked “How to Help” document urged people to contact her family .
She spoke at the Montana Democratic Party’s state convention , stayed on the ballot, and kept campaigning with county Democrats across the state.
Working with the party, all year.
Bankhead reached out to O’Brien before she launched in December and joined the party’s monthly candidate calls.
She attended the party’s Mansfield-Metcalf dinner in March, signed on to the party’s voter file, submitted a platform plank and was a delegate at the state platform convention in June, and has held weekly coordinated-campaign calls with party staff since July.
On July 17 she wrote to the full executive board and agreed to its review of her service record.
She has met with county central committees from Havre and Park to Broadwater, Powell, Madison, Lake, Cascade and Fergus, and the Lewis & Clark and Gallatin County Democrats endorsed her in July.
What O’Brien did as chair.
On July 30 O’Brien convened the party’s executive board to discuss leaving the Democratic line empty .
She requested a legal opinion from the Elias Law Group on whether the party could be forced to name a replacement if Bankhead withdrew.
Board members had not voted to seek it.
She then shared the “private and confidential” document with county party leaders, and some board members called for a review of her performance as chair.
On Aug.
11, a week after the memo, the party’s executive director, Emily Marburger, stood with Bankhead at a Helena press conference: “What happened to Alani Bankhead, myself, my staff, our executive board was unacceptable.” “Why Republicans wanted her on the ballot.” More Jobs, Less Government, a super PAC aligned with Sen.
Steve Daines, spent $901,179 “supporting” Bankhead in the last two weeks of the primary while spending $962,124 against Bodnar.
Bankhead never asked for it, and on May 21 her campaign publicly disavowed and denounced dark money .
The same PAC spent $12.9 million against Jon Tester in 2024.
It also spent $1,005,876 against Reilly Neill, in ads Inside Elections’ Jacob Rubashkin described as “intended to boost Reilly Neill in a Democratic primary.” The biggest spender for Bankhead in the primary was Bodnar’s own side: Progressive Vet PAC put in $2.3 million , funded by Contours, the same dark-money source behind Bodnar’s super PAC, and its treasurer, former Democratic legislator Moffie Funk, co-hosted a fundraiser for Bodnar .
Their bet was that she’d win the nomination and then drop out for him.
She won, and she didn’t.
“Questions about abortion rights.” Bankhead is pro-choice.
She supports the Women’s Health Protection Act and a constitutional amendment protecting abortion rights , and she opposes any federal abortion ban.
She is endorsed by NOW PAC and the National Women’s Political Caucus , and she answered yes to all 13 of Montanans for Choice’s questions .
She also answered the ERA Coalition’s Equal Rights Amendment survey, and its Elect Equality guide lists her as a supporter.
Bodnar never responded; the guide lists him as “unknown.” Planned Parenthood Action Fund never sent her its questionnaire.
A member of its board called three times in the week before the Aug.
10 deadline , and the calls were about getting her out of the race.
“Her views on people in poverty.” The clip comes from a December 2025 money-mindset coaching episode that opens with clients who earned over $300,000 a year and were still broke.
It was about money habits, and it had nothing to do with Montanans struggling to get by.
In her words: “There’s a difference between having the money and not spending it well… and a system that chooses to oppress its individuals and exploit and extract from the Montanans that we serve.” Her plan for working families is at alaniformt.com/positions .
“Almost brand new to Montana.” Bankhead grew up an Army and embassy kid, then served 21 years in the Air Force .
Every home before Montana was chosen by the military.
Montana is the first place she chose.
She has lived in Montana almost five years.
Bodnar has lived here about eight.
“No momentum or resources.” Through Aug.
31, the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee had sent $0 to the Montana Democratic Party this cycle, compared with $2.8 million over the same period in 2024 .
That was the plan before a single primary vote was cast.
National Democrats had already decided to back an independent in Montana, so whoever won the Democratic primary was going to get no national support or resources this year.
The national party starved its own nominee, then cited the starvation.
“Mudslinging at Bodnar, for no apparent reason.” The reason was public.
After Montana Democrats chose her in the primary, Bankhead asked Bodnar to leave the race so the anti-Alme vote could unite behind the Democratic nominee.
It is the same argument the party’s old guard has spent four months making to her.
In September, Bodnar’s campaign spent two weeks attacking her faith and the church she attended as a teenager.
Ballots went out Oct.
9.
Election Day is Nov.
3.
