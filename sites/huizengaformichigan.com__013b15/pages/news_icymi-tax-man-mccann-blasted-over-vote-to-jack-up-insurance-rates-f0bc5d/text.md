The bill was referred last week for consideration by the Senate Committee on Finance, Insurance, and Consumer Protection as a whole with the recommendation that the bill pass.
McCann was among the five voting in favor, with three senators voting against advancing the bill.
“Today’s vote shows again exactly why people all over Michigan are calling him Tax Man McCann: Sean simply cannot stop himself from voting to make life more expensive for families,” Calvin Moore, Huizenga’s campaign spokesman, said in a statement.
“Families are already finding it hard to get by and yet McCann voted to jack up insurance rates for cars, homes and more by 21%,” Moore said.
“For families looking to drive down costs, the worst choice they can make is Sean McCann for Congress.” Opponents of the proposal have pointed to analyses warning that similar insurance “bad faith” legislation could increase costs across the state.
The Michigan Chamber of Commerce warned last year that Senate Bill 245 would encourage additional insurance litigation, destabilize Michigan’s insurance market, and increase premiums for policyholders.
According to the chamber, an independent analysis by Milliman of similar policies projected $2.4 billion to $4.7 billion in added annual insurance costs, equal to premium increases of 11% to 21% for Michigan policyholders.
The chamber also pointed to states, including Florida and Louisiana, where supporters of the business group said similar policies contributed to increased litigation and rising insurance costs.
The American Property Casualty Insurance Association similarly warned that expanding “bad faith” causes of action against insurers could increase costs for consumers and small businesses while creating incentives for additional litigation.
Supporters of Senate Bill 245 argue the legislation would provide consumers with additional tools to challenge insurers they believe have delayed payments, acted in bad faith, or improperly handled claims.
The bill would allow plaintiffs to recover several forms of damages, including amounts owed under an insurance policy, monetary losses caused by delayed payment, legal costs, attorney fees and 12% penalty interest for untimely payment.
In some cases, courts could also award damages for emotional distress or punitive damages.
Business and insurance groups have argued those provisions would encourage lawsuits, increase settlements and ultimately pass costs along to consumers through higher premiums.
Huizenga’s campaign has repeatedly sought to brand McCann as “Tax Man McCann,” pointing to votes and policies Republicans argue would make life more expensive for Michigan families.
McCann’s vote last week gives Huizenga’s campaign another opportunity to tie McCann to policies that Republicans argue increase costs for Michigan families already dealing with inflation, housing expenses, and rising utility bills.
