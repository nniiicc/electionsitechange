‘Tax Man McCann’ vows to repeal Trump tax cut for seniors, families: ‘We’ve got to reverse it’
The Midwesterner | Killian Bies | August 26
Democratic congressional nominee Sean McCann renewed his call to reverse President Donald Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill this week, a move Republican U.S.
Rep.
Bill Huizenga’s campaign says would eliminate tax breaks benefiting Michigan seniors and families.
McCann, D-Kalamazoo, made the comments during a town hall Tuesday alongside U.S.
Rep.
Hillary Scholten, D-Grand Rapids, and U.S.
Rep.
Jamie Raskin, D-Maryland.
“This one big ugly bill is costing everyone hugely, and we’ve got to reverse it,” McCann said.
McCann has repeatedly called for repealing the Trump-backed legislation as he campaigns against Huizenga in Michigan’s 4th Congressional District.
The law, signed by Trump in July 2025, permanently extended several provisions of the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act while adding new temporary deductions for seniors, tipped workers, overtime pay and interest on some auto loans
Among those provisions is a temporary $6,000 deduction for taxpayers age 65 and older through 2028.
The deduction begins phasing out at $75,000 in modified adjusted gross income for individual filers and $150,000 for married couples filing jointly.
For a senior receiving the full deduction and falling within the 22% federal income tax bracket, the deduction can reduce federal taxes by as much as $1,320, according to an analysis from FedTools.
The deduction is not specifically a Social Security tax exemption.
Up to 85% of Social Security benefits can still be subject to federal income taxes under existing law, while the new provision instead reduces taxable income for qualifying seniors.
Huizenga’s campaign seized on McCann’s latest emarks Wednesday, arguing that reversing the law would eliminate the deduction.
“Southwest Michigan seniors are struggling already, but ‘Tax Man’ McCann promised to make things even harder by taxing their Social Security benefits and robbing seniors of the benefits they earned,” Huizenga campaign spokesman Calvin Moore said.
“Raising taxes on working families and seniors while ripping $173 million from rural hospitals shows voters exactly why they can’t afford to ‘Tax Man’ Sean McCann in Congress,” Moore added.
The Tax Foundation estimates the law will reduce federal taxes for Michigan taxpayers by an average of $3,131 in 2026 compared with what they would have paid if the expiring provisions had been allowed to lapse.
Nationally, the organization estimates about 62% of tax filers would have faced higher taxes in 2026 without the legislation.
Huizenga’s campaign also pointed to $173.1 million in federal rural health funding awarded to Michigan through the Rural Health Transformation Program.
The Michigan Department of Health and Human Services announced the award in December.
The funding is part of a $50 billion national rural health initiative that will distribute $10 billion annually from 2026 through 2030.
Michigan plans to use its first-year allocation to strengthen rural hospitals and clinics, recruit health care workers, expand telehealth and improve coordination among rural providers.
Gov.
Gretchen Whitmer praised the funding when it was announced despite criticizing other health care provisions in the federal law.
“This $173 million grant will help us connect more Michiganders to the care they need and provide essential wraparound supports,” Whitmer said in December.
McCann did not specify whether he wants to eliminate the rural health program or every provision of the law, saying broadly that “we’ve got to reverse it.”
The comments continue a line of attack Huizenga has used against McCann throughout the campaign.
In June, Huizenga’s campaign launched TaxManMcCann.com, a website highlighting McCann’s voting record and policy positions on taxes, energy, insurance and other household costs.
The campaign has also criticized McCann over his previous pledge to repeal the One Big Beautiful Bill after the U.S.
Department of Energy finalized a $1.6 billion loan for DTE Energy that federal officials say could save Michigan natural gas customers about $700 million over the 30-year life of the financing.
Huizenga and McCann will face each other in November in Michigan’s 4th Congressional District, which stretches across much of Southwest Michigan, including portions of Allegan, Berrien, Calhoun, Kalamazoo, Ottawa and Van Buren counties.
