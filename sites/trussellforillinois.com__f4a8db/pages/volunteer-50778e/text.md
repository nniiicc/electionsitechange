Illinois State Representative candidates need # signatures to secure a place on the ballot.
I am a candidate supported by Illinois Policy.
They committed to getting 500, and I agreed to match that.
Generally, the goal is to get double the minimum required.
Tuesday, August 5 th, was the first day of the 90-day petition season.
I needed an easy sell to build my confidence, so I went to a friend’s house.
She happily signed and took me across the street to have a relative sign as well.
I was off to a good start.
I decided to start in my immediate neighborhood, and that first day out was tough.
Candidates were trained to use a data app that identified Republican homes.
The first address had a female’s name.
The gentleman ignored my request to speak to her.
He immediately wanted to know what my plans were to deal with “Orange Man”.
I explained that I was running for the State House; therefore, I wouldn’t have any influence on the federal level.
He slammed the door in my face.
I realized that walking to the houses identified on the app meant I was walking past many other houses.
I decided to knock on every door.
My success was about 80% if they opened the door.
It’s important to me that I make connections with Independents and Democrats.
I ordered “I Heart DuPage’’ buttons just for them.
When a person refused to sign, I gave them a button and said, “We may not agree politically, but we both love DuPage”.
If they accepted the button, I made a connection.
My schedule during the week meant being up and out the door by 5:30 am to head to one of the 3 Metra stations in my district.
My daily goal was to get at least 15 signatures a day.
After the train station, I worked the lunch rush at Boss Dawgz.
In the afternoon, I targeted entire subdivisions until dinner time.
The results were consistently the same; if they opened the door 80% of the time, they signed.
Eventually, I became comfortable with approaching commuters and knocking on doors.
I went from thinking 500 was a nearly impossible goal to exceeding my goal two weeks before the deadline.
With the help of precinct committeemen and other volunteers, I collected just over 1000 signatures, bringing the total to about 1,550.
I left each subdivision reflecting on how nice everyone was.
One of the longest, most endearing conversations I had was with a woman who kindly told me she wasn’t a conservative and declined to sign.
I smiled as I walked home thinking about the story of how she bought her house.
Petition season was quite the positive experience.
Now the hard work begins.
