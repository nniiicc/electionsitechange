Protecting the Quabbin from Further Exploitation Alongside my legislative colleagues, local officials, and Quabbin region residents, I testified before the Massachusetts Water Resources Commission in Belchertown to urge the Commission to reject the MWRA’s application to withdraw an additional 20 million gallons of water per day from the Quabbin Reservoir.
Our communities have sacrificed enormously to provide clean, reliable Quabbin drinking water to millions across the state, all while not receiving a single drop for themselves.
These stewards continue to host and protect this invaluable resource, and they deserve transparency and a meaningful voice in decisions about its future.
The MWRA is asking for an absorbent increase in additional withdrawals without identifying where the water would go, who would receive it, or what demonstrated need justifies the transfer.
We need and deserve the full picture before even considering this request.
Working together with my legislative partners, I will continue to stop this application while pursing stronger protections, fairer investment, and a greater voice for Quabbin communities through our legislative work.
There is still time to be heard.
You can continue to submit written comments to Vanessa.Curran@mass.gov until 5 p.m. on Wednesday, October 7.

Take Care, Aaron Saunders, Quabbin Stewards Testify to the MWRA It was my honor to testify in front of the Massachusetts Water Resource Authority Board of Director's July me eting.
Alongside a group of my legislative colleagues, local officials, and incredible residents, I argued in favor of a fair partnership between MWRA and the communities that stewa rd the state's drinking water.
Each day, The Quabbin Reservoir provides 180 million gallons of water to supply eastern Massachusetts.
At the same time, the communities surrounding the water supply face water bans, shortages, and dry wells.
This is a fundamental inequity, and it is time for wholesale change in how this land is appreciated and valued.
I am very proud of the attendance and attention to this meeting, reflecting the grit and passion of the Quabbin communities.
I will continue advocating for the Quabbin stewards until they receive thoughtful PILOT investments, a seat at the table, and a genuine partnership.

At a time when book challenges and censorship efforts are increasing across the country, Massachusetts is once again standing up for free expression, academic freedom, and the fundamental right to read.
I recently joined Governor Healey as she signed the "Freedom to Read" bill into law, keeping book bans out of schools and libraries.

Saunders Names Carol Hillman as 2026 Commonwealth Heroine Photo and some information accredited to The Daily Hampshire Gazette writer Domenic Poli, who recently published a story on Carol.
It was my absolute honor to nominate New Salem's Carol Hillman for the 2026 Commonwealth Heroine Award, celebrating her as she joins 147 Massachusetts Women who have made outstanding contributions to their communities.
Carol has achieved a lifetime of service and commitment to advancing childhood education and has more recently opened up her very own cider farm.
At nearly 100 years old, she continues to attract tourists to her orchards, where patrons are known to relax outside and enjoy the view of the Quabbin Reservoir.
Her farm has been an extension of her classroom and a manifestation of her passion for teaching, as she continues teaching guests the importance of growing and harvesting food.
Her cider is absolutely incredible, and I highly recommend everyone visit her farm!
Incredibly, Carol has recently received an honorary doctorate from Smith College, where she earned her bachelor's degree in 1949.
She has chosen for her extraordinary impact as an educator and environmental steward throughout her lifetime.

News from the State House The House has passed expansive legislation addressing primary care and healthcare affordability across the state.
It expands patient access to primary care, investments money into healthcare workforce retirement, lowers prescription drug prices, and ensures AI does not replace humans in critical decision-making processes.
At a time when many residents struggle to access and afford basic healthcare services, it is essential that the Legislature make sweeping investments and changes to the healthcare system.
The bill is now being reconciled with the Senate version by a Conference Committee.
I was proud to join my colleagues in passing legislation to strengthen dementia care in our Commonwealth.
This bill supports families and caregivers, expands workforce and first responder training, and enhances public health efforts to better address Alzheimer's disease and other forms of dementia.
The bill also establishes a statewide Director of Dementia Care and Coordination and expands MassHealth care access, grant opportunities, and overall care options for affected individuals.
I am proud to be part of a legislative body that is committed to improving the lives of individuals living with Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, as well as the families and caregivers supporting them each day.
The bill is now being reviewed by the Senate.
The House passed vital legislation to support the health and wellbeing of our state's firefighters.
The bill mandates access to cancer screenings for firefighters through their healthcare plans provided by their employer and eliminates all out-of-pocket costs for covered screenings.
It is essential that we protect those who risk their lives to protect us.
I was proud to join my colleagues in voting to expand access to these life-saving screenings, ensuring early detection and consistent access without financial barriers.
The bill is now being considered by the Senate.
Signed Into Law: Governor Healey has officially signed a comprehensive bill to strengthen the state's child welfare system into law.
This far-reaching legislation works to fill gaps in existing DCF, OCA, CRA, and public school systems to ensure that each child has the tools necessary for success.
It also establishes a "Foster Child Bill of Rights" to better protect children in foster care.
Every child, regardless of their born circumstances, deserves to grow up feeling safe and supported.
I am glad to see the Commonwealth providing more support for its most vulnerable residents.
Office Hour Update Dear Friends and Neighbors, One of my priorities as your State Representative has been making sure that residents throughout the 7th Hampden District have meaningful and reliable access to my office.
With that goal in mind, I want to share an update to how we will be conducting office hours across the district.
Since taking office, I have scheduled monthly office hours in each of the seven communities I represent.
While these have provided a useful opportunity for some residents to stop in and talk, attendance has varied considerably.
At the same time, the demands of the legislative calendar, committee responsibilities, and meetings on behalf of the district have occasionally required me to cancel or reschedule office hours on short notice.
Beginning this fall, my office will move to a new model designed to provide greater consistency while allowing me to provide the best representation for the entire district.
We will continue to hold regular office hours in each community, with members of my staff providing coverage each month.
I will personally hold office hours in each community at least once per quarter that will include a presentation of important and timely issues facing our communities.
This change does not reduce access to me or my office.
You never need to wait for scheduled office hours to ask a question, raise a concern, request assistance with a state agency, or share your views on an issue.
Phone calls and emails are always welcome, and my staff and I regularly schedule individual conversations and meetings with constituents when that is the most effective way to address an issue.
In fact, I hope this new approach will make our availability more dependable.
Having staff regularly available in each community will reduce the likelihood that office hours need to be cancelled because of an unexpected conflict in my schedule, while my quarterly office hours will continue to provide opportunities for residents to meet with me directly in their community.
All of my office hours are open to constituents throughout the district.
While it may be most convenient to attend in one's own community, everyone is welcome regardless of where the location happens to be.
Representing seven communities means balancing time at the State House with meetings, events, constituent services, and issues throughout a geographically large and diverse district.
My goal is to use that time where it can have the greatest impact while maintaining the accessibility that residents should expect from their representative.
As always, if there is something you would like to discuss, please reach out.
You do not need to wait for office hours.
My office is here to serve you, and I look forward to continuing to hear from residents throughout the district.

State House Student Government Day 2024 Many thanks to the students who participated in the State House's annual student government day!
Every year, a select number of high school students from across the Commonwealth travel to Boston for a day of experiential learning with the state legislature.
Students hear from members of the legislature and participate in both a mock committee hearing and formal session as either a State Representative or Senator.
It is important to foster civic knowledge and responsibility in each new generation.
The participating students have set a great example for their peers through their active interest and engagement with state government.
