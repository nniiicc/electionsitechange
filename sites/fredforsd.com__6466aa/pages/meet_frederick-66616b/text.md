👨👩👧 Background & Family
My father was a Holocaust survivor.
I learned lessons from him about the importance of life, liberty, and human dignity.
I still carry these lessons with me today.
As a boy, I joined the Scouts, eventually earning the rank of Eagle Scout.
At 14 I started my first job flipping burgers at Hardee’s and worked my way through eight years of college — janitor, factory line, grocery store clerk — whatever it took to pay the bills.
I studied pre-med, earned my bachelor’s degree in 1979, then went on to chiropractic school in St.
Paul, where I met my wife Kathleen.
We married in 1981, and after graduation in 1983, moved to rural Watertown.
Together we opened Deutsch Chiropractic and cared for our community for decades.
Along the way, we were blessed with four daughters and ten grandchildren.
Over the years, I built and ran three healthcare practices from scratch.
Those experiences taught me what every small business owner knows—how to stretch a dollar, meet payroll, and serve people with integrity.
They also shaped how I see public service: government should make life easier for those who work hard, not harder.
That same belief led me to get involved in the community—serving on the school board, volunteering, and eventually running for the legislature.
