Yet electricity costs continue to climb, broadband gaps still divide our rural areas, and too many roads, bridges, and rail lines are overdue for repair.
Smaller communities face another challenge: the high cost of building infrastructure that makes growth possible.
In many rural towns, even when there’s strong demand for housing, developers can’t afford to install new streets, water lines, and sewer systems without help.
The result is predictable—families are priced out, employers struggle to hire, and small towns lose their next generation. 🛠️Fred’s Plan Support a balanced energy mix — including biofuels, natural gas, hydro, and renewables — while keeping rates affordable.
Expand rural broadband so families, students, and businesses can compete in today’s economy.
Invest in critical infrastructure like roads, bridges, and railroads that keep agriculture and small towns moving.
Strengthen housing infrastructure through programs like Sen.
Crabtree’s SB41 , ensuring smaller towns can grow without raising local taxes.
Defend local control so counties and landowners have a real voice.
Protect taxpayers with transparent bidding, cost caps, and accountability.
Open to data centers, but growth on our terms, not theirs (see below). ✅ Progress So Far Voted for SB 41 (2023) — unlocking $200 million in state and federal funds to build water, sewer, and street infrastructure for new housing across South Dakota.
Those investments have already helped Milbank, Lake Norden, and Estelline begin housing developments that create jobs, expand the tax base, and keep families in our region.
Supported broadband expansion funding that brought high-speed internet to rural homes and farms (SB55, 2022) Backed upgrades in the Milbank-Sisseton Railroad to move ag products to market more efficiently (SB16, 2023) Supported funding for maintenance of roads and bridges so rural drivers stay safe and connected (SB124, 2024). → See the full record on Promises Made & Kept . 🖥️ Data Centers: Growth on Our Terms Deuel County dealt with this up close when Applied Digital proposed a large data center near Toronto and asked for a 50-year property tax exemption in exchange.
I said publicly that was the wrong deal — fifty years is longer than most of us have lived, and it would have left county government and local schools holding the bag for decades.
I still believe that.
The project has since been withdrawn.
That doesn't mean I'm against data centers.
It means I'm against bad deals.
Data centers are part of the country's infrastructure future, and South Dakota can benefit from hosting them, but only if the terms are fair.
I support attracting the right data centers to the right communities: ones that play by clear rules, create local jobs, sit down at the table with local leaders, address concerns honestly, and pay for the infrastructure they require.
What I won't support is a giveaway with no end date.
If I'm sent back to Pierre, I'll keep pushing for local siting control, real transparency, and developer-paid infrastructure costs on any future proposal. 💡 Why This Matters Energy and infrastructure aren't luxuries — they're the backbone of rural life and South Dakota's economy.
Every new street and utility line is an invitation for families to put down roots, for businesses to hire locally, and for schools to thrive.
