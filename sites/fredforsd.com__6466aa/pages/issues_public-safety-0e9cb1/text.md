Meet Fred Promises Kept Issues Photos Volunteer Vote Contribute Home ❭ Issues ❭ 👮Safe Communities 👮Safe Communities 🚨 The Problem Meth and fentanyl are tearing through small towns and families.
Counterfeit pills made to look like real prescriptions, some laced with fentanyl, are reaching our young people, and opioid deaths in South Dakota went up last year.
Law enforcement is stretched thin, courts are overburdened, and too many repeat offenders cycle right back through the system. 🛠️ Fred’s Plan Back law enforcement with training, resources, and tools to fight crime and keep officers safe.
Crack down on illegal drugs with tougher penalties for traffickers and greater support for local task forces.
Teach kids the facts about opioids.
Make sure every public school student, at least once between 6th and 12th grade, learns how addiction develops, how to recognize and respond to an overdose, where to find treatment, and why counterfeit pills are so dangerous.
Expand access to treatment and rehabilitation so those trapped in addiction have a chance to rebuild their lives.
Strengthen community partnerships between sheriffs, counties, and state agencies to coordinate efforts against drugs and repeat crime.
Hold repeat offenders accountable with policies that protect victims and stop the revolving door of crime.
Support a safe, modern prison with cost caps and oversight to protect taxpayers.
Set clear statewide rules for license plate readers so data is used only for active criminal investigations, deleted on a firm deadline, and protected with real privacy accountability. ✅Progress So Far Publicly supported the new prison proposal in Sioux Falls with contract caps and accountability to control costs.
Voted to increase state correctional capacity and reduce overcrowding.
Backed legislation to impose stronger penalties on repeat violent offenders . 💡Why This Matters Public safety is government’s first responsibility.
When law enforcement has the tools to crack down on drugs, when violent criminals are held accountable, and when addicts can access treatment, families are safer and communities stronger.
A modern prison protects citizens, keeps staff safe, and saves money long term.
Prevention matters too.
State health officials found that in more than 60% of overdose deaths, someone had a chance to step in and save a life.
When a student knows what an overdose looks like and what to do, a friend can live.
License plate readers have helped solve real crimes, including finding a 9-year-old girl abducted in Clark County in 2023.
But South Dakota has no state law on how that data is used, how long it’s kept, or who can see it.
I’ll work with Attorney General Jackley to set real limits: legitimate investigations only, a deletion deadline, a warrant for anything beyond a short window, and consequences if the rules are broken.
Public safety and privacy aren’t opposites.
