Sorry so long!
Basically, I'm a YES on all four.
Amendment I is about Medicaid expansion .
In 2022, South Dakotans voted to expand it, on the promise that Washington pays 90% and we pay 10%.
This amendment changes nothing as long as Washington keeps that promise.
But if Washington breaks it, the state currently has no choice but to cover the difference itself, and every 10% Washington doesn't pay costs us roughly $36 million a year.
Amendment I simply hands that decision to the legislature instead of locking the state into paying no matter what.
It does nothing unless Washington changes the deal.
I support Amendment I.
Amendment J would write into our constitution that only U.S. citizens can vote in South Dakota , in any election, state or local.
That's been the rule since statehood, but it currently rests on statute rather than the constitution itself.
This puts it in the constitution.
I support Amendment J.
Amendment K changes how the state handles unclaimed property , things like old bank accounts, uncashed checks, and forgotten insurance payouts that revert to the state after three years.
Right now that money, roughly $60-65 million this year, just gets spent as ordinary revenue.
Amendment K puts it into a trust fund instead, so the state lives off the interest, the principal stays intact, and the rightful owner can still claim it anytime.
That's the fiscally responsible way to handle other people's money, IMO.
I support Amendment K.
Amendment L raises the threshold to amend our constitution from a simple 50%-plus-one majority to 60% .
Our constitution isn't supposed to shift with every election cycle like an ordinary law, it's the foundational document that sets the rules for everything else.
A bare majority means a well-funded campaign, often bankrolled by out-of-state interests, can rewrite it with barely half of voters showing up in favor.
That's a low bar for something this permanent.
60% doesn't stop the constitution from changing, it just makes sure it reflects real consensus among South Dakotans, not whoever spent the most on ads that year.
