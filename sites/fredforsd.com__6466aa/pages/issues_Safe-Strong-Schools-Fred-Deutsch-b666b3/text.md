🛠 The Problem
Education in South Dakota faces mounting challenges, especially in rural communities.
State testing shows fewer than half of students meet proficiency standards in reading or math, and the gaps are widest in small districts with limited staff and resources.
Many rural schools struggle to hire and retain qualified teachers, forcing educators to cover multiple subjects and grades or causing schools to simply eliminate the class.
Safety adds more strain.
While serious incidents remain rare, rural schools face unique vulnerabilities—long law enforcement response times, aging buildings, and limited mental health support.
Together, these pressures create a system where too many students graduate from high school without the skills or confidence to thrive in college, tech school, or today’s economy.
🛠️ Fred’s Plan
- Raise academic standards.
Focus on mastery of core subjects rather than seat time so every graduate is ready for tech school, college, or the workforce.
- Protect parental rights.
Keep parents informed and involved in what’s taught in classrooms.
- Keep schools free from ideology.
Stop the spread of political or social indoctrination that divides students or undermines family values.
- Strengthen school safety.
Expand school safety programs, strengthen threat-assessment teams, and ensure mental-health resources serve both students and staff.
- Invest in excellence.
Reward innovation and transparency in education, especially in rural schools willing to lead on mastery-based learning.
✅ Progress So Far
I actively supported and advocated for stronger, safer schools and higher academic standards across South Dakota:
- Supported funding for school safety grants and resource officer training across the state, SB 34 (2024).
- Encouraged schools to form local threat-assessment teams that identify risks early.
- Supported discussions on a legislative study for education reform focused on teacher-led innovation and mastery learning.
- Continued advocating for transparency and accountability in both safety planning and student performance.
💡 Why It Matters
Parents deserve to feel confident their children are safe at school and focused on learning.
South Dakota must strengthen academics to better prepare children for post-secondary studies, the workforce, and life.
