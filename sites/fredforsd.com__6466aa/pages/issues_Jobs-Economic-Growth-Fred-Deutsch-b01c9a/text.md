📈Jobs & Growing South Dakota
South Dakota should be a great place to build — for the farmer, the small-town shop, and the next big idea.
Growth is good when it lifts people up.
But too often, big projects show up with promises and leave taxpayers holding the bag.
I’m for growth that plays by the rules.
Growth that respects the people who already live here.
Growth that makes life better for South Dakotans — not harder.
🚨The Problem
Lately, some companies have called South Dakota a “tough place to do business.” Others say we’ve handed out too many breaks to too few players.
Both have a point.
When the rules aren’t clear, or when government cuts special deals behind closed doors, trust breaks down.
Our state shouldn't be known as a "tough place to do business" but as a "fair place to do business" - a place that is trustworthy and transparent for all parties.
🛠️Fred’s Plan
New jobs and economic growth should make life better for the people who already live here.
Incentives should reward projects that build lasting value.
Any major project seeking public support must:
- Respect Property Rights – No strong-arming landowners.
Eminent domain is for public use, not private gain.
- Provide Local Jobs & Paychecks – Projects should create good quality, steady jobs for South Dakotans, not short-term promises for outsiders.
- Be Honest with Taxpayers – If a company takes public money, the public deserves to see what we’re getting in return.
- Focus on Community First – Local people deserve a voice before big projects move in.
Decisions should be made with communities, not over them.
These standards strike a balance between opportunity and accountability.
They ensure South Dakota remains open for business while protecting taxpayers, ratepayers, and natural resources that make our state strong.

💡Why this Matters
South Dakota needs to be a place where growth is welcome and accountable -- where good businesses want to come because the rules are clear, the taxes are fair, and the people are respected.
The right projects—think dairies, data centers, advanced ag—strengthen our tax base, generate good jobs, and keep young people here at home.
That means more resources for local schools, better infrastructure, and breathing room on property taxes.
Responsible development is how South Dakota doesn’t merely survive—it thrives.
