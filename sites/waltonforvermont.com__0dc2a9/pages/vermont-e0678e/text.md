04 Draft campaign content Climate, Energy & Resilience Prepare Vermont for the world that exists.
Build practical resilience while keeping energy, housing, transportation, and heat affordable.
Climate change is real, but slogans do not lower a heating bill, strengthen a bridge, protect a farm, or rebuild a town after a flood.
Vermont needs an approach grounded in resilience, affordability, and local control.
That means stronger infrastructure, healthier forests and watersheds, reliable energy, serious flood preparation, and federal support that reaches communities without burying them in mandates and administrative costs.
The transition to cleaner energy must improve life in Vermont—not make housing, transportation, construction, and heat unaffordable for the people already struggling to remain here.
Draft principles Protect Vermont communities from flooding and extreme weather.
Invest in roads, bridges, drainage, utilities, forests, and watersheds.
Expand reliable and affordable lower-carbon energy.
Support weatherization and efficiency without impossible compliance costs.
Preserve consumer choice during practical energy transitions.
Help farms, towns, homeowners, landlords, and small businesses adapt.
Demand measurable Vermont results from federal climate spending.
Reject symbolic rules that impose large local costs for negligible benefit.
Treat energy reliability and affordability as essential public interests.
Protect Vermont land without turning rural communities into museum exhibits.
Vermont should lead through competence, resilience, and practical results—not expensive political theater.
