Your trust.
Your vote.
A mandate to build.
A campaign usually begins by asking people for money.
This one begins by asking a more important question: who should have the authority to represent Vermont?
For too long, Vermont has handed that authority to the national political parties almost automatically.
When our political mandate is given away without conditions, Vermont loses its leverage.
We ask little of the parties, and too often receive little in return.
I am asking Vermonters to make that decision deliberately again.
Not to give me authority over Vermont, but to entrust me with a mandate from Vermont: to represent this state independently, to challenge federal power when necessary, and to begin building the ideas and institutions Vermont will need next.
Your vote is not a party inheritance.
It is Vermont’s authority to give.

05 The independent argument Vermont should not give away its political authority for free.
The major parties ask Vermont for loyalty before they offer Vermont leverage.
An independent representative changes the negotiation.
Vermont’s support can no longer be assumed, inherited or taken for granted.
It must be earned.
Independence does not mean refusing to work with Democrats or Republicans.
It means working with either when it benefits Vermont—and belonging to neither when it does not.
Vermont is not a branch office of a national party.
Vermont is Vermont.

06 Campaign finance THE WALTON STANDARD Campaign finance · The Walton Standard We would rather have your participation than your money.
Imagine a political system in which a qualified person could stand for office, speak to the public and earn a mandate without first assembling an investor class.
That is not the system we have today.
So Walton for Vermont has adopted a voluntary contribution standard designed to keep fundraising small, local and finite.
