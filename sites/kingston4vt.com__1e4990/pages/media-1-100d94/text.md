There are many reasons for this, but it starts at the provider level.
Just under half of all healthcare spending takes place at our hospitals that have some of the highest administrative costs in the country.
Since Medicaid and Medicare charges are capped, these high costs are shifted onto private payers which in turn results in the high cost for health insurance for individual and business plans.
As your hopeful state representative in Montpelier, I would support legislation that does the following: · Fund access to primary and preventive care initiatives.
Doing this well is simply the right thing to do to support good health and to also start bending the cost curve of healthcare down. · Fund incentives for more primary care physicians to stay and relocate to Vermont. · Fund incentive programs that encourage the use of primary care services and healthy lifestyles.
We can pay for this by further empowering the Green Mountain Care Board with proactive oversight to reduce the administrative costs burdening Vermont hospitals and rate payers.
We must also use the Federal funding from the Rural Health Transformation Program (“RHTP” $195 Million grant for each of the next 5 years) to support these initiatives.
Our challenges with healthcare will require realistic solutions.
Promising additional services without any means to support and fund those services does not help Vermont.
Our healthcare providers need to be held accountable to high standards of service provided in an efficient and cost-effective manner.
The primary oversight for that is our Green Mountain Care Board, which in turn needs to be held accountable as our key regulatory body to oversee healthcare services.
Healthcare is a complicated issue, and I would welcome discussing it with you.
Follow-up to this post: Since this post we have seen some recent developments around healthcare in Vermont.
Much of this is positive and moving us broadly in the right direction.
But there is much work left to be done: · UVMMC, under the new leadership of Dr.
Stephen Leffler, is demonstrating a commitment to the Green Mountain Care Board to work more transparently and collaboratively.
This includes a recent commitment by UVMMC to cut $100 million per year over the next three years, largely in administrative costs.
I will support legislation that provides the necessary oversight of healthcare providers and insurers to both ensure fiscal responsibility and quality service. · The RHTP is a generational opportunity.
A grant of almost $1 billion over the next five years.
I will support legislation that assures that the Vermont Department of Health uses this funding to provide ready access to primary and preventative care for all Vermonters.
One of the primary objectives of this funding should be to attract and retain primary care physicians.
These funds should NOT be used to fund operating losses at Vermont hospitals. · The Larner College of Medicine at the UVMMC is working closely with the Green Mountain Care Board to provide more transparency around the fiscal structure at this school of medicine.
This is a wonderful Vermont institution and increased fiscal transparency will benefit the school, hospital, students and patients.
There is much work left to do to address the healthcare challenges in our state, but there is also positive momentum on a number of fronts.
We must continue moving in the direction of helping Vermonters be healthier with access to quality primary care; and by addressing the cost of healthcare with fiscal discipline and transparency.
I will support legislation that continues those efforts.
Thank you.

6440) Hello again, my name is John Kingston and I will be running as an Independent to be your State Representative here in Hinesburg (Chittenden District 4).
Over the course of the next several months leading to the general election on November 3rd (I am running as an Independent and therefore will not be in a primary) I will be posting on issues which I believe are central to the challenges facing our community and state.
Maybe no other issue has received more recent attention than school funding and its impact on property taxes.
This is a significant burden for many Vermonters both young and old.
Our present school funding structure is largely attributable to Act 60 passed in 1997 which established a single statewide education fund with the goal of providing equitable funding across all of Vermont.
This law has been modified over the years and is very complex and not well understood.
I would argue that the complexity of the current structure has led to a lack of transparency and accountability resulting in the sharp rise in education costs (approximately 40% since 2021) while student counts have been in steady decline.
Today Vermont spends over $30,000 per student placing it among the most costly states in the country to educate its K-12 students.
As a result, communities are now burdened with some of the highest property taxes in the country.
This is not sustainable and over the last two legislative sessions school property taxes have been "bought down" in each year by more than $100 million by reallocating funds from the state's general and road funds.
Yet again, this is not sustainable.
Maybe even more concerning is that standardized test scores measuring student performance have fallen over the last decade.
Where Vermont was once a national leader in education outcomes, today our standardized test scores (NAEP) rank near the middle in performance nationally for 4th and 8th grade math, while reading scores rank better.
So, what do we do?
I believe the enactment of Act 73 (to be implemented in 2029), while not a perfect or complete solution, can move Vermont in the right direction.
The foundation formula contemplated in that act should bring more accountability for both academic performance and fiscal responsibility to the leadership of the school districts.
It's clear at this point that the political will to execute on school consolidation is not present in the Vermont legislature.
Maybe it's a better approach to have the school districts, who are much closer to their communities, tackle this.
As your hopeful State Representative in Montpelier, I would look to promote legislation that enacts a foundation formula and follow-on legislation to Act 73 as might be needed that accomplishes the following: • First and foremost, that every child in the state receives a quality education.
Equal educational opportunity for all children. • Establishing a foundation formula that stems the cost growth presently experienced and reduces cost increases to the rate of inflation or below. • Consider legislation that would address the inefficiencies in Vermont's educational cost structure.
Today, Vermont has the highest ratio of staff and teachers (per 100 students) in the country. • Increase decision making and accountability for both academic and fiscal performance to the school districts.
I grew up in a household of modest financial means where education was always emphasized in importance.
It was the catalyst for rewarding work and a rich life.
We must address our challenges with education in Vermont so that our students and educators have a sustainable education system.
The opportunity awaiting our children depends on it.

I think it is important to keep in mind that the primary driver of the recent increase in property taxes was associated with school taxes.
