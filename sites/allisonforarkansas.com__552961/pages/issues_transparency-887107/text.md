Skip to content About Issues Contact About Issues Contact Donate Transparency You have a right to know how the government is spending our tax dollars, funding their own political campaigns, and using the power entrusted to them.
Protect Freedom of Information Act laws Arkansas has long been a state that prioritizes government transparency and the power of the people to access government information.
Governor Sanders called a Special Session in 2023 to roll back the Freedom of Information laws that protect this right.
My opponent voted ‘yes’ for this assault on transparency , restricting your right to know how your tax dollars are spent.
I will always stand for openness and transparency from our government.
Restore direct democracy and the people’s ability to change laws In Arkansas, The People Rule, and it’s time our lawmakers act like it.
Our constitution protects your right to bring initiatives and referendums to a vote of the people.
My opponent sponsored the bill making it harder for Arkansans to put issues on the ballot , taking power out of your hands and increasing his own power as a legislator.
I am committed to protecting your rights to bring issues to a vote and remembering that in Arkansas, The People Rule.
Commit to holding regular in-district town halls Elected officials have a responsibility to listen to the people they represent.
Regular in-district town halls should be expected of lawmakers asking for your vote every few years.
My opponent has not held one town hall or open constituent meeting since being elected nearly four years ago .
This is not only an abdication of his responsibility as a legislator, it is a slap in the face to the people he represents.
If given the honor of representing Northern Pulaski County, I will hold regular town halls, the first of which will happen within one month of the adjournment of the 2027 Legislative Session.
Hold Big Tech and AI Accountable The people of Arkansas need bold leadership against the rapid, reckless development of data centers and artificial intelligence use that threatens our jobs, pocketbooks, and privacy.
I support a statewide moratorium on new data center development for two reasons: (1) allowing everyday Arkansans to meaningfully engage in decisions that affect them the most, and (2) providing time and space for transparency throughout the development process.
So far, the process and decision making has been largely hidden from the people.
A moratorium will slow development until and unless We The People have our concerns heard.
What’s more, data technology is allowing corporations to use your personal information to drive up prices to the highest you will pay.
This is corporate greed disguised as regular market adjustments, and working families are feeling it with every dollar they add to our grocery bills.
Other states have passed laws regulating this unfair pricing practice , and it’s time for Arkansas to do the same.
