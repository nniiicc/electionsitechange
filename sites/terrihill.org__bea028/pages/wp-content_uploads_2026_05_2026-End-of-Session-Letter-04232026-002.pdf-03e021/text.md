BUDGET We are constitutionally obligated to pass a balanced budget annually, and the $70.8 billion FY 2027 budget, closes a projected structural deficit through spending restraint and targeted adjustments, without raising broad-based taxes or fees on Maryland families and with bipartisan support.
It preserves more than $2.4 billion in the Revenue Stabilization Account (Rainy Day Fund) and maintains a general fund surplus.
Budgets reflect values.
Ours reinforces the Speaker’s stated commitment to affordability, accountability, and opportunity in her first session leading the House.
In partnership with the Senate, the FY27 budget carries forward on promised investments in K-12 education with $10.2 billion in funding the Blueprint for Maryland's Future half-way into its 10-year implementation, expands access to behavioral health and community programs, and protects local governments from cost shifts.
Marylanders are looking for relief from the economic punches we’ve taken recently, especially over the last year from inflation, tariffs, devastating federal jobs and program cuts, and fuel price increases at the pump on top of the higher energy costs we’re all experiencing.
This budget includes • $384 million in low-income rental assistance, and $42 million specifically to help renters in rural communities. • $1.7 billion into the SNAP program, providing emergency food aid to 687,000 Marylanders. • $14 billion to gap support 1.47 million low-income participants in Medicaid and the Maryland Children’s Health Program in the wake of federal cuts. • $20 million in assisted living subsidies and congregate living support for 4,000 senior citizens.
It delivers significant ratepayer relief by • applying $100 million from the Strategic Energy Investment Fund to cover costs of the EmPOWER program; • allocating $73 million to help Marylanders switch to heat pumps, which improve energy efficiency and save money; • giving an additional $37 million to partially offset ratepayer costs in the Limited Income Program; • including more than $300 million for the Office of Home Energy Programs; and • allotting $82 million for Maryland Energy Assistance Program; State and local mandate relief and examples of nonbudgetary legislation that support the budgetary actions and the goal of improving the economic environment include advancing affordable housing and transit-oriented development through the Maryland Transit & Housing Opportunity Act, lowering energy costs for families through the Utility RELIEF (Reducing Energy Load Inflation for Everyday Families) Act), and safeguarding consumers from predatory grocery pricing.
These actions enhance the financial well-being of Maryland families and reflect balanced, strategic planning for economic resilience and growth, especially considering growing federal uncertainty, to better secure the State's longterm future.

SPONSORED BILLS ENACTED DURING 2026 SESSION HB0593/SB482 - Criminal Law - Interference With Critical Infrastructure or a Public Safety Answering Point Strengthens cybersecurity protections by expanding existing law to prohibit unauthorized access to computers with the intent to interrupt or impair “critical infrastructure” and classifying such violations as a felony punishable by up to five years in prison and/or a $25,000 fine.
The bill also creates a new felony offense for unauthorized access that results in denied access, interruption, or impairment of critical infrastructure or a Public Safety Answering Point (i.e., 9-1-1 system) with penalties of up to 10 years imprisonment and/or a $50,000 maximum fine.
HB 1249 - Certified Recovery Residences - Refusing Services to Individuals Receiving Medication - Assisted Treated - Prohibition Prohibits, as a condition of certification, recovery residences from denying housing to individuals receiving prescribed medication-assisted treatment (MAT) for opioid use disorder or otherwise requiring they reduce, change, or discontinue medically prescribed treatment.
HB1300 - African American Heritage Preservation Program and Maryland Commission for Women Renaming and Alterations Renames the African American Heritage Preservation Program for Speaker Emeritus Adrienne Jones, and updates the duties of the Commission to reflect the current status and evolving priorities and needs of Maryland women, emphasizing research, education, advocacy, and collaboration with local government, local commissions and aligned community organization, and removes requirements making the Executive Director and instead establishing the job as a merit-based, skilled service employee in the State Personnel Management System answerable to the Commission. * HB701/SB523 - Department of Natural Resources - Water Resources - Identification of Vernal Pools (Jack Cover Vernal Pool Wetlands Protection Act of 2026) Requires the Department of Natural Resources (DNR), in coordination with the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) to create and maintain a statewide list of non-tidal seasonal, temporary wetlands that are necessary ecosystems for breeding, egg-laying, and larval development of obligate species needed for completion of their lifecycles in fish-free wetlands, and for DNR to train MDE staff in identifying a potential vernal pool while performing their wetlands protection duties.
MDE staff will notify DNR of potential pools for inclusion on this list. *HB892-HB893/SB233 - Business Regulation – Cemeteries and Abandoned Cemeteries – Sale, Transfer, or Government Acquisition and Disposition Restructures Maryland law governing the sale, transfer, and government acquisition of cemeteries, strengthening protections for historical burial grounds connected to African- American communities, rural families, faith communities, and veterans.
The bill mandates public notice and community engagement prior to a cemetery sale or transfer and gives defined parties of interest meaningful opportunity to preserve and steward these sacred sites and to challenge a proposed sale.
The bill gives the state, county and local jurisdictions authority to assume ownership of an abandoned cemetery for the purpose of ensuring upkeep and maintenance while facilitating, with notification to the descendants families, and to transfer to a entity that will assume care and maintenance responsibility or, failing to finding such an entity, see to the reinternment of the remains to an operational cemetery. *Denotes: Passed via the Senate crossfile (emboldened) SPONSORED HOUSE PASSED BILLS - NOT PASSED BY SENATE HB599 Health - Licensure of Hospitals - Ownership Requirements Required Maryland hospital to be non-profit entities under both federal and state law and that ownership of licensed hospitals may only be transferred to nonprofit entities.
The House vote was 96-37.
HB633 - Health Insurance - Ovarian Cancer Prevention With Salpingectomy - Required Coverage 4 Would have required certain insurance carriers to cover without a copayment, coinsurance, or deductible on the coverage, elective opportunistic salpingectomy - removal of the fallopian tubes - under certain situations as a proven preventive for ovarian cancer prevention.
Population based data shows the procedure can reduce the risk by as much as 80% of ovarian cancer, the fifth leading cause of cancer death among women and for which there is no effective screening.
Cost-sharing prohibitions would not apply to high-deductible health plans until the annual deductible was met.
The House passed it 126-8.
HB915 - Workgroup on Aging Out of Foster Care This bill would have created a workgroup of relevant agencies, advocates, and 15 young adults ages 18-25 with a foster care lived experience to examine the challenges faced in aging out of the foster care system that contribute to the higher rates of homelessness, financial insecurity, and behavioral health and somatic health challenges seen in this population as compared to non-fostered aged-matched peers, the effectiveness of existing resources and supports, and what additional or alternative programs and supports of proven effectiveness Maryland could provide to break the cycle.
A stipend would have been provided for the previously fostered adults workgroup participants.
Passed the House 136-1.
HB931 Health Care Facilities - Nursing Homes- Medical Director Requirements Would have required medical directors in nursing homes to hold a valid Medical Director Certification or be making active progress toward obtaining certification and, by 2030, that only physicians with certification be employed as Medical Directors in nursing homes in the state.
Would have required nursing homes to post on their website its medical director's name, credentials, and certification status.
Nationally, facilities whose medical directors have such certification demonstrate measurable improvements in residents’ health and safety outcomes and quality of life.
It passed the House 133-0.
HB1625 Health - Newborn Screening Program - Fees and Core Conditions Would have raised the fee cap the Maryland Department of Health can set for newborn screening.
It also would have adjusted the responsibilities of the State Advisory Council on Hereditary and Congenital Disorders, given MDH the authority to delay or forgo certain screenings based on the council's advice, and modified related reporting requirements.
The House vote was 121-14.
HB598 Physicians- Licensing - Internationally Trained Physicians and Licensed Physicians Residing in Other Jurisdictions Would have created a licensure pathway, without repeating residency training, for qualified and experienced physicians trained and licensed in foreign countries to help address our enlarging physician shortage crises without compromising care quality or standards.
Passed House 99-34.
HB1263 Public Health - Workgroup on Postoperative Cosmetic Care Would have created a Workgroup on Postoperative Cosmetic Care to study the pervasiveness and nature of unregulated postoperative cosmetic and other body-altering services offered by unlicensed practitioners and whether they present risks to patient safety warranting regulation of some form.
House vote 98-36.

SCHOLARSHIPS Each year, members of the General Assembly are allocated funds to support the awarding of Delegate or Senatorial scholarships to Maryland residents pursuing post-secondary education at a two- or four-year Maryland college, university, or private career school.
Applications for The Delegate Terri Hill Scholarship are accepted in late winter - early spring, with awards determined by summer based on a demonstrated combination of scholarship, accomplishments, need, commitment to service, and grit.
