A short history of LD29 Since 2000, when voters approved Proposition 106, Arizona's legislative maps have been drawn by the five-member Arizona Independent Redistricting Commission instead of the Legislature.
The commission redraws all 30 districts after every census.
Before 2022, the number 29 belonged to a very different place.
From 2013 through 2022, Legislative District 29 covered west Phoenix, including much of Maryvale, and part of Glendale.
It was represented in the Senate by Martín Quezada (2015–2023) and in the House by lawmakers including Richard Andrade and César Chávez.
After the 2020 Census, the commission redrew the map.
The new lines, finalized in early 2022, moved LD29 to the fast-growing northwest West Valley.
The 2022 election was the first under the new district, and Janae Shamp won the Senate seat.
In 2024, Eric Stafford ran as the Democratic nominee and earned 48,608 votes (40.7%).
He is running again in 2026.

How long is an Arizona State Senate term?
Two years.
Each of Arizona's 30 legislative districts elects one state senator and two state representatives every two years, and lawmakers are limited to four consecutive terms in the same chamber.
Who draws LD29's boundaries?
The Arizona Independent Redistricting Commission, a five-member commission created by voters through Proposition 106 in 2000.
It redraws legislative maps after each census.
The current LD29 was first used in the 2022 election.
