Read the plan → All initiatives The American Undersea Compact: A 30-Year Guarantee for Rhode Island Labor Aaron Guckian • October 07, 2026 “When an adversary looks across the ocean, they don’t care about political friction.
They care about the steel we put in the water.” Washington argues endlessly over labor statutes, project labor agreements, and regulatory frameworks.
I’m not going to pretend that friction doesn’t exist.
But here is what I know.
Rhode Island’s trade unions carry one of the most critical national security missions in the country.
Our goal is not to weaken the skilled trades that build this nation.
It’s to give your members the longest, most lucrative, and most protected industrial pipeline in modern American history.
That’s the Shipyard Compact: industrial capacity, wage security, and American production , built to last for decades, not budget cycles.
Three Commitments to Rhode Island Workers A Maritime Industrial Accord We will recognize the private trade unions at Quonset Point and our regional yards as essential national security co-producers, with multi-year contract stability.
What it means: IBEW, Boilermakers, and Machinists members get stability, and the Navy gets uninterrupted production.
A Dual-Use Maritime Guarantee We will turn port and dockworker investments at Quonset and Providence into dual-use deepwater logistics hubs, staging commercial shipbuilding, sealift vessels, and naval maintenance.
What it means: permanent maritime logistics jobs instead of grant cycles that come and go.
A Technical Career Escalator at NUWC Newport Modernization at the Naval Undersea Warfare Center should cut red tape, not people.
We will protect specialized engineering and technical trade bands with enhanced retention pay.
What it means: modernization built on better pay and career growth, not attrition.
Pillar 1 · Economic Certainty The 30-Year Order Book Commercial construction rises and falls with interest rates and real estate.
Defense shipbuilding doesn’t.
Two Virginia -class attack submarines a year and continuous production of the Columbia -class fleet mean federal work that runs through the 2040s.
That’s certainty a family can plan around.
“Other administrations offer short-term stimulus programs that vanish with the next budget cycle.
We are offering a 30-year industrial moat.
As long as American submarines patrol the seas, Rhode Island union halls will not see an empty bench.” Pillar 2 · Protecting Trade Value Domestic Content Sovereignty Global procurement undercuts American wages by importing cheaper modular assemblies and foreign-forged steel.
Strict Buy American enforcement and defense tariffs will keep subcomponents, casting, precision milling, and piping right here at home, where they belong.
“Tariffs and procurement rules are the shield around your contract.
Every valve, pipe, and plate that goes into an Ocean State hull will be cast, shaped, and inspected by American hands at American prevailing wages.” Pillar 3 · Direct Pipeline Funding Union-Led Apprenticeship Capital Our submarine yards face real attrition and a shortage of certified welders and machinists.
Instead of routing workforce dollars through top-down bureaucracies, we will send Submarine Industrial Base funding directly into joint union-management training centers, the Westerly Education Center, and community college trade pathways.
The masters already know how to train the next generation.
Our job is to fund the tools, the facilities, and the stipends.
“We won’t tell you how to train a welder.
You have the masters and the apprentices.
We will write the checks for the tooling, the facilities, and the stipends so you can bring the next generation into the hall debt-free.” Pillar 4 · Honoring the Trades Dignity of the Hard Hat Too many working Rhode Islanders feel that leadership values office credentials over the industrial trades.
We will raise the economic and cultural standing of heavy manufacturing.
Rhode Island’s machinists aren’t a line-item cost.
They are national defenders.
“For forty years, Washington told your kids to go into debt for four-year degrees while our industrial heartland was stripped for scrap.
We are putting capital back where value actually exists: on the shop floor, under the welding hood, and on the waterfront.” Our First Steps A joint roundtable at Quonset with the Metal Trades Council, IBEW, Ironworkers, General Dynamics leadership, and Navy acquisition chiefs to lay out the 10-year production backlog.
Direct equipment grants for local trade union training centers, funding AI-powered robotic welding simulators and 5-axis CNC training rigs.
A Naval Systems Integration Advanced Center , a public-private partnership bringing underwater data centers, drones, robotics, and submarine technology to Rhode Island to protect America’s ports.
A Local Supply-Chain Guarantee , working with the Rhode Island Manufacturers Association and machine shop stewards so Tier 2 and Tier 3 defense contractors here get procurement preference.
Job transition protections , developed with the Department of Labor and contractors, to protect workers and their families through market volatility.
“We have adversaries ramping up their fleets at speeds this country hasn’t faced since 1941.
We cannot meet that threat with white papers or corporate consultants.
We can only meet it with you: the men and women of Rhode Island who can lay a weld that holds under thousands of feet of ocean pressure.” “Let Washington fight over politics.

We cannot defend it with an unreliable energy grid.
And we cannot defend it with paper promises written in Washington.” Rhode Island sits at the center of American sovereignty.
It’s time we started treating it that way.
This plan is about making sure the work of defending this country is done here, by Rhode Islanders, with American materials and American energy.
Five commitments will get us there.
1.
Maritime Supremacy, Built in Narragansett Bay Through a public-private partnership, we will launch the Naval Systems Integration Advanced Center alongside NVIDIA.
Rhode Island will engineer the underwater edge data centers, robotics, and autonomous drone systems that shield our East Coast ports and vital sea lanes from adversary attacks.
If anyone challenges American waters, the technology that stops them cold will have been developed right here.
2.
Ending Foreign Dependence on Critical Materials From rare-earth magnets to domestic iron and naval-grade steel, every critical alloy entering Rhode Island’s defense pipeline will be sourced, cast, and finished domestically.
Period.
We are insulating our shipyards and local machine shops from foreign leverage once and for all.
3.
A Reliable Grid for a Manufacturing State You can’t forge high-yield steel or mill precision components while worrying about blackouts.
That’s why we are backing natural gas pipeline redundancy , securing reliable baseload power for our industrial parks and protecting working families from brutal winter utility spikes.
4.
Defense Dollars Stay in Rhode Island Working hand-in-glove with the Rhode Island Manufacturers Association, we will establish a strict Local Supply-Chain Guarantee .
When Navy procurement dollars are spent, Rhode Island machine shops, precision fabricators, and local trade stewards get first call.
Rhode Island’s small and mid-sized machine shops are the backbone of the defense supply chain.
The work stays on your floor.
5.
Protecting the Worker Contracts shift.
Lines retool.
But your family shouldn’t pay the price for market volatility.
In partnership with the Department of Labor and our prime defense contractors, we will back wage-continuity protections and dedicated unemployment shields for workers in transition.
When production lines change, your household income stays protected.
“This isn’t theory.
This is complete industrial sovereignty.
Reliable domestic energy.
American raw materials.
Cutting-edge subsea AI defending our coast.
