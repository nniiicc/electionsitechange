Focused on the issues that matter to District 57
- Lower Costs for Floridians In the last four years, the Legislature has passed laws that have raised costs for families while reducing consumer protections:
- SB 2‑A (2022) — reshaping the property insurance market by limiting homeowners’ ability to challenge insurers and reducing accountability
- SB 7052 (2023) — further limiting litigation options for consumers
- HB 1417 (2023) — blocking local tenant protections, including notice requirements and anti‑price‑gouging measures
- SB 102 “Live Local Act” (2023) — overriding local zoning while failing to ensure affordability for working families
- Utility and energy policies that allow rate increases without meaningful consumer input
- Transportation and toll policies that increase costs for commuters without expanding affordable alternatives
These decisions have contributed to skyrocketing insurance premiums, rising rents, higher utility bills, and increased financial pressure on seniors and working families across Pinellas County.
I oppose policies that raise costs, favor special interests, or shift financial burdens onto everyday Floridians.
I will support and/or sponsor bills that directly lower costs for families, including: Property Insurance Reform
- Require property insurance rate caps
- Provide statewide tax credits for roof, window, and wind mitigation upgrades for owner-occupied as well as rental properties
- Provide roof replacement, window, and wind mitigation grants for landlords to help reduce pass-through costs for renters
- Allow an independent roof inspection if the insurer requires a new roof prior to property insurance renewal to be paid by the insurance company if the roof meets underwriting standards
- Restore the ability of homeowners to challenge unfair insurance practices
- Require insurers to justify rate increases with transparent data
- Create a state‑backed reinsurance program that lowers premiums
- Crack down on insurers that delay or deny legitimate claims
Housing Affordability
- Create state incentives for small landlords (ex. having 5 or less properties) for verifiably lowering or stabilizing rent including lowering or freezing rents for at least 12 months and capping yearly increases, e.g.
- Providing damage reimbursement, vacancy coverage, and mediation support
- Providing landlord offset credits for landlords who commit to rent stabilization
- Providing temporary tax credits tied directly to rent relief
- Providing rehab and safety grants for repair for participating landlords
- Protect renters from algorithm-driven price inflation that lets large landlords raise rent by collaborating together and squeeze out working families
- Allow more attainable dwelling units (ADUs) subject to septic tank requirements, site plan reviews, and local rules.
These would be for smaller single-family layouts, an additional small home on a large family lot, and attached home options like townhomes, duplexes, and triplexes
- Provide incentives to cities for increasing housing supply
- Provide timelines to speed up review processes
- Restore local authority to enact tenant protections such as notice requirements, anti‑price‑gouging measures, and fair‑chance housing policies
- Require developers receiving state incentives to include truly affordable units
- Expand down‑payment assistance for first‑time homebuyers
- Increase funding for workforce housing near jobs, transit, and schools
Utility and Energy Costs
- Require utility bill caps
- Strengthen oversight of utility companies to prevent unnecessary rate hikes
- Expand energy‑efficiency programs that reduce monthly bills
- Support community solar programs that lower costs for renters and homeowners
Grocery Store Sales Tax

Transportation
- Expand affordable public transit options including investing in a fleet of smaller, more fuel-efficient buses for non-rush hours and for use in neighborhoods where people who live too far to walk to a bus stop can get to a larger bus stop
- Give employers with large numbers of mass-transit reliant employees subsidies and/or tax credits for purchasing buses to shuttle their own employe
Healthcare and Medicaid
- Protect seniors from rising prescription and healthcare costs through state‑level consumer protections
- Accept Medicaid expansion dollars which will lead to:
- Hundreds of thousands of working Floridians receiving affordable health coverage
- Strengthening rural hospitals and reducing medical debt across the state
- Bringing billions of federal dollars home
- Lowers insurance premiums

- Oppose Government Overreach into Private Lives In recent years, the Legislature has passed laws that restrict reproductive freedom, target LGBTQ+ families, and insert the state into private medical decisions.
These include:
- HB 5 (2022) and SB 300 (2023) — restricting abortion access and imposing government control over private medical decisions.
- The “Don’t Say Gay or Trans” expansions (HB 1557 in 2022; HB 1069 in 2023) — limiting how families, teachers, and counselors can support LGBTQ+ students.
- The Stop WOKE Act (HB 7, 2022) — restricting how educators and employers can discuss race, gender, and history.
- HB 1069 (2023) — expanding book challenges and limiting how teachers can address identity.
The Legislature has also weakened community safety by expanding gun access without training or local oversight:
- HB 543 (2023) — creating permit‑less concealed carry with no training requirement.
- Long‑standing firearm preemption laws — preventing cities and counties from adopting local gun safety ordinances tailored to community needs.
These policies remove tools that law enforcement and local leaders once used to reduce gun violence and protect families.
I oppose government intrusion into personal healthcare, family decisions, community safety, and private life.
I will support and/or sponsor bills that return reproductive freedom, safeguard LGBTQ+ families, and ensure personal medical decisions remain between individuals and their doctors — not politicians.
I will also support and/or sponsor common‑sense gun safety legislation, including restoring local authority to enact gun safety ordinances, requiring training and background checks for concealed carry, and giving communities the ability to adopt measures that keep residents safe.
- Defend Voting Rights and Local Election Control Over the last four years, Florida has passed laws that restrict voting access and reduce local control:
- SB 90 (2021) — restricting vote‑by‑mail, limiting drop boxes, and adding new ID requirements
- SB 524 (2022) — creating the Office of Election Crimes and Security and increasing voter roll purges
- SB 7050 (2023) — further restricting vote‑by‑mail, limiting third‑party voter registration groups, and shifting authority away from county supervisors of elections
These laws have made it harder for seniors, working families, and people with disabilities to vote — especially in counties like Pinellas, where vote‑by‑mail participation is among the highest in the state.
I oppose barriers that make it harder for Floridians to vote and efforts that strip local election officials of control.
I will support and/or sponsor bills that:
- Restore secure drop boxes
- Expand vote‑by‑mail access
- Restore common sense voter ID including student ID cards
- Prevent improper voter purges
- Protect third‑party voter registration groups
- Return election administration authority to local communities
- Support Public Schools, Libraries, and Community Services State laws passed in the last four years have reshaped Florida’s education system:
- Stop WOKE Act (HB 7, 2022) — restricting instruction on race, gender, and history
- HB 1467 (2022) — requiring book reviews and enabling mass book removals
- HB 1069 (2023) — expanding censorship rules, restricting pronoun use, and accelerating book challenges
- HB 1 (2023) — creating universal vouchers and diverting public funds to private and religious schools without accountability or disability accommodations
- SB 266 (2023) — eliminating equity programs in higher education and restricting academic freedom
In Pinellas County, these mandates have forced schools to remove books within five days of a challenge, cover classroom libraries, rewrite curriculum, and eliminate supports that help students and educators feel safe and respected.
Meanwhile, the rapid expansion of vouchers and publicly funded private programs redirects money away from neighborhood public schools — the only schools legally required to serve every child.
I oppose classroom censorship, book bans, political interference in libraries, attacks on public education, and policies that siphon public funds away from public schools.
I will support and/or sponsor bills that:
- Eliminate book bans and protect libraries from political pressure
- Invest in strong public schools that serve every child
- Require charter schools and public schools to be regulated the same way
- Restore programs that promote fairness and inclusion
- Require transparency and accountability for any program using taxpayer dollars including home school programs
- Return decision‑making about classrooms and community programs to local hands
- Increase teacher pay
- Safeguard Our Coastline and Environment Recent state actions have weakened environmental protections:
- CS/SB 492 (2025) – allowing developers to buy wetland mitigation credits outside the watershed when local credits are not available breaking the long-standing rule that environmental impact must be offset in the same basin as where the damage occurs
- SB 540 (2023) — allowing developers to sue local governments for protecting wetlands or modifying development proposals
- State‑level wetland permitting rollbacks (2020–2024) — reducing local authority to protect sensitive areas
- Underfunding of coastal resilience programs despite rising storm intensity
- Policies allowing development in vulnerable coastal and wetland areas
These decisions threaten Brooker Creek Preserve, the Anclote River watershed, and coastal communities across Pinellas County which are one of the most flood-prone, built-out counties in Florida.
I oppose rollbacks of environmental safeguards and policies that put short‑term interests ahead of long‑term environmental health.
I will support and/or sponsor bills that:
- Keep mitigation within the same watershed
- Expand or create local or regional mitigation options so developers have credits without exporting restoration elsewhere
- Strengthen coastal resilience and flood mitigation protections
- Protect clean water and reduce pollution
- Preserve wetlands and wildlife habitats
- Prioritize stormwater improvements
- Restore local authority to protect environmentally sensitive areas
- Restore Community Decision-Making The Legislature has repeatedly overridden local governments in Pinellas County — taking away the ability of residents, city councils, school boards, librarians, and election officials to make decisions that fit our communities.
State mandates have:
- Overridden local zoning rules through the Live Local Act (SB 102), forcing cities like St.
Petersburg, Largo, and Clearwater to approve high‑density developments even when they conflict with local height limits, traffic plans, or neighborhood protections.
This same loss of local control could happen in our district as well, leaving neighborhoods with little say over development that affects traffic, safety, and quality of life.
- Stripped schools and libraries of authority through HB 1467 and HB 1069, requiring Pinellas County Schools to remove books within five days of a challenge and restricting what teachers can teach or say.
- Reduced local election control through SB 90, SB 524, and SB 7050, limiting drop box hours, imposing new vote‑by‑mail restrictions, and shifting authority from the Pinellas Supervisor of Elections to the state.
- Blocked local responses to housing and business needs through HB 1417 and HB 403, preventing cities from enacting tenant protections, regulating short‑term rentals, or setting local business rules.
- Weakened local environmental protections through SB 540, which allows developers to sue cities and counties for denying or modifying development proposals — discouraging local leaders from protecting wetlands, coastal areas, and neighborhood character.
I oppose state preemption that removes decision‑making power from local communities and prevents Pinellas residents from shaping the future of their own neighborhoods, schools, libraries, and elections.
I will support and/or sponsor bills that restore local authority so residents — not distant politicians — decide how our communities grow, how our schools operate, how our elections are run, and how we protect the character and quality of life that make Pinellas County home.
- Regulate Data Centers Florida is promoting large data center development and offering major tax incentives, but these deals often lack transparency and shift long‑term costs onto residents.I oppose policies that hide true costs, build expensive utility capacity without verified demand, allow utilities to charge themselves inflated internal prices, and leave loopholes that prevent independent review of affiliate transactions.
These incentives mainly benefit large corporations while Floridians face higher base rates, reduced public revenue, and limited consumer representation in rate cases.I support a moratorium on new data centers including pausing new incentives and fast‑track approvals until an independent study evaluates the real costs and benefits.
Florida should require full transparency in contracts, independent auditing of affiliate pricing, verification of actual demand before construction, stronger funding for the Office of Public Counsel, and performance‑based incentives tied to job creation, renewable energy use, and local hiring.Developers should also commit to community benefit agreements that support infrastructure, workforce training, and environmental mitigation.
Florida can welcome innovation, but not at the expense of taxpayers, public services, or our environment.
- Protect Worker Choice, Stability, and Fairness Florida’s 2026 legislation imposed new hurdles on public‑sector unions — raising membership thresholds, increasing recertification requirements, limiting payroll deduction, and expanding state oversight.
It also added a financial urgency provision, allowing employers to declare a financial emergency and reopen contracts, even when the “emergency” is not supported by independent financial data.I oppose these measures because they weaken the voices of teachers, caregivers, and frontline workers who already face low pay, high turnover, and demanding conditions.
The financial urgency clause is especially harmful: it creates instability, undermines negotiated agreements, and gives political actors a tool to bypass worker protections.As someone who has led assisted living facilities, Head Start centers, and home‑health education programs, I’ve seen firsthand how stable, supported workers create safer environments and stronger communities.
I will work to restore fairness, protect worker choice, and ensure that contracts cannot be undone by manufactured crises or political pressure.
Florida’s workers deserve stability — and Florida’s families depend on it.
- Support Our Veterans Florida veterans face unique environmental challenges tied to the state’s climate, geography, and history of military activity.
These issues affect veterans’ health, housing stability, disaster recovery, and long‑term well‑being, especially older and disabled veterans.
Issues include water quality; hurricane, storm damage and disaster recovery; mold, mildew and indoor air quality; burn pit-related respiratory issues; red tide and algae blooms; and extreme heat which can worsen service-connected conditions.
I support:
- Expanding veterans’ healthcare access with more clinicians, extended clinic hours, and satellite mental‑health sites.
- Creating a Florida Veterans Dental Program for cleanings, dentures, implants, and emergency dental care.
- Increasing capacity in state veterans’ nursing homes and accelerating construction of new facilities.
- Establishing a Florida Veterans Emergency Relief Fund for rent, utilities, hurricane recovery, and crisis support.
- Building a Veterans Benefits Navigation Corps with accredited advisors to prevent scams and help with claims.
- Launching a Rural Veterans Access Initiative with mobile clinics, telehealth hubs, and transportation vouchers.
- Creating a Veterans Workforce & Apprenticeship Program with paid training and guaranteed job placement.
- Funding a statewide Mental Health First Aid training for all veteran‑serving organizations and first responders
