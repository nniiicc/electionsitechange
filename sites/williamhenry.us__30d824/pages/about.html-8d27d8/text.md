Through the Food First Initiative (FFI) , Henry leads research and scientific inquiry into the nutritional composition and potential applications of whole-plant hemp ( Cannabis sativa L. ) as a human food and animal feed resource.
His work centers on the nutritional characterization of federally compliant hemp biomass—including flowers, leaves, and seeds—an area of research that remains substantially underrepresented in publicly available nutritional data.
Henry informed and urged scientists at USDA Food Data Central about the ability and need to study whole hemp biomass.
The USDA FDC refused to add whole hemp biomass to their list of priorities, and refused to conduct any further studies regarding hemp.
Henry filed a FIOA for the USDA's Food Data Central's lab standard operational procedures and reasons why they internally decides not to add hemp as a priority.
Hemp has been legal federally since 2018.
In 2025, FFI conducted its first Hemp Basic Nutritional Study, “Preliminary Nutritional Characterization of Organic Whole Hemp Biomass ( Cannabis sativa L. )” , establishing preliminary compositional data for minimally processed fresh and dried hemp biomass, both with and without seeds.
Independent laboratory analyses by AGQ Labs and Eurofins Scientific characterized macronutrients, dietary fiber, minerals, sugars, fatty acids, and amino acids using standardized analytical methodologies.
The study, covered nationally here , detected protein, dietary fiber, multiple amino acids—including seven essential amino acids—minerals, and fatty acids across the tested biomass groups, while demonstrating meaningful differences in nutrient concentration between fresh and dried material and between seeded and seedless biomass.
The hemp study was a breakthrough look at the whole plant nutrition.
Building on these findings, Henry is expanding FFI's research into more comprehensive amino acid and fatty acid profiles, vitamins and minerals, antioxidants, flavonoids, other naturally occurring phytonutrients, and measures of nutritional quality and bioavailability.
Through independent laboratory testing, nutritional research, and collaboration with scientific professionals, his work seeks to address gaps in the scientific literature, establish foundational compositional data for whole-plant hemp, and support the development of evidence-based standards for its potential inclusion in human food systems, dietary applications, and livestock nutrition.
His research advances the scientific understanding of hemp as a versatile agricultural crop and investigates the nutritional significance of the whole plant beyond its conventionally studied applications.
