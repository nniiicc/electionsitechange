October 13, 2026 Three out of four Republicans said no to Chuck Gray.
Say it again in November.
By William P.
Schwartz Mr.
Schwartz, a third-generation Wyoming lawyer raised in Casper and now living in Jackson, representing voters challenging the constitutionality of Wyoming’s “crossover voting ban.” He is a registered Republican opposed to the Wyoming Freedom Caucus.
In August, Chuck Gray won the Republican nomination for Congress with only 25 percent of the vote.
Roughly 3 of every 4 Wyoming Republicans who voted chose someone else; not one of the eight Republicans he defeated has publicly endorsed him.
These Republicans all had good reason to reject Gray.
I’m asking them, and others, to trust that judgment again in November.
Gray’s partisan posturing for higher office offends most Wyomingites, but it is his performance as Wyoming’s chief election officer that should disqualify him from becoming our sole representative in the U.S.
House.
Though voter fraud is virtually non-existent in Wyoming, his proudest achievement as Secretary of State has been working against our most sacred right as citizens: the right to vote in free and open elections, unfettered by needless government conditions.
He has made it harder for Wyoming citizens to vote.
Echoing unsupported claims of voter fraud from right-wing extremists, Gray pushed one “election integrity” law after another, now making ours among the most restrictive election codes in the nation.
Thanks to Gray, we are now one of only six states requiring voters not only to show photo identification but to prove their citizenship with specific paperwork many don’t have.
He is equally proud of leading the effort to take away our historic freedom to change our party affiliation up to election day so we could vote in the primary of our choice.
None of this fixed a real problem.
Wyoming elections have always been clean and well run.
The effect of Gray’s laws is simple: fewer eligible Wyomingites voting with less opportunity to vote for the candidates we prefer, or don’t.
He handed our private information to Washington.
Unlike the Republican Secretaries of State in surrounding states, Gray was the first in the nation to turn over the voter records of roughly 280,000 Wyoming citizens to the federal Department of Justice, including our driver’s license numbers.
Our election code forbids public officials from disclosing that confidential information.
No court ordered Gray to do it, and twenty-six federal courts have since rejected the same federal demand.
Fremont County Clerk Julie Freese, a Republican who has run elections there for three decades, pointed out that state law bars clerks from giving out anyone’s private information, yet Gray handed it to the feds without so much as a written agreement, and without telling a single clerk.
The clerks and Wyoming voters were angry, she said, and she doesn’t blame them.
Wyoming people guard their privacy and are rightly suspicious of federal overreach.
Yet our own election chief opened the file cabinet to the feds anyway.
He turned a blind eye to federal agents intimidating our election officials.
On primary day, Justice Department agents made a surprise appearance at Laramie County polling places, approaching poll workers and saying they intended to interview voters.
Gray’s office had advance notice but kept it to itself, catching Laramie County Clerk Debra Lee completely off guard.
“This was a poorly managed exercise and one that showed complete disregard for our state laws and administration of our elections,” Lee said.
Republican Gov.
Mark Gordon called the federal conduct “aggressive” and asked for an investigation.
A chief election officer’s first duty is to protect the people who run our elections and the voters who use them.
Gray did neither.
Gray’s diminishment of our sacred voting rights while seeking to elevate himself to higher office through our votes should not be acceptable to any Wyoming person.
What should we ABC (“anyone but Chuck”) voters do?
If we split our votes among several candidates as we did in the primary, Gray wins with a minority again.
So we need to unite behind one candidate who can actually beat him.
That candidate is Lisa Kinney.
Yes, Lisa is a Democrat.
But she is also a person with great courage to stand by her party even as its national brand has become so tarnished in Wyoming.
She is also a Wyoming native, a longtime Wyoming lawyer, librarian and educator, and a ten-year veteran of the state legislator who learned to work across the aisle.
Former Republican colleagues Rex Arney, Mike Massey and Patty Macmillan have publicly endorsed her, as has former Gov.
Mike Sullivan.
She is a commonsense centrist, not an ideologue, and she does not march in lockstep with her national party, particularly its “Democratic Socialist” faction.
She will win the votes of virtually all Wyoming Democrats.
If independents and common-sense Republicans join them, she can win.
Crossing party lines for the right person is a Wyoming tradition, not a betrayal.
Not so long ago, our Republican-dominated state elected Democrats Ed Herschler, Mike Sullivan and Dave Freudenthal as governor for a combined 24 years.
We judged the person, not the party label, and Wyoming was well served.
One-party control of every office has made Wyoming government more extreme and less accountable.
Centrist candidates of both parties do best in a real two-party system that rewards competence over loyalty.
We need that back.
Most Wyoming Republicans already said no to Chuck Gray and his Freedom Caucus compatriots.
This November, let’s finish the job.
Don’t reward Chuck.
Vote for Lisa Kinney.
Your Republican friends won’t even need to know.

Lisa Kinney, Candidate for the US House of Representatives September 17, 2026 Why Can’t Drones be Built in Wyo?
Kinney proposes exploring federal partnerships to make Wyoming a center for American drone research, testing and manufacturing WYOMING — Lisa Kinney today called for exploring a Wyoming economic-development and national-security initiative aimed at making the state a center for American unmanned-aircraft research, testing and manufacturing.
“Instead of asking only what industries want to come to Wyoming, we should ask what industries Wyoming can build,” Kinney said.
Modern conflicts have demonstrated the growing importance of relatively inexpensive unmanned aircraft systems and the challenges created when costly defense systems must be used to counter them.
Kinney said the United States should apply those lessons by strengthening domestic research, manufacturing and supply chains for unmanned aircraft technologies.
Wyoming should compete to be part of it.
Kinney proposes examining federal partnerships that could bring together Wyoming businesses, entrepreneurs, researchers, the University of Wyoming and Wyoming community colleges to develop a domestic drone-manufacturing ecosystem.
Wyoming’s wide-open spaces could offer opportunities for appropriate research and testing, while a growing unmanned-aircraft industry could support jobs in engineering, electronics, software, advanced manufacturing, fabrication, maintenance and supply-chain operations.
“I want Wyoming students to be able to graduate, find a good-paying technology or manufacturing job here and raise their families here,” Kinney said.
Kinney said Wyoming should pursue industries that complement the state’s existing strengths while creating opportunities beyond its traditional economic sectors.
That means connecting education, research, entrepreneurship and manufacturing.
“We have talented young people.
We have wide-open spaces.
We have a strong tradition of building, fixing and solving problems,” Kinney said.
“Those are Wyoming advantages.” A QUESTION OF NATIONAL PRIORITIES Kinney said the proposal is also part of a broader conversation Congress should have about federal spending and national priorities.
The federal government faces major decisions about how taxpayer dollars should be invested.
Traditional defense systems can cost billions of dollars, while rapidly evolving technologies are changing the economics of national defense.
“This isn’t a choice between national defense and Wyoming,” Kinney said.
“The question is how America gets the greatest long-term return from the dollars taxpayers entrust to us.” Kinney said federal investment in domestic unmanned-aircraft research and manufacturing could advance several national objectives at the same time: strengthening national security, rebuilding American manufacturing capacity, expanding domestic supply chains and creating skilled jobs.
“America should build more of the technology America needs,” Kinney said.
“There is no reason Wyoming cannot help build it.” “Wyoming can help feed America.
Wyoming can help defend America.
And Wyoming can build things America needs.” Wyoming innovation.
Wyoming manufacturing.
Wyoming jobs.

Lisa Kinney was born and raised in Laramie and received her undergraduate degree from the University of Wyoming, her master’s degree from the University of Oregon and her law degree from the University of Wyoming.
She was elected to the Wyoming State Senate from Laramie and served for ten years from 1985-1995 where she served as minority floor leader from 1993-1994.
Park County is comprised of more public land than any other county in the state and public lands remain an issue at the forefront of our readers’ minds, although opinions differ on how they should be managed.
How would you exercise your position as a federal officeholder to steward Wyoming’s public lands?
Public lands are our heritage, our livelihood and our responsibility.
This is perhaps the most important issue as many of our current congressional reps are pushing bills to remove or transfer lands.
I will engage with local communities to streamline processes to open and protect access to OUR public lands in a process already in place – not by selling or transferring the land.
We must work with current landowners reasonably and do everything we can to protect our national forests and parks.
We must maintain our right to give input.
How can Wyoming’s federal officeholders address the cost of living and housing in the state?
I feel that removing tariffs and increasing international trade will be the first step to lowering the cost of living for our citizens.
Taxing the wealthy is another way to improve services to everyone while lowering taxes for those making less than $100,000.
Housing costs are so high and I will work to improve access for first-time home-buyers as well as renters.
A number of hospitals and care centers in Wyoming face financial stress, tight budget margins, and struggle with insurers.
How, if at all, can the federal government support our rural healthcare providers?
I want to bring back the ACA subsidies and then work toward Universal Healthcare for All.
Every other country in the world provides healthcare for its citizens and it is unbelievable that the US cannot do the same.
The health insurance industry is a mess and I will do everything I can to clean it up.
The Big Beautiful Bill gave rural areas 5 years of help and then terminated the program.
I would continue that very necessary contribution.
What is an issue that is unique to Wyoming that you would work to address if elected (e.g. mental health, energy dominance, etc)?
Describe how you would work to achieve progress on this topic.
Wyoming is in a crucial time with the cost of everything being so high.
Healthcare, food and groceries as well as housing are rising every day and people’s wages are not keeping up.
I want to make a difference in Washington by working for a better future for our citizens, not just in Wyoming, but across the country.
I have written four books about the legal rights of the mentally ill.
Money for these programs have been continually cut since 1977.
I will work to bring back funding as well as expanding programs for everyone.
What experiences, traits, or actions distinguish you from your fellow candidates?
The Constitution, the rule of law, and due process have been ignored by the current administration, in my opinion.
I want to strengthen our rights under the Constitution by reaffirming the Voting Rights Act, putting some teeth in Civil Rights laws, getting rid of all violent ICE activities, honoring equal rights laws and taking other steps to put us on a solid future for our kids and grandkids.
Thank you for your support!
