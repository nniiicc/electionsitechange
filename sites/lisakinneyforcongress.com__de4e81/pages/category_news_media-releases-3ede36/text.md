Kinney proposes exploring federal partnerships to make Wyoming a center for American drone research, testing and manufacturing WYOMING — Lisa Kinney today called for exploring a Wyoming economic-development and national-security initiative aimed at making the state a center for American unmanned-aircraft research, testing and manufacturing.
“Instead of asking only what industries want to come to Wyoming, we should ask what industries Wyoming can build,” Kinney said.
Modern conflicts have demonstrated the growing importance of relatively inexpensive unmanned aircraft systems and the challenges created when costly defense systems must be used to counter them.
Kinney said the United States should apply those lessons by strengthening domestic research, manufacturing and supply chains for unmanned aircraft technologies.
Wyoming should compete to be part of it.
Kinney proposes examining federal partnerships that could bring together Wyoming businesses, entrepreneurs, researchers, the University of Wyoming and Wyoming community colleges to develop a domestic drone-manufacturing ecosystem.
Wyoming’s wide-open spaces could offer opportunities for appropriate research and testing, while a growing unmanned-aircraft industry could support jobs in engineering, electronics, software, advanced manufacturing, fabrication, maintenance and supply-chain operations.
“I want Wyoming students to be able to graduate, find a good-paying technology or manufacturing job here and raise their families here,” Kinney said.
Kinney said Wyoming should pursue industries that complement the state’s existing strengths while creating opportunities beyond its traditional economic sectors.
That means connecting education, research, entrepreneurship and manufacturing.
“We have talented young people.
We have wide-open spaces.
We have a strong tradition of building, fixing and solving problems,” Kinney said.
“Those are Wyoming advantages.” A QUESTION OF NATIONAL PRIORITIES Kinney said the proposal is also part of a broader conversation Congress should have about federal spending and national priorities.
The federal government faces major decisions about how taxpayer dollars should be invested.
Traditional defense systems can cost billions of dollars, while rapidly evolving technologies are changing the economics of national defense.
“This isn’t a choice between national defense and Wyoming,” Kinney said.
“The question is how America gets the greatest long-term return from the dollars taxpayers entrust to us.” Kinney said federal investment in domestic unmanned-aircraft research and manufacturing could advance several national objectives at the same time: strengthening national security, rebuilding American manufacturing capacity, expanding domestic supply chains and creating skilled jobs.
“America should build more of the technology America needs,” Kinney said.
“There is no reason Wyoming cannot help build it.” “Wyoming can help feed America.
Wyoming can help defend America.
And Wyoming can build things America needs.” Wyoming innovation.
Wyoming manufacturing.

August 28, 2026 History Cannot be Re-Written https://newslj.com/kinney-blasts-removal-native-american-history-signage CHEYENNE (WNE) — A candidate for Wyoming’s lone U.S.
House seat is criticizing the removal of signage at Grand Teton National Park that described 19th-century explorer Gustavus Doane’s role in a massacre of Native Americans, calling the move an attempt to rewrite state history.
The signs were taken down under a federal executive order titled “Restoring Truth and Sanity to American History.” The administration has described the removed material as “improper partisan ideology.” Lisa Kinney, a Democratic candidate for the U.S.
House, said the order does neither.
“This is neither ‘truth’ nor ‘sanity.’ It is outrageous,” Kinney said in a news release.
“Why should we allow Washington to determine and rewrite Wyoming history?” Kinney said the federal government should not have the final say over how the state’s history is told.
“Wyoming is independent, and we do not just roll over when told to do so by the federal government,” she said.
She added that the removal amounts to “a disparaging and significant insult to the Native Americans in our state.” The same order also stripped information from the Fort Laramie National Historic Site that addressed the persecution, forced relocation and displacement of Native Americans, according to the release.
In Yellowstone National Park, several signs were flagged for removal, but conflicting guidance has so far kept them in place.
The order’s legality is being contested in federal court.
A federal judge had ordered the signs restored by July 4, but a U.S.
Court of Appeals has temporarily blocked that ruling, leaving the case unresolved.
Wyoming is not alone: signage and interpretive exhibits have been ordered removed at national parks in other states as well.
Roughly 400 National Park Service employees nationwide say they are now barred from discussing the removed historical material with visitors.
“National parks teach our tourists about the history of Wyoming, and the signs should be immediately restored and the teachings resumed,” Kinney said.
“We need to stop this Washington attack on our tribes and on Wyoming.” — The above item was written by Claude.ai off a news release from the Kinney campaign.
