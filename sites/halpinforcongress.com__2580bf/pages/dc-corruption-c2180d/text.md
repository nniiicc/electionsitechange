The Contrast with Garbarino's Pay-to-Play
When politicians become dependent on corporate money, the public has every right to question whose interests are being represented.
Recent reporting has highlighted concerns that Representative Andrew Garbarino accepted tens of thousands of dollars in campaign contributions from corporate PACs affiliated with companies that received Department of Homeland Security contracts after he became Chairman of the House Homeland Security Committee.
Watchdog groups have argued that this creates the appearance of a "pay-to-play" system and raises legitimate questions about whether Congress can effectively oversee the very industries funding political campaigns.
Andrew Garbarino ran for Congress for himself—not for the people he is supposed to represent.
He has said he has "always wanted to be a congressman" and appears to enjoy the perks and privileges that come with his office.
