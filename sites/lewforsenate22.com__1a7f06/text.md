Why I'm running.
Dear Friends and Neighbors, When I look at the people of Senate District 22, I see people who deeply care about their communities and want them to be the best they can be.
But I've seen the quality of life in Sarasota-Manatee deteriorating year-by-year and observed the enormous economic stress everyday people are living under, here at home.
I'm running to fight and solve these problems.
Currently, property insurance costs are out of control, the cost of living keeps climbing, and our natural lands and clean water systems urgently need protection.
We continue to see unprecedented growth affecting our infrastructure and many of our neighbors are without quality and affordable healthcare.
We are failing our students, parents, and teachers and voting rights are increasingly under attack.
Our home rule prerogatives have been usurped in Tallahassee, taking from the people in Sarasota-Manatee control of their own destiny.
Having spent my entire adult life dedicated to public service I know that when we work together, change happens.
We can make positive change happen not as ideologues but across our shared values.
Now it is time to take a pragmatic approach to fix our problems and not be distracted by culture war creations intended to divert our attention from the real issues.
I am ready to bring my experience to fight for a better Florida and carry this mantle into the future.
We have to vote out the politicians in Tallahassee who keep voting against our best interests.
We must strengthen Democratic representation in Tallahassee, develop new leaders who can take the reins, and build a movement that can continue long after this election.
When you send me to Tallahassee, I will hit the ground running and deliver the tangible benefits my colleagues on the other side have failed to deliver.
I will fight to make sure that our communities receive the attention, resources, and representation they deserve.
Sincerely, Lew Wasserman Candidate for Florida Senate District 22 About Lew Wasserman "Public service has been the central theme of my entire career." Lew Wasserman is an attorney, educator, and public servant whose career has been defined by a combination of technical expertise and a deep commitment to people.
He has spent his career working through complex questions of law, public policy, school finance, and civil rights to solve difficult problems and stand up for those who need a strong advocate.
Before joining the faculty at the University of Texas, Lew practiced law with work that included public education, labor and management, disability rights, and civil-rights litigation.
As principal attorney in major class-action and constitutional cases, he helped establish legal precedents protecting children with disabilities and individuals facing discrimination in the workplace.
Before beginning his legal career, Lew served as an assistant superintendent of schools, overseeing finance and business operations, personnel, labor relations, and special programs in a school district serving more than 10,000 students.
That experience gave him a practical understanding of how large public systems work—and how thoughtful leadership can make those systems function more effectively for the people who depend on them.
At the University of Texas, Lew taught graduate courses in constitutional law, public policy, educational leadership, and civil liberties.
His academic work brought together rigorous research and real-world experience, and he received the Educational Finance Conference's award for outstanding scholarship in school finance.
Lew holds a Ph.D. in Psychology from Hofstra University and a J.D. from St.
John's University School of Law.
His background in psychology, law, education, and public administration gives him an unusually broad understanding of both the systems that shape people's lives and the human consequences of public policy.
Lew lives in Lakewood Ranch and serves on the Manatee County Democratic Executive Committee.
He is also a former Acting Chair of the Manatee County Democratic Committee.
He brings decades of legal, academic, administrative, and public-service experience to this race, and just as importantly, he brings a lifelong belief that government should work for people.
Throughout his career, Lew has taken on complicated challenges, defended civil rights, strengthened public institutions, and fought for fairness.
He understands the details, sees the bigger picture, and never loses sight of the people affected by the decisions government makes.
Lew knows how to fight the good fight, and how to do the work required to win it.
Issues Fighting for Florida Families Real solutions for Sarasota and Manatee Counties Featured Issue Fighting the Property Insurance Crisis Florida now has some of the highest property insurance costs in America and the pain this causes our neighbors demands immediate action.
We can stabilize the property insurance market, when we: harden homes against storm damage; strengthen building codes and state funded mitigation retrofits; build systems for rigorous oversight of insurance companies, rate manipulation, and their accountability; and reduce reinsurance costs through a Public Catastrophe Layer.
I intend on implementing these changes from day one.
I will strongly advocate for legislation which increases civil and criminal penalties for abuse by contractors, insurance companies, and fraudulent homeowners.
Public Education We are failing too many of our students, teachers, and parents through the way we currently approach both K–12 education and higher education.
At the same time, Florida has made meaningful progress in expanding career and technical education.
We should continue building on that progress by creating more opportunities for students to gain practical skills, earn valuable credentials, and prepare for good-paying careers, whether they choose to enter the workforce, attend college, or pursue both.
Healthcare Access Our healthcare system does not come close to meeting its full potential.
Too often, healthcare decisions are driven by price gouging, corporate interests, and political motivations rather than by what patients need.
I believe every person has the right to access the high-quality, affordable care they need.
As your senator, I will work to make healthcare pricing more transparent and accountable, protect the right of patients and doctors to make healthcare decisions together, and build the broad coalition needed to make universal healthcare a reality.
Voting Rights The right to vote is one of the fundamental principles upon which our country was founded.
It is how the people exercise their inherent political power and make their voices heard.
No eligible Floridian should face unnecessary barriers to participating in our democracy.
Polling locations should be safe and accessible, and voting by mail should be available to every eligible voter who requests it.
Environment & Water Quality Our district's natural environment is not only part of what makes our community beautiful, it supports our economy, protects our quality of life, and sustains the waterways, beaches, and wildlife that define this region.
We have seen the consequences of delayed investment in wastewater infrastructure and the damage that nutrient pollution can cause to our bays and waterways.
Florida must make long-term investments in clean water, modern infrastructure, and the protection of our natural lands.
We must also ensure that growth is planned responsibly and does not come at the expense of the communities we love.
Restore Local Control Over Local Issues We know what it is like when state government plays political chess with our local autonomy.
Year-over-year reductions in local revenue (sometimes imposed with little or no advance notice) put our schools, our roads, our public safety, and the essential services our communities depend on at risk.
Amendment 3, which will appear on the November ballot, would amend the Florida Constitution and place new limits on how counties and municipalities can use property-tax revenue.
Disguised as "tax relief," this amendment could undermine the financial stability of our local governments and weaken the services that keep our communities safe and functioning.
Similar proposals did not pass the Florida Legislature this year.
As your senator, I will work to protect local control, defend the ability of communities to make decisions that reflect their own needs, and serve as an independent check on the Governor's power.
We cannot allow harmful policies to be imposed on our communities without meaningful input from the people who live here.
