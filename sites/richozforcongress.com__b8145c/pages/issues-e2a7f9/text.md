- The ideal way to establish "Land Back" in the United States, and the state of Georgia, involves a combination of federal and state legislative land transfers, tribal co-management of public lands, and voluntary private returns.
Much of this land is already in Treaty with the United States and simply has to be honored with the tribes.
(Legal cases have already been decided across America, simply not enforced.
United States v Sioux Nation; Worcester v Georgia re: Cherokee Land, etc)
Because "Land Back" is a diverse movement rather than a single policy, advocates and legal scholars both point to several key pathways to return land and decision-making power to Indigenous nations.
Georgia recognizes both Georgia Tribe of Eastern Cherokee
and Cherokee of Georgia Tribal Council.
Legislative and Public Land Transfers
Federal Actions
The U.S. government can return national forests (Chattahoochee-Oconee National Forest), Bureau of Land Management (BLM owns subsurface rights) acres, and national parks directly to the original tribal nations through acts of Congress (or presidential executive orders).
State Returns
Georgia can return state parks or state-owned properties to their stewards, the local tribes.
Title Transfers
The ultimate goal of this pathway is transferring the actual legal deed (fee title or trust status) of ANY unoccupied or unowned lands back to the sovereign tribal governments.
Co-Management Agreements
Shared Tribal and State Authority
When full land transfer is not immediate, tribes and federal or state agencies share management of those public lands and any financial upkeep for them.
Restoring Stewardship
Once restored, tribes will utilize traditional ecological knowledge to manage wildlife, water, and fire safety with the states participation and willingness to learn this sacred knowledge in how to care for our Ancestral homelands.
Decision-Making Power
This ensures tribal voices guide how these ancestral lands are protected and utilized for future generations.
(This was recently tested in Bartow County with a proposed data center being built near the Etowah Mounds archaeological sites.)
Private Land Returns and Conservation
Land Back Donations
Private landowners can and have started gifting their property and returning deeds directly to local tribes or Indigenous-led land trusts across the nation.
We need full disclosure of what has occurred on the property while not in entrusted to the tribes, and what current EPA/EPA of Georgia recommends or assesses on the property for any clean up.
There needs to be some level of cooperation between entities.
Conservation Easements
Private land owners can place legal agreements on their land that give permanent management rights or cultural access back to Indigenous groups.
This is a very appreciated measure especially for our sacred sites and burial grounds.
Purchasing Land
Philanthropy and fundraising campaigns have helped buy back private parcels of culturally significant land to then hand them over to tribes free and clear of all liens.
The red tape needs to be cut to enable this action to be easier for all interested parties.
Restoring Tribal Sovereignty
Legal Reforms
Changing federal laws to further remove that red tape that makes it difficult for tribes to put returned land into federal trusts.
(Carcieri v.
Salazar 2009 which disregards currently recognized tribes as well as some tribes of past that have incorporated.)
Jurisdiction
Ensuring tribal courts and governments have full authority over the returned areas.
This would relieve the current issue seen on reservations and legal jurisdictions outside of the reservation.
- Self-sovereignty and legal personhood is to be extended for the right to bodily autonomy, freedom from exploitation, and moral consideration to ALL living beings, bridging human rights and animal ethics in the United States.
Self-Sovereignty
The moral or natural right of a living being, Plant or Animal, to own its body, direct its own life, and remain free from outside control or property status within the confines of collective agreed upon law of the land.
Legal Personhood
Legal status to be granted to ALL beings related to their fundamental rights of self-determination, standings in court, and protections under the law of the land.
How to Expand Personhood to Animals
Sentience is the Baseline
Philosophers and legal scholars alike state that because animals feel pain, fear, loss, happiness and joy, they possess interests that deserve direct moral and legal protection under the law of the United States.
Ending Property Status
Granting animals personhood means they would no longer be legally classified as property or commodities that can be bought, sold, or harmed for human benefit.
All animal husbandry MUST be ethical and in line with the agreed upon law.
The American food production method will prioritize high animal welfare, environmental stewardship, and fair treatment of workers over maximum mass-production efficiency.
Practical Representation
Just as human children or incapacitated adults have legal guardians to represent their interests in court, non-human persons will have advocates to defend their inherent rights to life and habitat as well.
Things to Recognize with Shared Vulnerability and Ethics
Interdependence
Humans and animals share a mutual dependence on Earth’s ecosystems, making the exploitation of one an indirect harm to the collective whole.
The United States government shall enshrine this recognition in its laws and/or Constitution.
Compassion Without Species Boundaries
Extending sovereignty universally to redefine justice, shifting human responsibility from being "masters" and stopping the continued attempts at domination of the planet to equal participants in the only shared natural home we have, Earth.
