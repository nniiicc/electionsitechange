Taxes and Affordability
The current amount that Kansans are paying in property taxes, at fuel pumps, and at grocery stores is no longer affordable.
Farmers and ranchers, as well as single-family homeowners, are struggling to meet these financial demands.
Land that is devoted to agricultural use is taxed at 30%, since it is not based on the fair market value of the property.
Single-family homes are taxed at 11% of fair market value, but so are multi-family residences; commercial property owners are taxed at 25% of fair market value.
The prices at the fuel pumps keep going higher, with only small pockets of relief that never stick too long.
Sticker shock continues when people go to the grocery stores.
Families are having to decide how much they can afford to purchase, and hope that it lasts long enough to feed everyone for longer than one meal.
Farmers and ranchers, especially, as well as single-family homeowners are having to make tough decisions regarding what they can continue to afford versus what personal expenses they will have to remove or cancel.
One way to help working Kansans is to find a more viable and accurate way to assess the value of each property.
It is critical to Kansans to promote a firmer financial standing.

KPERS - Kansas public employees required retirement fund
A question came up while I was out canvasing.
A retired Police Chief asked about KPERS, the monthly payments that come from it and why there hasn’t been an increase in the money sent.
Background: For those of you who are not familiar with that acronym, it is the Kansas Public Employees Retirement System.
KPERS is a mandatory program that all public employees in Kansas contribute to in every pay period.
Employees are required to put 6% of their paycheck into the system, this is what KPERS a prefunded retirement system.
In addition, employees receive an annual interest of 4% quarterly to their account, however it can depend on the investment return.
The KPERS payment is pretax for federal income taxes, however it is after tax for State of Kansas incomes taxes.
(Pre-tax deductions lower the current taxable income, however you will pay taxes on the money when it is paid out.
Therefore, employees will be paying Federal taxes every month when they receive their KPERS checks.)
Now to the question: Why has there not been a Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA) to the monthly KPERS payments?
Basically, since KPERS is prefunded through your service as a public employee, you are now being paid out the money the state required and nothing more.
Now, this is only true for KPERS tiers 1 & 2.
KPERS tier 3 has the option to self-funded COLA at 1% or 2%.
To be in KPERS tier 3, you would have had to start working as a public employee after January 1, 2015.
Why does this matter: Many us who have been public employees for a while and may be looking towards retirement where not given the opportunity to add more monies into our KPERS.
For example, if you started as a state employee right out of college or high school and are now retired, your contributions started in the 1980s (or earlier) and ended in the 2010s-2020s.
Within that time frame, there has been a HUGE increase in the cost of living across all facets of daily life.
Can the 6% of your income from the 1990s cover much now in the 2026?
Absolutely not.
Who can help: Only the Legislative branch of the State of Kansas can help.
They have the ability to inject funding into KPERS for the benefit of the public employees who have retired.
According to the Kansas Silver Haired Legislature, the last time the State granted a COLA increase to retirees was in 1998 and it was at 3 increase%.
That’s 28 years since the last time the legislation actually did something to help retired public employees.
There have been groups who have and will continue to push for COLAs for retired public employees.
Every year, the legislature has the opportunity to inject money, however, no such action has been taken.
Solution: It is high time that the State Legislature inject another increase into KPERS.
While I would love to see an increase across the board, specific age groups definitely need the injection more than employees you are still working currently.
I would also love to see KPERS allow tier 1 & 2 employees to stat putting in an extra 1% or 2% towards their KPERS.
