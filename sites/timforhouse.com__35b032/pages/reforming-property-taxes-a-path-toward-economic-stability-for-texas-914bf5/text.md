For homeowners, escalating valuations drive up monthly costs, eroding housing affordability and threatening financial stability, especially for low-income families and retirees on fixed incomes.
Renters indirectly suffer as landlords pass on hikes, limiting access to stable housing.
Small businesses face squeezed profits from high assessments, deterring expansion and innovation in capital-intensive sectors.
To combat this property tax tyranny, reforms are gaining traction.
In 2025, voters approved Proposition 13, raising homestead exemptions to $140,000 for school taxes.
Broader proposals include abolishing property taxes through surplus buydowns or replacing them with a revised progressive sales tax system.
Governor Abbott’s signed laws, like SB4, further compress rates and cap appraisals but it is not nearly enough.
These changes promise economic revitalization: Lower taxes could attract businesses, create jobs, and boost personal income by freeing capital for investment.
By shifting to a progressive consumption-based revenue, Texas can foster growth, reduce unemployment, and secure financial futures for all residents.
