My Country Philosophy
A Lifetime of Service: From the Military to District 32
Before I ever cast a vote in Annapolis, I spent a career in uniform learning what real accountability looks like.
As a retired Colonel, I didn't just serve my country, I led others through it, carrying the weight of decisions where the stakes were measured in lives, missions, and outcomes that couldn't afford to fail.
That kind of service leaves a mark.
It taught me discipline not as a buzzword, but as a daily practice: show up, do the work, own the outcome, and never ask more of others than I'm willing to demand of myself.
That military discipline is the same discipline I bring to representing District 32.
A Colonel doesn't get to make decisions based on convenience or popularity, decisions get made based on what the mission requires and what the people depending on you need.
I carry that same mindset into every budget vote, every piece of legislation, and every dollar of taxpayer money I am asked to steward.
It is not a coincidence that my economic philosophy discipline and accountability equal success reads like something out of a military planning manual.
It is one.
I didn't learn financial discipline from a textbook; I lived it leading operations where discipline was not optional.
That same foundation shapes how I think about education.
In the military, you don't send people into complex situations without proper training, and you don't leave anyone undertrained because it was easier or cheaper in the moment.
I bring that same standard to how I think about Maryland's students, every child deserves to be fully prepared, fully supported, and given the tools to succeed, because leaving any of them undertrained is a failure the whole community pays for later.
My E2C2 philosophy: Education, Economy, Community, and Country aren’t abstract to me.
It's the same interdependence I saw firsthand in the military: readiness in one area determines success in every other.
My time in service also shaped how I think about community.
In the military, no mission succeeds without trust between people who show up for each other, day in and day out and that's exactly the standard I hold myself to as a delegate.
It is why I am the one handing out backpacks personally, standing in the park talking to neighbors, and sitting down with other elected officials to coordinate on public safety instead of waiting for someone else to solve it.
I learned a long time ago that a team that doesn't communicate and doesn't show up for each other doesn't succeed, and I see District 32 the same way as a team I am proud to serve.
I didn't set aside my commitment to country when I left the military I brought it home to District 32.
Every principle I learned wearing the uniform, discipline, accountability, readiness, and showing up for the people beside me now shows up in how I legislate, how I prioritize education and economic opportunity, and how I show up in this community, rain or shine.
My service didn't end with retirement.
It just changed uniforms.
