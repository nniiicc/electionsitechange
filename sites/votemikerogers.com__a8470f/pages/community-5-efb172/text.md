My Community Philosophy
Presence and Service Builds Trust
Representing District 32 isn't just about the votes I cast in Annapolis; it is about showing up where people live.
I have built my service around a simple principle: government works best when it is present in the community, not just present in the statehouse.
That is the philosophy behind every event my office organizes, supports, and shows up personally: rain or shine.
The Back-2-School Backpack Giveaway is one of the clearest examples of what I mean.
Every year, families across District 32 who are stretching every dollar to get their kids ready for the new school year get real, tangible help: backpacks, school supplies, and a reminder that their community sees them and has their back before the first bell even rings.
It's a small thing on paper and a big thing for a parent trying to make ends meet in August.
Power in the Park brings that same spirit to a different setting, bringing neighbors, families, and community resources together in a shared public space, turning a simple day in the park into a moment of connection, information, and support for the people who call District 32 home.
I want residents to know my office is not a distant institution; I am a neighbor invested in the same parks, schools, and streets they are.
None of this works in isolation, which is why I make it a priority to sit down regularly with other elected officials, county council members, fellow delegates, local law enforcement leadership, and county executives to talk honestly about what it takes to keep District 32 safe and give it the conditions to thrive.
Public safety isn't solved by one office alone, and neither is economic opportunity or quality of life.
Those conversations, sometimes formal and sometimes just a phone call or a conversation after an event, are where a lot of the real problem-solving happens comparing notes on what's working, coordinating resources instead of duplicating them, and making sure residents aren't caught in the gaps between jurisdictions.
This kind of work isn't new for me; it reflects a long-standing commitment I have carried alongside my legislative work in Annapolis.
Whether it is a Volunteer Appreciation Celebration honoring the people who power my campaign, a Back-2-School Block Party, or partnering with organizations on food bank fundraisers and energy efficiency workshops through BGE, my approach stays consistent: real people, real needs, real presence.
That's the standard I hold myself to, not just legislating on behalf of District 32, but being physically present in it.
Backpacks handed out one by one.
Conversations in the park, not just in committee rooms.
This is the kind of representation I believe in, built on relationships, not just a résumé, and it is one District 32 residents can see for themselves at every community event, because I am there.
