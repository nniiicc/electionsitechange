My Economic Philosophy
Discipline and Accountability equals Success
Government works best when every level: local, state, and federal functions as part of one system, each supporting the others for the good of the community it serves.
When government creates the right conditions, businesses grow.
When businesses grow, the goods, services, and revenue they generate flow back into people's lives, making our community stronger, more livable, and better to call home.
My approach to economic policy comes down to fundamentals that have never gone out of style: don't spend beyond your means, keep something set aside for hard times, know your priorities, invest with the future in mind, and meet your obligations on time.
These are the same rules millions of households already live by to keep their finances in order, and they're the same rules I apply to how District 32 does its part to keep our local economy strong.
Every dollar of tax revenue matters and should be spent like it does.
Government budgets are more complex than a household ledger, and I don't pretend otherwise but the discipline underneath both is the same.
I bring the same judgment I built managing multi-billion-dollar contracts to how I approach community investment.
Every project District 32 supports should go to the businesses that deliver the most value for our residents.
I have spent my career studying and applying the frameworks used to evaluate exactly this kind of decision weighing return on investment against real, measurable impact for the people we serve.
I insist on clear, unbiased decision-making wherever taxpayer money is involved, and I hold discretion, transparency, and accountability as non-negotiable standards for how this district conducts its business.
None of this is complicated, it is the basic blocking and tackling of financial responsibility.
It's the same foundation that built the world's strongest economy and rebuilt entire nations after two world wars.
It's what I've taught my own kids, and what we owe it to every child in this community to teach as well, because financially disciplined kids grow into financially disciplined adults.
Like any discipline, it takes practice before it becomes habit but once you feel the payoff, that discipline starts reinforcing itself.
Looking ahead through 2026, I continue to be guided by a framework I call E2C2 — Education, Economy, Community, and Country built on the belief that these four pillars rise or fall together.
I look forward to implementing more of this vision with District 32 in the months ahead.
