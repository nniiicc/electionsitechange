Slow Down in Indiana School Zones
As students return to class, drivers will once again encounter active school zones, crossing guards, school buses, and more children walking or riding bicycles near our roads.
Indiana allows local communities to establish school-zone speed limits as low as 20 miles per hour.
Around House District 88, drivers will commonly see posted limits of 20 or 25 miles per hour.
Follow the posted speed limit whenever a school zone is active.
Speeding near a school when children are present is a Class B infraction and can result in a civil judgment of up to $1,000.
The legal penalty matters, but it should not be the primary reason anyone slows down.
Children do not always behave predictably near traffic.
A student may step between parked cars, drop something in the street, run toward a waiting parent, or arrive late and hurry toward a bus without checking for vehicles.
Drivers need to expect the unexpected and leave themselves enough time to react.
The Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles Driver’s Manual provides additional information about Indiana speed limits, traffic laws, and safe driving practices.
Know When to Stop for an Indiana School Bus
Indiana law requires drivers to stop when a school bus has its red lights flashing and its stop arm extended.
The signals are straightforward:
What the lights mean
- Flashing yellow lights mean the bus is preparing to stop.
Slow down and prepare to stop your vehicle.
- Flashing red lights and an extended stop arm mean the bus has stopped to load or unload children.
Stop and remain stopped.
- Do not begin moving again until the red lights stop flashing, the stop arm retracts, and the bus begins moving.
These requirements generally apply whether you are traveling behind the bus or approaching it from the opposite direction.
The Divided-Highway Exception
There is one important exception.
When a highway is divided by a physical barrier, concrete wall, cable barrier, or grassy median, only vehicles traveling in the same direction as the school bus are required to stop.
A center turn lane or painted roadway line is not the same as a physical barrier.
On a roadway without a physical divider, traffic approaching from both directions must stop.
The Indiana Criminal Justice Institute’s school bus safety guide and the Indiana State Police school bus safety page provide additional guidance for motorists.
Passing a Stopped School Bus Has Serious Consequences
Disregarding a school bus stop arm is a Class A infraction in Indiana.
A violation can result in a substantial fine and the suspension of driving privileges.
A court may suspend a driver’s license for up to 90 days for a first stop-arm violation.
A driver with a previous violation may face a suspension of up to one year.
Recklessly passing a stopped school bus can also lead to criminal charges, particularly when the violation results in an injury.
These laws exist because children are at their most vulnerable when approaching or leaving a school bus.

Leave Earlier, Put the Phone Down, and Pay Attention
None of this is complicated.
Leave a few minutes earlier.
Put the phone down.
Slow down near schools, neighborhoods, intersections, and bus stops.
Watch carefully when backing out of a driveway or garage.
Expect children to be present before sunrise and during the afternoon commute.
Be especially cautious on rural roads where hills, curves, crops, and limited visibility can make it harder to see a stopped bus.
Parents should also review these rules with teenage drivers.
A newly licensed driver may never have encountered a bus making repeated stops on a two-lane road or experienced the congestion surrounding a school during morning arrival.
A five-minute conversation at home could prevent a life-changing mistake later.

Do drivers traveling in both directions have to stop for an Indiana school bus?
Yes, unless the roadway is divided by a physical barrier or median.
On a road without a physical divider, vehicles traveling in both directions must stop when a school bus displays flashing red lights and an extended stop arm.
What do flashing yellow school bus lights mean?
Flashing yellow lights mean the bus is preparing to stop to load or unload students.
Drivers should slow down, create additional following distance, and prepare to stop.
