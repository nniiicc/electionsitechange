The record behind this endorsement Four enacted laws, one in each of four consecutive sessions.
Every date, vote count and public law number below comes from the Indiana General Assembly’s own records, and Rep.
Jeter’s individual votes come from the recorded roll calls.
2022: cutting the income tax and killing the utility receipts tax House Enrolled Act 1002 (2022) Passed the House 68 to 25 on January 20, 2022, with Rep.
Jeter voting yes House adopted the conference committee report 82 to 17 on March 9, 2022, with Rep.
Jeter voting yes again Signed March 15, 2022 , enacted as Public Law 138-2022 It cut the individual income tax rate from 3.23% to 3.15% and repealed both the utility receipts tax and the utility services use tax.
Every business in Indiana pays a utility bill, so the repeal reached storefronts and shops as directly as it reached households.
2023: taking the rate to 2.9% and removing the conditions House Enrolled Act 1001 (2023) Passed the House 66 to 29 on February 23, 2023, with Rep.
Jeter voting yes Signed May 4, 2023 , enacted as Public Law 201-2023 In the General Assembly’s own words, it “reduces the individual income tax rate to 2.9% by 2027 and eliminates all trigger provisions in current law.” Most Indiana small businesses are pass-through entities, so the owner pays business income at the individual rate.
Removing the triggers is what turned a possible cut into a date a business owner can plan against.
The rate is 2.95% in 2026 and falls to 2.9% on January 1, 2027 .
2024: when the agency has to pay your legal bill House Enrolled Act 1003 (2024) Passed the House 85 to 10 on January 23, 2024, with Rep.
Jeter voting yes Passed the Senate 40 to 7 on March 5, 2024 House concurred 71 to 24 on March 6, 2024, with Rep.
Jeter voting yes again Signed March 13, 2024 , enacted as Public Law 128-2024 This is the least famous of the four and, for a small business fighting a state agency, possibly the most useful.
It made the office of administrative law proceedings the ultimate authority in administrative cases under its jurisdiction, and it set out when a state agency can be required to pay a party’s reasonable attorney fees in a judicial review.
The practical problem it addresses is familiar to anyone who has been on the wrong end of a state agency: the agency can afford to keep going and you cannot.
2025: raising the business personal property exemption Senate Enrolled Act 1 (2025) Passed the Senate 37 to 10 on February 17, 2025 Passed the House 65 to 29 on April 10, 2025, with Rep.
Jeter voting yes Signed April 15, 2025 , enacted as Public Law 68-2025 The property tax package is known for the homestead credit, but it also raises the business personal property tax exemption , the threshold below which a business does not pay tax on its equipment.
For a shop with a few machines, a couple of trucks and some computers, that is the difference between filing and not filing.
Common questions about this endorsement What is NFIB?
The National Federation of Independent Business, a small business advocacy organization representing independently owned and operated businesses.
It works at the state and federal level on taxes, regulation, health costs and labor mandates, and it surveys its own members to set its positions.
What is the NFIB Indiana PAC?
The political action committee of NFIB’s Indiana membership.
It is made up solely of NFIB members from across the state, and it decides endorsements by reviewing a candidate’s small business voting record along with their working relationship with the organization.
In the 2026 cycle it endorsed candidates for both chambers of the Indiana General Assembly.
What does a 100% NFIB voting record mean?
NFIB tracks how legislators vote on the bills it identifies as key small business votes and scores them against its own positions.
The 100% figure is NFIB’s assessment of Rep.
Jeter’s record, stated in its endorsement letter.
The scoring standard and the choice of which votes count are NFIB’s own.
What has Rep.
Jeter actually voted for?
Four enacted laws carry most of it: the 2022 income tax cut and utility receipts tax repeal (Public Law 138-2022), the 2023 budget that set the income tax on a path to 2.9% and removed the revenue triggers (Public Law 201-2023), the 2024 administrative law overhaul that can make an agency pay a party’s attorney fees (Public Law 128-2024), and the 2025 property tax package that raised the business personal property exemption (Public Law 68-2025).
He voted yes on all four.
