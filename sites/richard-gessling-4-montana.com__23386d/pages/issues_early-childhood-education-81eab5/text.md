Montana's Constitutional Promise "It is the goal of the people to establish a system of education which will develop the full educational opportunity of each person.
Equality of educational opportunity is guaranteed to each person of the state." — Montana Constitution, Article 10, Section 1 The state is not currently meeting this goal.
In fact, we fall far short.
Montana does not financially support early childhood education — and that needs to change.
Why Early Childhood Education Matters There are lifetime advantages for children who have had early childhood education.
The critical years are ages 3, 4, and 5 — when foundational learning takes place.
100% School Readiness Currently in Montana, 40% of 5-year-olds are not ready for kindergarten.
Those with early childhood education?
All ready.
They start school prepared to learn.
Top Tier Academic Success By 3rd grade, children with early childhood education test at the highest proficiency rating.
They're set up for long-term academic success from the start. +25% High School Graduation 25% more children with early childhood education graduate from high school.
That's a significant difference in life outcomes.
More Success Career Success Children with early childhood education are more likely to be successful no matter what they choose — university, blue collar work, or any career path.
The Economic Case For every $1 spent, $13 is returned.
This isn't just good for children — it's good for Montana's budget.
Special Education, which has the highest costs in education, is reduced by half when children have early childhood education.
That's real savings for taxpayers.
We're not just investing in children's futures — we're investing in Montana's economic health.
