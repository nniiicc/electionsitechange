Our Healthcare Blessings We are blessed by good access to healthcare in Carbon County.
Through generous donations by our citizens, we built and opened a first-rate modern hospital — Beartooth Billings Clinic — offering excellent care with 24-hour emergency services and a variety of essential services for our community.
Healthcare Providers in Our County Beartooth Billings Clinic Red Lodge Intermountain Health Red Lodge Riverstone Health Clinics Joliet & Bridger It is critically important that we keep these facilities and providers in our county by using them as our first choice for healthcare.
The Healthcare Challenge This is a very challenging time in healthcare.
We face: Rising Premiums Health insurance premiums continue to increase, making coverage less affordable for families.
Policy Confusion Navigating different companies and policy choices is increasingly confusing for consumers.
Employer vs.
Self-Employed Self-employed individuals have been hit the hardest — especially those enrolled in the Affordable Care Act.
The Self-Employed Healthcare Crisis Without extended subsidies, self-employed people were looking at premiums of 3–4 times what they were paying, with deductibles up to $5,000 .
Those with hazardous jobs — farmers and ranchers — face even more costly premiums.
This is unsustainable for rural Montana families trying to make a living.
The Medicaid Burden We have 2,000 people on Medicaid in our county.
Working-age recipients face a complex, burdensome system: Early Application Requirement Recipients must apply 45 days in advance — because that's how long approval takes in Montana.
Frequent Re-Application Working-age recipients must reapply every 6 months to prove employment and meet all requirements.
Digital Barrier All applications must be done electronically — but many recipients don't have a laptop, smartphone, or dependable car.
"I can't imagine a single mother with children working 80 hours a month, paying for child care while she works, having to navigate this system.
It makes no sense to me." I recently met a college graduate who had applied for 145 jobs .
She accepted four part-time jobs just to be able to live in Montana — the state she was born and raised in.
This is what working families are up against.
Why Pushing People Off Medicaid Doesn't Work Instead of supporting those on Medicaid, the state is trying to push them off the rolls.
This is a false economy: 1 People without Medicaid put off going for care 2 When they finally seek care, it's more costly 3 They're treated but can't pay 4 Providers essentially treat them for free 5 Providers raise rates to compensate for free care 6 Costs rise and insurance premiums increase This is not a sustainable situation.
