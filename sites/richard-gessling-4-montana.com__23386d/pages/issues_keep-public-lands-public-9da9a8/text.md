Our Shared Responsibility These lands belong to all of us.
Many of us know that Montana has public lands.
We have our own state lands whose lease income supports our public schools, providing 50% of their funds .
The Federal Government manages Forest Service Lands and Bureau of Land Management (BLM) lands.
These lands are owned by all of us — an important fact to remember when they are threatened.
I cherish these lands, as well as our right to enjoy our water with access in many locations.
These lands and water access come before the legislature every session, but not always in a good way.
Instead of protecting them, as granted in our state constitution, bills are suggested to change access and trade or sell our lands.
Where I Stand Four commitments I will carry into every session in Helena.
Resist Land Sales & Trades I will vote to resist any attempt to remove access or to sell or trade our public lands.
These lands belong to all Montanans and must remain in public ownership.
Protect Water Access Our right to enjoy Montana's water with access in many locations is fundamental.
These water rights and access must be protected and preserved for all citizens.
Defend Constitutional Rights Montana's constitution grants us the right to access and enjoy public lands and waters.
I will fight to uphold these constitutional protections against any threats.
Reclaim Lost Access Access to land and water often ends up in courts due to lost county records or unclear specifications.
I support reporting lost access to advocacy groups to reclaim it before costly litigation.
Current Threats to Public Lands What we're up against right now.
Federal Land Trades The Federal Government has been trading public lands for private land, reducing public access and ownership.
Cancelled BLM Leases Cancellation of leases on BLM lands affects resource management and public use.
Forest Service Changes Changing uses of Forest Service lands from wilderness to logging permitting threatens conservation.
Legislative Threats Bills are regularly introduced to change access and trade or sell public lands.
What You Can Do Four practical ways every Montanan can help defend our public lands.
1 Report Lost Access If you notice you've lost access to public lands or water, report it to public access advocacy groups so it can be reclaimed before court action becomes necessary.
2 Support Conservation Groups Join and support organizations dedicated to protecting public lands and water access in Montana.
3 Contact Your Representatives Reach out to state and federal legislators to voice your support for protecting public lands and opposing land sales or trades.
4 Stay Informed Follow legislation and policy changes affecting public lands.
Knowledge is power in protecting our shared resources.
My Commitment to You A legacy worth fighting for.
As your representative in the Montana House, I will use my position to prevent federal intrusions into our state's management of public lands.
While the state doesn't have direct control over federal lands, I will vote and advocate to protect Montana's interests and ensure our public lands remain public.
Public lands are a legacy we must preserve.
They represent freedom, opportunity, and our connection to Montana's natural heritage.
I am committed to fighting for their protection, today and for future generations.
