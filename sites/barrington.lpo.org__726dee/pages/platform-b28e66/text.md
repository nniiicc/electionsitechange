Unlike campaign finance or balloting procedures, those directly thwart the will of the people as expressed through the actual votes cast, and thus impede necessary action on every other issue.
The solutions are simple: For Senate elections, ranked choice voting , which mostly prevents spoilers from altering outcomes, and allows options outside the two-party system to gain traction without being wasted votes.
For House elections, proportional representation on the Swiss model : each state’s House seats are filled by a party’s list in proportion to vote share (with independent candidacies allowed), with no winner-take-all districts.
If, for example, the Republicans got 50% of the House vote in a state with eight seats, Republican candidates would fill exactly four seats, neither more nor less.
Party lists can be determined in primaries, or by a party organization according to its rules.
This allows small parties and independents with dispersed support to win representation in accordance with voter preferences, eliminating the duopoly.
It would force legislation to reflect consensus among a broad range of ideas in the Capitol, representative of the sweep of Americans’ views, instead of the status quo of one specific vision using slim pluralities in a slim majority of districts to roll over all opposition and produce wild swings in policy.
The Senate filibuster is a kludge for this; my proposal is a real fix.
Recent polling shows majority support for this system once people understand what it is.
This would work best if tied to expanding the House .
That the House has 435 Representatives was set by statute more than a century ago, and has not been adjusted to population increase.
More Representatives would allow more personal attention from each Representative to their constituents, and improve proportionality under the proposed PR system for almost all states.

Privatization of Regulation: Insurance, Not Compliance A core justification for regulation is that absolute application of the non-aggression principle fails because entities can be judgment-proof .
This breaks the market incentive system by imposing an effective maximum on the costs which anything can incur; there is no way within pure libertarian rules to prevent someone from doing something who does not have anywhere near the financial resources to pay for its worst-case consequences (whether a nuclear explosion, airliner crash, or even just a fatal car accident).
A rational actor may accept a 90% chance at $10 million in profits against a 10% chance of a $1 billion catastrophe that results in his ruin or even death, because the cost to him is only at most what the rest of his life was worth to him, not $1 billion; but this has a net expected value to society of negative $91 million.
Worse, in competitive markets, it can then become obligatory to take such risks or be out-competed.
The solution is suggested by our approach to liability for car accidents : states don’t prohibit driving (despite it being more dangerous than almost all workplaces); they just require liability insurance .
The same approach can be taken to preventing catastrophes.
Instead of a federal agency mandating standards for nuclear power plants, airliner safety, hazardous waste storage, or even food processing, the government would simply require that any entity operating something which can fail catastrophically have sufficient assets and/or insurance coverage to pay all claims in the event of a worst-case catastrophe (from an insurance company with sufficient capital).
If it is impossible to obtain insurance coverage at a price which makes a risky project or activity possible, it cannot happen; the market has determined that it is not worth it to society.
Regulatory functions are then effectively outsourced to insurance companies which, unlike government bureaucrats, have a strong economic incentive to get the cost-benefit analysis right and no political interference with how they do so.
For example, in this regime, there would be no law specifically prohibiting building a nuclear reactor with no containment vessel in downtown Columbus, but no sufficiently capitalized insurer would offer coverage for liability from an accident at a price low for the project to be profitable, and so it would not happen.
However, if the builder had developed a new technology which eliminated the risk of radioactive material release, so that no containment vessel was needed, an insurer’s experts could simply review the specifications and have the insurer approve cheaper coverage, allowing construction to go ahead without a Nuclear Regulatory Commission having to approve new rules.
If large numbers of people are harmed by a company’s actions, they can and should bring class-action lawsuits against it.
This mechanism, not fully developed or common in the 19th century, would have been adequate to prevent most of the abuses associated with 19th-century capitalism.
This approach can be generalized to a wide range of economic activities currently restrained by DC regulations.
Rules which fail cost-benefit analysis could be set aside at once, instead of waiting for slow bureaucratic action or the resolution of political brinkmanship in Congress.

Details: Discrimination Law Immigration: Open to Workers, Closed to Parasites Because of the strength of American institutions and our first-mover advantages, workers are far more productive in the United States than they are in most countries of origin.

To prevent immigrants from becoming a fiscal drain, they would be totally in eligible for all federal benefits (but remain subject to all taxes).

If an immigrant is convicted of a crime or incurs civil damages or debt, the deposit is deducted from to cover the court-ordered costs, damage award, or payment of the debt, and must then be restocked by the depositor for him to remain in the USA.
Immigrants would also pay into a fund for compensation of victims who have suffered harm in excess of one deposit from a crime committed by an immigrant.
This policy addresses legitimate objections to large-scale immigration: crime, welfare use, and the incentives created by welfare use.
It would actually mitigate the issues with unfair competition with H1-B visa holders because companies could no longer use the visa restrictions to underpay immigrant workers.
The deposit system and need for steady employment would keep out those seeking an effort-free higher standard of living and whose children would vote accordingly; it would select for those who are willing and able to do hard work that benefits our economy and standard of living, the sort of people whose children vote to protect their earnings and property from the native-born underclass, not in “solidarity” with it.
End the War on Drugs The time has long since come to admit the reality: drugs have won the War on Drugs.
Despite many decades of prohibition, violent enforcement, sting operations, long prison sentences, and civil asset forfeiture, use of illicit drugs has not decreased at all , and overdose deaths have risen dramatically .
Today, illegal drugs can be easily sold via text message and the Internet.
First principles of economics show why the government’s approach cannot succeed.
It attempts to eliminate the supply of drugs, by blaming the “drug dealers” for the problem, jailing them, and seizing their property.
But the cause of the phenomenon is demand: if there is no demand for drugs, there is no supply, because then no one can make a profit selling them.
As long as there is demand for drugs, there will be supply, because every supplier eliminated increases the profits available from satisfying that demand.
Hence, the federal government should eliminate all laws restricting what drugs people choose to possess, consume, and transact in.
(The only exception is substances which no one would deliberately consume, used primarily to harm others nonconsensually, such as poisons and “date rape” drugs.) By eliminating the unfunded mandate for hospitals to provide lifesaving care to overdose victims unlimited numbers of times, the problem would self-correct: those willing to risk killing themselves with drugs do so, suffering the consequences of their choices.
Those who can use drugs responsibly, or at least function in society while using them, would be made free to do so without negotiating with criminals or fearing arrest.
Reform Taxation: Reward Productivity; Tax Rent-Seeking and External Costs My core approach to taxation is simple: taxes should be applied to unearned, not earned, wealth to the greatest extent possible.
Aside from the question of fairness, this encourages productive activity that produces earned wealth, and reduces the rewards from finding ways to extract wealth from others.
My overall goals are to reduce the total amount of tax collected (on you, and on your children who will have to pay the ballooning national debt), and change the tax system to allow you to keep more of what you actually earn through your work.
Some of what follows from that principle: End special exemptions and privileges in the income tax code , including means of using corporations to reduce tax liability.
Where there are genuine positive externalities from something, that should be handled through a separate, explicit subsidy so that it is clear what the costs and benefits are.
Every exception to a tax increases the burden of it on everyone else.
Every exception eliminated allows reduction of base tax rates.
Tax real (inflation-adjusted) profits from home sales at a high rate, with associated taxes on profits from raising real rent (with an exception for spare rooms in a primary residence).
This would greatly reduce harmful incentives to hoard housing, charge high prices for it, and use local governments to increase artificially its price.
It obtains much of the benefits of a Georgist land value tax without forcing people essentially to rent their homes from the government.
By deterring speculation, it would lessen wasteful boom-bust cycles in real estate markets by reducing the gains from buying into an appreciating area.

American fossil fuel companies would be made to pay for their pollution, along with overseas manufacturers (through a border adjustment tax based on the fossil fuel used to make the goods being imported).

School and similar rampage shootings cause a minuscule fraction of violent deaths of children (let alone deaths of children overall 12 ), but are amplified out of all proportion to the danger by a “if it bleeds, it leads”, media chasing ratings and virality.
Total firearm-related deaths are much greater, but that simply demonstrates the reality that it is almost impossible to stop someone determined to possess a gun and use it to harm others from doing so.
Societies which have mostly eliminated civilian gun possession (e.g., Britain and New Zealand) had far lower gun ownership rates to begin with, lower baseline levels of violent criminality and fewer dangerous large animals (which increased willingness to surrender guns), and a more naturally compliant population; this would not be possible in the United States even if it were desirable.
The best thing the government can do is get out of the way of the right to self-defense (which also reduces the need for taxes for highly salaried law enforcement officials).
The right to possess firearms is of no use without the right to use them to protect one’s person and property.
Carrying a gun into a school is not “interstate commerce” , and can and should be handled by local or state governments with a much better appreciation of the context of the act.
This should not result in criminal charges or expulsions in rural areas where gun ownership is common and there is no intent to harm anyone.
Private ownership of fully automatic weapons including heavy machine guns has been legal for decades , and more than 600,000 such guns are registered , but such guns have never once been used in a mass-casualty incident in the United States.
This is why the ban on selling newly manufactured versions of such weapons to civilians should be eliminated.
Citizens in rural areas who can be easily outnumbered by a carful of criminals (or a group of aggressive feral hogs) and are far from law enforcement or other help should be allowed machine guns to protect themselves.
(Machine gun owners are also more useful in the unorganized militia, such as for decentralized national defense, if such needs arise.) However, I am also opposed to mandating universal concealed carry reciprocity, as I consider it inconsistent with federalism.
If the people of Hawaii or Massachusetts wish to limit the carrying of items which are, we should not forget, dangerous by design, there is no good cause for DC to interfere.
Those who want to carry handguns are free to move to other states which allow this (as Ohio does).

Restoring prosperity and security in Ohio need not mean tolerating these things.

Jury deliberations are kept private for good reason; this should be no different.
Artificial Intelligence There is minimal discussion of artificial intelligence advances in the rest of this platform, because the rest of it is based on the implicit assumption that how world-transforming AI will be is at the low end of the plausible range.
In scenarios with very powerful AI, most of these policy debates become moot, and it is impossible to effectively prepare.
However, I share concerns about AI safety, to a degree almost inconsistent with my party’s ideology.
It should go without saying that Skynet killing us all would cause harm far beyond anyone’s ability to pay compensation, so the insurance mechanism proposed in an earlier section cannot be applicable.
I support strong federal regulation, including negotiations with China as necessary, to ensure that any potential artificial superintelligence is verifiably transparent, corrigible, and aligned with human interests.
Permanent Standard Time, Not A Permanent Lie I support ending the institution of Daylight Savings Time, replacing the twice-yearly clock change with permanent standard time.
The time zones were crafted around the goal of a reasonable approximation of local solar noon.
Noon is when the sun is highest and most southerly in the sky.
That is what the clock means : “a.m.” and “p.m.” stand for ante meridian and post meridian : before and after the sun has crossed the meridian (north-south) line.
In a time of ever-increasing disconnection from the natural world due to overuse of air conditioning, addictive devices with screens , and the irrational fear of child abductors, we should not be further severing the rhythm of our lives from that of the Earth’s spin and Sol’s apparent jaunt across the sky.
Why can’t businesses change their own work and business hours as seasonally appropriate?
