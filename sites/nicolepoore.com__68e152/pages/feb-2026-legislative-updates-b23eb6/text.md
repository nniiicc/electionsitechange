Community Update Regarding Data Center Proposed in District 12
READ THE LATEST DECISION FROM DNREC REGARDING PROJECT WASHINGTON COASTAL ZONE ACT APPLICATION
In 1971, Governor Russell Peterson and the General Assembly passed the Coastal Zone Act, with the foresight to protect Delaware’s vital coastal areas from a further influx of heavy industry that could threaten quality of life for future Delawareans.
In addition to clear provisions around power generation and emissions, the Coastal Zone Act puts significant power in the hands of Delawareans through DNREC’s hearing process, where thousands of public comments were submitted regarding the Project Washington Data Center proposal.
Today’s announcement from Secretary Patterson, that Project Washington as currently conceived cannot be constructed in the coastal zone, is evidence of a regulatory process that works, and that centers community outreach.
From the first public meeting Speaker Minor-Brown and I held in our district last summer to last month’s Coastal Zone hearing, we have taken seriously public concerns about the potential environmental impacts of this project.
Now DNREC has agreed that some components of this project are incompatible with our community.
Today’s ruling is not a cause for celebration for everyone, however.
Delaware needs companies willing to invest in our State.
We all benefit from the tax revenue created when new employers choose Delaware to grow.
And we need more good-paying jobs for our building trades.
Project Washington had guaranteed an all-union workforce, and as lawmakers we need to find ways to incentivize future companies who locate here to do the same.
Protecting our quality of life and creating good paying jobs are not at odds with one another.
In fact, they are intrinsically related, and as a State we need to show that we can do both and do both wisely and for the greater good.

Resources for Energy Bills
This week, I joined all of my Senate colleagues in sending a letter to the Public Service Commission asking them not to approve Delmarva’s latest rate increase request.
Families are already struggling to keep pace with the rising costs of essential goods and services, and the increased cost of gas and electricity is out of control.
In the meantime, here are some resources for you to help manage rising energy costs:
Energize Delaware Home Energy Check-Up and Counseling
Energize Delaware’s checkup program connects you with a professional advisor to assess your home’s energy efficiency.
This includes a review of your heating and cooling systems, insulation, and appliances.
After the review, your advisor will suggest energy-saving changes that are specific to your home.
They can also share potential rebates, repairs, and assistance you may qualify for.
This check-up is an eligibility requirement for the Delmarva Power Customer Relief Fund.
To learn more about how to qualify for a home energy check-up, click here.
Delmarva Power Customer Relief Fund
The Delmarva Power Customer Relief Fund was recently established to support customers of limited income (up to 60% of the state median income) or moderate income (between 200% and 350% of the federal poverty line).
In order to qualify, customers must complete the Energize Delaware Home Energy Checkup and Counseling program.
Moderate income customers must also carry a balance of at least $250.
If a customer is approved, they will be awarded a one-time credit up to $500 towards their energy bill.
To learn more about the fund and if you qualify, click here.
Energize Delaware “Home Performance with Energy Star” Assessments
Through the Energy Star Assessment program, homeowners can apply for rebates for energy-efficiency upgrades installed by a participating contractor to reduce out-of-pocket costs for improvements.
To learn more about the program, click here.

Community Resources
The Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles is urging the public not to click on links in suspicious text messages.
Remember: DMV and E-ZPass will never request payment via text message.
This latest reminder comes after a new Short Message Service (SMS) phishing scam has surfaced referencing the Delaware Division of Motor Vehicles.
The scam alert states the recipient has an outstanding traffic violation and needs to “click on a link” to pay the amount due by a specific date to avoid the suspension of their vehicle registration and driving privileges.
These text message alerts are a scam designed to trick recipients into paying an undisclosed amount, while also gaining access to their personal, private, and financial data.
The DMV urges all residents and customers not to click on any links soliciting money for unpaid traffic violations, toll violations, or account replenishment.
The Delaware DMV and E-ZPass Delaware will not contact you via email or text demanding payment for a violation.

Fans Don’t Let Fans Drive Drunk.
The Delaware Safety Council is urging drivers to be safe this weekend.
Super Bowl Sunday is consistently one of the most dangerous days of the year for impaired driving.
National data shows that approximately 45% of all fatal crashes on Super Bowl Sunday involve alcohol, significantly higher than the daily average of 30%.
Risk increases markedly during evening and overnight hours, particularly between 9:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m.
Please be careful and do not drive impaired this Super Bowl Sunday.
