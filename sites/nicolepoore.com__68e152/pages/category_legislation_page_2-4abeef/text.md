The Delaware Office of Women’s Advancement & Advocacy is now accepting nominees for this year’s class of Delaware’s Women’s Hall of Fame and the She’s On Her Way Award!
The Delaware Women’s Hall of Fame was established in 1981 to recognize the achievements of outstanding Delaware women and their contributions to communities across the state.
Members represent a variety of backgrounds and fields including artists, athletes, community advocates, public servants, military personnel, and scientists.
There is no minimum age requirement, but nominees must have made an important and lasting impact on the lives of Delawareans, and must have resided in Delaware for at least 10 years during their lifetime.
The She’s On her Way Award celebrates the achievements and contributions of talented young women leaders making a difference in Delaware.
All nominees must be between the ages of 18 and 40 by June 30, 2025.
Nominees must also be native-born Delawareans or have resided in Delaware for at least five years.
Nominees must show that they have made a positive impact upon the lives of Delawareans and have exhibited characteristics of good character, promise and hope; as well as have information and or examples that illustrate the nominee’s positive reputation and or leadership among her peers, and or her community.
You can help celebrate and recognize the achievements of Delaware women by submitting nominations.
Nomination forms are available online at de.gov/women .
The deadline for nominations is midnight on March 31, 2025.

Senator Poore 2024 Legislative Recap October 28, 2024 admin Business & Economic Development/Policy , Consumer Protection , Education , Guns , Health and Social Services , Legislation Comments are Closed 0 SB 334 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 11 AND TITLE 28 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE UNLAWFUL DISCHARGE OF FIREARMS Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Introduced on June 25, 2024, SB 334 amends sections of Titles 11 and 28 of the Delaware Code concerning the unlawful discharge of firearms.
It addresses the risks posed by discharging firearms during events like target shooting near residential and business areas, aiming to reduce the risk of injury or death.
The bill provides conditions under which firearms can be discharged, such as using artificial or natural barriers.
It also reduces the required distance for archery equipment, and exempts land designated for training by specific agencies like the Delaware National Guard or law enforcement.
The bill aligns with existing safety zone regulations for hunting and repeals a section related to the regulation of shooting galleries.
Status : Awaiting consideration in the Senate Judiciary Committee SB 331 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 16 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE UNIFORM CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES ACT Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Amends the Delaware Code’s Uniform Controlled Substances Act, specifically sections 4732 and 4733.
This bill regulates the manufacture, distribution, and dispensing of controlled substances in Delaware.
It requires out—of—state practitioners— licensed through practice privilege, interstate compact, telehealth, or military registration—must also obtain a Delaware-controlled substance registration to prescribe such substances within the state.
This ensures that these practitioners are subject to Delaware’s regulations when prescribing controlled substances to Delaware patients.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/29/2024.
SB 306 w/SA1, SA2 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 19 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO WORKPLACE SAFETY PROGRAM.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Amends Title 19 of the Delaware Code, updating the state’s workplace safety program.
Under existing law, employers who pay $3,161 or more annually in workers’ compensation premiums may qualify for reduced insurance premiums through the workplace safety program.
This act modifies eligibility criteria, applying to employers who qualify under the uniform experience rating plan, as approved by the Insurance Commissioner, or under parameters set by regulation.
Additionally, when determining safety credits for employers not previously experience-rated, the formula will now incorporate amounts determined by the Insurance Commissioner through regulation.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 7/17/2024 SB 281 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 14 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE DELAWARE INTERSCHOLASTIC ATHLETIC ASSOCIATION Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Amends Title 14 of the Delaware Code and stems from the Delaware Interscholastic Athletic Association (DIAA) Task Force findings.
The 152nd General Assembly established this Task Force through Senate Concurrent Resolution 19 to study and recommend solutions for improving the DIAA’s functionality and addressing the needs of student-athletes.
After eight months of deliberations, the Task Force identified the waiver process for students transferring between schools as a key issue.
This Act specifically addresses that concern, focusing on how student transfers impact eligibility for interscholastic athletics.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/2/2024 SB 280 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 14 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE DELAWARE INTERSCHOLASTIC ATHLETIC ASSOCIATION Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Amends Title 14 of the Delaware Code and results from the Delaware Interscholastic Athletic Association (DIAA) Task Force’s findings.
This Act elevates the DIAA’s Executive Director position from an education associate level to a director-level role within the Department of Education.
This change reflects the Task Force’s recommendations for enhancing the leadership and oversight of the DIAA to better support interscholastic athletics in Delaware.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/2/2024 SS2 for SB 278 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLES 6 AND 21 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO COMMERCE AND TRADE.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act amends Titles 6 and 21 of the Delaware Code concerning motor vehicle dealers.
It clarifies sections of Chapter 49 (Title 6) and Chapter 63 (Title 21) to enhance Delaware’s franchise laws.
The modifications aim to support a stable, reliable, and competitive retail network for automobile sales and service in the state, ensuring fairness in relationships between dealers, manufacturers, and distributors while prioritizing consumer protection.
The changes reinforce a balanced, equitable approach to the motor vehicle industry in Delaware.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 9/24/2024 SB 277 – AN ACT TO AMEND CHAPTER 53, TITLE 24 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO MASSAGE AND BODYWORK.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act amends Chapter 53, Title 24 of the Delaware Code, relating to the practice of massage and bodywork.
The bill stipulates that the Board of Massage and Bodywork will no longer issue initial certifications for individuals seeking to practice as certified massage technicians for applications received after December 31, 2024.
This change marks a shift in the certification process for new massage practitioners in Delaware.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/29/2024 SB 272 w/SA 1 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLES 18 AND 29 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO HEALTH INSURANCE, AND TITLE 31 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO PHARMACIST CARE Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act amends Titles 18 and 29 of the Delaware Code relating to health insurance and Title 31 regarding pharmacist care.
The bill ensures that pharmacists, who already provide services such as immunizations, prescribing birth control, and HIV prophylaxis, are recognized as a provider type for health insurance reimbursement.
Currently, pharmacists offer many of the same services as physicians and nurse practitioners but are not reimbursed similarly by insurance providers.
This Act mandates that pharmacists be reimbursed at the same rates as advanced practice nurses and physician assistants for equivalent services.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 9/24/2024 SB 260 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 14 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION AND THE DELAWARE INTERSCHOLASTIC ATHLETIC ASSOCIATION Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act stems from the work of the Delaware Interscholastic Athletic Association (“DIAA”) Task Force.
It modifies the process for creating regulations related to interscholastic athletics by simplifying the current structure.
Previously, the Department of Education (“Department”) held the regulatory authority, which was then delegated to DIAA.
This process also included extra steps beyond those required under the Administrative Procedures Act, delaying regulatory changes needed for interscholastic athletics.
The Act removes certain regulatory oversight by the Department.
It grants DIAA direct authority to promulgate its own regulations, streamlining the process and providing more flexibility for adapting to the needs of student athletics.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/2/2024 SB 259 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 14 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE DELAWARE INTERSCHOLASTIC ATHLETIC ASSOCIATION Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act is a result of the Delaware Interscholastic Athletic Association (“DIAA”) Task Force.
It modernizes the DIAA Board of Directors composition to better align with the current needs of interscholastic athletics in Delaware.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 9/30/2024 SB 209 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 29 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE DIVISION OF FORENSIC SCIENCE AND THE DEFINITION OF NEXT OF KIN Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act amends Chapter 47 of Title 29 of the Delaware Code, expanding the definition of “next of kin” within the Division of Forensic Science to include adult siblings of a decedent.
This change allows adult siblings to receive the personal property of a decedent if there are no other next of kin.
Additionally, it enables the Medical Examiner to provide a post-mortem examination report to an adult sibling upon written request when no other next of kin is present.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 6/20/2024 SB 207 – AN ACT TO AMEND CHAPTER 170, VOLUME 84 OF THE LAWS OF DELAWARE RELATING TO THE UNIFORM CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES ACT AND DRUG TESTING STRIPS.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act eliminates the sunset provision in Chapter 170, Volume 84 of the Laws of Delaware related to xylazine.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 6/20/2024 SB 196 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 16 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO 24/7 EMERGENT PHYSICIAN CARE CENTERS.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This legislation establishes a voluntary certification program for 24/7 Emergent Physician Care Centers and is designed to help Delawareans choose the right level of walk-in medical care.
The importance of 24/7 Physician Care Centers in the delivery of urgent and emergent health care services in times of crisis was exhibited during the recent COVID-19 Pandemic.
Currently, Delawareans need assistance in determining the varying levels of services available at urgent care facilities.
This legislation creates the category of and defines the minimum level of services necessary for 24/7 Emergent Physician Care Centers.
The minimum requirements for 24/7 Emergent Physician Care Centers include being open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, having a Delaware licensed physician and registered nurse on-site at all times, providing lab and diagnostic imaging services, providing on-site splinting, fracture care, and suturing, and not refusing care to any person due to inability to pay or lack of identification.
Status: Awaiting action in Senate Health & Social Services Committee Senator Poore 2023 Legislative Recap October 28, 2024 admin Consumer Protection , Crime and Public Safety , Education , Health and Social Services , Legislation , Social Policy Comments are Closed 0 SB 189 w/SA 1 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 16 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE UNIFORM CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES ACT AND DRUG TESTING STRIPS.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act adds xylazine and its isomers, esters, ethers, salts, and salts of isomers, esters, and ethers to Schedule III of the Delaware Uniform Controlled Substances Act.
It expands the authorization for the distribution of testing strips to determine the presence of controlled substances.
According to the United States Drug Enforcement Administration, the emergence of xylazine across the United States appears to be following the same path as fentanyl, starting in the Northeast and then spreading to the South and working its way into drug markets westward.
The low cost of xylazine contributes to xylazine’s increased presence in the nation’s illegal drug supply.
Xylazine is approved only for veterinary use.
Xylazine is a non-opioid with increasing presence as an adulterant, often in conjunction with opioids, in the illicit drug supply.
When used alone and in conjunction with other drugs, xylazine is implicated as a cause or contributing cause of death in the United States.
When used in conjunction with an opioid, such as heroin or fentanyl, xylazine may worsen respiratory depression in the event of a drug overdose, and because xylazine is not an opioid, naloxone is not known to be effective at reversing overdoses, and there is no known antidote or reversal agent for xylazine overdose in humans.
This Act also exempts testing strips from the drug paraphernalia statute.
It expands the limitations on liability for lay individuals and organizations that provide a drug testing strip to an individual who uses drugs to reduce the likelihood of the individual experiencing harm.
This Act becomes effective upon the expiration of the Emergency Order issued by the Secretary of State placing Xylazine in Schedule III of the Uniform Controlled Substance Act or November 29, 2023, whichever is earlier.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/17/2023 SB 165 w SA1 & 2 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 24 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO PHARMACY.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act amends Chapter 25, Title 24 of the Delaware Code relating to the Practice of Pharmacy.
The amendments authorize pharmacists to engage in “collaborative pharmacy practice” with one or more “practitioners,” meaning individuals authorized by law to prescribe drugs in the course of professional practice.
Pharmacists may also do so pursuant to a “collaborative pharmacy practice agreement,” which means a written and signed agreement between one or more pharmacists and one or more practitioners that provides for collaborative pharmacy practice.

Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/31/2023 SS 2 for SB 72 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 30 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO PERSONAL INCOME TAX Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly In 2017, the federal Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), Public Law No.
115-97, suspended many itemized deductions from individual federal taxes until January 1, 2026, including the itemized deduction for costs to maintain membership in a labor organization.
This Act amends Delaware’s tax code to allow an itemized deduction, up to $500 annually, for the cost of maintaining membership in a labor organization, provided the individual has not already claimed this deduction on their federal income tax return.
It excludes payments for employee benefits, pensions, lobbying, or legal costs from the deduction.
Unlike previous versions, this Act does not sunset, as it prevents double-dipping if federal law restores a similar deduction.

Health & Safety Announcements The Delaware State Fire Marshal’s Office would like to remind Delawareans that home fires occur more in the winter months than any other season throughout the year.
In fact, half of all home heating fires occur in December, January, & February.
Nationwide, heating equipment is involved in 1 out of every 6 home fires and 1 in every 5 fire deaths.
Please follow these winter fire safety tips to keep you and your family safe this winter season.
Heating Keep anything that can burn at least 3 feet from any heat source like fireplaces, wood stoves, radiators, or space heaters.
Plug only 1 heat-producing appliance (ex. space heater) into an electrical outlet at a time.
Store cooled ashes in a tightly covered metal container.
Keep it outside at least 10 ft from your home and any nearby buildings.
Never use your oven to heat your home.
Carbon Monoxide Often called the invisible killer, carbon monoxide (CO) is an invisible, odorless, colorless gas created when fuels (such as gasoline, wood, coal, natural gas, propane, oil, and methane) burn incompletely.
In the home, heating and cooking equipment that burn fuel can be sources of carbon monoxide.
CO alarms should be installed in a central location outside each sleeping area and on every level of the home.
Test CO alarms at least once a month; replace them according to the manufacturer’s instructions.
If the CO alarm sounds, immediately move to a fresh air location outdoors or by an open window or door.
Make sure everyone inside the home is accounted for.
Call for help from a fresh air location and stay there until emergency personnel declare that it is safe to re-enter the home.
Only use gas or charcoal grills outside, as they can produce CO.
Portable Generators It’s important to know that portable generators emit carbon monoxide (CO), a poisonous, deadly gas.
Use portable generators outdoors in well-ventilated areas at least 20 ft from all doors, windows, and vent openings.
Never use a generator in an attached garage, even with the door open.
Place generators so that exhaust fumes are directed away from the building.
Turn off generators and let them cool down before refueling.
Never refuel a generator while it is hot.
Other Reminders Have a qualified professional clean and inspect your chimney and vents every year.
During and after a snowstorm, make sure vents for the dryer, furnace, stove, and fireplace are clear of snow build-up.
If you need to warm a vehicle, remove it from the garage immediately after starting it.
Do not run a vehicle or other fueled engine or motor indoors, even if garage doors are open.
Make sure the exhaust pipe of a running vehicle is not covered with snow.
