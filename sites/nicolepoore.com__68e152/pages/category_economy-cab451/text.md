Sincerely, Nicole Poore Senator, District 12 News from Legislative Hall Our neighbors deserve a seat at the table when it comes to large-scale data center development in our communities.
Across the nation, these projects are being built on the backs of every day families who are left to shoulder the environmental and financial consequences, and that is why I introduced SB 312 .
As legislators, it is our responsibility to protect our residents, our environment, and the future we are leaving for the younger generation.
Economic development should never come at the expense of the people who call Delaware home, and it is my hope that this legislation cements those protections and expands transparency on these projects.
Thank you to Bay to Bay news for covering this legislation and the ongoing conversations being had across our state.
With the support of my colleagues in the House and Senate, I introduced and passed SCR 176 to recognize the importance of Diabetic Retinopathy screenings .
In April, the state of Delaware held its first-ever statewide screening event, expanding vision-care access for Delawareans living with diabetes.
The event was a success, and I had the pleasure of hosting two amazing optometrists at Legislative Hall for the passing of SCR 176.
Thank you to Dr.
Paula Ko , President of the Delaware Academy of Ophthalmology, and Dr.
Alissa Long , President of the Delaware Optometric Association for your advocacy and joining me in Dover.
For far too long, women’s menopause and perimenopause symptoms have consistently been dismissed and downplayed by medical professionals.
Rep.
Kam Smith and I introduced Senate Bill 319 because it is time for women’s pain to be heard, taken seriously, and supported through every stage of life.
SB 319 will bring Delaware into alignment with our neighboring states and make healthcare more accessible for Delawareans experiencing menopause and perimenopause symptoms.

FORT DELAWARE TOUR WITH DNREC I was honored to join DNREC Secretary Greg Patterson at Fort Delaware as part of his “Amazing Park Race” journey across Delaware’s state parks.
Secretary Patterson set out to visit all 17 of Delaware’s beautiful state parks to learn more about their history, the communities they serve, and the important role they play throughout our state.
It was a pleasure to accompany him during his visit to Fort Delaware and share the rich history of this remarkable landmark, including its significance as Delaware’s second-oldest state park.
Fort Delaware is more than a historic site, it’s a source of pride for Delaware City and the surrounding communities.
Its rich history, educational opportunities, and tourism impact help connect visitors to Delaware’s past while supporting our local economy today.
Thank you, Secretary Patterson, for taking the time to experience and learn about one of Delaware’s treasured state parks.

Sincerely, Nicole Poore Senator, District 12 Special Session on Tax Reassessments Special Session to be held on August 12 Delaware’s recent property tax reassessment sent shockwaves through New Castle County, as many homeowners saw their school tax bills spike while the burden on non-residential properties dropped.
On Tuesday, the Senate will take action to address this imbalance with legislation that delivers immediate relief to those most affected.
You can read more here .
The proposal allows taxpayers to set up payment plans without late-fee penalties, requires counties to share ongoing data to pinpoint who is most impacted, and directly tackles the school tax shift unique to New Castle County.
The Senate will also consider a resolution committing to a full review of the reassessment process — likely paving the way for further reforms in the 153rd General Assembly.
This timely, responsible relief package reflects extensive conversations with school districts, county officials, and other stakeholders, and should earn broad support.
I look forward to convening with my colleagues in Dover this week to address these issues in their entirety.
My colleagues and I in the Senate will work closely with our partners in the House to craft thoughtful, lasting solutions to this turbulent issue that will serve all Delawareans.

Updates from Town Halls Starwood Data Center On July 24 I co-hosted a town hall meeting with Speaker Melissa Minor-Brown to discuss the proposed data center being built in New Castle County.
The data center, backed by Starwood Digital Ventures, would be an 11-building wide facility that spans over 6 million square feet and built in Delaware City.
Starwood discussed the potential employment opportunities, the environmental impact, and the effects it would have on energy bills.
Representatives from Starwood Digital Ventures shared their proposal with the community — and yes, it did raise a number of unanswered questions.
As our counterparts on County Council have said, this project is just in the earliest phase.
And thank goodness for that, because that gives County Council time to make the needed policy changes to better address the challenges posed by this massive proposed data center.
We will continue to work with the city and county councils to investigate the creation of the data center, and will keep our residents and community members updated.
Air Monitor Implementations Thanks to the hard work from my colleagues on the Bond Committee, and DNREC Secretary Greg Patterson, and Founder of CHEC Penny Dryden, additional funding has been added to the FY 2026 Bond Bill to expand community air monitoring efforts.
Penny Dryden has been a champion of these monitors since the beginning of this process, and due to recent events, has made this project a priority for our communities.
With input from residents, DNREC, and other environmental leaders, Delaware is taking clear steps to ensure future accountability.
Some of these new monitors will be placed near the Delaware City refinery — joining the number of air monitors that already exist in that location.
I look forward to seeing the implementation of these new monitors, and I am so proud of the collective work we have accomplished in protecting our community.
Around the 12th Senate District Route 40 Widening Project Construction on the Route 40 widening project have officially begun.
The Delaware Department of Transportation’s (DelDOT) multimodal improvement project will provide an additional travel lane in each direction from Salem Church Road to Walther Road.
Currently there are no closures or restrictions in this stage of the project, but be sure to keep up-to-date with updates on the DelDOT website here .
The project is expected to be completed by Winter 2027.
Route 9 Repaving and Repairs DelDOT is applying approximately 5 inches of permeable pavement overlay in several of the most flood prone areas of Route 9.
While this is a temporary solution to rising sea levels, it will give the traveling public some relief while short-term and long-term strategies are implemented throughout the corridor.
Here are the locations that are being addressed and the status of the construction: The following locations are completed: Route 9 – River Run location which is section south of Dobbinsville Route 299 – east of Odessa to Old Corbitt Road The following locations are under construction and should be completed this week: Silver Run Road – from Silver Run to Vance Neck Road Silver Run Road – Twin Bridges south of Vance Neck Road in the area of the Appoquinimink River According to the construction schedule, the next planned location is Bayside Drive.
