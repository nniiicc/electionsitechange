HB 242, SB 206, and How They Affect You In August, the 153rd General Assembly passed House Bill 242 — legislation that allowed New Castle County to reset the original tax rates set in July.
This legislation was the result from our special session held in Legislative Hall after the turbulent outcome of the Tyler Technologies tax reassessments that occurred earlier this year.
House Bill 242 “split” these rates into residential and non-residential rates, and also provided additional support to Delaware residents by offering payment plans and refunds for those who may have overpaid based off their original assessment.
We also passed Senate Bill 206, which extended the property tax payment deadline in New Castle County from November 30 to December 31, giving residents the time they need to review their bills with confidence.
FAQs With these changes, many residents have been left confused and questioning what they’re supposed to do regarding their taxes.
Many of these frequently asked questions are listed below: When are my taxes due?
As soon as the Governor signs Senate Bill 206, school and property taxes will now be due on December 31, 2025.
When will I receive my revised tax bill?
Now that the Delaware Supreme Court has issued a final determination in a case that challenged the school districts’ ability to use different tax rates for residential and non-residential properties, New Castle County is able to reissue school and property tax bills accounting for that distinction.
Revised bills will be mailed to property owners and mortgage companies beginning November 18-20, 2025.
Please note: this is the date bills are mailed, not the date they will arrive in your mailbox.
Revised tax amounts due will be available for view in Parcel Search (using the “View Tax and Sewer Bills” link at the top of each parcel’s entry) by November 20, 2025.
My mortgage company pays my school and property taxes.
Do they know a new tax bill is coming?
New Castle County has been in constant communication with mortgage companies, and these companies are aware the County will be issuing revised school and property tax bills on November 18-20, 2025.
The mortgage companies are also aware of the new December 31, 2025 due date for school and property tax payments.
A mortgage company typically takes three weeks to pay a property owner’s tax bill after receiving it.
Questions on how a property owner’s mortgage company may handle payments and escrow for each particular property is best directed to the mortgage company itself.
Do taxpayers need to do anything to receive a new tax bill?
No.
A revised tax bill will be issued and mailed to all parcel owners once the bills have been generated and are available.
The second bill will only reflect the amount of the adjustment created due to the new split school tax rate.
All allowable exemptions have already been calculated as part of the initial tax billing in July.
Please monitor your mail for correspondence from the County, as tax bills will begin to be sent between November 18-20, 2025.
What do I do if I already paid my County school and property tax bill?
The amount you previously paid will be credited against the second bill.
If you have a credit, the amount will be posted to your account.
You may request a refund if the credit balance is $50 or more.
A link to request a refund will be available once revised bills are posted on Parcel Search.
Please note that refunds are issued to the original payee. ﻿ ﻿﻿ ﻿If your mortgage company pays your school and property taxes and is owed a refund, we will refund the mortgage company directly.
You do not need to request a refund if your mortgage company pays your taxes for you, as the County will be working directly with the mortgage companies to refund your previous overpayment.﻿ ﻿﻿ ﻿In addition, if you pay your school and property taxes directly to New Castle County and are owed a refund, you may request a refund if the balance of your previous overpayment is over $50.
In the alternative, you may have your credit offset against your 2026 sewer bill.
There will be a refund form available on the website to provide you with these options.
If you choose not to request a refund or have your credit applied to your 2026 sewer bill, your previous overpayment will remain as a credit on your County account and offset future tax bills.
I haven’t paid my initial tax bill yet.
What do I do?
Your current tax obligation is the combination of both the initial July tax billing and the forthcoming revised November tax billing.
Both bills should be read in conjunction with one another to determine your full tax obligation.
Any balance outstanding from the first bill will carry over to the second bill, reflecting the total amount due.
How can taxpayers learn more about payment plan options?
New Castle County is committed to helping as many residents as possible.
A link to request a payment plan will be available once revised bills are posted.
Any residential taxpayer may enter into a payment plan for taxes due for their primary residence, and when the plan is completed as arranged will have all late penalties waived for the 2025-2026 annual tax bill.
Who qualifies for the payment plan due to economic hardship following the reassessment?
Owners of residential property who occupy the home.
A taxpayer who is in good standing with New Castle County (i.e., they are not delinquent on any other payment(s) owed to the County) that experienced an increase in residential property tax liability of no less than $50 when compared to the amount invoiced by the County in the prior tax year may qualify for a written installment payment plan.
In addition, per state legislation passed in August 2025, a residential property taxpayer may enter into a payment arrangement for school taxes if their school tax liability increased by $300 or more compared to the prior tax year’s school tax bill.
Are there any late fees or penalties to the payment plan?
No late fees, interest, or penalties may be assessed against a residential taxpayer who enters into and complies with a school tax payment plan.
Recent state legislation also reduces late payment penalties forschool taxes in New Castle County to 1% per month.
I think my property was classified for tax purposes incorrectly.
How can I change this?
New Castle County classifies all properties as residential or non-residential for tax billing purposes.
Within each of these classifications, properties can be classified as residential, farmland, except residential and exempt farmland if residential.
Properties can be classified as apartment, commercial, exempt commercial, industrial or utility if non-residential.
If you believe your property has been classified incorrectly, there are two ways to formally request a classification change: A property owner can complete this form and return by email to T axClassificationChallenge@newcastlede.gov ; or A property owner can email TaxClassificationChallenge@newcastlede.gov with the property’s parcel number or postal address, the reason why the challenged classification is factually erroneous, and a requested remedy.
A certified assessor will review the information and provide a response within 60 days.
Are appeals officially closed to challenge assessments?
Is there a possibility they will reopen?
The appeal window to challenge a property’s assessment for the 2025-2026 tax year is closed.
The next opportunity to file a formal appeal challenging a property’s assessed value will be after January 1, 2026, and the deadline to file will be March 14, 2026.
But, if any taxpayer (residential or non-residential) believes that their property’s tax classification is incorrect due to a factual error regarding the physical characteristics of the taxpayer’s property (such as square footage, number of bedrooms, condition, etc.), they may seek a correction to that error by contacting the County’s Assessment Office at assessment@newcastlede.gov or 302-395-5520 regardless of whether that taxpayer previously filed a timely appeal for the 2025-2026 tax year.
Shouldn’t we just revert back to prior assessed values (which were effective as of July 1, 1983) since this assessment is so inaccurate?
Returning to calculate taxes on 1983 assessed values in New Castle County would repeat the unconstitutional actions that prompted all three of Delaware’s counties to reassess property in the first place.
In addition to violating the Court of Chancery’s In re Delaware Public Schools Litigation decision and the subsequent resolutions reached between the County and the plaintiffs in that case, returning to 1983 values would likely cause valuation disparities and violate the Uniformity Clause of the Delaware Constitution.
It would also conflict with several sections of Titles 9 and 14 of the Delaware Code.
Operationally, it would destabilize the County’s and the local school districts’ budgets and operations.
Delaware law aims to correct errors in assessed value, offering multiple avenues for taxpayers to contest valuations and classifications.
Taxpayers were offered to participate in an informal appeal process in November and December 2024 (administered by the County’s third-party reassessment vendor) and can utilize the statutory assessment appeal process (which includes the right to appeal a decision to the Delaware Superior Court).
Taxpayers can also request corrections or challenge classifications through available administrative remedies.

Reminder: Parents’ Rights in Education During the 147th General Assembly, I introduced SB 299 , legislation that gives parents the freedom to opt their children out of the DCAS standardized test.
As the parent of a child with a cognitive disability, I understand that every child learns differently — and that each one’s abilities and potential cannot be captured by a test score.
I wanted school to be a place that enriched my son’s life, not one that mesured his worth against academic benchmarks.
My son’s value was never defined by a DCAS Alt-1 score.
Our family understood his strengths, challenges, and what success looks like for him — not what a test says it should be.
But this legislation wasn’t written just for parents like me.
It also supports students who are still learning English and may struggle to understand test questions, or children who experience test anxiety and cannot perform their best under pressure.
SB 299 is about giving parents the right to decide what’s best for their child’s academix journey.
As we enter a new school year, let’s continue to remember that every child is unique, and that education should lift them up, not limit them.
If you are interested in opting your child out of DCAS testing, please talk with their school administrator and teacher.

The Delaware Office of Women’s Advancement & Advocacy is now accepting nominees for this year’s class of Delaware’s Women’s Hall of Fame and the She’s On Her Way Award!
The Delaware Women’s Hall of Fame was established in 1981 to recognize the achievements of outstanding Delaware women and their contributions to communities across the state.
Members represent a variety of backgrounds and fields including artists, athletes, community advocates, public servants, military personnel, and scientists.
There is no minimum age requirement, but nominees must have made an important and lasting impact on the lives of Delawareans, and must have resided in Delaware for at least 10 years during their lifetime.
The She’s On her Way Award celebrates the achievements and contributions of talented young women leaders making a difference in Delaware.
All nominees must be between the ages of 18 and 40 by June 30, 2025.
Nominees must also be native-born Delawareans or have resided in Delaware for at least five years.
Nominees must show that they have made a positive impact upon the lives of Delawareans and have exhibited characteristics of good character, promise and hope; as well as have information and or examples that illustrate the nominee’s positive reputation and or leadership among her peers, and or her community.
You can help celebrate and recognize the achievements of Delaware women by submitting nominations.
Nomination forms are available online at de.gov/women .
The deadline for nominations is midnight on March 31, 2025.

Senator Poore 2024 Legislative Recap October 28, 2024 admin Business & Economic Development/Policy , Consumer Protection , Education , Guns , Health and Social Services , Legislation Comments are Closed 0 SB 334 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 11 AND TITLE 28 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE UNLAWFUL DISCHARGE OF FIREARMS Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Introduced on June 25, 2024, SB 334 amends sections of Titles 11 and 28 of the Delaware Code concerning the unlawful discharge of firearms.
It addresses the risks posed by discharging firearms during events like target shooting near residential and business areas, aiming to reduce the risk of injury or death.
The bill provides conditions under which firearms can be discharged, such as using artificial or natural barriers.
It also reduces the required distance for archery equipment, and exempts land designated for training by specific agencies like the Delaware National Guard or law enforcement.
The bill aligns with existing safety zone regulations for hunting and repeals a section related to the regulation of shooting galleries.
Status : Awaiting consideration in the Senate Judiciary Committee SB 331 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 16 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE UNIFORM CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES ACT Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Amends the Delaware Code’s Uniform Controlled Substances Act, specifically sections 4732 and 4733.
This bill regulates the manufacture, distribution, and dispensing of controlled substances in Delaware.
It requires out—of—state practitioners— licensed through practice privilege, interstate compact, telehealth, or military registration—must also obtain a Delaware-controlled substance registration to prescribe such substances within the state.
This ensures that these practitioners are subject to Delaware’s regulations when prescribing controlled substances to Delaware patients.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/29/2024.

Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly Amends Title 19 of the Delaware Code, updating the state’s workplace safety program.
Under existing law, employers who pay $3,161 or more annually in workers’ compensation premiums may qualify for reduced insurance premiums through the workplace safety program.
This act modifies eligibility criteria, applying to employers who qualify under the uniform experience rating plan, as approved by the Insurance Commissioner, or under parameters set by regulation.

After eight months of deliberations, the Task Force identified the waiver process for students transferring between schools as a key issue.
This Act specifically addresses that concern, focusing on how student transfers impact eligibility for interscholastic athletics.

The bill ensures that pharmacists, who already provide services such as immunizations, prescribing birth control, and HIV prophylaxis, are recognized as a provider type for health insurance reimbursement.
Currently, pharmacists offer many of the same services as physicians and nurse practitioners but are not reimbursed similarly by insurance providers.
This Act mandates that pharmacists be reimbursed at the same rates as advanced practice nurses and physician assistants for equivalent services.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 9/24/2024 SB 260 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 14 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION AND THE DELAWARE INTERSCHOLASTIC ATHLETIC ASSOCIATION Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act stems from the work of the Delaware Interscholastic Athletic Association (“DIAA”) Task Force.
It modifies the process for creating regulations related to interscholastic athletics by simplifying the current structure.
Previously, the Department of Education (“Department”) held the regulatory authority, which was then delegated to DIAA.
This process also included extra steps beyond those required under the Administrative Procedures Act, delaying regulatory changes needed for interscholastic athletics.

Status: Signed by the Governor on 6/20/2024 SB 196 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 16 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO 24/7 EMERGENT PHYSICIAN CARE CENTERS.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This legislation establishes a voluntary certification program for 24/7 Emergent Physician Care Centers and is designed to help Delawareans choose the right level of walk-in medical care.
The importance of 24/7 Physician Care Centers in the delivery of urgent and emergent health care services in times of crisis was exhibited during the recent COVID-19 Pandemic.
Currently, Delawareans need assistance in determining the varying levels of services available at urgent care facilities.
This legislation creates the category of and defines the minimum level of services necessary for 24/7 Emergent Physician Care Centers.
The minimum requirements for 24/7 Emergent Physician Care Centers include being open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, having a Delaware licensed physician and registered nurse on-site at all times, providing lab and diagnostic imaging services, providing on-site splinting, fracture care, and suturing, and not refusing care to any person due to inability to pay or lack of identification.
Status: Awaiting action in Senate Health & Social Services Committee Senator Poore 2023 Legislative Recap October 28, 2024 admin Consumer Protection , Crime and Public Safety , Education , Health and Social Services , Legislation , Social Policy Comments are Closed 0 SB 189 w/SA 1 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 16 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE UNIFORM CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES ACT AND DRUG TESTING STRIPS.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act adds xylazine and its isomers, esters, ethers, salts, and salts of isomers, esters, and ethers to Schedule III of the Delaware Uniform Controlled Substances Act.
It expands the authorization for the distribution of testing strips to determine the presence of controlled substances.
According to the United States Drug Enforcement Administration, the emergence of xylazine across the United States appears to be following the same path as fentanyl, starting in the Northeast and then spreading to the South and working its way into drug markets westward.
The low cost of xylazine contributes to xylazine’s increased presence in the nation’s illegal drug supply.
Xylazine is approved only for veterinary use.
Xylazine is a non-opioid with increasing presence as an adulterant, often in conjunction with opioids, in the illicit drug supply.
When used alone and in conjunction with other drugs, xylazine is implicated as a cause or contributing cause of death in the United States.
When used in conjunction with an opioid, such as heroin or fentanyl, xylazine may worsen respiratory depression in the event of a drug overdose, and because xylazine is not an opioid, naloxone is not known to be effective at reversing overdoses, and there is no known antidote or reversal agent for xylazine overdose in humans.
This Act also exempts testing strips from the drug paraphernalia statute.
It expands the limitations on liability for lay individuals and organizations that provide a drug testing strip to an individual who uses drugs to reduce the likelihood of the individual experiencing harm.
This Act becomes effective upon the expiration of the Emergency Order issued by the Secretary of State placing Xylazine in Schedule III of the Uniform Controlled Substance Act or November 29, 2023, whichever is earlier.

Click here for more info Dedication of the Equal Suffrage Study Club Historical Marker in Delaware City The Equal Suffrage Study Club was founded in 1914 by and for Black women advocating for women’s voting rights and the inclusion of Black women in the suffrage movement.
Earlier this month, we celebrated the Club’s ties to the 12th District.
As one of the most active suffrage organizations in the state, the Club led many lobbying efforts, marches, and community outreach initiatives.
On April 13, 1920, with the encouragement of two Delaware suffragettes, Florence Bayard Hilles and Alice Dunbar-Nelson, a verbal resolution in support of the 19th Amendment was passed by the Club right here in the 12th District, at a colored schoolhouse formerly located near the site of a newly erected historical marker along the Michael N.
Castle Trail.
It was an honor to join Rep.
Longhurst, Jackie Griffith, and Dick Carter to recognize the work and leadership of the Equal Suffrage Study Club, Mrs.
Bayarad Hilles, and Mrs.
Dunbar-Nelson, to secure our right to vote, advance women’s equality, and make our state a better place for all women.
Where to find the marker: South of Kathy’s Crab House along the Mike Castle Bike Trail in Delaware City.
Unveiling the Delaware Women’s Suffrage Centennial Monument at Legislative Hall Looking back 101 years, the ratification of the 19th Amendment seems so obvious to us now.
But like all rights won by women, our right to vote did not come without a fight.
Brave women, strong women, like Mary Ann Shadd Cary, Alice Dunbar-Nelson, Blanche Stubbs, Ethel Black and so many others whose names we know and whose names have been forgotten to time fought and fought hard to have their voices heard.
They were shunned.
They were arrested.
They took up legal action again and again and were told to sit down and shut up.
Nevertheless, they persisted.
Earlier this month, we celebrated their determination and their legacy that has allowed women not only to be empowered, but in power, with the dedication of a monument at Legislative Hall.
