Reminder: Parents’ Rights in Education During the 147th General Assembly, I introduced SB 299 , legislation that gives parents the freedom to opt their children out of the DCAS standardized test.
As the parent of a child with a cognitive disability, I understand that every child learns differently — and that each one’s abilities and potential cannot be captured by a test score.
I wanted school to be a place that enriched my son’s life, not one that mesured his worth against academic benchmarks.
My son’s value was never defined by a DCAS Alt-1 score.
Our family understood his strengths, challenges, and what success looks like for him — not what a test says it should be.
But this legislation wasn’t written just for parents like me.
It also supports students who are still learning English and may struggle to understand test questions, or children who experience test anxiety and cannot perform their best under pressure.
SB 299 is about giving parents the right to decide what’s best for their child’s academix journey.
As we enter a new school year, let’s continue to remember that every child is unique, and that education should lift them up, not limit them.
If you are interested in opting your child out of DCAS testing, please talk with their school administrator and teacher.

Legislative Updates House Bill 23 (HB 23) Human trafficking is the second largest and fastest-growing criminal industry in the world, with an estimated 27.6 million victims globally, including 1.1 million in the United States.
For years, I have worked with Rep.
Kim Williams to combat this issue and keep Delaware safe from trafficking practices.
Our collaboration has produced new legislation, House Bill 23 (HB 23) , which aims to strengthen Delaware’s laws against human trafficking.
This bill makes several important updates to better protect vulnerable individuals and hold traffickers accountable.
By expanding forfeiture laws, all offenders will be required to forfeit property used in or gained by their crimes.
HB 23 will also cement protection for minors by removing outdated language that implied minors could be charged with prostitution.
Delaware law has since recognized that minors involved in such situations are indeed victims, not criminals.
As legislators, it is our duty to govern with empathy, equity, and understanding — especially for this vulnerable population who deserve our support more than ever.
This legislation also allows courts to stay juvenile delinquency proceedings and connect minors with specialized services.
If the minors comply with these services, their charges may be dismissed after one year.
I am proud to continue working on legislation that helps those affected by human trafficking, and I look forward to future collaborations with my colleagues in the House and the Senate to keep Delaware safe.
House Bill 27 (HB 27) Rep.
Kim Williams and I have also passed legislation to support the Delaware Anti-Trafficking Action Council (DATAC) .
House Bill 27 (HB 27) makes several updates to enhance the Council’s effectiveness and transparency.
It will streamline governance by reducing the quorum requirement from 13 to 10 members, and clarifies voting procedures to ensure the Council can act efficiently.
There will also be an enhancement in accountability, by requiring the Council to report how all funding — state, federal, or otherwise — was spent during the previous fiscal year, which will increase financial transparency.
DATAC will also be able to hold virtual meetings, as long as a staff member is present, which makes meetings more accessible.
These changes ensure that the Council can operate more efficiently and transparently as it continues the critical work of preventing trafficking and supporting survivors in Delaware.

Community News & Resources To my constituents of New Castle County, I understand many of you are concerned about the recent property reassessments conducted by Tyler Technologies, and I want to provide you with some clarity on the situation.
These decisions were not made by the state legislature but rather ordered statewide by the Delaware Chancery Court in 2020.
The Court found that the property tax system in each county was unconstitutional due to the outdated violations – New Castle County’s properties in particular, had not been reassessed since 1983.
In response, the General Assembly passed House Bill 62 in 2023, mandating statewide property assessments at least every five years to avoid gaps like the decades-old one we’ve experienced.
HB 62 brings us in-line with common practices across the country, where “rolling assessments” take place every three to five years.
While the reassessments may seem unsettling, I want to reassure you that the process is intended to be revenue neutral.
This means that the reassessment is not designed to raise additional revenue for the county.
If your property’s valuation has increased, it doesn’t necessarily mean your taxes will increase proportionally.
In fact, the county is committed to ensuring no additional revenue is raised through this process.
Tyler Technologies was contracted by the County to conduct the reassessments and have issued data mailers containing your property’s characteristics and new assessed value.
For example, Mariner’s Watch homes have been assessed between $482,000 and $895,000, though recent sales in the area have ranged between $465,000 to $740,000.
I recognize these discrepancies and encourage you to review the data closely.
If you disagree with the new valuation, you have the right to appeal.
The revised value represents the fair market value as of July 2024.
To determine if your taxes will increase or decrease, compare your property’s reassessment to the average countywide increase.
Information on the average increase will be made available soon.
The deadline for submitting your appeals will be March 31 .
For more details on this issue, or to file your appeal, please visit here , where you can access further resources to guide you through the process.
Thank you for your patience and understanding.
Please feel free to reach out if you have any further questions or concerns.

According to the United States Drug Enforcement Administration, the emergence of xylazine across the United States appears to be following the same path as fentanyl, starting in the Northeast and then spreading to the South and working its way into drug markets westward.
The low cost of xylazine contributes to xylazine’s increased presence in the nation’s illegal drug supply.
Xylazine is approved only for veterinary use.
Xylazine is a non-opioid with increasing presence as an adulterant, often in conjunction with opioids, in the illicit drug supply.
When used alone and in conjunction with other drugs, xylazine is implicated as a cause or contributing cause of death in the United States.
When used in conjunction with an opioid, such as heroin or fentanyl, xylazine may worsen respiratory depression in the event of a drug overdose, and because xylazine is not an opioid, naloxone is not known to be effective at reversing overdoses, and there is no known antidote or reversal agent for xylazine overdose in humans.
This Act also exempts testing strips from the drug paraphernalia statute.
It expands the limitations on liability for lay individuals and organizations that provide a drug testing strip to an individual who uses drugs to reduce the likelihood of the individual experiencing harm.
This Act becomes effective upon the expiration of the Emergency Order issued by the Secretary of State placing Xylazine in Schedule III of the Uniform Controlled Substance Act or November 29, 2023, whichever is earlier.
