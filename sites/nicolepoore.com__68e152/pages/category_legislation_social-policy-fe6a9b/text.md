Sincerely, Nicole Poore Senator, District 12 Legislative Updates House Bill 1 (HB 1) As Chair of the Veterans Affairs Committee, I was proud to lead the charge on House Bill 1 in the Senate.
This is a game-changing bill that creates a Cabinet-level Office of Veterans Affairs here in Delaware – a long-overdue requirement for our vets.
This office will give our veterans the voice, support, and representation they’ve long deserved.
After years of advocacy by Rep.
Bill Carson and many others, we’re finally getting this across the finish line.
Thank you to my colleagues for standing with me and our vets.
I hope to bring this home with a unanimous vote in the House on Thursday!
If you are a vet or currently serving in our military, please join us in Legislative Hall on Thursday, June 26 to see this legislation pass in the Senate.
Senate Bill 174 (SB 174) For many of us, our pets are irreplaceable members of our families.
Sadly, too many animals find themselves in the hands of people who pose a serious risk to their welfare.
That’s why I’m proud to sponsor Senate Bill 174 , establishing an animal abuse offender list under the Office of Animal Welfare.
This legislation not only makes offenses of animal abuse public information – it also legally prohibits shelters from approving adoptions to people on the list.
House Bill 210 Introduced Earlier this month, I held a joint town hall meeting with Rep.
Minor-Brown , Delaware Department of Natural Resources and Environmental Control (DNREC) Secretary Greg Patterson , and the Delaware City Refinery General Manager Mike Capone to discuss the excess levels of sulfur dioxide that had been caused by a water leak in their boiler.
We wanted to come together to explain this issue, let us hear directly from our constituents, and inform everyone of our next steps collectively.
With the assistance of many legislators, including House sponsor Rep.
Lambert , we were able to introduce House Bill 210 , legislation that will create the Pollution Accountability Act.
This bill will increase penalties for environmental violations and directs shares of fines to the communities being affected by it.
HB 210 is a necessary step forward in protecting our environment and maintaining public health and safety.
By increasing the fine structure for our biggest polluters, we’re not only deterring future violations, but also directing revenue back into the neighborhoods most affected.

May freedom continue to ring—louder, stronger, and with purpose.

Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act adds xylazine and its isomers, esters, ethers, salts, and salts of isomers, esters, and ethers to Schedule III of the Delaware Uniform Controlled Substances Act.
It expands the authorization for the distribution of testing strips to determine the presence of controlled substances.
According to the United States Drug Enforcement Administration, the emergence of xylazine across the United States appears to be following the same path as fentanyl, starting in the Northeast and then spreading to the South and working its way into drug markets westward.
The low cost of xylazine contributes to xylazine’s increased presence in the nation’s illegal drug supply.
Xylazine is approved only for veterinary use.
Xylazine is a non-opioid with increasing presence as an adulterant, often in conjunction with opioids, in the illicit drug supply.
When used alone and in conjunction with other drugs, xylazine is implicated as a cause or contributing cause of death in the United States.
When used in conjunction with an opioid, such as heroin or fentanyl, xylazine may worsen respiratory depression in the event of a drug overdose, and because xylazine is not an opioid, naloxone is not known to be effective at reversing overdoses, and there is no known antidote or reversal agent for xylazine overdose in humans.
This Act also exempts testing strips from the drug paraphernalia statute.
It expands the limitations on liability for lay individuals and organizations that provide a drug testing strip to an individual who uses drugs to reduce the likelihood of the individual experiencing harm.
This Act becomes effective upon the expiration of the Emergency Order issued by the Secretary of State placing Xylazine in Schedule III of the Uniform Controlled Substance Act or November 29, 2023, whichever is earlier.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/17/2023 SB 165 w SA1 & 2 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 24 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO PHARMACY.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act amends Chapter 25, Title 24 of the Delaware Code relating to the Practice of Pharmacy.
The amendments authorize pharmacists to engage in “collaborative pharmacy practice” with one or more “practitioners,” meaning individuals authorized by law to prescribe drugs in the course of professional practice.
Pharmacists may also do so pursuant to a “collaborative pharmacy practice agreement,” which means a written and signed agreement between one or more pharmacists and one or more practitioners that provides for collaborative pharmacy practice.

Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/31/2023 SB 98 w/HA 1 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 16 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO THE DELAWARE HEALTH CARE COMMISSION HEALTH CARE PROVIDER LOAN REPAYMENT PROGRAM.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act allows dental clinicians who possess a DDS or DMDS to participate in the Health Care Provider Loan Repayment grant program.
The Act also extends the time in which providers may apply for a Health Care Provider Loan Repayment grant from six months to two years following completion of their graduate education.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/31/2023 SS 2 for SB 72 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 30 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO PERSONAL INCOME TAX Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly In 2017, the federal Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), Public Law No.
115-97, suspended many itemized deductions from individual federal taxes until January 1, 2026, including the itemized deduction for costs to maintain membership in a labor organization.
This Act amends Delaware’s tax code to allow an itemized deduction, up to $500 annually, for the cost of maintaining membership in a labor organization, provided the individual has not already claimed this deduction on their federal income tax return.
It excludes payments for employee benefits, pensions, lobbying, or legal costs from the deduction.
Unlike previous versions, this Act does not sunset, as it prevents double-dipping if federal law restores a similar deduction.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 8/31/2023 SB 70 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 10 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO ANIMALS IN PROTECTION FROM ABUSE PROCEEDINGS.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This Act adds the following acts against a person’s companion animal or service animal to the definition of abuse for protection from abuse proceedings: 1.
Engaging in cruelty.
2.
Inflicting physical injury.
3.
Engaging in a course of alarming or distressing conduct.
In addition, this Act provides specific authority for the Court to include provisions in a protection from abuse order that grant a petitioner exclusive care, custody, or control of a companion animal and order a petitioner to stay away from the companion animal.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 6/27/2023 SB 55 w/SA1 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 29 OF THE DELAWARE CODE ESTABLISHING THE DELAWARE RARE DISEASE ADVISORY COUNCIL.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly This bill establishes the Delaware Rare Disease Advisory Council, which, among other things, is intended to educate medical professionals, government agencies, legislators, and the public about rare diseases.
About 7,000 known rare diseases create major public health challenges.
The Advisory Council is charged with engaging in activities intended to benefit rare disease patients in Delaware, including encouraging and securing funding to develop new treatments for rare diseases.
Status: Signed by the Governor on 7/26/2023 SB 32 w/SA1 – AN ACT TO AMEND TITLE 14 OF THE DELAWARE CODE RELATING TO EDUCATION.
Bill Detail – Delaware General Assembly The bill adds visual impairments, including blindness, to the list of programs to be conducted on a 12-month schedule.

While all healthcare professionals play a critical role in our lives, pharmacists are increasingly being asked to provide basic medical services that were once left to physicians, nurses and nursing assistants.
That includes administering vaccines, prescribing birth control and HIV preventatives and treatments, as well as testing and treating for a variety of conditions.
Pharmacists deserve to be reimbursed for their services at the same level as other medical professionals.

Are you a Delaware business owner?
Delaware EARNS, a new retirement savings program for small businesses and non-profits, launched on July 1.
The Delaware General Assembly passed legislation in 2022 that created EARNS, which brings coverage to the estimated 150,000 Delaware workers without retirement savings.
Administered by the Office of State Treasurer Colleen Davis, the EARNS program allows employers to give their employees access to retirement savings accounts at no cost to the employer.
Employers have until October 15 to register for EARNS or certify they are exempt.
Visit https://earnsdelaware.com/ for more information.
