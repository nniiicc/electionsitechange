Sincerely, Nicole Poore Senator, District 12 On March 28, DNREC Secretary Greg Patterson signed a final agreement with the Clean Air Council (CAC) that aims to bring in additional funding for the 2026 Implementation Plan.
This agreement will bring $250,000 in Bond Bill funding along with another $240,000 in DNREC penalty funds, which replaces the rescinded EPA grant funding.
The framework’s main goal is to track CEC’s — both naturally occurring and human-made — that pose health risks to people and wildlife.
To monitor the presence of these CEC’s, we will need to track not only PFAS, but also pharmaceuticals, microplastics, pesticides, and substances still unknown to be a threat.
With the implementation of this project, residents in the area can expect to see new air monitoring equipment installed in the immediate area.
Argos Scientific, a consultant chosen by the CAC, will be installing these monitoring systems at two separate locations within three miles of the refinery over the next five months.
The sensors in the monitors will track key pollutants, including: benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, xylenes, and sulfur dioxide.
For over a decade, Delaware has worked to address PFAS contamination, along with other airborne health threats, and sought policy action that can correct and improve the health effects these contaminants have on our communities.
This Implementation Plan is our most current course of action in correcting these issues, and expands on the previous work already accomplished since 2016.
The CAC will make their findings available to the public in monthly newsletters, as well as inviting the community to join in on these discussions in quarterly community meetings.
This initiative is an important step toward improving air quality oversight, increasing transparency, and ensuring that our community voices are part of this process.
Thank you to DNREC and our other state agencies for prioritizing this critical work.
I look forward to seeing the effects of this plan, and will keep the community updated on its progress.

Reminder: Parents’ Rights in Education During the 147th General Assembly, I introduced SB 299 , legislation that gives parents the freedom to opt their children out of the DCAS standardized test.
As the parent of a child with a cognitive disability, I understand that every child learns differently — and that each one’s abilities and potential cannot be captured by a test score.
I wanted school to be a place that enriched my son’s life, not one that mesured his worth against academic benchmarks.
My son’s value was never defined by a DCAS Alt-1 score.
Our family understood his strengths, challenges, and what success looks like for him — not what a test says it should be.
But this legislation wasn’t written just for parents like me.
It also supports students who are still learning English and may struggle to understand test questions, or children who experience test anxiety and cannot perform their best under pressure.
SB 299 is about giving parents the right to decide what’s best for their child’s academix journey.
As we enter a new school year, let’s continue to remember that every child is unique, and that education should lift them up, not limit them.
If you are interested in opting your child out of DCAS testing, please talk with their school administrator and teacher.

Sincerely, Nicole Poore Senator, District 12 Special Session on Tax Reassessments Special Session to be held on August 12 Delaware’s recent property tax reassessment sent shockwaves through New Castle County, as many homeowners saw their school tax bills spike while the burden on non-residential properties dropped.
On Tuesday, the Senate will take action to address this imbalance with legislation that delivers immediate relief to those most affected.
You can read more here .
The proposal allows taxpayers to set up payment plans without late-fee penalties, requires counties to share ongoing data to pinpoint who is most impacted, and directly tackles the school tax shift unique to New Castle County.
The Senate will also consider a resolution committing to a full review of the reassessment process — likely paving the way for further reforms in the 153rd General Assembly.
This timely, responsible relief package reflects extensive conversations with school districts, county officials, and other stakeholders, and should earn broad support.
I look forward to convening with my colleagues in Dover this week to address these issues in their entirety.
My colleagues and I in the Senate will work closely with our partners in the House to craft thoughtful, lasting solutions to this turbulent issue that will serve all Delawareans.

The Delaware Office of Women’s Advancement & Advocacy is now accepting nominees for this year’s class of Delaware’s Women’s Hall of Fame and the She’s On Her Way Award!
The Delaware Women’s Hall of Fame was established in 1981 to recognize the achievements of outstanding Delaware women and their contributions to communities across the state.
Members represent a variety of backgrounds and fields including artists, athletes, community advocates, public servants, military personnel, and scientists.
There is no minimum age requirement, but nominees must have made an important and lasting impact on the lives of Delawareans, and must have resided in Delaware for at least 10 years during their lifetime.
The She’s On her Way Award celebrates the achievements and contributions of talented young women leaders making a difference in Delaware.
All nominees must be between the ages of 18 and 40 by June 30, 2025.
Nominees must also be native-born Delawareans or have resided in Delaware for at least five years.
Nominees must show that they have made a positive impact upon the lives of Delawareans and have exhibited characteristics of good character, promise and hope; as well as have information and or examples that illustrate the nominee’s positive reputation and or leadership among her peers, and or her community.
You can help celebrate and recognize the achievements of Delaware women by submitting nominations.
Nomination forms are available online at de.gov/women .
The deadline for nominations is midnight on March 31, 2025.
