Jobs for Delaware Graduates (JDG) is one of the nation’s oldest and most established youth workforce development organizations.
It was created in 1978 under the leadership of Governor Pierre S. du Pont IV to help Delaware students successfully transition from school to careers and postsecondary education.
Delaware became the first state to launch what became the national Jobs for America’s Graduates (JAG) model.
In 1981, the program’s success led to the establishment of Jobs for America’s Graduates through a White House initiative, creating a national network that has since expanded to more than 35 state affiliates across the country.
Today, JDG continues to serve thousands of Delaware students each year by providing career readiness, leadership development, mentoring, and workforce preparation programs that connect young people with opportunities for lifelong success.
When did Nicole become President of JDG?
Nicole accepted the position as President of Jobs for Delaware Graduates (JDG) in 2014.
Does holding both a legislative seat and leading JDG present a conflict of interest, and how is it handled?
To ensure complete adherence to state laws and ethical standards, proactive legal guidance was sought from the outset.
Formal legal opinions were requested and reviewed prior to accepting the position to confirm that holding the executive role alongside public service presented no ethical conflicts or violations.
How are legislative votes handled regarding the state budget and the Grant-in-Aid (GIA) process?
Transparency and legal compliance guide all legislative participation.
Additional legal counsel was formally sought specifically regarding voting on the state budget and the Grant-in-Aid bill.
Those legal opinions confirmed that participating and voting on broad legislative funding measures—which encompass numerous state organizations and programs rather than JDG alone—is fully permissible and compliant.
Does Nicole control the spending of JDG budget monies?
No.
Nicole does not unilaterally control or manage the allocation of JDG budget monies.
Financial administration and internal controls are managed through the organization’s Director of Finance and executive staff, operating under the direct oversight of JDG’s independent Board of Directors and subject to annual independent financial audits.
How is JDG’s governance and financial integrity managed?
JDG operates under a rigorous governance model designed to maintain complete transparency and accountability: Independent Board of Directors: The organization is guided by an independent Board of Directors comprising leaders from diverse professional backgrounds who oversee organizational policy, direction, and executive evaluation.
Annual Audits: To ensure financial transparency and proper stewardship of resources, an independent financial audit is conducted every single year.
How is executive compensation determined, and is it public information?
Yes.
As a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization, JDG’s financials and executive compensation are transparently reported annually via IRS Form 990.
Nicole’s salary reflects standard executive compensation for managing a statewide multi-million-dollar youth workforce development program serving thousands of students across dozens of schools, with the compensation package reviewed, approved, and set entirely by the organization’s independent Board of Directors.
