Fair Wages
Minimum wage in Indiana hasn’t increased from $7.25 an hour since 2009.
This only happened as a result of federal law.
Tipped employees only have to be paid $2.13.
There is also a provision that allows employees under the age of 20 to be paid $4.25 an hour for their first 90 days.
This is abhorrent.
All hard working Hoosiers deserve fair pay for their labor.
We must raise the minimum wage to $15 an hour across the board for all employees, regardless of age, training status, or if they receive tips.

Strong Public Education
Public schools are absolutely vital in giving every person equal opportunity for advancement in our society.
We all benefit from a more educated society.
Our lawmakers in Indiana chose to invest in charter schools and to offer private school vouchers with no income cap, meaning the wealthiest can now send their children to private schools on the taxpayer’s dime.
We need to follow the examples of other states with better education outcomes and invest in our public schools.
Healthcare
Healthcare is a human right.
The costs are too high.
The outcomes are too poor.
The accessibility is too low.
We are losing OB/GYN providers to other states, creating healthcare deserts that result in higher maternal and fetal fatality rates.
Access to preventative care and screenings not only lead to better outcomes by catching problems earlier, but they have been proven to save on healthcare costs down the road.
It’s time to start investing in the health of Hoosiers.
It’s time to lower drug costs and change people’s first thought when they get sick or injured from, “How am I going to afford this?” and let them focus on healing.
There are several solutions for this problem, a few that I support are expanding HIP or creating a public option.
We must also protect Hoosiers who are already overburdened with medical debt by protecting against wage garnishment and bankruptcy for procedures deemed medically necessary.
At a time when healthcare has become a growing threat in Indiana and nationwide, Indiana has chosen to limit the help given by cutting Medicaid benefits and adding so many obstacles to the process that many give up or delay the care they need.
We operate with a budget surplus and have a massive “rainy-day-fund.” Working Hoosiers being unable to access affordable, quality healthcare sounds like a storm to me.
Protecting LGBTQIA+
There is a targeted attack on LGBTQIA+ communities by elected officials on every level of government.
Data shows that queer individuals are particularly vulnerable to abuse and mental health challenges.
The suicide rates among these communities create a ripple effect of trauma.
Removing what little support and protections they have will only make things worse.
We must protect our LGBTQIA+ neighbors— starting with our children, who are especially vulnerable— with policy changes.
Put Our Kids First!
Universal school meals: 1/5 kids in Indiana worry about where their next meal is going to come from.
We could offer the highest quality education, but we can’t expect kids to focus on learning if they have to focus on where they're next meal will come from.
Universal childcare/preschool: Childcare costs are getting so far out of hand that Hoosiers are leaving the workforce and delaying having children.
We must address this in a real way instead of passing band-aid bills that pretend to improve the issue rather than fix it.
Child Safety Campaign: One of my highest priorities is to support the statewide Child Safety Campaign starting out of Michiana, a nonpartisan effort to make the safety and well-being of our children a top priority in our state legislature.
I have supported this campaign in my private life since it’s launch and it will remain a priority when I am a State Legislator.
When the lives of children are on the line, division caused by partisan politics is not an option.
Property Taxes
The property tax rate in Indiana is currently at a fixed rate, but our mortgages keep going up.
There are many reasons for this that are outside of our control.
My solution is to freeze any further increases and to base property tax rates on the value of the home at the time of sale for homeowners, that way Hoosiers know exactly what their future costs will be and their property will be protected from outside factors beyond their control, and the state can maintain its balanced budget without cutting services elsewhere.
Workers Protections
Our current State Senate is made up of people who employ other people.
What we’re missing in the halls of power is the voices and experiences of people who are at the mercy of the person or company that employs them.
The fear and powerlessness felt when you know that your ability to take care of your family is completely contingent on how your employer feels about you at any given moment is not freedom.
You should be able to trust that if you do your part, work hard, follow procedures, and do your job, you will be protected.
In a society where you need employment to access healthcare, feed your family, apply to rent, remain housed, or secure loans, you should be entitled to basic rights to protect you from wrongful termination.
All of us have heard at one time or another, “Well, Indiana is an ‘employment at will’ state so there’s nothing you can do.” It’s time we change that and write laws that protect the people that sacrifice the most to keep our communities running.
Labor and Union Rights
Democratizing workplaces is one of the strongest tools that workers have to keep the balance of power in check and to ensure their own futures.
Every employer benefit we enjoy today came from the work and sacrifice of labor unions.
It is essential that we, as a governing body, provide strong protections to those working to unionize, penalize union-busting actions and behaviors, and set an example of our support for unions with labor contracts.
Immigration
Our immigrant communities enrich our neighborhoods and make the United States what it is.
I support immigration reform, creating more efficient pathways to citizenship.
I condemn in the strongest manner the reign of terror that ICE is bringing on cities and communities across the country.
It only serves to waste resources desperately needed by our nation’s most vulnerable and puts the safety of all involved at risk.
I will do anything in my power, as a legislator and as a private citizen, to keep my neighbors safe, regardless of where they come from.
Cooperation with ICE in it’s current form is something I will vote against every time.
Data Centers
Hoosiers have made their stance on the development of large-scale data centers known.
We do not want them.
If it becomes clear that they are necessary for economic development, we need to put strong protections in place to ensure local communities aren’t sacrificed in the process.
My plan is to work with other legislators and communities to draft these protections and prepare us for the technological development coming our way to avoid repeating past mistakes.
Cannabis
Legalizing cannabis would add millions of dollars in tax revenue to our budget.
It would lessen the strain on our congested criminal justice system, allowing resources to be focused on removing violent offenders from the streets and the increased tax revenue could be used to strengthen social programs that have proven to reduce crime rates.
We continue to lose millions of dollars each year to out of state purchases.
Every year we wait to legalize is another year of lost potential.
