During the 89th Legislative Session, the Texas House passed an unprecedented $51 billion property tax relief package to ease the burden on Texas families, homeowners, small businesses and job creators.
The work is not finished, with Speaker Burrows working with lawmakers and state leaders to secure additional relief during the 2027 legislative session.

Under Speaker Burrows’ leadership, the Texas House passed additional tax reforms during the 89th Legislative Session to reduce the burden on families, job creators and taxpayers.
Permanently banned a state death tax in the Texas Constitution to help families keep property and assets after the loss of a loved one (HJR 2) Prohibited taxes on securities transactions and securities-related occupations to protect investments and support Texas’ economic competitiveness (HJR 4) Helped families and teachers save on school supplies by moving the back-to-school sales tax holiday to the first weekend in August (SB 1415) Eliminated taxes on vehicles inherited from a deceased family member (SB 2064) Created a centralized database of local tax rates and bond information to give taxpayers easier access to important tax and spending information (HB 103) Exempted animal feed from inventory taxes, helping reduce costs for livestock producers, feed suppliers and consumers (HB 1399) Kept more local tax dollars in the communities where they were generated by closing loopholes in housing finance corporation tax exemptions (HB 21) Helped counties stretch taxpayer dollars further by exempting public service vehicles from state motor fuel taxes on gas and diesel (HB 1109) Protected farmers and ranchers from losing valuable tax benefits when agricultural land changes ownership but continues to be used for agriculture (HB 1244) Provided tax relief for Texas’ exotic and game animal industry by exempting these animals from state sales and use taxes (HB 135) Eliminated taxes on intangible personal property, including stocks, trademarks, cryptocurrency and other intellectual property (HB 22) More Relief Ahead Despite the Texas Legislature delivering significant tax relief, rising property values and runaway local government spending continue to eat away at those gains.
Speaker Burrows believes there is more the state can do to improve transparency, strengthen taxpayer protections and identify opportunities for further relief.
As lawmakers prepare for the 2027 session, Speaker Burrows has directed House committees to explore a range of property tax issues, including appraisal practices, unchecked local government spending and ways to make the entire system more transparent, predictable and taxpayer friendly.
