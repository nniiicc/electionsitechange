The article covered Vic Mellor’s recent visit to Havana.
During the trip, Vic met with Raúl Guillermo Rodríguez Castro, the grandson of former Cuban leader Raúl Castro.
Vic traveled to Cuba in late May to deliver humanitarian supplies and held two lengthy conversations with Rodríguez Castro.
During those discussions, Vic presented a vision for updating Cuban policies.
He believes those changes could help both the Cuban people and Americans in Rhode Island prosper.
“He’s confident.
He wants the Cuban people to prosper,” Vic told USA Today .
“He kept saying it was an update, Cuba needs to update its policies.” Vic has been clear about the nature of the trip.
He is a private citizen and businessman, not a government official.
The State Department confirmed he is not authorized to speak on behalf of the United States government.
Vic has emphasized that he is not negotiating on behalf of the administration.
Instead, he describes his meetings as open conversations about potential opportunities.
“I’m not back-channeling and I’m not going to circumvent Marco Rubio,” Vic said.
“A conversation isn’t betrayal.
We’re having an open dialogue.
I don’t see how I could hurt anybody.” Vic has described the Cuban people as resilient.
Furthermore, he believes the United States and Cuba share an interest in greater prosperity.
As a Marine veteran and entrepreneur, Vic has built businesses from the ground up.
Now, he is exploring practical opportunities that could benefit American companies and workers.
Ultimately, Vic believes those opportunities could include businesses and workers in Rhode Island.
For him, the Cuba trip represents another effort to identify new possibilities through direct conversation and private-sector initiative.
