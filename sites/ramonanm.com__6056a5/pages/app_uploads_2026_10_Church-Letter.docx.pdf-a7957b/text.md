Dear Faith Leader, Pastor, and Priest, As New Mexico approaches the November election, I am writing with a simple request: please encourage the members of your congregation to participate in our civic process and, most importantly, to vote.
Let me be clear at the outset: this letter is not a request for you, your church, or your congregation to endorse any candidate.
It is an encouragement to remind God's people that we have a responsibility to be engaged in the communities in which He has placed us.
Scripture does not teach God's people to withdraw from their communities or remain passive about their welfare.
When the people of Israel were carried into captivity in Babylon, God gave them an important instruction through the prophet Jeremiah: “And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.”​ — Jeremiah 29:7 (KJV) Think about the circumstances in which those words were given.
God's people were living in a land that was not their own, under a government they had not chosen.
Yet God did not tell them simply to withdraw and wait.
He told them to pray for the community where they lived and to seek its welfare.
There is an important lesson in that instruction for us today.
We have been blessed to live in a nation where citizens have the opportunity to participate in choosing those who govern.
Voting is not the only way we serve our communities, but it is one of the most fundamental responsibilities entrusted to us as citizens.
Our faith should never lead us to indifference about what happens to our neighbors, our communities, our state, or our nation.
That is why I am asking New Mexico's pastors, priests, and faith leaders to encourage their congregations to pray, become informed, and vote.
Encourage your members to make certain they are registered.
Encourage them to learn about the candidates and the issues.
Encourage them to consider the decisions before them prayerfully and thoughtfully.
And when Election Day arrives, encourage them to exercise the precious right and responsibility of casting their ballot.
Churches and religious organizations may conduct nonpartisan voter education, voter-registration, and get-out-the-vote activities consistent with federal tax law.
Encouraging eligible citizens to participate in an election in a neutral, nonpartisan manner does not require supporting or opposing a candidate.
Again, I am not asking you to endorse a candidate.
I am asking you to encourage participation.

New Mexico needs its people to participate.

Pastors and faith leaders have a unique opportunity to remind people that citizenship should not be taken for granted.
