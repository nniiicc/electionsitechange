5 WAYS YOUR CHURCH CAN ENCOURAGE VOTING Without Endorsing a Candidate Faithful citizenship begins with participation.
Churches and faith leaders have an important opportunity to encourage members of their congregations to be informed, responsible citizens.
A church does not have to endorse a candidate or political party to encourage its members to participate in the electoral process.
Here are five practical ways your church can encourage voting while remaining nonpartisan: 1.
ENCOURAGE EVERY ELIGIBLE MEMBER TO VOTE Remind your congregation that voting is both a privilege and a responsibility of citizenship.
A simple message can be powerful: “Pray.
Become informed.
Participate.
Vote.” Encourage everyone who is eligible to cast a ballot—without telling them which candidate or political party to support.
2.
HELP PEOPLE REGISTER AND PREPARE TO VOTE Churches may conduct neutral, nonpartisan voter-registration and get-out-the-vote activities.
Consider providing information about: ●​ Voter registration ●​ Updating registration information ●​ Election dates and deadlines ●​ Print Sample Ballots for members ●​ Early voting ●​ Absentee voting ●​ Polling locations and voting hours Make voter assistance available equally, regardless of a person’s political views or party affiliation.
3.
PROVIDE NONPARTISAN VOTER EDUCATION Encourage members to learn about the offices, candidates, constitutional amendments, bond questions, and other matters appearing on their ballots.
Churches may provide nonpartisan voter-education materials, but those materials should not favor or oppose a candidate.

4.
PRAY FOR OUR COMMUNITIES AND THOSE WHO SERVE Scripture reminds God’s people to care about the welfare of the communities in which they live.
“And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.”​ — Jeremiah 29:7 (KJV) Consider setting aside time to pray for our communities, our state, our nation, election workers, public officials, candidates for office, and the wisdom of those who will cast ballots.
Prayer and civic participation are not mutually exclusive.
We can pray for our communities while also taking responsibility for the opportunities entrusted to us as citizens.
5.
MAKE PARTICIPATION PART OF YOUR CHURCH CULTURE Civic responsibility should extend beyond a single election.
Encourage members to remain engaged in their communities—to attend public meetings, understand the decisions being made by government, serve their neighbors, volunteer, communicate respectfully with elected officials, and participate in elections.
Faith should not produce passivity.
It should produce faithful service.
A SIMPLE MESSAGE FOR YOUR CONGREGATION Pray for our state and nation.​ Become informed about the candidates and issues.​ Participate in your community.​ And please—VOTE.
A Note for Churches Section 501(c)(3) organizations, including churches, are prohibited from participating or intervening in a political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to a candidate.
However, the IRS recognizes neutral, nonpartisan voter education, voter-registration, and get-out-the-vote activities as permissible.
Church leaders should ensure that church-sponsored election activities are conducted impartially and do not favor or oppose a candidate or political party.
