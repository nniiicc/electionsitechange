Dear New Mexico Business Leader, New Mexico businesses should be able to expect something basic from the Office of the Secretary of State: accurate records, timely filings, accessible customer service, and clear answers when assistance is needed.
The Secretary of State is much more than the state's chief election officer.
The Business Services Division is a critical part of New Mexico's commercial infrastructure.
It maintains corporate and other business records, UCC filings, agricultural liens, trademarks, notary commissions, apostilles and authentications, service-of-process records, and other legally significant filings.
The office also administers New Mexico's athlete-agent registration requirements.
These are not political responsibilities.
They are recordkeeping, compliance, and customer-service responsibilities that require attention to detail, knowledgeable employees, reliable technology, and accountability.
Today, Business Services faces a significant staffing challenge.
The Secretary of State's FY2028 Strategic Plan reports that the division is authorized for 20 positions, but an August 2026 snapshot showed only 13 filled—seven vacancies, representing a 35 percent vacancy rate.
At the same time, demand is substantial.
In 2025, Business Services handled 200,079 business transactions, 41,454 customer calls, 12,743 emails, and 2,228 walk-in customers.
Moving services online has helped, but technology does not eliminate the need for trained employees to review filings, resolve errors, answer questions, maintain records, and ensure compliance with the law.
A Customer-Service Mission My plan begins with restoring the 20 positions already authorized for Business Services.
Before asking taxpayers for additional positions, we should fill the positions already approved, cross-train employees, improve retention, and measure whether staffing matches the workload.
We will establish meaningful customer-service standards and publicly measure results: telephone answer and abandonment rates, email response times, filing processing times, unresolved backlogs, errors and rework, and customer complaints.
These are consistent with performance measures already identified in the strategic plan.
Technology should make government easier to navigate, not make it harder to reach a person.
We will continue improving online services while maintaining a clear pathway to knowledgeable staff when a business owner has a problem that cannot be resolved online.
New Responsibilities in a Changing Economy The commercial world is also changing.

The SOS currently processes Athlete Agent Registrations through its Business Services online filing system.
State law also gives the Secretary responsibilities involving registration, public records, compliance and inspection of certain records maintained by registered agents.
As NIL opportunities and the economic relationships surrounding student athletics expand, these existing responsibilities take on greater practical importance.
Student athletes, their families, universities, and businesses should be able to determine whether an athlete agent is properly registered and understand the protections provided by New Mexico law.
I would review the athlete-agent program, improve public access to registration information, and provide clear educational information for athletes, families, universities, and athletic programs.

Those revenues cannot simply be retained by the Secretary of State without legislative authority, but taxpayers and policymakers deserve to see clearly what the division collects, what services it provides, what those services cost, and whether performance standards are being met.
The Secretary of State is one of New Mexico's most important recordkeeping offices.
Whether maintaining a corporate filing, agricultural lien, athlete-agent registration, or election record, the principles should be the same: accuracy, transparency, security, timely service, and accountability.
My mission for Business Services is straightforward: answer the phone, answer the email, process filings accurately and promptly, protect our records, measure our performance, and be accountable for the results.
