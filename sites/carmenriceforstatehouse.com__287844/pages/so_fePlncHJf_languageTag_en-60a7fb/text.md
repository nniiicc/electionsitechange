It was Budget Week at the Capitol.
Instead of holding week-long sessions, the House and Senate attended combined Appropriations Committee meetings to hear from state agencies about their budget needs.
The General Assembly is constitutionally required to pass a balanced budget each year, so these meetings are very important.
Governor Kemp started the meetings by presenting his budget proposals, divided into two legislative parts: the Amended Fiscal Year 2026 (AFY 2026) budget, which allocates funds for the remaining part of this fiscal year ending on June 30th, and the Fiscal Year 2027 (FY 2027) budget, which sets state spending for the coming fiscal year starting July 1, 2026.
Here are a few budget examples: Governor Kemp’s AFY 2026 budget is set on a revised revenue estimate of $42.3 billion.
The FY 2027 budget is set on a revenue estimate of $38.5 billion.
Governor Kemp noted that more than $9.7 billion has been returned to Georgia taxpayers through rebates, income tax cuts, and other relief measures.
Building on this tax relief, the AFY 2026 budget includes a one-time tax rebate returning $1 billion to Georgia families.
Budget includes $625 million to provide a one-time salary supplement of $2,000 to full-time regular state employees.
To help pulp and paper mills in southeast Georgia recover from Hurricane Helene, a Georgia Timber Task Force has been launched to support recovery. $11 million is set aside. $218 million is allocated for the University System of Georgia. $325 million in the budget for the DREAMS scholarship, which will create a needs-based scholarship for university enrollment. $50 million for the State Housing Trust Fund to establish a homelessness grant program. $5 million to establish a defined contribution plan for State Law Enforcement Officers. $2.4 billion to expand and improve state interchange corridors and other transportation projects. $2.5 million to establish the Georgia Foster Care Scholarship, which provides postsecondary education grants to eligible students who are current or former foster youth.
Governor Kemp's Budget Reports Georgia Opts into the Federal Tax Credit Program for School Choice.
Governor Kemp has opted Georgia into the federal tax credit scholarship program for school choice, made possible by the passage of the One Big Beautiful bill last summer.
Georgia has a tax credit program called Georgia Goal, and this will be in addition to it.
Individual taxpayers can donate up to $1,700 to fund scholarships in exchange for a discount of the same amount on what they owe in federal taxes.
These tax savings can be used on private school tuition and expenses.
Tax Relief Bills Introduced.
House Bills 1000 and 1001 are two priorities highlighted in Gov.
Kemp’s State of the State address.
HB 1000 would provide one-time tax relief by issuing a credit to individual taxpayers based on income taxes paid in recent years, and HB 1001 would lower Georgia’s income tax rate to 5.19% to 4.99%.
