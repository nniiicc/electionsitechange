House Bill 52, the Veteran Spouse Homestead Exemption, has been sent to the governor!
This legislation extends the homestead tax exemptions to unremarried surviving spouses of disabled veterans or their minor children.
It covers any future homesteads, as families often relocate following a tragedy.
The bill also allows for a tax exemption of the greater of $32,500 or the maximum amount that may be granted to a disabled veteran.

Dream Scholarship Act, SB 556 .
This legislation will establish Georgia's first needs-based scholarship program for Georgia students.
The scholarship would provide up to $3,000 per year for a maximum of eight semesters or 12 quarters.
Georgia Early Literacy Act, HB 1193 .
This legislation will improve literacy rates throughout Georgia by providing for literacy coaches in every public school to ensure that every student is reading at grade level by the 3rd grade.
This bill also provides the tools for teachers to apply the science of reading into instructional materials.
This legislation also establishes the Georgia Literacy Task Force within the Office of Student Achievement.
This task force will select high-quality instructional materials to teach our students to read.
Education Omnibus, HB 1396 .
This bill incorporates several bills into one, SB 425, HB 114, HB 1269, SB 498, and HB 971.
This omnibus includes the teaching of cursive writing, establishes age-appropriate digital literacy courses throughout all grades and financial literacy courses for high school students, and establishes security measures for weapons detection at schools.
This bill also includes clean-up language from Ricky and Alyssa's law from last year, which provides for a mobile panic alert system to connect emergency services with schools in the event of an emergency.
Income Tax Cuts and Property Tax Relief Income Tax Relief, HB 463, would lower the state’s income tax rate from 5.19% to 4.99% this year and continue to decrease it to 3.99% over the next eight years if the state achieves certain financial benchmarks.
The bill would also increase the standard deduction for married couples filing jointly from $24,000 to $30,000 next year, and for other filers from $12,000 to $15,000.
Additionally, the bill would exempt up to $1,750 in cash tips and $1,750 in overtime income from taxation.
Property Tax Relief, SB 33, will establish the Local Homestead Option Sales Tax (LHOST) to shift a portion of the tax burden from property taxes to a locally controlled sales tax.
The bill also aims to streamline how local governments use collected tax revenue, ensure school tax calculations accurately reflect homestead exemptions, and strengthen protections for taxpayers by preventing retroactive tax bills when exemptions are granted in error.
Overall, this measure is designed to provide meaningful tax relief while preserving essential local services.
Fiscal Year 2027 Budget, HB 974, The General Assembly passed a balanced budget, it's only constitutional duty.
The FY 2027 budget is set by a revenue estimate of $38.5 billion, an increase of $738 million (1.95%) over the FY 2026 budget.
This budget includes: $100 million to support a statewide literacy initiative to improve reading outcomes for students in K through 3rd grade. $70.4 million for statewide elementary school-based literacy coaches. $4 million to strengthen student mental health. $9.7 million to expand access to Pre-K programs. $100 million to give state retired employees a cost-of-living adjustment. $11.2 million to increase graduate medical education opportunities for medical students. $3.7 million to expand the Department of Public Health's maternal home visiting program to an additional 33 counties. $13.6 million for local infrastructure projects across the state.
Georgia Insurance Affordability and Claims Integrity Act, HB 1344 We passed the nation's strongest insurance affordability legislation!
From Speaker Burn's press release: ATLANTA - Today, House Speaker Jon Burns (R-Newington) applauded the final passage of a priority for the 2026 Legislative Session, House Bill 1344, the Georgia Insurance Affordability and Claims Integrity Act.
HB 1344 was drafted based on the findings and recommendations of the House Blue-Ribbon Study Committee on Insurance Rates appointed by Speaker Jon Burns and chaired by Rep.
Matt Reeves (R-Duluth).
“Georgia’s families, homeowners, drivers and businesses depend on their insurers to help them face life’s most difficult moments with confidence and security,” said House Speaker Jon Burns.
“The passage of the Insurance Affordability and Claims Integrity Act is a direct reflection of the Georgia House’s unwavering commitment to making life more affordable and insurance more accessible for the citizens of our great state.” HB 1344 aims to address insurance costs by: ● Increasing around 40 fines on insurance companies when laws or insurance policy provisions are violated during the insurance claim process ● Enforcing uninsured motorist laws ● Cracking down on insurance fraud ● Improving storm claim processing ● Enhancing the Fortified Homes program to help Georgians protect their homes from storm damage ● Requiring local governments to report how their premium tax revenue is used to lower citizens’ insurance premiums ● Limiting premium tax refund requests to three years ● Restricting how insurance companies can use aerial imaging ● Ensuring that at least two years are allowed to make property and casualty claims ● Enacting provisions concerning short-term rental insurance policies “The Insurance Affordability Act will make insurance more affordable for every hardworking Georgian across the state by making claims processing faster, fairer and more transparent.
Georgia is leading the nation in addressing insurance affordability, and we look forward to delivering this critical relief when it’s needed most,” said Rep.
Matt Reeves.
