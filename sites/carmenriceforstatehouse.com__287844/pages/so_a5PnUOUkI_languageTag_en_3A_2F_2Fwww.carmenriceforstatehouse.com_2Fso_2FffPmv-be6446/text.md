Legislation is moving fast as we approach Cross-over Day, the last day for legislation to pass its respective chamber and cross over to the other.
This week, the House passed 23 bills and resolutions.
Last week, I announced the launch of the Georgia Early Literacy Act of 2026 (HB 1193), and this bill has already passed the Education Committee!
The bill invests in K-3 education to help every child become a competent reader by third grade.
Under this legislation, each school will receive funding to hire a literacy coach.
The bill will also provide first-grade readiness assessments and require every school system to have a unified literacy plan, among other provisions.
I am very excited to see this legislation move forward, and I will keep you informed on its progress.

Legislation is now progressing through the legislative process.
Here are some notable bills that have passed the House: House Bill 383 , the Education Student Athletics High School Protection Act, passed.
This bill would regulate Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL) compensation for prospective and current Georgia high school student-athletes.
Right now, student athletes can receive NIL compensation, and this bill puts guardrails in place to protect students from exploitation.
It also provides for the automatic expiration of contracts upon the student-athlete's graduation.
Without an automatic expiration of contracts, these minors could be at risk of agreeing to a long-term contract that could affect their ability to play in college and beyond.
House Bill 907 passed.
This bill updates the Completion Schools Act, formerly known as alternative schools, which help students complete their education.
Currently, seven completion schools offer flexible classes, including evening and online options, and must partner with local school systems.
Schools must clearly communicate these options to parents and students.
The legislation also requires schools to share student leads with completion schools to encourage dropping-out or at-risk students to complete their education.
House Bill 903 , the Georgia Administrative Procedures Act, passed.
This bill applies to all executive-branch agencies with rule-making authority, ensuring that when a rule or regulation is changed, it must be subject to the same public notice, comments, and procedural requirements that govern formal rule-making.
House Resolution 251 passed.
This resolution will be put before the voters to decide whether the Georgia Constitution should be amended to elect probate judges on a nonpartisan basis.
House Resolution 999 passed.
This resolution recognizes America’s 250th Anniversary and Georgia’s vital role in our nation’s founding.
House Bill 948 passed.
This bill allows foreclosed debtors to learn about funds they might receive after foreclosure, requiring notices to include their right to claim surplus funds and instructions, with language warning that unclaimed funds may be considered abandoned and returned to the state after five years.
House Bill 57 passed.
This legislation adds the step-parent relationship to the definition of incest to close a loophole in the code.
House Bill 657 passed.
This bill supports mental health and substance use recovery services by defining a certified peer specialist as someone with lived experience trained to support individuals and families.
It requires certification through the Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities to ensure quality care statewide and sets standards for recovery community organizations to provide appropriate treatments.
