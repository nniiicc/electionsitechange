Dear Friends, Legislative Week 6 is complete, marking the halfway point of the legislative session.
During this week, the House approved key legislation to protect Georgia ratepayers and aid the most vulnerable children.
As a foster parent, I am proud of the measures the House has passed to support foster families and children.
This week, I presented House Bill 1107, the Excellent Teacher Preparation Act, to the Education Committee, where it successfully passed.
The House has prioritized literacy and math readiness, and to achieve these aims, we need to equip our teachers with the right tools to teach effectively.
My bill, the Excellent Teacher Preparation Act, aims to accomplish this.
As the bill’s author, I’m proud of the collaboration behind this initiative.
Working with the Professional Standards Commission, the University System of Georgia, the Governor’s Office of Student Achievement, and Georgia’s Literacy Council, we are developing a unified system that promotes transparency, accountability, and clear performance data for teacher preparation programs across the state.
HB 1107 requires the Georgia Professional Standards Commission to publish standardized performance measures, including enrollment requirements, completion and retention rates, graduate and employer surveys, and student performance.
This data will be available to the public while protecting privacy and ensuring transparency.
This bill will also require the Board of Regents to review performance data annually and determine whether any intervention is necessary in the schools of education.
By establishing consistent standards focused on classroom readiness, teacher effectiveness, and long-term retention, we help future educators start their careers confidently and be well prepared from day one.
Strong teachers lead to strong students, and strong students shape Georgia’s future.
Foster Placements Bill of Rights, HB 256, passed.
This bill expands protection of foster care providers under the state law to ensure that all caregivers are treated equally.
This bill extends the same legal rights afforded to foster parents to relative caregivers and fictive kin (persons with no biological or legal relationship but are treated as loved members of the family).
Foster care placement caregivers are extended new protections as well.
This bill will protect caregivers from retaliation for filing complaints with the Division of Family and Children Services (DFCS) and affirm their right to seek and obtain independent legal counsel or advice regarding the foster placement caregivers.
This new legislation will help with the rights, training, planning and retention of foster care parents.
Foster Children & Autism, HB 943 will create a five-year pilot program to provide autism spectrum disorder screenings and clinical evaluations for children in foster care.
With early detection, children will receive the necessary care that they need and will receive proper placement.
This bill requires DFCS to provide training and resources for providers including supervisors, case managers and caregivers so children with autism in foster care can be identified earlier.
Mandi Ballinger Act, HB 1061 is named in honor of our late colleague and former chair of the House Judiciary Juvenile Committee.
This legislation creates a 12-member committee composed of the chairpersons of the House Committee on Judiciary Juvenile and the Senate Judiciary Committee along with other designated state and criminal justice officials.
This committee is tasked with preparing Georgia for the potential inclusion of certain 17-year-olds within the juvenile court system.
Based on best practices from other states, the committee would consult with law enforcement, juvenile court attorneys, youth advocates and other experts to evaluate raising the juvenile court age in Georgia.
Currently, Georgia treats most 17-year olds as adults in the criminal justice system, one of only a few that do so.
Raising the juvenile court age to 18 and focusing on rehabilitation, and helping young people redirect their lives will improve outcomes for young people before adulthood.
The House this week passed significant legislation to protect Georgia ratepayers and announced the launch of legislation that will further help with the affordability of the insurance market.
Protecting Georgia’s Ratepayers HB 1063, passed the House!
This legislation protects residential and retail electricity customers from costs associated with data center construction and operation.
HB 1063 safeguards energy affordability for Georgia families and small businesses by ensuring that data centers and large-load consumers pay their own way upfront.

With this legislation, Georgia’s insurance market will further stabilize and provide relief to ratepayers.
HB 1344, The Insurance Affordability & Claims Integrity Act Increase nearly 40 insurance fines in Georgia’s insurance code.
Strengthens enforcement of uninsured motorist laws Cracks down on insurance fraud.
Implements the Fortified Homes Program to shore up homes against storm damage.
HB 1274, Excess Auto Insurance Profits Act If a company profits 5% above expected profit for 3 straight years, it must file a rate decrease.
Florida and several other states do this, and in one case, it reduced rates by $1 billion for a single company.
HB 1262, Fine Increase Bill Surprise Billing, Mental Health, and General Fine authority is increased from $2,000 to $10,000 and from $10,000 to $25,000.
HB 1263, The Premium Tax Clawback Act Currently, insurance companies can pursue refunds for premium tax payment “errors” over long periods of time.
This bill shortens the clawback period to 3 years.

HB 662 amends the definition of “rural hospital organization” to include “rural freestanding emergency department.” With this definition change, more rural hospitals will be eligible to receive tax credits.
Preston Fant and Brant Chesney Firefighter Safety Act, HB 1086 , will require the owner of any commercial or industrial building that was constructed using light-frame truss-type construction to post a notice.
This will protect firefighters since this type of construction burns more quickly, which can cause sudden roof and floor collapse.
HB 970 will expand the list of qualified healthcare professionals who can conduct sports physicals for student athletes.
These physicals will also include a cardiovascular prescreening to take proactive steps to identify health risks for student athletes.
