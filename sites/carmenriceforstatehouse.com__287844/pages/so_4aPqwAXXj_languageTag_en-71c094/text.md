Dear Friends, Legislative Week 11 is finished, and we are in the final stretch.
We have only one week remaining in this session—two committee workdays and two legislative days.
April 2nd is Sine Die, the last day of the session.
This week is critical because any legislation that hasn't passed both chambers and been sent to the governor for his signature will not become law this year.
I am pleased to report that my bill, the Excellent Teacher Preparation Act, HB 1107, has received final passage and has been sent to the governor for his signature!
This act will prepare teachers with the right tools to teach literacy and math.
This bill requires the Georgia Professional Standards Commission to publish standardized performance measures, including enrollment requirements, completion and retention rates, graduate and employer surveys, and student performance.
This data will be available to the public while protecting privacy and ensuring transparency.
This bill will also require the Board of Regents to review performance data annually and determine whether any intervention is necessary in the schools of education.
As Vice Chair of the Education Committee, I am proud of our achievements.
Here are some key highlights of the legislation we've worked on, which the House and Senate have passed and forwarded to the governor for his signature.
Distraction-Free Education Act, part 2, HB 1009, received final passage.
This legislation requires that all public school students in grades nine through twelve be prohibited from accessing personal electronic devices, such as cell phones, during the school day, from bell to bell.
Students with an IEP or medical plan mandating a cell phone will still be able to use one.
The Math Matters Act, HB 1030, has received final passage.
This bill expands opportunities for students to enroll in advanced math courses, increases math instructional time, and updates teacher certification standards to ensure educators possess the necessary math skills to teach evidence-based instructional strategies.
Redshirting Bill and the You Are Not Alone Awareness Act, SB 589 (which incorporates parts of HB 1045), have been sent to the Governor.
This legislation allows parents to decide at what age to send their child to kindergarten.
It ensures that 4- and 5-year-olds are treated equally for pre-K slots.
The You Are Not Alone Awareness Act requires public schools that issue student ID badges to students in grades 6 through 12 to include information about the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.
Charter Schools and Dropout Recovery Act, SB 369, has received final passage.
This legislation establishes standards for nontraditional education programs such as dropout recovery, credit recovery, and flexible scheduling, as well as how to measure their performance.
It allows existing charter schools to adopt this model.
It also amends the Dexter Mosley Act to allow virtual school students to participate in extracurricular and athletic activities at their local public schools.
This week, the following House and Senate bills and resolutions received final passage and have been sent to the governor: SB 622 creates the Joint Study Committee on Evaluating Escalating Costs in Georgia’s Foster Care System.
This committee will have 20 members: 6 from the Senate, 6 from the House, 1 appointed by the governor, and officials from the Dept of Human Services, the Dept of Juvenile Justice, and other related organizations.
Costs within the foster care system have risen 159% since 2022, and this study committee will address this problem.
Rio’s Law, SB 433, has received final passage.
This bill allows individuals with autism or other developmental disabilities to obtain a specialized Autism Spectrum Disorder or Developmental Disability license plate, enabling law enforcement to be aware of the individual's diagnosis so they can interact appropriately.
The bill also includes provisions for training law enforcement officers on best practices.
HB 1118, Paid Maternal Leave, has received final passage.
This bill will increase the number of paid maternal leave weeks from 6 to 9 weeks for state employees, giving moms and babies more time together.
Human Trafficking, SB 547 has received final passage.
This legislation designates both pimping and pandering as felonies, regardless of whether they are first or subsequent offenses, sending a clear message that Georgia will hold offenders accountable.
AI Companion Chatbots, SB 540 was sent to the Governor.
This bill establishes safeguards for minors who engage with chatbots, which are designed to simulate relationships.
This bill mandates parental control tools and protocols for responding to self-harm prompts.
SB 500 will expand the behavioral health workforce database and include more provider licensing boards to help address shortages.
SB 503, The Georgia Insurance Fairness Act, updates our state’s insurance laws to enhance consumer protections.
This bill will better safeguard those who rent their homes by ensuring that guarantees offered through rental home marketplaces are not classified as property insurance.
It will also strengthen oversight of private passenger automobile insurance.
Additionally, it extends filing deadlines after declared disasters and sets timelines for processing claims from catastrophic events.
Furthermore, it authorizes law enforcement to issue tickets to drivers without valid insurance coverage.
SB 472 will ensure school board accountability and allow the State Board of Education to recommend to the governor that local school board members be suspended without pay when the state auditor flags a district for financial mismanagement or misconduct.
SB 442 will repeal a noncitizen’s commercial driver’s license once their visa has expired.
It will also provide for testing requirements for renewal of commercial drivers’ licenses for noncitizens.
SB 150 allows retired public school teachers to return to work and continue receiving their retirement allowance and any post-retirement benefits alongside their teaching compensation, strengthening our teacher workforce.
HB 428 addresses the gap where individuals need mental health support but do not require institutional care.
This bill will enable the Georgia Dept of Community Health to submit waiver requests to Medicare and Medicaid on behalf of people over 21 who need mental health services and have a history of incarceration, homelessness, or hospitalization.
SB 443 establishes an offense for purposefully or recklessly obstructing any highway or street.
Individuals who commit this offense will be held civilly liable for any resulting damages.
