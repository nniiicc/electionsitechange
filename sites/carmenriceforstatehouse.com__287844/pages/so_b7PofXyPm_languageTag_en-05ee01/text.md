Dear Friends, Legislative Week 7 has concluded, marking a busy period.
During this time, legislation was passed to lower income tax rates, increase insurance affordability, enhance education, and more.
An important upcoming event is Crossover Day on Friday, March 6th, which is the deadline for legislation to pass its chamber and "crossover" to the other if it is to have a chance at becoming law.
The House is expected to work until midnight to process as many bills as possible on this day.
I am proud to say that House Bill 1107, the Excellent Teacher Preparation Act, has passed the House!
The House has prioritized literacy and math readiness, and to achieve these aims, we need to equip our teachers with the right tools to teach effectively.
My bill, the Excellent Teacher Preparation Act, aims to do this.
HB 1107 mandates that the Georgia Professional Standards Commission publish standardized performance measures, including enrollment, completion, retention rates, surveys, and student performance data, ensuring public access while protecting privacy.
It requires the Board of Regents to review data annually and decide on interventions.
By setting standards for classroom readiness, teacher effectiveness, and retention, we prepare future educators, fostering strong teachers, students, and Georgia’s future.
Income Tax Relief The House has passed income tax relief for hardworking Georgians!
House Bill 1001, which proposes accelerating the state income tax reduction by another 2 basis points, from 5.19 percent to 4.99 percent, effective July 1, 2026.
From Speaker Burn’s press release, “No one knows how to spend their money better than the ones who earned it.
That’s why the Georgia House was proud to pass another historic income tax reduction, which would keep millions of dollars in the pockets of hardworking Georgians,” said House Speaker Jon Burns (R-Newington).
“This is just one of the measures the General Assembly is considering to deliver meaningful tax relief for families, taxpayers, and homeowners across the state.
We look forward to working alongside Governor Kemp and our colleagues in the Senate to ensure Georgians can keep more of their money where it belongs—in their pockets.” Insurance Affordability The House has passed insurance reform.
This will increase fines for bad actors, enforce uninsured motorists laws, crack down on insurance fraud, and tighten insurance processing after storms.
The House Blue-Ribbon Study Committee on Insurance Rates met over the summer and worked alongside Insurance Commissioner John King to draft the following legislation.
This legislation will further stabilize Georgia’s insurance market and provide relief to ratepayers.
Insurance costs have risen by about 60% nationwide since 2020, placing a significant financial strain on Georgia drivers, homeowners, and ratepayers.
Fortunately for Georgia, since the passage of tort reform in 2025, the following insurance companies have reduced their rates: State Farm, Liberty Mutual, Safeco, Southern General, Progressive, Hanover, Go Auto, Allstate, and Country Mutual.
Two new insurance companies have reentered the market, American Integrity and Donegal.
But there is more work to be done.
HB 1344, The Insurance Affordability & Claims Integrity Act Increase nearly 40 insurance fines in Georgia’s insurance code.
Strengthens enforcement of uninsured motorist laws Cracks down on insurance fraud.
Implements the Fortified Homes Program to shore up homes against storm damage.
HB 1274, Excess Auto Insurance Profits Act If a company profits 5% above expected profit for 3 straight years, it must file a rate decrease.
Florida and several other states do this, and in one case, it reduced rates by $1 billion for a single company.
HB 1262, Fine Increase Bill Surprise Billing, Mental Health, and General Fine authority is increased from $2,000 to $10,000 and from $10,000 to $25,000.
HB 1263, The Premium Tax Clawback Act Currently, insurance companies can pursue refunds for premium tax payment “errors” over long periods of time.
This bill shortens the clawback period to 3 years.
Amended Fiscal Year 2026 Budget The House passed the 2026 Amended Fiscal Year Budget.
This fulfills our constitutional duty to pass a balanced budget each year.
HB 973 includes a revised revenue estimate of $43.6 billion.
The bill includes: $2 billion in income and property tax relief. $325 million to the Georgia Student Finance Commission for the new need-based DREAMS scholarship program, $600 million to provide state employees with a one-time $2,000 salary supplement, $400 million to construct a new state mental health hospital, $250 million for local road improvements. $81 million for foster care support contracts. $45 million for the State Housing Trust Fund to address homelessness. $5 million to assist homeless veterans. $100 million to strengthen the Employees' Retirement System of Georgia's pension stability.
This week, the House passed major education reform bills.
The Georgia Early Literacy Act of 2026 was passed by the House.
This marks the largest education initiative since the passage of the HOPE Scholarship.
This bill will allocate funding through the Quality Basic Education formula to provide literacy coaches for every public school, ensuring that every student is reading at grade level by grade 3.
It also establishes a Georgia Literacy Task Force to ensure oversight.
It further provides professional learning grounded in the science of reading for teachers and literacy coaches to ensure consistency in statewide literacy teaching.
The Math Matters Act, HB 1030, requires all students who test at the end of 7th grade as “Distinguished” learners to be automatically placed in an Enhanced Math class.
It requires 60 minutes of math instruction time for students in grades 4 and 5.
It will allow students and their parents to decide whether to opt out of advanced math classes, ensuring this choice isn’t determined solely by the teacher.
The Distraction-Free Education Act, HB 1009, builds on last year’s cell phone ban in schools by extending it to high schools.
Students with an IEP or medical plan will be able to access devices as necessary.
School Safety, HB 1023, will require local school boards to implement one or more weapon detection systems at all main points of entry for permanent school buildings in Georgia’s public schools to prevent individuals from unlawfully bringing weapons onto school campuses.
Expanding Access to After-School Programs, HB 1123, will expand access to after-school programs to help working families.
Public schools that offer after-care programs to students in kindergarten, primary, or upper elementary grades and that also operate pre-K programs will be required to make those programs available to pre-K students on the same basis.
Financial Transparency, HB 1164 , will require the State Board of Education establish an audit committee and define its duties.
It updates the financial audit rules for school districts and charter schools.
The Department of Audits and Accounts will develop a tiered monitoring system to assess the financial health of these entities.
If a school is flagged as financially distressed, specific intervention strategies will be implemented.
