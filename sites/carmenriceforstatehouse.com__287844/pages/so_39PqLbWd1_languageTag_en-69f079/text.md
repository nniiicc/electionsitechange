Dear Friends, Legislative Week 10 is complete.
At the beginning of the session, House Republicans promised to address affordability issues.
The week concluded with the Governor signing two major pieces of legislation to do just that.
House Bill 1000 was signed, which will provide a one-time tax refund to eligible taxpayers who file their tax returns for the 2024 and 2025 tax years, offering $250 for single filers or $500 for married couples filing jointly.
House Bill 1199 will temporarily suspend the gas tax for 60 days to provide relief at the pump from higher prices caused by overseas conflicts.
This bill will save Georgians between 35 and 37 cents a gallon on gas and diesel fuels.
You will see the price change at the pump in a few days.
House Bill 1199 is also known as an IRS Conformity bill.
This bill will put Georgia's tax code in alignment with many of the tax-cut provisions of the One Big Beautiful Bill, saving individuals and small businesses money.
As the end of the session nears, many pieces of legislation have now passed both the House and Senate and await the Governor's signature.
Here are a few of those bills: Education and Workforce Strategy Act, HB 1302, consolidates all workforce development programs under a single agency and establishes the Technical College System of Georgia as the apprenticeship authority.
This will help ensure Georgia remains a Top State For Talent.
Farmer Bridge Assistance Program, HB 1159, exempts farmers from paying income taxes on loans obtained through the program during disasters like Hurricane Helene.
License Update for Contractors, SB 553 , modernizes Georgia's licensing framework for the construction industry by establishing a tiered classification system for electrical, plumbing, and air-conditioning contractors, along with detailed experience and examination requirements for each license.

Kemp Signs Major Tax Relief for Hardworking Georgians This week, the House also passed the following House and Senate bills: SB 570 , Georgia Human Trafficking Prevention Training Act.
This bill will require inn and short-term rental property employees to complete annual training on identifying and reporting suspected human trafficking.
SB 452 is a bill designed to improve the recruitment and retention of state troopers.
Georgia ranks 49th in trooper pension benefits, and this bill aims to change that.
It will increase employer contributions to qualifying 401(k) accounts for Georgia’s law enforcement officers.
A new contribution structure will also be established.
SB 470 , the Emergency and Public Safety Signal Protection Act, will ban the possession, operation, and distribution of signal jammers that aim to interfere with the transmission of communication signals over any wireless system.
Since these devices can block signals used by law enforcement and other emergency responders, they pose a threat to public safety, and this legislation will address that.
SB 177 , Government Transparency and Campaign Finance, is a bill aimed at increasing transparency in elections by requiring agents of hostile foreign entities such as North Korea, Iran, China, and Russia to register with the Georgia Ethics Commission to disclose campaign funding.
SB 553 updates Georgia’s licensing framework for the construction industry by establishing a tiered classification system for electrical, plumbing, and air-conditioning contractors, along with detailed experience and examination requirements for each license.
SB 439, Fair Business Protection Act , will provide for the regulation of referral agencies for assisted living communities and personal care homes.
SB 170 is a bill aimed at strengthening our healthcare infrastructure.
It establishes a grant program to help rural hospitals buy and install backup generators for power outages.
This will help rural hospitals maintain care during severe weather events.
SB 383 is a GBI agency bill aimed at preventing child fatalities.
Local child fatality review committees will be responsible for examining child deaths to identify opportunities for intervention and prevention of similar tragedies.
SB 427 is a bill designed to help with physician shortages.
This bill creates a pathway for an internationally trained physician who has earned a medical degree or its equivalent from a school outside the United States to be eligible for a provisional license.
Applicants will be required to meet many qualifications, including a current or recently active medical license in good standing.
SB 523 is a bill designed to strengthen protections against acts of discrimination in Georgia’s public schools.
This bill will require the Department of Education to designate a statewide Title VI coordinator to monitor discrimination and harassment complaints in public schools.
SB 399 is a bill named after Mason Sells, a 20-year-old Mercer student who lost his life while playing soccer.
This bill will give 9-1-1 callers maps of all AEDs (automated external defibrillators) and step-by-step instructions to help save lives.
HB 571 is a healthcare workforce expansion bill.
This legislation will place the licensing and regulation of radiologist assistants under the Georgia Composite Medical Board.
This board will set a framework for the licensure and continuing education of radiologist assistants, promoting improved standards of care and efficient services.
HB 185 is a bill to modernize Georgia’s dietetics and nutrition framework.
This bill will also expand job pathways for qualified nutrition therapists to obtain licensure in the state by joining the Dietitian Licensure Compact.
