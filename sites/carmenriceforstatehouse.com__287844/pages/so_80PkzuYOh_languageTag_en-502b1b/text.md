Dear Friends, The 2026 Legislative Session has begun, and week 1 is complete.
This week, we had two very important events in which the Speaker, Lt.
Governor, and Governor all presented their legislative priorities.
The first event was the Georgia Chamber of Commerce Eggs and Issues, and the other was Governor Kemp's final State of the State Address.
Speaker Burns stated at the Eggs and Issues event, "We are laser-focused on keeping Georgia the best place in the nation to live, work, raise a family, and do business for generations to come.
We (the House) believe we will accomplish that by making life and the cost of business more affordable for every family and company here in our state." Some of the issues that the House will address are: Addressing the cost and accessibility of insurance Strengthening transparency and accountability within our financial institutions Deliver additional property tax relief while maintaining current property tax exemptions Expand resources for the Georgia DREAM down-payment and mortgage assistance program for first-time buyers Reduce regulations and red tape that add to the cost of building homes Increase access to quality, affordable healthcare Champion literacy efforts in our schools; strengthen curriculum, increase accessibility to screenings for learning challenges, and provide literacy coaches for all of our schools Speaker Burn's Eggs and Issues Speech This week, the House and Senate established their legislative calendar for this session, which I’ve included below.

For those interested, you can review the governor’s proposed budget here: Governor Kemp's Budget Reports 2026 State of the State Address On Thursday, January 15th, Governor Kemp delivered his final State of the State Address to a joint session of the General Assembly.
It was a bittersweet moment.
Over the past seven years, Governor Kemp has steered our state through many challenges, including COVID lockdowns and Hurricanes Michael and Helene.
Through it all, our state is safer and more prosperous than it was in 2019, prior to Governor Kemp's term.
"The Georgia of today stands on a firm foundation – a foundation built on the character of our people, the resolve of leaders both past and present, and by the Grace of our Creator.
So, when the storms of tomorrow come, it is my hope that we will share in a commitment to keeping the state we all love on that firm foundation," stated Governor Kemp.
Where we stand today: Through a balanced budget process, our state reserves are at a record high.
4.1 billion dollars of capital projects have been funded with cash rather than debt.
Cash funding of capital projects has allowed us to reduce our debt by 20 percent over 3 years.
The lowest since the state began issuing debt bonds in the 1970s.
Historic tax cuts were implemented, returning billions to taxpayers.
Invested 6 billion in water, sewer, and transportation projects across the state.
Workforce Development- we have reduced the state employee turnover rate by 25 percent, saving taxpayers in training new employees.
Prioritized economic development, over the last 7 years, Georgia has seen over 219,700 new jobs.
Since 2019, we have welcomed 3 of the largest economic projects in the state's history.
Continued Georgia's streak of 12 years for the best place to do business.
Fully funded the Quality Basic Education fund 7 times while also adding an additional 184.5 million dollars to programs that prepare workers for jobs.
School safety; added an additional 184.5 million in school security grants.
Raised teacher pay by $#!
Kept the cost of post-secondary school in check by putting an additional 2 billion towards higher education.
Launched the GRACE commission to combat human trafficking.
Since its launch, there have been 70 convictions and 200 children saved.
Established the Anti-Gang Taskforce and the Gang Prosecution Unit.
Since then, GBI has arrested 2,500 offenders with gang affiliation.
Where we are going: All across Georgia, citizens are struggling to make ends meet, which is why the Governor's latest budget proposal includes a fourth, one-time, 1 billion tax rebate.
The state's personal and corporate income tax rate will be lowered an additional 20 basis points to 4.99%.
Lowering the income tax rate below 5 percent 3 years ahead of schedule.
Establish the DREAM scholarship.
Investing 325 million dollars into the state's first needs-based scholarship program.
Establishing a new enhanced law enforcement retirement plan.
The state will provide a higher 401 (k) match, doubling the state's contribution rate.
Introducing the state's 11th bill to help eliminate human trafficking.
It is modeled after Trey's law, which has been passed in other states. $2,000 one time pay supplement for all state employees, including teachers and law enforcement.
