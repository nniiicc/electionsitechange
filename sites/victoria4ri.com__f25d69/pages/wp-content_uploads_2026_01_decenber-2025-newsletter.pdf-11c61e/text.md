December Newsletter: Why Hard Work Matters Dear Friends and Neighbors, Happy Holidays!
I hope everyone is having a restful holiday break.
The end of the year is a good time to reflect on the work we’ve done and look forward to the new year.
I’m grateful for the community here that has so many thoughtful and civically engaged people (see the Rail Listening Session below).
A hard-working community deserves a hard-working leader.
Over the past three years, I’ve devoted extraordinary time and energy to this “parttime” role because I take the responsibility seriously.
I’ve focused on listening to the community (holding town halls and personally responding to emails) and building strong relationships with colleagues and community partners—and I’ve wrestled with a number of difficult votes and tradeoffs.
That commitment, persistence, and follow-through continue to shape my work in the Senate.
This work has also brought new challenges, including a shadowy Republican group trying to buy influence in the RI Senate by finding loopholes in campaign finance laws in order to unseat Democrats in 2026, including me.
I see it as a sign that the work matters—and a reason to start campaign efforts earlier than usual.
Reaching voters through newsletters, mail, canvassing, and community events depends on campaign funding, which ensures voters can make informed decisions.
As 2025 closes, I’m focused on raising the resources needed to start the re-election campaign strong.
Contributions can be made online or by check.
Thanks in advance for all the support shown so far, and I hope everyone has a good start to the New Year!

Testifying against energy efficiency program cuts Earlier in December, I testified in front of the Public Utilities Commission to urge them to reject harmful cuts to our state’s energy efficiency programs.
The vast majority of my colleagues in both the Rhode Island House and Senate signed a letter opposing these cuts.
I’ve heard from constituents who’ve used the program: it helped insulate and weatherize their homes, lower their energy bills, and reduce demand on the energy grid — benefits that ultimately protect all ratepayers especially during the coldest, “peak demand” days of the year when RIEnergy has to ship in liquified natural gas on tankers, an expensive fuel of last resort.
