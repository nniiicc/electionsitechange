Please read the tab labeled “ Guiding Principles ”.
If elected, I will base my decisions on those principles of limited government, a citizenry that enjoys the freedom to choose, and election integrity.
In addition, there are two issues important to me, taxes and homelessness.
I will vote against every proposed tax increase.
We have a spending problem, not a revenue problem.
The state government has grown too big and too costly.
In addition, too much collected tax money is being wasted.
I will work to reduce homelessness and the cruel pain suffered by those experiencing it, whether directly or in related families or by nearby residents.
I recognize this is a heavy lift.
Yet, I must try.
This is a government created issue and only government can solve it.
Other states have done better and shown what works and what doesn’t.
The federal HUD provides a lot money and is a great informational resource for anyone interested.
California needs to do much more.
Not by spending money on consultants but by directly addressing housing, mental health, drug use, and violence of all kinds.
This is a public safety issue.
Homeless people are part of the public and deserve better.
Limited Federalism Today, we still call ourselves a federalist republic, yet the original constitutional restraints on government power have been steadily eroded.
Both the federal government and State of California have grown too large, too expensive, and too intrusive into the daily lives of the people.
The Democratic party has held super-majority control of California for nearly 20 years.
During that time, California’s leaders have burdened us all with ever increasing taxes, suffocating regulations, forced cultural change, failed projects, and state mandates.
Unaccountable bureaucrats have tried to dictate how we raise our children, how we protect our health, what cars we drive, and even how to cook our food.
They have positioned themselves as our caretakers.
That must stop.
I believe we individuals know better than central planners how to live our own lives and how to spend our own money.
Central planners do indeed have the opportunity to persuade citizens with facts and reason.
Instead, they have resorted to threats, mandates, and intimidation.
That must stop.
We must break the Democratic party super-majority in Sacramento.
I will work to restore a true balance of power – returning our state government to its proper, limited role and making it more accountable to the people.
Freedom to Choose One corollary of the Freedom to Choose is that no incumbent should run unopposed.
Each election, the incumbent should describe their actions in office and explain why they deserve another term.
Each election, the voters should have a genuine choice between that incumbent and alternatives.
I am one of those alternatives.
Election Integrity We cannot have a healthy democracy without trust in our elections.
I support three common-sense reforms: Voter ID – California should enact its own voter ID law before the federal government imposes one on us.
Clean Voter Rolls – Regular maintenance of registration rolls must resume immediately.
California is losing court cases for failing to do this.
Paper Ballots and Transparent Counting – Every vote should be cast on paper, counted per fixed schedule, and followed by published audits of random samples in every county.
Yes, the paper ballots can be scanned for faster counting.
No, the voters must not be asked to enter their votes directly into a programmable machine.
These are not partisan issues.
They are basic requirements for honest elections.
Without them, confidence in government itself collapses and our society will fragment.
