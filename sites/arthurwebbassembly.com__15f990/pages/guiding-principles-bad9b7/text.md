Guiding Principles
Limited Federalism
The Constitution defines a federal government created by a free people who consent to be governed by a limited government, but who retain the authority to define, limit, or replace that government.
The same principle applies to states and their constitutions.
Both federal and state governments exist to serve the people – not the other way around.
Their proper role is to protect individual liberty, provide a fair legal framework, and safeguard minority rights from majority overreach.
The government can encourage and persuade but cannot dictate or mandate except in the narrow areas where the people have explicitly granted that authority.
The Bill of Rights protects individuals from both elected officials and unelected bureaucrats who might exceed their proper bounds.
It should be noted that “the people”, as used in the Constitution, refers to American citizens, not visitors or guests, invited or uninvited.
Citizenship carries a distinct status and set of protections that must be preserved.
Free Citizenry – The Freedom to Choose
True freedom requires the freedom to choose.
If we cannot choose for ourselves, then are we truly free?
I am of course paraphrasing the great Milton Friedman.
There has always been in history and will always be those few free individuals who need and by that need both qualify and deserve help.
Such assistance can best come from the private sector, such as family, churches and social organizations.
Government also has a legitimate role.
It should be a goal that such help does not make the recipients dependent on the government or that the government does not expand to a caretaker role for the people in general.
Such expansions shrink personal freedom and shifts power from the individual to the government.
Election Integrity – Trust in the Outcome
Without trusted elections, we cannot have trust in the outcomes.
We cannot then “get behind” our government leaders and support their efforts on behalf of the majority that elected them.
In history, mostly free societies have always been weakened by internal fighting and distrust.
They then either descended into dictatorships or been conquered.
We must protect our children and grandchildren by delaying that failure for another 250 years.
We must restore election integrity.
The Citizens United decision gave companies, unions, and NGOs – some controlled by foreign interests - more election influence than individual citizens.
Political parties are unlimited as to money they can bring to a campaign, both in amounts and geographic sources.
California voters and candidates are subordinated to political parties and outside money.
Election Integrity depends on:
- Accurate and up-to-date voter registration lists
- Verification of voter eligibility
- Secure voting systems and ballot chain of custody
- Transparent counting, auditing, and post-election verification
- Protection against fraud, foreign interference, or procedural errors
- Reform of campaign finance laws
