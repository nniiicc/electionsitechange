Meet Arthur Yard Signs Volunteer Contribute September 4, 2026 Quick Legend: SB - Senate Bill SCA - Senate Constitutional Amendment AB - Assembly Bill ACA - Assembly Constitutional Amendment CPB - Citizen Petition Bill CPA - Citizen Petition Constitutional Amendment 14 Statewide Ballot Propositions The opinions below are mine.
I used publicly available input from Cal Matters, East Bay Times, California Taxpayers Association, Contra Costa Taxpayers Association, Reform California, and various social media accounts.
Proposition 1 (SB) Vote NO This hurts Cost of Living SB 417 (Limón) The Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond Act of 2026 (Ch.
16, 2026) (PDF) This Proposition is to issue $11.5 long term debt bonds, paid off by future tax increases, unspecified in the bill.
While veterans deserve help, the actual benefits to veterans are doubtful at only 10% of this bill.
Better those benefits are paid from income tax General Fund money, at existing income tax rates.
Proposition 2 (ACA) Vote NO This hurts Cost of Living ACA 20 (Gabriel) Save for California's Future Act.
(Res.
Ch.
130, 2026) (PDF) This Proposition allows the State to further bend the Gann amendment, which says the State must return tax money that is not spent.
Currently, the State is prudently saving 10% of unspent taxes per year into a “rainy day fund.” This Proposition doubles that to 20% per year.
More taxes can be collected and left unspent, but not returned.
Proposition 3 (CPA) Vote NO E liminate S pecial F unding Taxes Provides permanent funding for schools and healthcare by extending existing tax on high incomes.
This Proposition seeks to make permanent a temporary large income tax increase that was passed in 2012 for schools and modified in 2016 to include some Medi-Cal healthcare.
Yes, the actual State spending for schools and healthcare should absolutely be paid from the income tax based General Fund and decisions by elected legislators.
Local school bond issues should not be necessary.
Special income taxes for special purposes detract from income tax priorities by earmarking certain funds for certain expenses.
Leave this in the hands of your elected legislators.
A better proposition would be to add a higher tax rate to the existing income tax schedule without restriction on purpose.
Proposition 4 (SB) Vote NO Right Idea – Cart Before the Horse SB 42 (Umberg) Political Reform Act of 1974: public campaign financing: California Fair Elections Act of 2026.
(Ch.
245, 2025) (PDF) This Proposition asks to spend State tax money for political campaigns, in competition with unlimited PAC money.
Until the disastrous Citizens United decision is reversed, your money is better spent for schools, healthcare, and housing than for politicking.
Proposition 5 (SCA) Vote NO Undermines Recall Elections SCA 1 (Newman) Elections: recall of state officers.
(Res.
Ch.
204, 2024) (PDF) This Proposition undermines Recalls.
To fill a recalled person by appointing a member of the existing government undermines the purpose of the Recall.
Proposition 37 (CPB) Vote NO This H urts Cost of Living and is Financially Dangerous Creates loan program for middle-income buyers of qualified new homes.
This proposition issues long term debt bonds.
From that debt money, the State will offer partial mortgages for certain buyers, and then sells those mortgages to private parties, likely investment funds.
The State thus becomes a mortgage broker.
This is similar to the heavily discounted mortgage programs that led to the widespread default and foreclosure fiasco in 2008.
Let’s not repeat history.
Proposition 38 (CPB) Vote NO Bill Steers Money to Sponsors Lab Authorizes bonds for immunology research.
This Proposition is a close call for me.
On the one hand, the bonds will be paid off by the State from income tax money and some or perhaps all of that will be paid back from future earnings on successful drug development, which we need as a society.
The State takes the risk of this research.
On the other hand, this proposition was sponsored by a prominent lab director and his particular lab is targeted as the primary recipient of the funds.
Cal Matters has a very good article on that point.
There is also no guarantee that other labs receiving California tax money will be located in California.
Proposition 39 (CPA) Vote YES Trusted Elections - Avoid Federal Mandate Establishes additional voter identification and citizenship verification requirements.
This Proposition has two parts.
The first part requires county election offices to resume voter role cleanup.
This will save money by avoiding legal penalties of lawsuits which are targeting California.
The voter roles are out of date.
I see it in my own campaign’s voter roles.
The second part is about showing a Voter ID when voting, or writing that ID on the mailed ballot envelope.
This avoids total reliance on the name and signature, which varies, and which is on the ballot itself.
The Voter ID is not on the ballot, is a 4 digit number, a PIN if you will, and much more easily scanned for accuracy than a signature.
That 4 digit number can be chosen from one of seven different IDs, one of which is a special voting ID that can be requested by the person registering to vote.
In that latter case, a physical Voter ID card would be issued to the voter for current and future elections.
Benefits are high.
By reducing the risk of signature fraud, the 100% mail-in voting procedures are actually reinforced and become more likely to continue.
By implementing this Voter ID, California might avoid federal mandates and interventions into our elections.
By cleaning the voter roles, an estimated million ballots for people who have moved will not have to be mailed (863 thousand in LA County alone), saving money and further avoiding fraud complaints.
By improving credibility in our elections, perhaps we can better trust the outcomes.
The option to file a provisional ballot in person continues, just as existed when IDs were required in the past.
Proposition 40 (CPA) Vote NO Threatens Future California Innovation Imposes one-time tax on certain individuals and trusts.
This Proposition is dangerous in that it allows the State to tax a small group of people for immediate gain but risks losing much more income tax money in the longer term.
These billionaires do not need protection from me.
I am worried that these billionaires will simply leave the State.
Many already have.
It is not difficult to leave California.
We have seen many middle class people already do that.
At some point, the innovation engine that is Silicon Valley will fade.
New innovations will occur somewhere else.
Jobs will be created somewhere else.
Taxes from those high incomes will be paid somewhere else.
California will lose it’s place as the innovative state.
For example, imagine what would happen to SF if Marc Benioff were to leave and take his Salesforce with him.
How many times do you see his name on hospitals and other edifaces?
How many salary dollars and resulting taxes will leave California?
The South of Market area of SF will see a recession.
There are startups being planned right now, which will result in unrealized gains which cannot be used to pay taxes due if this proposition passes.
How many of those startups will simply choose to start somewhere else?
Proposition 41 (CPA) Vote YES Proteccts against new Special Taxes Requires audits of programs funded by new state special taxes, prohibits new state taxes that are excluded from existing voter-approved state spending limit.
This Proposition requires an audit by the State Auditor before new special taxes can be imposed.
The nature of that audit is a review of the receiving agency and answer if they could cut spending by 10% instead of the tax.
Any new taxes would apply to the Gann amendment limit, leading to possible tax refunds.
Proposition 42 (CPA) Vote YES Protects against Savings and Wealth Taxes Prohibits new state personal property taxes and certain retroactive state taxes.
This Proposition prohibits any new state tax on personal property, such as investments or equity in your home.
This Proposition protects us from the proposed savings tax and any wealth tax.
Currently, we are taxed on income and consumption.
Just holding money and things of value is not taxed.
This Proposition continues that historical convention.
Proposition 43 (ACA) Vote YES Return to 2/3 Majority to Raise Taxes ACA 22 (Wicks) Local taxes: limitation.
(Res.
Ch.
132, 2026) (PDF) This is a compromise bill between the citizen petition Save Prop 13 and the legislature.
This bill will make it harder to raise taxes in the future.
Currently, many taxes can be raised by a simple majority, a relatively recent change from the 2/3 convention of Prop 13.
Proposition 44 (CPB) Vote NO Adds Costs to Overburdened Healthcare Requires community health clinics spend 90% of revenue on program services.
This Proposition mandates FQHC centers (and them only) to spend at least 90% of their revenue on programs services.
That sounds good, until we realize these centers already are subject to Federal and State laws.
This extra burden increases administrative costs and potential penalty costs.
Our healthcare system is already stretched far enough.
Proposition 45 (CPB) Vote YES Promotes Housing without Bonds Modifies environmental review for certain projects .
This Proposition prohibits unending environmental reviews.
It mandates such reviews be completed in one year.
It turns each review into an “yes or no” decision instead of the current time consuming agency review of alternatives and the costly feedback that requires of developers.
It limits court reviews to 270 days.
This Proposition will end the permanent pocket veto of housing projects by individuals in public agencies.
One year is plenty long enough for due diligence in environmental impact reviews.
This Proposition will promote housing in California.
