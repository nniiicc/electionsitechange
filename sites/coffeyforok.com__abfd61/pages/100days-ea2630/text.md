Donate Nick's First 100 Days With # days left until Election Day, Nick Coffey rolls out the first phase of priorities for his first # days in office if elected in November. ​ "These aren’t campaign promises.
These are thoughtful, achievable objectives that I will get done if Oklahoma chooses to put a prosecutor – not a politician – in the Attorney General’s office." Create the Office of Public Integrity Coffey will create a new unit, the Public Integrity Division, in the Attorney General’s office dedicated to overseeing the prosecution of public corruption–crimes related to elected officials abusing their position in office, misusing tax dollars, and violating our trust.
This unit will also monitor the proposal of potentially unconstitutional legislation in the legislature, which could later result in costly lawsuits.
Crack Down on Scam Calls Scams targeting Oklahoma’s elderly and vulnerable adults have exploded in scale.
And with quickly evolving AI voice-cloning technology, it is increasingly difficult for Oklahomans to spot these schemes.
These calls can drain a lifetime of savings in days, often before anyone outside the household notices – posing a real threat to Oklahomans.
Starting November 1, 2026, Senate Bill 2067 finally gives Oklahoma banks and credit unions real tools to fight back: employees can flag suspected exploitation internally, place a temporary hold on a reported account, or reach out to a trusted contact, all with good-faith legal immunity.
But the law will only work if banks know exactly who to call and trust that the referral goes somewhere.
Right now, “notify an appropriate agency” is dangerously vague.
As Attorney General, Coffey will formalize that pipeline through a statewide MOU with the Oklahoma Bankers Association, building on Oklahoma County’s existing OK CAFE model, so every bank in the state has one clear point of contact, standardized training on red flags, and confidence that a referral leads to real coordination with DAs, DHS, and law enforcement to protect our elderly and vulnerable.
Aggressive Enforcement on Illegal Marijuana Grows In 2025, the Oklahoma Bureau of Narcotics reported that Oklahoma exported roughly $153B of illicit marijuana .
Oklahoma accounted for 66% of the country’s marijuana seizures .
Coffey will prioritize the work of the Organized Crime Task Force to target illegal and black-market marijuana operations.
As a federal prosecutor, Coffey saw firsthand that the only way to dismantle these organizations is to eradicate their profits, and he will prioritize criminal forfeiture and money laundering investigations.
Eliminating these illicit grow facilities is critical to cleaning up organized crime and ensuring Oklahoma land isn’t being used my foreign entities and bad actors.
Coffey is the only candidate in the race for Attorney General with the experience and skill set to execute the eradication of illegal drugs in Oklahoma.
