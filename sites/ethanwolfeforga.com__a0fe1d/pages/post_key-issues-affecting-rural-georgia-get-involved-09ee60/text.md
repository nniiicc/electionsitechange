Economic Challenges Facing Georgia House District 151
A Platform for Change
Overview
Georgia House District 151 encompasses some of the most economically vulnerable communities in the entire state.
Spanning Chattahoochee, Marion, Schley, Stewart, Terrell, and Webster counties in their entirety, along with portions of Dougherty and Sumter counties, this district is home to hard-working families in communities like Americus, Dawson, and Lumpkin who deserve real representation and real solutions.
The challenges facing District 151 are not abstract policy problems — they are daily realities that affect whether families can put food on the table, access a doctor, or find a good-paying job close to home.
Ethan Wolfe is committed to confronting these challenges head-on with honest leadership and community-driven solutions.
1.
Declining Job Opportunities
The Reality on the Ground
Southwest Georgia has experienced decades of economic decline, leaving District 151 communities struggling to recover.
The counties within this district consistently rank among Georgia's most economically distressed.
According to the Georgia Department of Labor (2023), unemployment rates across the counties of District 151 significantly outpace the state average:
• Terrell County has historically maintained unemployment rates nearly double the Georgia state average
• Stewart County is one of the most persistently economically distressed counties in Georgia, regularly appearing on the USDA's list of Persistent Poverty Counties — defined as counties where 20% or more of residents have lived in poverty for the past 30 years¹
• Webster County, one of Georgia's smallest counties by population, has seen significant outmigration as residents leave in search of employment elsewhere²
Agriculture: Changing but Still Critical
Agriculture remains the economic backbone of District 151, but the industry looks dramatically different from the way it did a generation ago.
• Terrell County and Schley County are significant producers of peanuts, cotton, and pecans — crops that have increasingly shifted toward large-scale mechanized operations
• The USDA 2022 Census of Agriculture confirms that the number of farm operators across southwest Georgia has steadily declined, even as total acreage farmed has remained relatively stable — meaning fewer people are doing the same amount of work due to automation and consolidation³
• Dawson, the seat of Terrell County, has seen its agricultural economy shift, with many farmworkers finding seasonal or part-time work replacing what were once full-time year-round positions
• In Sumter County, the closure or downsizing of agricultural processing operations has compounded job losses in the sector
Manufacturing: A Sector in Retreat
District 151 once benefited from a modest but meaningful manufacturing base that provided stable, middle-class wages.
That base has eroded significantly:
• Americus (Sumter County) experienced the devastating closure of several manufacturing operations over the past two decades.
The loss of manufacturing jobs in Americus has been a long-term contributor to the city's economic struggles⁴
• Dougherty County's Albany area, which borders District 151, serves as a regional economic hub — but manufacturing job losses there, including significant layoffs in previous decades, have had ripple effects throughout neighboring counties in the district
• Chattahoochee County, home to Fort Moore (formerly Fort Benning), has an economy heavily tied to the military installation.
While Fort Moore provides some civilian employment, the broader Chattahoochee County economy outside the base offers limited private-sector opportunity

✅ Push for expansion of the Georgia Department of Economic Development's rural investment programs, specifically targeting persistent poverty counties like Stewart and Terrell
✅ Support agricultural diversification programs through the University of Georgia Cooperative Extension to help farmers adapt to changing markets while maintaining a viable workforce
✅ Champion workforce development partnerships between local industries and technical colleges to create pipelines for good-paying local jobs
2.
Limited Access to Education and Workforce Training
The Brain Drain Crisis
One of the most damaging long-term trends facing District 151 is the consistent outmigration of young people.
Students who graduate from local high schools and go on to college or vocational training frequently do not return, leaving communities without the next generation of workers, entrepreneurs, and leaders.
• Schley County, Marion County, and Webster County school systems serve small student populations with limited local tax bases to fund competitive educational programming⁵
• According to Georgia's Governor's Office of Student Achievement, graduation rates and standardized test performance in many District 151 counties fall below state averages, reflecting chronic underfunding and resource shortages⁶
• Stewart County Schools has faced serious accreditation challenges in recent years, reflecting the severe resource constraints facing one of Georgia's poorest counties⁷
Vocational and Technical Training Gaps
The lack of accessible vocational training is a critical gap that prevents District 151 residents from securing better-paying jobs, even when those jobs are nearby.
• South Georgia Technical College, based in Americus, serves as a vital resource for Sumter County residents — but geographic barriers make access difficult for residents of more remote counties like Webster, Marion, or Stewart
• Transportation challenges (discussed further below) mean that even when training programs exist, many residents cannot reliably attend them
• Industry-recognized credentials in fields like healthcare, skilled trades, and advanced manufacturing remain out of reach for many District 151 residents due to distance and cost
What Ethan Wolfe Will Do:
✅ Fight for equitable state education funding formulas that account for the unique challenges of small, rural school districts
✅ Advocate for expanded dual enrollment programs so students in Schley, Marion, Webster, and Stewart counties can access college and technical coursework while still in high school
✅ Support mobile vocational training units and satellite programs through South Georgia Technical College to bring training directly to underserved communities
✅ Push for student loan forgiveness incentives for graduates who return to work and live in District 151 counties
3.
Healthcare Access
A Region in Crisis
Perhaps no issue is more urgent or more personal for District 151 families than access to healthcare.
Southwest Georgia has long been identified as one of the most medically underserved regions in the country.
• According to the Georgia Rural Health Association, the majority of counties within District 151 are designated as Health Professional Shortage Areas (HPSAs) — meaning there are not enough primary care physicians to serve the population⁸
• Terrell County lacks a full-service hospital.
Residents requiring emergency or specialized care must travel to Albany (Dougherty County) or Americus — trips that can exceed 30 to 45 minutes under the best conditions
• Stewart County and Webster County residents face some of the longest travel distances to medical facilities of any communities in Georgia, with the nearest hospitals requiring significant travel on rural roads
• Phoebe Putney Memorial Hospital in Albany serves as the regional hub for much of District 151, but capacity constraints and the distance from outlying counties create serious barriers⁹
•Phoebe Sumter Medical Center in Americus provides critical services for the eastern portion of the district, but rural residents in Marion and Schley counties still face substantial travel burdens
The COVID-19 Legacy
The pandemic exposed and deepened the healthcare disparities already facing District 151:
• Southwest Georgia was among the hardest-hit regions in the United States during the early months of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, with Dougherty County's Albany experiencing death rates that drew national attention¹⁰
• The pandemic strained Phoebe Putney and Phoebe Sumter, highlighting the fragility of the regional healthcare infrastructure that District 151 depends upon
• Recovery has been slow, and the region continues to grapple with long-term public health consequences
Mental Health Services
The mental health crisis in District 151 is significant and largely invisible in state-level policy conversations:
• River Valley Regional Commission service area data consistently shows shortages of licensed mental health providers across the district's counties
• Stigma surrounding mental health in rural communities, combined with a severe lack of local providers, means many residents go without needed care
• Substance use challenges, including issues related to prescription medications and alcohol, are compounded by the absence of treatment resources in many District 151 communities
What Ethan Wolfe Will Do:
✅ Advocate for Medicaid expansion policies that would extend coverage to uninsured working adults across District 151 — a change that would directly benefit thousands of district residents
✅ Support Rural Hospital Tax Credit Program reforms to strengthen hospitals like Sumter Regional that serve as lifelines for district communities
✅ Push for expanded telehealth infrastructure and reimbursement policies that allow District 151 residents to access specialists remotely
✅ Champion community health worker programs that bring basic preventive care and health navigation services into rural communities
✅ Advocate for additional mental health funding through the Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities, targeted specifically at rural southwest Georgia
4.
Infrastructure and Transportation
Roads That Don't Work for Working Families
District 151's road infrastructure is a daily challenge for residents trying to get to work, school, medical appointments, and essential services.
• Many roads throughout Stewart, Webster, Marion, and Schley counties are maintained by county governments with extremely limited budgets, resulting in deteriorating pavement, inadequate drainage, and safety hazards
• Georgia DOT's rural transportation plans acknowledge significant maintenance backlogs across southwest Georgia, but funding priorities have often favored urban and suburban corridors¹¹
• For agricultural communities in Terrell and Schley counties, poor road conditions also directly impact farm-to-market transportation costs, affecting the bottom line for farmers and agribusinesses
The Public Transportation Desert
District 151 is, for practical purposes, a public transportation desert:
• With the exception of limited demand-response transit services in some counties, there is virtually no public transportation infrastructure connecting District 151 communities
• Americus has some local transit capacity through private resources, but connectivity to outlying counties is minimal
• For residents of Lumpkin (Stewart County), Ellaville (Schley County), or Cuthbert (Randolph County, adjacent to the district) who do not own reliable personal vehicles, accessing employment, medical care, or education can be nearly impossible
• The elderly population — which is disproportionately large in District 151, given outmigration of younger residents — is particularly vulnerable to transportation gaps
What Ethan Wolfe Will Do:
✅ Fight for District 151's fair share of Georgia DOT rural road maintenance and improvement funding
✅ Advocate for regional transit coordination across southwest Georgia counties to create meaningful transportation connections between communities
✅ Support federal grant applications through programs like the Federal Transit Administration's Rural Area Formula Program to fund transit infrastructure in District 151
✅ Push for broadband infrastructure investment as a complement to physical transportation — ensuring residents can access telehealth, remote work, and online education without having to drive long distances
5.
Environmental Concerns
Water Quality and Agricultural Runoff
District 151's environment faces real pressures that affect both public health and the agricultural economy:
• The Flint River, which flows through portions of the district, has been subject to ongoing disputes over water use, with agricultural irrigation demands and downstream environmental health in tension¹²
• Water quality concerns related to agricultural runoff affect both drinking water sources and recreational waterways in counties throughout the district
• Stewart County and Terrell County communities have raised concerns about local environmental conditions related to industrial and agricultural activities
Conservation of Natural Assets
District 151 is home to significant natural and historical resources that deserve protection and thoughtful stewardship:
• Providence Canyon State Park in Stewart County — known as "Georgia's Little Grand Canyon" — is one of the state's most unique natural landmarks and a potential driver of heritage tourism if properly supported¹³
• The agricultural landscapes of Schley, Marion, and Terrell counties represent a natural heritage worth preserving for future generations
• Wildlife corridors and wetland areas throughout the district provide ecological services that benefit water quality and flood control
What Ethan Wolfe Will Do:
✅ Support sustainable agricultural practices through UGA Extension partnerships that protect water quality while keeping farms economically viable
✅ Advocate for state investment in heritage tourism around assets like Providence Canyon to diversify the local economy
✅ Push for environmental justice considerations in permitting decisions affecting District 151 communities
