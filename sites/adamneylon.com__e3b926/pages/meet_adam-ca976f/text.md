Meet Adam Waukesha County Winning Media Volunteer DONATE Meet Adam Waukesha County is where Adam built his life.
He came here for Carroll University and stayed.
After graduating in 2008, he started a business, met Hannah, and settled in Pewaukee to raise their three kids.
The county isn’t just the place he represents.
It’s the place he chose.
Before he ran for office, Adam worked for Congressman Jim Sensenbrenner and then as a policy advisor in the Assembly.
In 2013, a special election opened for the seat Paul Farrow was leaving.
Adam ran, won, and has represented the area ever since.
Today that is the 15th Assembly District: Pewaukee, Brookfield, and part of the city of Waukesha.
He also knows what it takes to make a payroll.
In his twenties, he started The Neylon Group, a commercial window-cleaning company he ran for more than a decade.
Later he became a partner in Starting Point Fitness.
That experience still shapes how he looks at government: watch the dollars, cut the unnecessary rules, and leave room for people to build something.
In the Assembly, he has focused on the same idea.
He wrote Wisconsin’s REINS Act, the first law of its kind in the country, so rules with a big price tag have to come back to elected officials instead of being written by agencies.
He has worked on privacy, robotics grants for schools, and clearer information for parents.
As chair and now co-chair of the committee that reviews administrative rules, he has spent years making sure unelected offices stay accountable to the people who pay for them.
Adam is running for County Executive because he wants the next family to have the same chance he had.
That means holding the line on the county’s share of the tax bill, making sure new growth covers its own cost, keeping the roads maintained, and making sure a deputy still comes when you call.
He isn’t running to change what already works here.
He’s running to keep it working for the people who are already here and the ones who want to stay.
When he isn’t working, he is with Hannah and the kids.
He played baseball at Carroll and still stays tied to the university and the community that gave him a start.
Faith, family, a small business, and more than a decade in the Assembly have left him with a simple standard: listen first, be straight about the numbers, and remember who the government is supposed to work for.
Waukesha County gave Adam a place to build a life.
He wants the next generation to be able to do the same.
Keep Waukesha County winning.
