Honoring the Constitution & Our Service Members The unauthorized and unconstitutional use of our military without proper Congressional approval is a violation of the “Sacred Trust” between our government and its people.
Stop the Spending Drain: Decades of undeclared overseas conflicts have cost taxpayers trillions of dollars—money that should be invested right here in Missouri's infrastructure, schools, and communities.
Fighting Inflation at the Source: Unchecked foreign military spending is a primary driver of rising prices.
It fuels federal deficits, devalues the dollar, and forces families in Pettis County to pay more for gas, groceries, and basic necessities.
Protecting Our Troops & Force Readiness: Continuous unauthorized overseas entanglements deplete vital munitions stockpiles and severely overextend our service members.
Pushing troops into continuous combat rotations without a clear constitutional mandate harms troop welfare, worsens recruitment crises, and weakens overall force readiness.
Restoring Our National Defense Arsenal: Sustained strikes and air defense engagements in Middle Eastern conflicts have depleted key interceptor and precision-missile inventories to critical levels.
Our military assets and defense supply chains must be safeguarded for core national defense—not exhausted on undeclared foreign wars.

Standing with Pettis County Farmers Agriculture is the lifeblood of Pettis County, but today our producers are caught in the crossfire of global conflict and trade instability that threaten the very survival of the family farm.
Safeguarding our farmland and rural legacy: Rene Vance is committed to standing up for Pettis County’s agricultural heritage and safeguarding our local family farms.
Our farmland is the backbone of our local economy and our community’s way of life—not a target for regional landfills, energy-draining data centers, or predatory industrial development.
Rene believes land-use decisions must respect property rights, protect clean air and water, and keep local control where it belongs: with the families who live, work, and farm right here in Pettis County.
In Jefferson City, Rene will fight tirelessly to defend our rural legacy and ensure local community voices are always heard above outside special interests.
Reclaiming Lost International Markets: For decades, Missouri grain has fed the world.
However, recent tariff wars have left us without a seat at the table.
Since 2025, we’ve seen a staggering decline in international grain sales—with soybean exports to major partners like China plummeting by over 70%.
When we use tariffs as a blunt instrument, our competitors in South America step in to fill the void, and once those trade relationships are broken, they are incredibly difficult to win back.
I will fight for a trade policy that prioritizes market access over political posturing.
Lowering Input Costs Amid Global Conflict: The current conflict in Iran has crippled the supply of fertilizer components that flow through the Strait of Hormuz.
We’ve seen nitrogen and urea prices spike by over 30% in a single week.
I will advocate for state-level oversight to prevent price gouging and support initiatives that reduce our dependence on foreign fertilizer.
Ending the “Tariff Tax” on Tools: Tariffs don’t just hurt what we sell; they hurt what we buy.
The rising cost of steel and imported machinery parts acts as a hidden tax on every farmer in District 52.
An imported tractor that cost $500,000 now faces an $80,000 duty—that is money coming directly out of a family’s pocket.
Biofuels and Energy Independence: To offset the loss of international sales, we must lean into Missouri-made solutions.
I support expanding our role in biofuel production—using our own corn and soybeans to fuel our state—reducing our vulnerability to global energy shocks and creating a reliable domestic market for our grain.
Why Rene Vance for Agriculture?
A “Fixer” Perspective: Rene understands that you can’t run a farm—or a country—on ad-hoc bailouts.
We need long-term stability and predictable markets.
An Airman’s View of the World: Rene’s service in the Air Force taught her how global instability in places like the Persian Gulf translates directly into $800-per-ton fertilizer prices back home in Sedalia.
A Neighbor Who Shows Up: Whether it’s serving on the Senior Center board or advocating for our 1,000+ local farms, Rene is ready for her Second Service.
