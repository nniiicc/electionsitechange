A call to every city council and county commission in District 20 “` What’s Already Happening Families Are Building Real Resilience.
Across Oakley, Midway, Heber, Kamas, Duchesne, Myton, Roosevelt, and Vernal — families are already doing this work.
They’re capturing rainwater and graywater, filtering it through living systems, planting Utah-native landscapes, growing food, raising chickens, and powering homes with solar.
These aren’t just yards.
They are closed-loop systems that save water, lower energy costs, produce local food, and strengthen household resilience.
Right now, most of that effort goes unrecognized.
That should change.

They are one system — and good design connects them.
The Proposal Annual Legacy Landscapes Beautification Awards.
I’m calling on every city council and county commission in District 20 to launch annual Legacy Landscapes Beautification Awards — recognizing families and properties that demonstrate water-wise, self-reliant design.
This isn’t a state mandate.
It’s a local recognition program that any city or county can start with one resolution.

The same principle that guides district-level infrastructure planning applies at the household level.
When water, energy, food, and land use are designed together — instead of treated separately — you get resilience instead of strain.
Reduces municipal water demand — less pressure on shared systems Keeps food production local — supports the farm-to-table connection Lowers household energy costs — rural grid stability from the bottom up Preserves native landscapes — water recharge, habitat, and beauty Builds community identity — neighbors recognizing neighbors “` “` The Principle These aren’t just yards.
