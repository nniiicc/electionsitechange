These aren’t project commitments — they’re the constraints that govern how every decision gets made, regardless of pressure, urgency, or convenience.
I Safety Is a Design Requirement Safety must be incorporated from the outset — not delegated to compliance checks after the fact.
II Prevention Is Superior to Reaction Upstream risk elimination outperforms downstream mitigation.
Design the harm out before it happens.
III Avoidable Risk Must Not Be Shifted Onto the Public or Workforce Risk should be borne by those with the authority to reduce it — not by workers or communities who can’t control it.
IV Systems Fail Before Individuals Do Chronic congestion, recurring accidents, and repeated exposure events are system failures — not individual choices.
V Interdependent Systems Must Be Governed as Systems Infrastructure, transportation, energy, water, and emergency response are interconnected.
Optimizing one at the expense of others creates hidden risk.
VI Accountability Must Be Measurable and Transparent Safety claims without measurable outcomes aren’t sufficient.
Metrics inform — they don’t punish.
VII Safety and Liberty Must Advance Together Public health protections must be achieved without suspending constitutional rights or bypassing lawful process.
VIII Responsibility Must Align With Authority Those who control design, siting, and operations must bear responsibility for the safety outcomes those decisions create.
IX Safety Must Be Continuous, Not Static Frameworks must adapt to evidence — changing conditions, growth, new risks.
Static systems fall behind.
X Public Safety Decisions Must Be Understandable Plain language.
Disclosed assumptions.
Clear explanation of who bears risk and why.
Workforce Protections Truck Drivers Bill of Rights.
No safety system is legitimate if it depends on drivers accepting unsafe conditions to keep it functioning.
Right I Safe & Timely Unloading Drivers have the right to unloading processes that don’t require excessive waiting, unsafe queuing, or prolonged idling.
Safe unloading is a system obligation — not a driver burden.
Right II Safe Waiting & Staging When waiting is unavoidable, drivers have the right to designated staging areas with safe conditions and environments free from unnecessary hazard.
Right III Freedom From Retaliation Drivers must not face retaliation for refusing unsafe conditions, complying with hours-of-service laws, or prioritizing safety over throughput.
Right IV Safety-Aligned Scheduling Operational schedules must not incentivize speeding or penalize lawful rest.
Systems that require drivers to break the law to function are unsafe by design.
Right V Clarity & Predictability Drivers have the right to clear communication about unloading expectations and predictable scheduling windows where feasible.
Right VI Protection From Design Failures Drivers must not be treated as the last line of defense against congestion caused by inadequate infrastructure.
Design failure is not driver failure.
Right VII Dignity & Professional Respect Drivers are entitled to humane treatment, professional recognition, and working conditions consistent with their role as safety-critical operators.
