That's not changing.
What needs to change is the assumption that only government inspectors can decide which physical locations are safe enough for your child to spend time in.
Right now, states have patchwork location-based regulations that block childcare in spaces that parents and providers both think are perfectly suitable.
A provider can't run childcare in a home because regulations say the staircase height is wrong.
A parent can't use a space they've assessed themselves and deemed safe because it doesn't match some mandate written for institutional facilities, not family homes.
Congress will commission a comprehensive checklist of all the safety features currently mandated by states, based on the strictest requirements nationwide.
This checklist gets published online.
A provider can print it, give it to parents, and parents conduct their own inspection of the premises.
They look for the things that actually matter: working fire extinguishers, electrical hazards, paint chips, stair safety, the things you would check if you were assessing any space your child would spend time in.
If the parent finds an issue they think is serious, they decide what to do.
Fix it, find a different space, negotiate with the provider.
That decision stays with the parent, not a distant regulator who has never seen the location or met the child.
The parent signs a simple affidavit saying they got the checklist, did the inspection, found the place reasonably safe, and consent to the exemption from state location-based rules.
If conditions change, the provider notifies parents immediately.
If a parent changes their mind, they revoke consent anytime.
This covers childcare in homes, in other people's homes, mixed-age groups, all of it.
The barriers that exist because regulations were written for large facilities, not family-based care, get removed.
Childcare safety depends on trained, background-checked providers in spaces that parents have actually assessed.
It doesn't depend on a state bureaucracy deciding that the only acceptable kitchen is an institutional one, or that a staircase one inch the wrong height makes a family home unsuitable.
Trust parents to make informed decisions about their own children's safety.
Give providers the freedom to offer childcare in spaces that work, not just in spaces that check every box on a mandate designed for something else.
