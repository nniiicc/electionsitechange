Senate candidate calls on Governor Whitmer to request a DOJ civil rights investigation following three inmate deaths in less than a month at Michigan's only women's prison.
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE June 9, 2026 Contact: Lydia Christensen Senator@lydialynnchristensen.com lydiaforsenate.com IRONWOOD, MI — Independent U.S.
Senate candidate Lydia Christensen today sent a formal letter to Governor Gretchen Whitmer calling on her to request a federal civil rights investigation into Women's Huron Valley Correctional Facility following three inmate deaths in less than a month.
Khaira Howard, 28, died May 13.
Rebecca Fackler, 57, died May 17.
Ashley Hoath, 36, died June 6.
Attorneys handling civil cases for the families of Howard and Fackler allege both women were denied medical care before their deaths.
Inmates near Hoath say they spent three hours urging guards to check on her before anyone acted.
She had graduated from Jackson College while incarcerated less than two months before her death.
MDOC has not released an environmental assessment of the facility conducted in response to mold concerns.
On June 8, U.S.
Rep.
Debbie Dingell wrote to Governor Whitmer demanding that any studies, inspections, environmental assessments, or reports concerning toxic mold and other health and safety conditions at the facility be released to the public.
Christensen is calling on Whitmer to formally request a Department of Justice investigation under the Civil Rights of Institutionalized Persons Act, which authorizes the Attorney General to investigate state prisons where a pattern or practice of constitutional violations is causing grievous harm to residents.
"Three deaths in three weeks is not a coincidence.
It is a pattern," Christensen said.
"Governor Whitmer has the standing to request federal oversight today and she should use it.
If she acts, Michigan women are safer.
If she doesn't, Michigan voters will know she had the power and chose not to use it." Thirty Michigan lawmakers have called for MDOC Director Heidi Washington's resignation.
Christensen argues resignation alone is insufficient without structural federal oversight to identify root causes and produce enforceable remedies.
Members of the public can also file CRIPA complaints directly with the DOJ Civil Rights Division at https://www.justice.gov/crt/how-file-complaint .
The full letter to Governor Whitmer is available at https://www.lydialynnchristensen.com .
About Lydia Christensen Lydia Christensen is an independent candidate for the United States Senate in Michigan running under the Unchaining Michigan platform.
She is based in Ironwood in the Upper Peninsula.
Media Contact Lydia Christensen senator@lydialynnchristensen.com Full Letter to Governor Whitmer Governor Whitmer and Press Office, I am writing as an independent candidate for the United States Senate in Michigan to formally request that Governor Whitmer use her standing to request a federal Department of Justice investigation into Women's Huron Valley Correctional Facility under the Civil Rights of Institutionalized Persons Act (CRIPA).
Three women have died at Michigan's only women's prison in less than a month.
Khaira Howard, 28, died May 13.
Rebecca Fackler, 57, died May 17.
Ashley Hoath, 36, died June 6.
Attorneys representing the families of Howard and Fackler allege both women were denied medical care before their deaths.
Inmates report spending three hours urging guards to check on Hoath before anyone responded.
Thirty Michigan lawmakers have called for MDOC Director Heidi Washington's resignation.
I agree that change in leadership is necessary.
It is not sufficient.
Resignation alone does not identify root causes, does not produce enforceable remedies, and does not prevent the next death.
A federal CRIPA investigation does.
CRIPA authorizes the Attorney General to investigate state prisons where a pattern or practice of constitutional violations is causing grievous harm to residents.
Governor Whitmer can formally request that investigation today.
I am calling on her to do so.
A copy of my formal letter on this matter is attached.
It has also been released publicly and distributed to Michigan media.
