Senate candidate Lydia Christensen calls for the restoration of White House press access for CNN, MS NOW and Politico and proposes stronger protections for journalists covering the federal government.
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE September 21, 2026 Contact: Lydia Christensen | senator@lydialynnchristensen.com IRONWOOD, MICHIGAN — U.S.
Senate candidate Lydia Christensen is calling for the restoration of White House press access for CNN, MS NOW and Politico after reporters from the three organizations were denied entry and had their press credentials disabled or confiscated.
President Donald Trump announced the ban Friday, accusing the outlets of publishing what he called "fake news." The ban was enforced Saturday, when reporters for the three organizations were turned away from the White House.
The outlets have now announced plans to sue the Trump administration, arguing that the restrictions violate their First Amendment rights.
"The government should not be deciding which journalists are allowed to report from the White House based on whether the president likes their coverage," Christensen said.
"The press does not work for the president.
It works for the American people." Christensen said the issue goes beyond CNN, MS NOW or Politico.
"When the government can punish a news organization by taking away access because it doesn't like what that organization reports, every journalist has reason to be concerned.
A free press has to be able to question the people in power.
That includes presidents." Christensen said that, if elected to the U.S.
Senate, she would push for legislation establishing clearer protections for journalists covering the federal government.
Her proposed reforms would: Require clear standards: Federal agencies that issue press credentials should publish clear standards for granting, denying, and revoking credentials.
Require due process: Journalists should receive notice and an opportunity to respond before a press credential is revoked, except when an immediate security concern requires temporary action.
Prohibit retaliation: Federal officials should not use press credentials, press pool assignments, or government access as retaliation for an outlet's editorial position or critical reporting.
Increase congressional oversight: Congress should have greater oversight of federal press credentialing practices.
Christensen also said the Senate should examine the role of congressional press galleries in the federal press credentialing system and whether existing procedures provide sufficient transparency and accountability.
"The First Amendment belongs to everyone," Christensen said.
"It doesn't disappear when the person being questioned is the president." Media Availability Lydia Christensen is available for satellite, in-studio, and phone interviews regarding First Amendment rights, press access, congressional oversight, and executive power.
