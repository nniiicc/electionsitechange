Meet Lydia Christensen - built from the ground up, answering to no political machine, no donor, and no boss.
Lydia Christensen's Story Lydia Christensen's family comes from Ironwood, Michigan, in the heart of the Upper Peninsula.
She was born across the border in Ashland, Wisconsin, helicoptered there because the UP lacked the hospital infrastructure to deliver her safely.
Growing up connected to Michigan's Upper Peninsula shaped her understanding of hard work, resilience, independence, and community.
She didn't have an easy start.
Growing up in the foster care system, Lydia built her foundation from scratch, earning the equivalent of two degrees in computer science and computer programming before eventually founding her own technology company.
Her background in technology and entrepreneurship taught her how to build something from the ground up, a perspective she now brings to her campaign for the United States Senate.
Returning to Michigan after college, she found her purpose in the Upper Peninsula's untamed landscapes — its waterfalls, forests, and Great Lakes — and in the people who call Michigan home.
Protecting those places became an important part of her work, including her opposition to continued operation of Line 5 through the Straits of Mackinac and her focus on protecting the Great Lakes from environmental harm.
As the Libertarian Party nominee for U.S.
Senate in Michigan, Lydia's campaign focuses on issues that affect communities across the state, including Great Lakes protection, housing, tribal sovereignty, civil liberties, and economic opportunity.
Her approach comes from the same principle that shaped her life: problems should be confronted directly, and solutions should be built from the ground up.
Her vision is straightforward: a Michigan where every resident has a voice that is actually heard.
Where the Great Lakes are protected.
Where opportunity is not reserved for the well-connected.
And where the lessons of history are never forgotten.
