Affordability and a fair economy Families, education, healthcare, and quality of life Freedom, privacy, and democracy Strong communities and Georgia’s future Affordability and a fair economy A full-time job ought to be enough to build a stable life.
Cost of living + A person working 40 hours a week should be able to afford housing, groceries, utilities, childcare, and healthcare.
The focus is on the costs families actually face every day.
Living wage + Anyone working full time should earn enough for food, housing, healthcare, transportation, and a little breathing room.
Taxpayers should not subsidize major employers that pay poverty wages.
Housing affordability + Teachers, nurses, first responders, young families, and retirees should be able to afford a home in their own communities.
The campaign supports more starter homes, assessment protections, and long-term homeownership.
Taxes + Lower taxes for people in District 31 and oppose schemes that shift more of the burden onto homeowners and consumers while wealthy corporations receive exemptions.
Consumer protection + Stand up for honest prices, fair contracts, safe products, and control over personal information — including protections against hidden fees, price manipulation, predatory lending, and data misuse.
People over profits + Georgia’s economy should reward hard work, not only wealth and political connections.
Lower costs, better jobs, consumer protections, and accountable corporate citizenship belong together.
Families, education, healthcare, and quality of life Government should help people have more time, more security, and a fair shot at a good life.
Cradle to Certificate education + A lifelong education system that begins when parental leave ends and continues through college or a trade certificate, with affordable childcare, strong public schools, and accessible training.
No homework + Kids already spend most of the day in school.
Learning should primarily happen in the classroom, leaving room for family, play, rest, and creativity after the bell.
Family leave + Guarantee paid parental leave and meaningful bereavement and caregiving leave so people can be there during the moments that matter most.
Healthcare + Healthcare should be a basic right, not a profit center.
Georgia can build a system that puts patients first and makes care — including mental-health care — accessible and affordable.
Quality of life + Working families deserve real maternity and paternity leave, affordable childcare, strong schools, elder care that does not wipe out savings, and one outstanding meal a day for every student.
Freedom, privacy, and democracy Georgia should trust adults, protect rights, and make democratic participation easier — not harder.
Personal freedom + Adults should be free to make decisions about their bodies, families, beliefs, and private lives, so long as they are not harming others.
Government should protect rights, not micromanage them.
Marijuana legalization + Legalize, regulate, and tax marijuana for adults to create jobs, generate public revenue, reduce unnecessary arrests, and let law enforcement focus on serious crime.
Privacy and government surveillance + Require warrants for historical location data, set limits on data retention, require public approval for surveillance technology, create audit logs, protect biometrics, and prevent data misuse.
Voting rights + Keep voting secure, accessible, and convenient; protect early and absentee voting; oppose unnecessary barriers; and create automatic voter registration at 18 with a free Georgia Voter ID.
End gerrymandering + Voters should choose their representatives — not the other way around.
Political maps should reflect real communities and the people who live in them, not protect politicians or silence voters.
End partisan gerrymandering and draw fair districts the public can trust.
Strong communities and Georgia’s future Growth has to be planned — with jobs, infrastructure, and accountable government that makes everyday life better.
Jobs in the district + Build good jobs in Paulding and Polk Counties in healthcare, solar and clean energy, skilled trades, logistics, and advanced manufacturing, tied to local technical schools, colleges, and workforce training.
Transportation and traffic + Match District 31’s growth with better roads, smarter planning, and real transit options so people can get to work without spending their lives behind the wheel.
Strong communities + Build neighborhoods where families can thrive and growth does not erase the quality of life that brought people here.
Data centers and the AI rush + No giant industrial buildings dropped next to neighborhoods without a clear public benefit.
Data centers place major demands on land, power, water, and local infrastructure.
Georgia should not give blank checks to projects that eliminate jobs, expand surveillance, or send the benefits somewhere else.
Growth has to serve the people who live here.
Government that works + Make government transparent, accountable, responsive, and focused on practical solutions rather than political theater.
Building Georgia’s future + Prepare Georgia for the next generation with long-term investment in people, communities, opportunity, and the infrastructure to support them.
A better future is possible Let’s build a Georgia that works for the people who make it run.
