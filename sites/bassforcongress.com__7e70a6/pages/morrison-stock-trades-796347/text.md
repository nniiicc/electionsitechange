A very active trader in a very short time Morrison was sworn in at the start of 2025.
Reporting by the Minnesota Star Tribune found she executed 106 trades in her first 16 months in Washington, along with private investments running into the high six figures.
A separate NOTUS review counted about 90 trades during the 119th Congress at that point, which made her one of Congress’s more active traders.
That is a lot of activity for a member of Congress whose job is to vote on defense spending, health policy, and regulation.
Her office has said her portfolio is run by an investment adviser.
That may be true, but the disclosure forms carry her name, not her adviser’s.
STOCK Act Violation -- Late disclosures The STOCK Act of 2012 requires members of Congress to report their trades publicly within 45 days.
According to a NOTUS review of congressional financial records, Morrison failed to properly disclose eight trades worth between $1.41 million and $2.91 million.
Her office blamed the delays on her investment manager and said the reporting process had been updated.
Transparency rules exist so voters can see who benefits from decisions in Washington.
When millions of dollars in trades show up late, that purpose is defeated.
Morrison was months — in one case more than a year — late disclosing eight financial trades worth $1.41 million to $2.91 million, mostly stakes in Andersen Corporation.
The STOCK Act requires disclosure within 45 days regardless of who executes the trade.
Her office paid the standard $200 fine.

Why Saronic Matters Saronic is not an abstract investment.

She Can’t Have It Both Ways Morrison has been publicly opposed to the war in Iran. [7] Yet nine days after it began, she bought up to $50,000 in a company that builds warships for the Navy.
She is a sitting member of Congress who votes on the defense budget and on the spending that keeps contractors like Saronic in business.
Her office says she had no prior knowledge of the trade and that her investment adviser made it. [7] Maybe so.
But that leaves a question she has to answer: which is it?
Either she knew what she was buying and did it anyway, or she doesn’t know what is in her own portfolio.
Neither answer is acceptable from someone who votes on war and defense spending.
And the timeline doesn’t help her.
She bought on March 11 and held until May 4.
That is 54 days, nearly eight weeks, while American troops were in combat. [2] [6] A member of Congress who truly opposed this war would have dumped the stock the moment she found out.
She sold only after the reporters started asking.
When did she find out, and why did it take until then?
