What the SAVE America Act does The House passed the SAVE America Act 218-213 in February 2026 .
It does three things: Requires proof of citizenship, in person, to register to vote in a federal election.
Requires photo ID to vote.
Requires states to remove noncitizens from existing voter rolls.
That’s it.
It’s a simple safeguard, and it applies to every voter and every party.
If you win, it protects your win.
If you lose, it protects your confidence in the result.
Minnesota’s gaps Many Minnesotans don’t know how our system works, and they’re surprised when they find out.
If your registration is current, you don’t need to show any ID at the polls .
Not a driver’s license, not anything.
Minnesota also allows same-day registration.
State law lets one registered voter sign up to eight proof-of-residence oaths on Election Day, and the Secretary of State’s office calls this vouching .
I’m not claiming this means elections here are stolen.
I’m saying we ask for ID to board a plane, open a bank account, or buy a firearm, and a free and fair election deserves the same standard.

Where Kelly Morrison stands Minnesota’s delegation split straight down party lines on the bill.
Every Republican voted yes.
Every Democrat, including Kelly Morrison, voted no.
Her side says the bill could make registration harder for some eligible voters, like married women whose names don’t match their birth certificates, or people without a passport.
Those are real concerns, and they can be answered with free IDs and clear processes for name changes.
They don’t justify voting no on the whole bill.
Voting no means keeping the current rules, with all their gaps, over any change at all.
I’d like to hear her answer this question: what exactly was wrong with requiring proof of citizenship and a photo ID, and what would she support instead?
Why this matters in CD3 Our district was held by Republicans for decades and has been blue for only the last eight years.
This will be a close race, and in a close race, confidence in the process matters most.
Whoever wins in November, everyone should be able to trust the count.
I ran businesses for two decades, and I learned that you verify, you document, and you own your mistakes.
That’s how trust is built.
