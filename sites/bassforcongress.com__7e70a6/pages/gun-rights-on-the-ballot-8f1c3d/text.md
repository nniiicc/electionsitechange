He Wrote It Down.
In His Own Words.
Quentin Wittrock, my opponent in this Republican primary, used to run a political blog called Principle Based Politics.
Before he decided he wanted your vote, he put his real positions in writing — no filter, no campaign consultant softening the language.
Just his own thinking, published for anyone to read.
In a post titled “Federal Gun Control: Applying Principles,” Wittrock argued, in his own words, that the federal government should require background checks on every single gun sale in the country with zero exceptions — and that it should ban the consumer sale of assault weapons, semi-automatic weapons, and their ammunition.
This includes the AR-15, AR-10, any pistol that is not a single shot or revolver and shot guns.
Which is basically every weapon sold today across America used for home defense.
Read that again.
Ban the consumer sale of semi-automatic weapons.
That’s not gun-show talk.
That’s not a hypothetical.
That’s the AR-15 sitting in your safe right now.
That’s the rifle you sighted in at the range this summer.
That’s the platform half of us in this district use for home defense, for hunting, for competition shooting, for just enjoying a Saturday putting rounds downrange.
Wittrock wants it banned.
He said so himself.
He didn’t stop there either.
Three days before that post, he wrote a companion piece laying out the philosophical groundwork for exactly this position — walking through the Second Amendment step by step, only to arrive at the conclusion that the federal government should be in the business of deciding which guns you’re allowed to own.
Both posts have since vanished from his website.
Funny how that happens right around campaign season.
