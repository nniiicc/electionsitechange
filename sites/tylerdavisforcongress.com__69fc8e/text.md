Who You’d Be Voting For I’m Tyler L.
Davis, and I’m running for Congress to bring servant leadership back to District 1.
This campaign is about people—not politics or myself.

Family Time Shoreline Park, Santa Rosa February Highway Cleanup Bayou Blvd, Escambia Educational Experiences Gulfarium, Okaloosa Supporting Local The Leisure Club, Escambia Moments that Matter For me, public service doesn't begin in Washington—it begins in our neighborhoods.
I believe the best way to understand the needs of Florida's 1st District is to be present.
That means showing up in our communities, listening to residents, supporting local businesses, volunteering at community events, and spending time where people live, work, and raise their families.
Movement happens at the local level so much more than we recognize, but that’s something we should aim to rethink.
Whether I'm helping translate federal and state policies into what they actually mean for our communities, learning about hyperlocal challenges firsthand, or working alongside volunteers at community events, my goal is always the same: to make government more understandable, more accessible, and more responsive to the people it serves.
Every conversation matters.
Every neighborhood has its own priorities.
By staying connected to the people of District 1, I can better advocate for solutions that reflect the realities of our communities, not just headlines or party politics.
The moments that matter most aren't found in political speeches, shaking hands with career politicians, or poking fun at others using AI-generated content that’s killing the human connection so many people can’t seem to find any longer.
Moments that matter are found in listening to a small business owner, helping a family at a community giveaway, talking with veterans, meeting students, attending local events, and hearing directly from the people who call the Panhandle home.
That's the kind of leadership I believe our district deserves—leadership that's present, engaged, and grounded in the communities it represents.
I’ve had enough of big personalities who only grow themselves while leaving the Panhandle to fend for itself.
