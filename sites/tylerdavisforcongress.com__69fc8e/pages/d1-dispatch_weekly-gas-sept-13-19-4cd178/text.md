1.
Escambia commissioners hold the line on large data centers
The Escambia County Board of County Commissioners decided Thursday not to pursue an additional data-center moratorium because county attorneys say large-scale data centers are already prohibited in unincorporated Escambia County.
Commissioners adopted the prohibition in August after earlier determining that the facilities weren't appropriate under the county's Land Development Code.
(North Escambia)
Why it matters locally: This creates an interesting economic-development tradeoff.
Escambia is actively pursuing technology, advanced manufacturing and defense-related growth, but large data centers can bring substantial demands for electricity, water and land while often employing relatively few people after construction.
The bigger opportunity may be determining what kinds of high-tech development deliver the best ratio of local jobs and wages to infrastructure and environmental costs.

New federal data shows Escambia and Okaloosa employment slipped while Escambia wages grew
Fresh figures from the U.S.
Bureau of Labor Statistics show covered employment fell year-over-year by 0.9% in Escambia County and 0.7% in Okaloosa County as of March 2026.
Escambia nevertheless recorded a 4.3% increase in average weekly wages, matching Florida's statewide wage-growth rate.
Across Florida's 27 largest counties, 17 experienced employment declines.
(Bureau of Labor Statistics)
Why it matters locally: This gives us a useful baseline against which to judge all the major job announcements coming into Northwest Florida.
Announcing 1,000 or 2,000 future positions isn't the same as achieving net employment growth.
Over the next several quarters, BLS data should help show whether new maritime, aviation, defense and construction investment is actually expanding the region's employment base–and whether wages are keeping up.

That creates an opportunity for a more data-driven conversation about the next round of roads, parks, public-safety equipment and community infrastructure.

Walton County upgrades 911 so multiple agencies receive emergency calls simultaneously
Walton County Sheriff's Office announced yesterday that the South Walton Fire District and DeFuniak Springs Police Department have joined its Simultaneous 911 Ringing System.
Instead of a call having to be transferred sequentially between dispatch centers, appropriate calls can now ring at multiple agencies at the same time.
Walton County GIS built the geographic routing layers, and dispatchers spent roughly three months testing the system.
(Wiregrass Daily News)
Why it matters locally: Rapid population and tourism growth makes emergency-response infrastructure just as important as roads and utilities.
This is a relatively inexpensive kind of modernization that could shave time off emergency communication while giving police, fire and EMS better shared situational awareness.
It's a model worth comparing with dispatch systems elsewhere in Northwest Florida.

Skilled trades are becoming a national economic story at exactly the right time for Northwest Florida
A fresh national report today highlights growing institutional investment in apprenticeships and skilled-trades careers, driven partly by infrastructure demand and concern that AI will disrupt some traditional white-collar entry-level work.
Florida is already moving in the same direction: the state awarded $14.6 million this summer to 37 apprenticeship and pre-apprenticeship programs expected to serve more than 8,000 students.
(Axios)
Why it matters locally: Northwest Florida may be unusually well positioned to capitalize on this shift.
Maritime expansion in Pensacola, aviation around Whiting Field, defense work surrounding Eglin, and continuing construction growth all require welders, electricians, pipefitters, mechanics, technicians and other skilled workers.
The opportunity is to build a recognizable regional pathway from middle/high school exposure → technical education → paid apprenticeship → local career, rather than waiting until employers arrive and then discovering we don't have enough trained residents to fill the jobs.

New Esca-Rosa reefs move from planning into actual deployment
There's a meaningful update to last week's reef story: Escambia County announced yesterday that the Esca-Rosa Regional Reefs are now being deployed.
