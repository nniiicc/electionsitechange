Learning is for the birds (and for you too)
Education should prepare Americans not just for graduation, but for life.
Every student deserves the opportunity to become a strong reader, critical thinker, skilled worker, and engaged citizen.
As technology, industries, and our economy continue to evolve, our education system should prepare students to adapt, continue learning, and build meaningful careers throughout their lives.
I support:
- Strengthening literacy and reading comprehension from an early age.
- Expanding career and technical education alongside traditional college pathways.
- Preparing students for careers that don't yet exist by teaching adaptability and lifelong learning.
- Investing in workforce development so Americans can continue supporting themselves throughout their careers.
- Encouraging partnerships between schools, businesses, military installations, nonprofits, and community organizations.
- Promoting curiosity, innovation, and problem-solving as core educational values.
- Reading is one of the strongest predictors of lifelong success.
Literacy today extends far beyond simply reading words on a page—it includes understanding information, evaluating sources, and communicating effectively.
I support:
- Expanding early childhood literacy programs.
- Strengthening reading comprehension and writing instruction.
- Increasing access to books through schools, libraries, and community partnerships.
- Encouraging family literacy programs that promote reading at home.
- Teaching media literacy so students can distinguish facts from opinions.
- Teaching digital literacy so students can responsibly navigate online information.
- Helping students recognize misinformation and evaluate the credibility of sources.
Strong literacy skills prepare students for every subject, every career, and informed participation in society.
- Not every successful career requires a four-year college degree.
America needs skilled workers in industries that keep our communities functioning, who don’t have to worry about college loan debt, and keep our economy competitive.
I support:
- Expanding career and technical education in high-demand industries.
- Strengthening apprenticeship programs.
- Increasing partnerships between employers and educational institutions.
- Supporting trade schools and certification programs.
- Expanding opportunities in manufacturing, healthcare, aviation, maritime industries, agriculture, construction, cybersecurity, emergency services, and skilled trades.
- Giving students meaningful career exploration opportunities before graduation.
Every student should graduate with multiple pathways to success.
- Artificial intelligence will become one of the defining technologies of this century.
Rather than fearing it or blindly embracing it, we should prepare Americans to understand it and use it responsibly.
I support:
- Teaching students how AI works—not just how to use it.
- Helping students understand the difference between AI predictions and verified facts.
- Teaching "good data vs. bad data" so students understand that AI is only as reliable as the information it learns from.
- Expanding education on bias in datasets and how inaccurate information can influence AI-generated results.
- Promoting responsible AI use, including academic integrity, privacy, transparency, and intellectual property.
- Teaching students when human judgment should always take priority over AI recommendations.
- Encouraging AI as a tool to enhance creativity, productivity, and learning—not replace critical thinking.
- Supporting sustainable AI development by encouraging energy-efficient computing, responsible data center growth, and infrastructure planning that balances innovation with environmental stewardship.
- Preparing workers for careers that increasingly use AI through lifelong learning and workforce training.
The future belongs not to those who simply use AI, but to those who understand it, question it, improve it, and use it responsibly.
- Financial education should prepare students for real life, and I know all too well that most kids go into adulthood without understanding the basics behind even filing their taxes for the first time.
And maybe that’s because their parents didn’t have the savvy, and didn’t know how get it so they could pass it on to their kids.
Financial literacy isn’t just a necessity for our world, it’s a gatekeeper of financial freedom and success.
I support teaching:
- Budgeting and saving.
- Credit scores and responsible borrowing.
- Taxes and personal finance.
- Banking and investing.
- Insurance.
- Retirement planning.
- Homeownership.
- Entrepreneurship and small business fundamentals.
Financial literacy empowers people to build stable families, successful careers, and stronger communities.
- Teachers are one of our nation's greatest assets, yet too many spend more time navigating paperwork and administrative requirements than doing what they do best—teaching and mentoring students.
I support:
- Reducing unnecessary administrative burdens so teachers can spend more time in the classroom and less time completing paperwork.
- Expanding professional development opportunities that help educators keep pace with evolving teaching methods, technology, and workforce needs.
- Improving teacher recruitment and retention by creating supportive work environments and recognizing the importance of experienced educators.
- Providing teachers with modern educational resources, classroom technology, and access to high-quality instructional materials.
- Encouraging mentorship programs that pair experienced teachers with new educators to improve retention and professional growth.
- Giving educators greater flexibility to adapt lessons to meet the unique needs of their students while maintaining academic standards.
- Strengthening partnerships between educators, parents, businesses, and community organizations to create more real-world learning opportunities.
Teachers should be empowered to teach, innovate, and inspire—not overwhelmed by bureaucracy.
- Every student deserves to learn in an environment where they feel safe, supported, and respected.
School safety should focus not only on responding to emergencies, but also on preventing them through stronger relationships, better communication, and early intervention.
I support:
- Improving campus security where appropriate while maintaining welcoming learning environments.
- Strengthening emergency preparedness through regular training, updated response plans, and coordination with local first responders.
- Expanding access to school-based mental health resources for students and staff.
- Supporting anonymous reporting systems that encourage students to report potential threats before they escalate.
- Encouraging partnerships between schools, parents, law enforcement, healthcare providers, and community organizations to identify concerns early.
- Investing in violence prevention, conflict resolution, and anti-bullying programs that build stronger school cultures.
- Promoting digital safety education so students understand responsible technology use, cyberbullying prevention, and online security.
The safest schools are those where students feel connected, supported, and comfortable asking for help before problems become crises.
- Higher education should prepare students for meaningful careers while recognizing that success comes through many different educational pathways.
Whether someone chooses a university, community college, apprenticeship, military service, or technical certification, every path should lead to opportunities for lifelong success.
I support:
- Strengthening community colleges as affordable pathways into high-demand careers.
- Supporting universities that advance research, innovation, and workforce development.
- Expanding apprenticeship programs and industry-recognized certification opportunities.
- Encouraging stronger partnerships between educational institutions and employers so students graduate with skills that match workforce needs.
- Supporting career counseling that helps students explore multiple educational and career pathways before taking on significant student debt.
- Promoting internships, cooperative education, and work-based learning opportunities that provide real-world experience before graduation.
- Encouraging lifelong learning by making continuing education and professional certifications more accessible throughout a person's career.
Education should help people build fulfilling careers—not leave them uncertain about their next step after graduation.
- Education doesn't end when students leave the classroom.
Some of life's most valuable lessons happen through real-world experiences that connect people to their communities, history, environment, and future careers.
Communities play an essential role in education by encouraging curiosity, civic engagement, service, and lifelong learning.
Strong schools, engaged families, local businesses, nonprofits, libraries, museums, parks, military installations, and community organizations all have a role to play in preparing the next generation for success.
I support:
- Expanding partnerships between schools, libraries, museums, parks, nonprofits, military installations, and local businesses.
- Encouraging mentorship programs that connect students with experienced professionals across a variety of careers.
- Supporting internships, apprenticeships, volunteer opportunities, and service-learning projects that allow students to apply classroom knowledge in real-world settings.
- Promoting STEM and STEAM programs that inspire innovation through hands-on learning experiences.
- Encouraging local businesses, skilled professionals, first responders, veterans, artists, scientists, and tradespeople to participate in career exploration and classroom engagement.
- Supporting outdoor education, environmental stewardship, local history, and conservation programs that help students better understand and appreciate the communities they call home.
- Expanding opportunities for families to participate in educational activities together through literacy programs, community events, workshops, and public learning opportunities.
- Encouraging experiential learning through field experiences, historical landmarks, nature preserves, community service projects, entrepreneurship programs, civic engagement activities, and partnerships that allow students to learn by doing rather than only reading.
Education should inspire curiosity beyond the classroom.
When students experience their communities firsthand, they develop stronger problem-solving skills, greater civic responsibility, and a deeper appreciation for the people and places around them.
Those experiences create lifelong learners who are better prepared to contribute to their communities and adapt to an ever-changing world.

Economic Opportunity
I believe every American should be able to support themselves and their family through meaningful work.
My goal isn't simply to create jobs—it's to create opportunities for people to build lasting careers.
That means investing in education, apprenticeships, re-skilling, entrepreneurship, and lifelong learning so workers can adapt as industries evolve.
A stronger workforce leads to stronger families, stronger businesses, and a stronger Panhandle.
Protect the promises made to our people and modernize the systems so we can do so.
- Inflation makes it harder for families to afford the basics—from groceries and childcare to gas and housing.
While Congress cannot control prices directly, it can reduce many of the pressures that drive inflation.
I support:
- Reducing unnecessary federal spending that fuels long-term inflation.
- Encouraging domestic production so America relies less on unstable foreign supply chains.
- Strengthening competition to reduce monopolistic pricing.
- Expanding workforce participation so businesses can grow without driving unsustainable labor shortages.
- Investing in infrastructure that improves efficiency and lowers transportation costs.
A stronger economy begins with producing more of what Americans need at home while spending taxpayer dollars responsibly.
- Every dollar Congress spends belongs to the American people.
I support conducting regular reviews of federal agencies and programs to identify waste, duplication, and outdated initiatives.
Programs that continue to deliver measurable value should be maintained, while those that no longer serve the public effectively should be reformed or eliminated.
Reducing waste allows us to invest in priorities that strengthen our communities without unnecessarily increasing the national debt.
- The growing national debt threatens future generations by increasing interest costs that crowd out investments in education, infrastructure, defense, and innovation.
Reducing waste and making America money?
Oh yeah, I know a little about that.
I support:
- Responsible budgeting.
- Multi-year financial planning.
- Reviewing mandatory and discretionary spending for efficiencies.
- Growing the economy through productivity instead of excessive borrowing.
Debt reduction should be gradual, responsible, and avoid placing unnecessary hardship on working families.
- Taxes-still one of the hottest topics in America since that really famous tea party that took place back in the day.
The only thing that’s changed between the beginning of our country and where we are now is the level of greed our government has come to depend on from its taxpayers.
America's tax code has become unnecessarily complicated.
I support:
- Simplifying the federal tax code.
- Reducing unnecessary compliance burdens on families and businesses.
- Encouraging work, innovation, and entrepreneurship.
- Making it easier for small businesses to succeed.
Tax policy should reward productivity while remaining fair and understandable.
- Homeowners insurance has become one of the largest financial burdens facing Floridians while insurance companies give you less while asking you to pay more.
I support working with state and local leaders to:
- Encourage greater market competition.
- Improve community resilience through stronger building standards and mitigation efforts.
- Reduce fraud where it exists.
- Invest in disaster preparedness to reduce long-term insurance losses.
Families should not be forced to choose between protecting their homes and affording everyday necessities.
- Flooding affects communities throughout Northwest Florida.
I support modernizing flood risk assessments, improving mitigation efforts, and encouraging investments that reduce long-term flood losses while keeping flood insurance available for communities that need it.
Prepared communities experience lower recovery costs after disasters.
- Housing costs continue to outpace wages across much of Florida with no protections to those who have built the Florida up that everyone is so eager to buy-and that’s not right or fair.
I support:
- Increasing responsible housing supply.
- Encouraging workforce housing.
- Supporting infrastructure that allows communities to grow sustainably.
- Reducing unnecessary regulatory barriers that increase construction costs.
- Promoting mixed-income developments where appropriate.
Housing policy should ensure that teachers, nurses, first responders, military families, and young professionals can afford to live in the communities they serve.
- Small businesses create jobs, strengthen communities, and keep local economies resilient, but getting a foothold in an oversaturated market of any sort makes Americans feel like they can’t pursue their own happiness in a way that benefits them, and maybe even ultimately their community.
I support:
- Reducing unnecessary regulations.
- Expanding access to capital.
- Simplifying permitting where appropriate.
- Encouraging entrepreneurship.
- Supporting local businesses through workforce development and infrastructure investments.
Strong local businesses create stronger communities.
- America should build more of what it consumes.
We currently produce entertainment as our biggest export-we can do better.
I support:
- Expanding advanced manufacturing.
- Strengthening domestic supply chains.
- Investing in skilled trades.
- Encouraging innovation.
- Reducing dependence on foreign manufacturing for critical industries.
A stronger manufacturing base improves both economic and national security.
- Affordable, reliable electricity is essential for every household, business, school, and hospital.
Families shouldn't have to keep worrying about flickering lights or repeated outages every time even a minor storm moves through our community while continuing to pay higher utility bills.
I believe utilities should be held accountable not only for the rates they charge, but also for the reliability and resilience of the infrastructure those rates are supposed to maintain.
I support:
- Holding utilities accountable for delivering measurable improvements when rate increases are approved for infrastructure upgrades.
- Increasing transparency so customers can clearly see how infrastructure investments are being spent and what improvements have been delivered.
- Prioritizing investments that strengthen grid reliability and reduce the frequency and duration of outages.
- Supporting grid modernization technologies that improve reliability, shorten restoration times, and better withstand hurricanes and severe weather.
- Encouraging innovation and competition where appropriate to improve service and lower long-term costs.
- Promoting an all-of-the-above energy strategy that values affordability, reliability, resilience, and American energy independence.
Infrastructure investments should produce real results—not just higher monthly bills.
Utility Accountability When utilities request higher rates to modernize infrastructure, customers deserve to know:
- What improvements are being made.
- When those improvements will be completed.
- Whether those investments are actually improving reliability.
- How success is being measured.
If families are paying more each month to strengthen the electric grid, they should see fewer outages, faster restoration after storms, and a more resilient system—not continued service interruptions with little explanation.
I support improving transparency and performance reporting so customers can better understand how their money is being invested and whether those investments are delivering on their promises.
Long-Term Energy Vision America's energy future should be reliable, affordable, and resilient.
Rather than relying on a single solution, I support investing in technologies that strengthen our electric grid, encourage domestic energy production, modernize aging infrastructure, and improve disaster preparedness.
A resilient grid protects our economy, strengthens national security, supports military installations, and gives families confidence that essential services will remain available when they're needed most.
- Social Security is a promise that generations of Americans have paid into throughout their working lives.
Those benefits should be protected for current retirees while strengthening the program for future generations.
Simply delaying difficult decisions will only make the problem harder to solve.
We should pursue responsible reforms that improve the long-term financial health of Social Security without breaking the promises made to those who have earned their benefits.
I support:
- Protecting benefits for current retirees and those nearing retirement.
- Improving the long-term financial sustainability of the Social Security Trust Fund.
- Exploring prudent strategies to strengthen the Trust Fund's long-term investment performance while maintaining the safety and security of taxpayer contributions.
- Reducing waste, fraud, and administrative inefficiencies within the program.
- Improving customer service and reducing wait times for beneficiaries.
- Making the Social Security system easier to navigate for workers planning for retirement.
Americans deserve confidence that Social Security will remain available not only today, but for their children and grandchildren as well.
Strengthening the Trust Fund The Social Security Trust Fund should be managed with both security and long-term sustainability in mind.
I support examining ways to responsibly improve long-term returns on Trust Fund assets while maintaining strong safeguards to protect the retirement security of American workers.
Any reforms should prioritize preserving principal, protecting beneficiaries, and ensuring that the program keeps pace with inflation and future demographic changes.
Every reform should answer one question: Will this make Social Security stronger for the next generation without putting today's retirees at unnecessary risk?
- Access to healthcare should not depend on whether someone has private insurance or receives Medicaid.
Too many patients struggle to find physicians who accept Medicaid, while providers often face administrative burdens that make participation difficult.
The result is longer wait times, fewer choices, and unequal access to care.
I believe Medicaid should provide quality healthcare—not simply the minimum level of care available.
I support:
- Expanding provider participation by reducing unnecessary administrative burdens and improving the experience for physicians and healthcare professionals.
- Improving reimbursement systems where appropriate to encourage more providers to accept Medicaid patients.
- Simplifying paperwork and authorization requirements for both patients and providers.
- Modernizing Medicaid technology to reduce delays and improve coordination of care.
- Reviewing benefits regularly to ensure they reflect current healthcare needs and medical best practices.
- Improving access to preventive care, behavioral health services, dental care, and specialty care.
Patients enrolled in Medicaid deserve timely access to qualified healthcare professionals—not a healthcare system that feels separate or second-class.
Modernizing Medicaid Healthcare has changed dramatically over the past several decades, but many administrative systems have not.
I support modernizing Medicaid so it works better for everyone involved.
That includes:
- Streamlining enrollment and renewal processes.
- Reducing unnecessary paperwork.
- Improving communication between healthcare providers and state agencies.
- Expanding the use of secure digital tools where they improve patient care.
- Identifying outdated rules that increase costs without improving outcomes.
A better Medicaid system should save time, reduce frustration, and allow doctors to spend more time treating patients instead of navigating bureaucracy.

Community Solutions
Northwest Florida is home to one of the nation's strongest military communities, and I believe supporting our military means understanding every stage of military life—from enlistment through retirement.
While I have not served in the Armed Forces myself, military service has always been part of my family's story.
My grandfather served in the Army, some of the older members of my family are retired from the Army / Navy, and now my younger cousins are beginning their own journeys in our nation's military.
Growing up with those different perspectives has shown me that military life isn't one experience—it's many.
The challenges facing an 18-year-old recruit are different from those of a military spouse raising children through multiple deployments, a service member transitioning into civilian life, a veteran navigating the VA system, or a retiree continuing to serve their community after decades in uniform.
That's why my approach isn't to assume I have all the answers.
Just as I've done throughout this campaign, I want to listen first.
I want to hear directly from active-duty service members, reservists, National Guard members, military spouses, veterans, retirees, caregivers, and defense employers to better understand where government is succeeding, where it's falling short, and what practical improvements we can make together.
- I want to help:
- Improve access to quality healthcare and mental health resources.
- Support reliable housing and infrastructure around military installations.
- Expand affordable childcare options.
- Reduce unnecessary administrative burdens on military families.
- Ensure service members have opportunities to continue their education and earn professional certifications while serving.
- I want to help:
- Increase support for military spouses facing frequent relocations.
- Improve employment opportunities through license reciprocity and remote work initiatives.
- Strengthen educational continuity for children transferring between school districts.
- Expand childcare and family support services.
- Recognize families as essential partners in military readiness.
- I want to help:
- Expand apprenticeship and workforce transition programs before separation.
- Translate military experience into civilian licenses and certifications.
- Connect separating service members with local employers.
- Increase financial literacy and entrepreneurship resources.
- Improve awareness of available federal, state, and local benefits.
- I want to help:
- Improve access to VA healthcare and mental health services.
- Reduce delays in benefits processing.
- Expand employment and retraining opportunities.
- Increase veteran entrepreneurship and small business support.
- Strengthen community partnerships that reduce isolation and improve quality of life.
- I want to help:
- Support military installations as long-term economic and national security assets.
- Invest in transportation, housing, utilities, broadband, and infrastructure surrounding bases.
- Encourage partnerships between military installations, schools, colleges, universities, and local industries.
- Protect Florida's leadership in defense, aerospace, cybersecurity, aviation, and advanced manufacturing.
My Commitment
Military readiness doesn't begin on the first day of service, and it doesn't end at retirement.
By investing in service members, their families, veterans, retirees, and the communities that support them, we strengthen both our national security and the long-term prosperity of Northwest Florida.
Military & Veteran Readiness
Government should be a conduit for opportunity—
not a barrier to it.
I believe government's role is to create the conditions for people and communities to succeed, provide essential services responsibly, protect individual rights, and then empower citizens, businesses, nonprofits, and local communities to thrive.
Government should remove unnecessary obstacles, simplify processes, and help people accomplish their goals—not create additional layers of bureaucracy that slow progress or discourage innovation.
Every American deserves to understand how decisions are made, how taxpayer dollars are spent, and how to participate in the process.
Transparency builds trust, accountability strengthens institutions, and fiscal responsibility ensures government remains a responsible steward of public resources.
My goal is to make government more open, understandable, efficient, and responsive while allowing people the freedom to solve problems, build businesses, strengthen communities, and pursue opportunities without unnecessary interference.
Guiding Principle
Government should empower people, and move out of their way.
When government is transparent, accountable, fiscally responsible, and focused on removing unnecessary barriers, individuals, families, businesses, and communities are better positioned to solve problems, create opportunities, and build a stronger future together.
Priorities
- Make government easier to understand through plain-language communication.
- Increase transparency in public meetings, legislation, budgets, and agency decisions.
- Strengthen oversight to reduce waste, fraud, abuse, and unnecessary spending.
- Improve fiscal responsibility through long-term planning and measurable outcomes.
- Simplify permitting, licensing, and government processes whenever possible.
- Modernize government technology to improve efficiency, cybersecurity, and public access.
- Eliminate outdated or duplicative regulations that no longer serve the public interest.
- Encourage innovation while maintaining appropriate safeguards for public health and safety.
- Increase collaboration between federal, state, tribal, and local governments to reduce duplication.
- Expand opportunities for public participation and community feedback before major decisions are made.
- Uphold strong ethics standards, transparency, and accountability for elected officials and public servants.
- Regularly review government programs to ensure they continue delivering value to taxpayers.
