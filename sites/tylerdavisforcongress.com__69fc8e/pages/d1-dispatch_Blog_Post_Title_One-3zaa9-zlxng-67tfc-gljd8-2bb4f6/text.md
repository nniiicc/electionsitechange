Whether you support me, another candidate, or are still undecided, I believe voters deserve access to clear information about the conversations shaping our district.
Below is my recap of the Pensacola Beach Chamber and Pensacola Beach Advocates Candidate Forum, organized by topic to help readers compare each candidate's responses.
Introduction On August 3, 2026, the Pensacola Beach Chamber and Pensacola Beach Advocates hosted a Congressional Candidate Forum at Beach Church on Pensacola Beach.
The forum brought together candidates for Florida's First Congressional District to discuss a range of topics affecting Pensacola Beach and Northwest Florida, including infrastructure, transportation, environmental stewardship, economic priorities, federal spending, and the role Congress can play in supporting local communities.
As one of the participating candidates, I had the opportunity to share my own perspectives while also listening to the ideas presented by my fellow candidates: Douglas Chico (Republican), John Frankman (Republican), and Gay Valimont (Democrat).
Forums like these provide an important opportunity for voters to hear where candidates share common ground, where their approaches differ, and how each would address the challenges facing our region.
Rather than simply sharing my own remarks, I believe it's equally important to help voters understand the full conversation.
This recap is intended to summarize the discussion by topic, highlighting the perspectives shared by each candidate in a clear, organized, and factual manner.
Where appropriate, additional context is provided to explain why each issue matters to Pensacola Beach and Northwest Florida, helping connect the discussion to the everyday experiences of residents, business owners, military families, and visitors alike.
No single forum can fully capture a candidate's platform or every nuance of a complex issue.
Responses were necessarily brief, and many topics deserve deeper discussion than time allowed.
My hope is that this recap serves as a useful starting point for voters who want to better understand the priorities discussed during the forum and encourages everyone to continue researching the candidates, asking questions, and participating in the democratic process.
An informed community is a stronger community.
Whether you've already decided who you'll support or are still weighing your options, I appreciate you taking the time to stay engaged in the conversations that will help shape the future of Northwest Florida.
Candidate Introductions Section 2: Candidate Introductions The forum began with each congressional candidate introducing themselves and sharing the experiences and priorities they believe best prepare them to represent Florida's First Congressional District.
While each candidate came from a different professional and political background, several common themes emerged, including public service, economic opportunity, environmental stewardship, government accountability, and improving the quality of life for Northwest Florida residents.
Although introductory remarks are necessarily brief, they often provide insight into the values and experiences that shape a candidate's approach to public policy.
Below is a summary of each candidate's introduction.
Tyler Leeana Davis (No Party Affiliation) Tyler Leeana Davis introduced herself as a sixth-generation Pensacolian, finance professional, nonprofit founder, mother, and community volunteer.
She emphasized her belief that effective leadership begins with listening to the people being represented and understanding the unique needs of individual communities before proposing solutions.
Davis highlighted her work through A Pensacola Perspective , a nonprofit organization focused on civic engagement, literacy, environmental stewardship, volunteerism, and building partnerships throughout Northwest Florida.
She described these experiences as opportunities to work alongside residents, local businesses, schools, and community organizations to better understand the challenges facing different parts of the district.
Rather than focusing on a particular political ideology, Davis centered her introduction on accessibility, collaboration, and community engagement.
She stated that representation should involve listening first, bringing people together, and pursuing practical solutions that reflect local priorities.
Gay Valimont (Democrat) Gay Valimont introduced herself as a local resident, community advocate, and three-time candidate for Florida's First Congressional District.
She emphasized her continued commitment to serving the region through public advocacy and highlighted several policy areas that have remained central to her campaign.
Her introduction focused on affordability, healthcare access, veterans' services, and protecting Northwest Florida's beaches and natural resources.
Throughout her opening remarks, she presented herself as someone committed to representing local families and addressing issues affecting everyday quality of life.
Valimont's introduction reflected a campaign centered on expanding access to essential services while protecting the environmental and economic resources that support the region.
John Frankman (Republican) John Frankman introduced himself by emphasizing service, fiscal responsibility, and constitutional principles.
He focused on representing Northwest Florida's values while advocating for veterans, reducing the federal deficit, and increasing accountability within the federal government.
Frankman also discussed protecting individual liberties and expressed concerns about government overreach.
During his introduction, he noted the importance of having a representative who is closely connected to the district and understands the communities they serve.
Overall, his opening remarks reflected a platform centered on limited government, fiscal discipline, veterans' issues, and constitutional rights.
Douglas Chico (Republican) Douglas Chico introduced himself as a sixth-generation Northwest Floridian and graduate of the United States Naval Academy.
He emphasized his military background and commitment to public service while outlining several priorities that would guide his approach in Congress.
His introduction focused on protecting constitutional rights, securing the nation's borders, supporting conservative policy priorities, and promoting economic opportunity for future generations.
Chico also discussed defending individual freedoms and ensuring government remains accountable to the people it serves.
His opening remarks reflected a campaign focused on national security, constitutional protections, economic growth, and long-term planning for Northwest Florida.
Initial Themes Although each candidate approached the race from a different perspective, several common themes emerged during the opening introductions.
All four candidates emphasized a desire to improve the lives of Northwest Florida residents, though they proposed different paths toward achieving that goal.
Community service, economic opportunity, responsible government, and protecting the region's future each appeared in various forms throughout the introductions and continued to shape the candidates' responses during the remainder of the forum.
The discussion that followed provided an opportunity for each candidate to expand on these themes while addressing specific questions related to Pensacola Beach, Northwest Florida, and the role of the federal government in supporting local communities.
Final Reflections Candidate forums serve an important role in our democratic process.
While campaign websites, advertisements, and social media often highlight individual priorities, forums provide voters with the opportunity to hear candidates respond to the same questions, discuss shared challenges, and explain how they would approach the responsibilities of public office.
Throughout the Pensacola Beach Chamber and Pensacola Beach Advocates Candidate Forum, several areas of common ground emerged.
All four candidates recognized the importance of maintaining and improving infrastructure, supporting Northwest Florida's economy, protecting the Gulf Coast, and ensuring that residents have access to opportunities that strengthen their communities.
Although there was broad agreement on many of the challenges facing the region, candidates often proposed different approaches to addressing them, particularly regarding the role of the federal government, environmental policy, fiscal priorities, and the methods used to secure funding for local projects.
For Pensacola Beach residents, many of the discussion topics—including transportation, parking, environmental stewardship, tourism, hurricane resilience, and responsible development—will continue to shape conversations well beyond this election cycle.
Decisions made in Washington frequently influence the resources available to local governments, businesses, military installations, and community organizations throughout Northwest Florida.
Understanding how candidates approach these issues helps voters make informed decisions about who they believe will best represent their communities.
No single forum can fully define a candidate or capture every aspect of a campaign platform.
Time constraints naturally limit the depth of each response, and many important issues—including insurance affordability, beach renourishment, flood resilience, workforce development, veterans' services, military readiness, housing affordability, healthcare access, and economic diversification—deserve continued discussion throughout the campaign.
Voters are encouraged to attend future forums, review each candidate's platform, ask questions directly, and seek information from multiple sources before casting their ballot.
One of the goals of District 1 Dispatch is to help make government and public policy more accessible by sharing information in a clear, organized, and easy-to-understand format.
Whether highlighting local events, explaining policy issues, or recapping community conversations like this forum, my hope is that these updates help residents stay informed and engaged with the issues affecting Northwest Florida.
Thank you to the Pensacola Beach Chamber, Pensacola Beach Advocates, Beach Church, the moderators, volunteers, and everyone who attended the forum for providing an opportunity to discuss the future of Florida's First Congressional District.
Civic engagement is strongest when people come together to listen, ask thoughtful questions, exchange ideas respectfully, and remain involved in the decisions that shape our communities.
As we move closer to Election Day, I encourage every eligible voter to register if they have not already done so, learn about the candidates seeking to represent them, participate in community discussions, and make their voice heard at the ballot box.
Regardless of political affiliation or viewpoint, an informed and engaged electorate is one of the strongest foundations of a healthy democracy.
About District 1 Dispatch District 1 Dispatch is my bi-weekly community update, created to keep residents of Florida's First Congressional District informed about local events, public policy discussions, community initiatives, and issues affecting Escambia, Santa Rosa, Okaloosa, and Walton counties.
My goal is to provide timely, factual information that encourages civic engagement, community collaboration, and informed participation in the democratic process.
Thank you for reading, and I look forward to continuing the conversation with communities across Northwest Florida.
