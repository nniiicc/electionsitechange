POLICY & PRIORITIES
Eliminate Corruption and Money in Politics
Americans overwhelmingly support reducing the role of money in politics, but real change requires strong public pressure and legislative action.
At the State level, we can pass ethics and lobbying reforms, establish term limits, end gerrymandering, and other measures that put the power back in the hands of the people and hold elected officials and dark money donors accountable.
Economic Empowerment
Economic empowerment strengthens our communities, creates opportunities, and ensures financial stability for working families.
The GOP has destroyed Ohio's economy, which is now ranked #43 in employment and #36 in growth.
In addition, working families are crippled by the cost of childcare.
By strengthening Unions, raising our minimum wage, investing in vocational training, capping childcare costs, and protecting small businesses, Ohio's economy can thrive once again.
End Wealth Inequality
The richest 1% of Ohioans earn nearly as much as the bottom half of the population combined.
These disparities are glaring: CEO-to-worker pay ratios have skyrocketed, with executives earning over 350 times the average worker’s salary.
We can address this in Ohio by fixing our tax code and closing the loopholes that allow the wealthy to pay less, and expanding the tax credits that support young families and first-time home buyers.
We can increase taxes on "Super Owners".
All of this will strengthen Ohio's ability to support the programs that lift more families and individuals out of poverty for good.
Fix the Housing Crisis
Rent has doubled while wages stayed flat — and families need a chance to catch their breath.
Every Ohioan — in cities, rural areas and suburbs — deserves a shot at affordable housing.
Ohio underbuilt housing for over a decade, especially affordable units.
After the 2008 housing crash, construction slowed dramatically and never fully recovered.
Since 2020, institutional investors have bought thousands of Ohio rental properties, especially single-family homes in suburbs.
These firms often hike rents aggressively and charge junk fees.
Local landlords are now “competing” with Wall Street pricing models.
Ohio has the tools and the money to fix this crisis.
What we need now is elected officials willing to act.
Through reforms like statewide affordability initiatives, eviction reforms, and incentives for true affordable housing builds, while putting caps and accountability measures for large scale landlords and out-of-state property owners, we can add more units, ensure Ohio's homes are being purchased by Ohioans.
By expanding programs that help Veterans and other groups such as seniors and first-time homebuyers, we can be a state where families can stay and put down roots.
