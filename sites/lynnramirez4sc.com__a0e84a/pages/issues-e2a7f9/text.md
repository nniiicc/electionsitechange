I support a moratorium on the building of any new data centers in the state until we understand the risks and costs involved and can put adequate guidelines and guardrails in place to protect people’s health, their pocketbooks, their livelihood and our valuable resources here in SC.
I don’t support the huge tax abatements (FILOTs, MCIPs) currently being given for these projects that take money directly out of the communities where they are placed.

Flock Cameras, Signal Trace, and other Information Collection Systems
There’s no denying that these systems have been instrumental in catching criminals and locating missing persons.
However, they’ve also been instrumental in giving many unscrupulous actors the ability to use them illegally or unethically at the very least; stalking ex-lovers, scouting young children in parks and playgrounds, and other illegal and unconstitutional purposes.
We’re not talking about simple license plate readers.
Once integrated with other systems, we’re talking about systems capable of collecting electronic and other data:
- Unique vehicle markings, make, model, year
- Access to your car’s electrical system
- Facial recognition (even if you are a pedestrian)
- Mobile phone identification
- Browser history
- Visits to religious centers, political events
- Financial information
- Where you go for your morning coffee
- Who you talk to, and more
Some of this information being collected (even if it is being analytically synthesized afterward,) without personal consent is highly invasive.
How it will be used and why it is all “necessary” needs to be justified, and so far, it has not.
Trading personal freedoms for security should never be taken lightly and certainly never be controlled by and determined by a company with no legislative oversight and transparency.
Child Care
Costs are through the roof.
Average child care costs for an infant in Greenville County SC at a local commercial or chain type Child Care Center is between $300-$340 per week.
This is the equivalent of over $8/hr --that’s more than the minimum wage earned by a service worker – AND that’s just one child!
The Child Care Scholarship Program, sometimes known as the ABC voucher, HAD been an incredible program allowing more parents to access and utilize more affordable Child Care and re-enter the work force, school, or get job training.
It ended in December of last year.
It’s a program that pays for itself in several ways: Increased child care demand creates more jobs and helps open up underserved areas for child care facilities.
It also keeps rates lower for non-recipients of the scholarship by increasing enrollment.
When parents can afford to rejoin the work force because of affordable child care, they are often beneficiaries of employee sponsored health care plans.
Access to health care lowers the costs a community pays through missed productivity, contagious outbreaks, and unpaid bills.
Let’s re-new this program as soon as possible.
