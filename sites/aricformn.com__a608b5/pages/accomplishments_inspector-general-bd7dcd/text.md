I was a co-author of the bipartisan bill, which passed the Senate in May 2025 and cleared the House 127–5 a year later.
The Senate’s final vote was 66–0.
In July 2026 I was appointed to the commission that will recommend candidates for the state’s first Inspector General.
Passing the law was the first half of the job.
The rest is unglamorous: I changed how the Departments of Agriculture and Veterans Affairs process grants, closing gaps that let money go where it wasn’t supposed to, and we put $75 million toward replacing the outdated county systems that make errors and fraud easier to miss.
Most of preventing fraud is designing programs that are harder to cheat.
