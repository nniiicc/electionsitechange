Who is Aric Putnam?
Aric Putnam is a DFL state senator who has represented Minnesota Senate District 14, centered on St.
Cloud, since January 2021.
He chairs the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Veterans, Broadband, and Rural Development and serves on the commission that will recommend Minnesota’s first Inspector General.
Outside the Legislature, he is a professor of communication at the College of Saint Benedict and Saint John’s University, where he has taught since 2003.
He lives in St.
Cloud with his wife, Laurie.

Public money should be spent honestly, and anyone who steals from state programs should be held accountable.
Aric supports stronger penalties for fraud, including making sure anyone convicted of it can never receive state money again.
Aric co-authored the bipartisan law that created Minnesota’s first independent Office of Inspector General , which has subpoena power and can freeze public funds under a court order.
Aric now serves on the commission that will recommend the state’s first Inspector General by January 1, 2027.
More on his work on fighting fraud Is Aric Putnam responsible for fraud in state programs?
No.
Aric’s work on fraud has been about preventing it and making it easier to catch.
Beyond co-authoring the Inspector General law, Aric helped modernize the technology counties use to detect fraud.
As chair of the committee that oversees the Departments of Agriculture and Veterans Affairs, Aric also changed how those departments handle grants.
Where does Aric Putnam stand on the cost of living?
Aric Putnam authored the law that ended state taxes on Social Security benefits for more than 80% of Minnesota recipients, and he helped craft the largest tax cut in state history: $3 billion in savings for middle- and lower-income Minnesota families and seniors in 2023.
Aric also passed the Minnesota Child Tax Credit, that saves families up to $1,750 per kid, and he championed universal school meals, which saves the average Minnesota family $2000 per year.
Aric also supported bills to stabilize our health care costs, lower prescription drug prices, and expand medical assistance for new moms.
Aric also pressed Governor Walz to ease costs for farmers who have been hit hard by the record-breaking rise in the cost of diesel fuel caused by President Donald Trump’s war in Iran.
The war has pushed diesel prices in Minnesota up nearly 80 cents from last month.
Aric’s plan to allow farmers to use lower-taxed red dye diesel on public highways would allow farmers to save 32 cents per gallon.

Where does Aric Putnam stand on public schools?
Every child should have access to a quality education, no matter where they live or how much money their family has.
Aric has been a teacher for more than 20 years.
In the Senate, he supported more than $2 billion in school funding increases to lower class sizes and add counselors.
He passed new incentives to address Minnesota’s teacher shortage and strengthened the READ Act to improve literacy instruction in schools and improve reading scores for our students.
As Vice Chair of the Higher Education Committee, he also passed the North Star Promise, which provides in-state college tuition to more than 53,000 Minnesotans.
More on his education record Where does Aric Putnam stand on health care?
No one should have to choose between basic necessities and taking care of their health, and people in greater Minnesota deserve good care close to home.
Aric championed the creation of the University of Minnesota Medical School’s St.
Cloud campus, which opened in 2025 to train doctors for greater Minnesota.
He also passed laws to lower prescription drug costs and provide more medical assistance for moms, plus $24 million for rural emergency medical services and $300 million in direct aid to nursing homes.

Aric was chief author of Minnesota’s bipartisan 2025 veterans and military affairs budget .
It fully funded three new state veterans homes across the state.
In 2026, he helped pass the first base pay increase in nearly 30 years for National Guard members on state active duty, and eliminated the annual cap on the Beginning Farmer Tax Credit.
Closer to home, he has helped secure state funding for the Herb Brooks National Hockey Center, the Great River Children’s Museum, and the St.
Cloud Regional Airport.

Yes, regularly.
He is one of the most bipartisan Senators in the legislature.
