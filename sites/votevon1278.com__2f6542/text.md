VON-T TAYLOR GEORGIA STATE HOUSE 127 CHANNING “Americans Make America Great” My name is Channing Von Taylor, better known as TDO (The Discriminated Officer) or Von-T “The Hope.” I am an 18-year retired U.S.
Army captain, American rapper, DJ, singer, songwriter, actor, and animal rights activist.
I attended school at every level in the state of Georgia and graduated from Columbus State University with a degree in Criminal Justice.
Like many of you, I am simply a man who wants to live comfortably and in peace.
However, I became a survivor of racial discrimination in our own community.
As the cost of living has increased, I lost most of my life’s possessions, all of my animals, and eventually the home I purchased in Columbia County in 2016.
Like many people, I was left broken and forced to “figure it out.” Those experiences are why I include others facing similar struggles in my fight today.
I have also faced overwhelming odds while pursuing my musical dreams.
I believe our current government culture has normalized bullying and too often set aside the needs of everyday people.
Together, we can send a clear message that unlawful behavior threatening our progress and our children’s future will no longer be tolerated.
I stand with the people to address affordability, support economic growth, and most importantly, protect our children.
Together, we can bring hope and balance back to Columbia County and set an example for others by showing what true American values can accomplish.
I Made the Pledge This impacts all of our ability to afford homes, pay bills, and build financial security amid statewide economic pressures that have made it a defining issue in Georgia 2026.Housing, groceries, utilities, healthcare, property taxes, and cost of living is the top of my list.
I want to push bills that tackle tax relief, housing supply increases, tenant protections, and cost-of-living measures, as I know this affects your everyday life.
Our community has faced widespread housing affordability issues, with rising rents, home prices, property taxes, and insurance premiums making homeownership almost impossible to achieve.
I want to propose to phase out or reduce property taxes on homes, incentivize denser/more affordable housing, and expand low-income housing credits and even in single-family areas.
We are working families, suburban homeowners, and decent clean people who just want to live and raise our kids in a safe environment.
We are the perfect mix of urban, suburban, and rural residents, healthcare workers, everyday contractors with the touch of our military soldiers and veterans which some stay to better our community.
Many households deal with moderate incomes not enough to live comfortably anymore, forcing us to work in unfavorable conditions or worse...turn to a life of crime just to "get by".
We can make this right!
Some of us spend >30% of income on housing and with the never-ending APR amount that can go up and rarely comes back down.
If you fall short or foreclose then you default, when that happens your credit score is affected which in turn stops you from even being able to bounce back if you tried your hardest.
Our county faces barriers to new housing development, contributing to supply shortages and higher costs.
Affordability Protecting our children from online and physical harms directly impact on our ability to raise healthy, secure kids in a rapidly changing world.
It is critically important to prioritize this matter yet again.
Our schools have families connected to public schools, military communities, and healthcare sectors like Augusta University that directly feed America with top healthcare workers.
I want to ensure our displaced children have a place to call home in the city while they find their way back into the community cycle.
This includes bills on behavioral/mental health support in schools, depression/suicide training for personnel, firearm safety, and broader protections against predators that have normalized getting away with all kinds of abuse towards our children.
Data collection centers for AI are also a major threat.
We can do more for the Department of Family and Children (DFCS) to enhance fostering care reforms, addressing child welfare agency deficits, and expanding mental health concerns.
This administration has made it clear that our children's safety is on the backburner, and it is my goal to bring them back center stage.

In doing this we enhance family prosperity and align with the affordability problem we face.
Which can tackle things like tax reliefs, housing, and business-friendly policies to bring Hope and Balance back to Georgia.
This keeps us thriving even amid national and global shifts.
