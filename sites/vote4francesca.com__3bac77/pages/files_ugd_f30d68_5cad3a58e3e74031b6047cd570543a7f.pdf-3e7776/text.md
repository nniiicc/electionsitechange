A bill to be entitled An act relating to housing affordability and property insurance; creating the Florida Housing and Insurance Emergency Relief Act; declaring a statewide housing emergency; providing for an emergency rent freeze; providing for anti-speculation measures; providing for a statewide investigation of the property insurance market; providing duties of state agencies; providing enforcement; providing an effective date.
Be It Enacted by the Legislature of the State of Florida: Section 1.
Short Title This act may be cited as the “Florida Housing and Insurance Emergency Relief Act.” Section 2.
Legislative Findings and Intent The Legislature finds that: (1) Florida is experiencing a severe housing affordability crisis driven by rapidly increasing rent, property insurance costs, property taxes, and speculative property ownership.
(2) Excessive housing costs threaten the health, safety, and welfare of residents and contribute to displacement and homelessness.
(3) Rising insurance premiums and lack of transparency in the property insurance market significantly contribute to increased housing costs.
(4) Speculative ownership, including non-primary residences, bulk ownership, and vacant properties, reduces housing availability for residents.
(5) Immediate and comprehensive action is necessary to stabilize housing costs and protect residents.
(6) It is the intent of the Legislature to provide immediate relief to renters, ensure accountability in the insurance market, discourage harmful speculation, and address the root causes of housing cost increases.
Section 3.
Declaration of Statewide Housing Emergency (1) A statewide housing affordability emergency is hereby declared.
(2) The Legislature determines that this emergency requires immediate statewide action.
Section 4.
Emergency Rent Freeze (1) Effective Immediately.
This section shall take effect immediately upon the Governor’s approval of this act.
(2) Rent Freeze Requirement.
Beginning on the effective date of this act, a landlord, as defined in s.
83.43, Florida Statutes, may not increase rent for any residential tenancy within the state.
(3) Applicability.
This section applies to all residential rental units in the state of Florida.
(4) Duration.
The rent freeze shall remain in effect for a period of eighteen (18) months unless repealed or extended by the Legislature.
Section 4A.
Tenant Right to Challenge Unlawful Rent Without Prepayment (Phase One Reform of Pay-to-Play) (1) Legislative Intent.
The Legislature finds that conditioning a tenant’s ability to defend against eviction on the prepayment of disputed rent creates an unfair barrier to justice.
(2) Limitation on Court Registry Requirements.
Notwithstanding sections 83.56 and 83.60, Florida Statutes, a tenant shall not be required to pay or deposit any rent into the court registry as a condition of asserting a defense under this act prior to an initial hearing.
(3) Initial Hearing Requirement.
The court shall determine the lawful rent amount at an initial hearing before any payment requirement may be imposed.
(4) Preservation of Defenses.
A court may not issue a default judgment, waive defenses, or grant eviction solely based on a tenant’s failure to pay rent prior to such initial hearing.
(5) Protection from Retaliation.
A landlord may not initiate eviction, impose penalties, or take retaliatory action solely because a tenant challenges a rent increase under this act.
(6) Applicability.
This section applies to all residential tenancies in the state of Florida.
Section 4B.
Post-Freeze Rent Increase Oversight for Bulk Owners.
PostFreeze Rent Increase Oversight for Bulk Owners (1) Applicability.
This section applies to owners classified within the Bulk Ownership Tier under Section 5(3)(c) of this act.
(2) Rent Increase Threshold.
Following expiration of the Emergency Rent Freeze in Section 4, any rent increase exceeding a percentage established by rule shall require documented justification.
(3) Required Justification.
A landlord subject to this section must demonstrate that any qualifying rent increase is reasonably related to documented increases in operating costs, including but not limited to: (a) Property taxes; (b) Property insurance premiums; (c) Necessary repairs and maintenance.
(4) Review and Audit.
Local governments, including counties and municipalities, are authorized to review and audit rent increases by bulk owners to ensure compliance with this section. (a) Local governments may establish procedures for reviewing rent increases and receiving tenant complaints. (b) Local governments may coordinate with state agencies as necessary to enforce compliance.
(5) Enforcement.
Any rent increase in violation of this section shall be deemed an unlawful practice and subject to remedies provided under Section 9.
(6) No Retroactive Rent Recovery.
A landlord may not impose or collect retroactive rent increases, fees, or charges for any period during which a rent freeze or regulated increase was in effect under this act.
Section 5.
Anti-Speculation and Housing Fairness Measures (1) Intent.
It is the intent of this section to discourage large-scale speculative ownership practices that reduce housing availability for Florida residents, while not burdening small property owners or individuals with limited additional property ownership.
(2) Non-Primary Residence Tax Adjustment.
The Legislature shall provide for increased ad valorem tax treatment or surcharge mechanisms for residential properties that are not the owner’s primary homestead, with exemptions or reduced impact for individuals owning a limited number of additional properties.
(3) Tiered Ownership and Bulk Ownership Threshold. (a) Small Ownership Protection.
Individuals owning one (1) or two (2) additional residential properties beyond their primary homestead shall not be subject to bulk ownership penalties under this section. (b) Moderate Ownership Tier.
Ownership of three (3) to five (5) residential properties shall not be considered bulk ownership.
Such owners may be subject to standard reporting requirements as established by rule. (c) Bulk Ownership Tier.
Ownership of more than five (5) residential properties shall be considered bulk ownership and shall be subject to additional taxes, fees, and regulatory requirements under this section. (d) Enhanced Oversight for Large and Non-Local Ownership.
Corporate entities, institutional investors, and out-of-state ownership structures within the bulk ownership tier shall be subject to enhanced regulatory requirements, including but not limited to: 1.
Additional reporting and disclosure obligations; 2.
Increased tax or fee structures as authorized by law; 3.
Monitoring of ownership concentration and market impact. (e) Legislative Intent.
The Legislature intends to ensure that large-scale and non-local ownership does not disproportionately impact housing affordability for Florida residents while protecting small property owners.
(4) Vacant Property Penalties. (a) The state shall establish criteria to identify residential properties that remain vacant for extended periods of time. (b) Penalties, fees, or tax adjustments shall apply to such properties to encourage occupancy and availability in the housing market.
(5) Small Owner Protection. (a) Individuals owning one or two additional residential properties shall not be subject to bulk ownership penalties under this section. (b) The Legislature intends to protect small landlords and homeowners while targeting large-scale speculative practices.
(6) Rulemaking Authority.
Appropriate state agencies shall adopt rules necessary to implement this section.
Section 6.
Property Insurance Accountability and Independent Investigation (1) Establishment of Independent Investigation.
An independent third-party entity shall be designated or contracted to conduct a comprehensive investigation into the property insurance market within the state.
(2) Selection by Independent Expert Panel.
The independent investigating entity shall be selected by an independent panel composed of individuals with relevant expertise and no financial ties to the insurance industry, including: (a) One representative with expertise in forensic accounting; (b) One licensed actuary with experience in insurance rate-setting; (c) One consumer protection or public interest representative; (d) One member with legal or regulatory experience in insurance law.
(3) Appointment Process.
Members of the panel shall be appointed through a combination of legislative leadership and recognized independent professional organizations to ensure balanced representation and independence.
(4) (4) Conflict of Interest.
No panel member or selected entity may have any financial interest in, employment relationship with, or contractual relationship with any entity within the banking, insurance, real estate, homeowners’ association (HOA), or condominium association industries.
For purposes of this section, “financial or professional interest” includes, but is not limited to: (a) Ownership of stocks, bonds, or other equity interests in any banking, insurance, real estate, HOA, or condominium association-related entity or affiliated organization; (b) Receipt of campaign contributions, donations, or other financial support from such entities or their affiliates within the previous five (5) years; (c) Direct or indirect investments, including through partnerships, trusts, or investment funds where the individual has knowledge of such holdings; (d) Any current or recent (within five (5) years) employment, consulting, advisory, or board membership role with any such entity; (e) Any financial or professional relationship that could reasonably create a conflict of interest.
Any individual or entity with such interests shall be disqualified from participation in the investigation.
The panel shall select the investigating entity based on qualifications, independence, and demonstrated experience in forensic financial analysis and insurance markets.
(2) Independence Requirement.
The investigating entity must be independent and shall not have any financial or operational ties to insurance companies operating in the state or entities subject to investigation.
(3) Scope of Investigation.
The investigation shall include, but is not limited to: (a) Insurance rate increases and the justification for such increases; (b) Claims handling practices, including denials and dispute outcomes; (c) Financial performance of insurers operating within the state; (d) Relationships and financial transactions between insurers and affiliated entities; (e) Any practices that may contribute to increased costs for policyholders.
(4) Data Submission and Disclosure Requirements.
Insurers shall provide all data requested by the independent investigating entity necessary to complete the investigation.
This shall include full disclosure of all financial relationships, transactions, and fee arrangements with affiliated entities, including but not limited to managing general agents, administrative service providers, and related companies.
Such disclosure shall include detailed reporting of fees, transfers, and financial flows between insurers and affiliated entities to ensure transparency and prevent profit shifting or unjustified cost allocation.
(5) Public Reporting.
The independent entity shall prepare a report of findings and recommendations, which shall be submitted to the President of the Senate and the Speaker of the House of Representatives and made publicly available.
(6) Transparency.
All findings, methodologies, and supporting data used in the investigation shall be disclosed to the public to ensure full transparency.
Section 7.
Insurance Consumer Protection Review (1) Monitoring and Reporting.
The independent investigating entity, selected by the expert panel and appointed by the state, shall monitor insurance companies for compliance with this act, identify violations, and report any such violations to the appropriate enforcement authority.
(2) Mandatory Consumer Restitution.
If the independent investigation determines that insurance companies engaged in unjustified rate increases, excessive fees, or improper financial practices that resulted in higher costs to consumers, the state shall require restitution to affected policyholders.
(3) Timeline for Findings.
The independent investigation shall be completed and submitted within 180 days of appointment of the investigating entity.
(4) Mandatory State Action.
Within 60 days of receiving the investigation report, the appropriate enforcement authority shall issue determinations identifying insurers subject to restitution and rate correction.
(5) Rate Reduction Requirement.
Insurers found to have imposed unjustified rate increases shall reduce rates to levels consistent with documented risk and actual losses, effective within 30 days of determination.
(6) Refund and Compensation Requirement.
Insurers shall provide restitution to policyholders within 120 days of determination through one or more of the following: (a) Direct cash refunds; (b) Premium credits applied to future payments; (c) Other approved financial compensation mechanisms.
(7) Notice to Policyholders.
Insurers subject to restitution under this section shall provide written notice to all affected current and former policyholders within 30 days of determination.
The notice shall include: (a) A clear explanation of the reason for the refund or rate adjustment; (b) The amount of restitution owed to the policyholder; (c) The method of compensation, including whether it will be issued as a direct payment or credit; (d) The expected timeline for payment or credit issuance; (e) Contact information for questions and dispute resolution.
Notice shall be delivered by mail and, where available, electronically.
(8) Inclusion of Former Policyholders.
Restitution under this section shall apply to both current and former policyholders who were subject to unjustified rate increases or charges during the applicable review period.
Insurers shall make reasonable efforts to identify and notify former policyholders, including through last known contact information.
If a former policyholder cannot be located, the insurer shall maintain the owed funds for a period of not less than three (3) years, during which the individual may claim restitution.
(9) Public Notification Requirement.
Insurers subject to restitution under this section shall conduct a public notification campaign to inform current and former policyholders of their eligibility for refunds or compensation.
The campaign shall include: (a) Advertisements in widely circulated media, including television, radio, and digital platforms; (b) Notices on the insurer’s website and customer portals; (c) Targeted outreach using available contact information for affected individuals.
Such advertisements shall run for a period of not less than 90 days and clearly explain: 1.
Who is eligible for compensation; 2.
How to claim restitution; 3.
Deadlines and contact information.
(10) Insurer-Operated Claims Portal Requirement.
Each insurer subject to restitution under this section shall establish and maintain a dedicated online claims portal for affected policyholders.
The portal shall: (a) Allow individuals to verify eligibility for restitution; (b) Allow submission of claims by current and former policyholders; (c) Provide clear information regarding refund amounts, status, and timelines; (d) Be accessible, user-friendly, and available for a period of not less than three (3) years; (e) Be supported by customer service assistance for individuals who require help accessing or using the system.
The portal may not include unnecessary barriers, delays, or burdensome documentation requirements that would discourage or prevent valid claims.
(11) Public Disclosure.
All findings, affected companies, required refunds, and rate adjustments shall be made publicly available to ensure transparency and accountability.
(12) Penalties for Non-Compliance.
Any insurer that fails to comply with notification, restitution, rate adjustment, advertising, or portal requirements shall be subject to: (a) Fines of up to $10,000 per day per violation; (b) Additional penalties based on the number of affected policyholders; (c) Suspension or revocation of authority to operate in the state.
(13) Misleading or Inadequate Advertising.
Insurers may not provide misleading information, obscure eligibility requirements, or discourage claims.
All advertising materials shall be clear, accurate, and subject to review under applicable law.
Section 8.
Housing Supply and Affordability Measures (1) The Department of Economic Opportunity shall coordinate with local governments to identify and reduce barriers to housing development.
(2) The state shall promote and expand programs that incentivize the development of affordable and workforce housing.
Section 9.
Enforcement (1) Enforcement Authority.
The Department of Legal Affairs and other appropriate state agencies shall enforce all provisions of this act, including the authority to investigate violations, compel production of records, issue orders, and impose penalties.
(2) Application of Enforcement Provisions.
All enforcement mechanisms established under Sections 6 and 7 of this act, including but not limited to document disclosure requirements, limitations on claims of privilege, in camera review, restitution, rate adjustments, monitoring, and penalties, shall apply to any violation of this act.
(3) Civil Penalties.
Any person or entity that violates this act shall be subject to: (a) Fines of up to $10,000 per day per violation; (b) Additional penalties based on the number of affected individuals; (c) Enhanced penalties for repeated or willful violations.
(4) Restitution and Court Authority.
Courts may order full restitution, damages, and interest for any violation resulting in financial harm and may enforce compliance with all provisions of this act.
(5) License Suspension or Revocation.
The state may suspend or revoke the authority of any insurer or entity to operate within the state for serious or repeated violations.
(6) Fraud and Misrepresentation.
Any person or entity that knowingly provides false, misleading, or incomplete information shall be subject to additional penalties and referral for criminal investigation where applicable.
(7) Private Right of Action.
Any affected individual may bring a civil action to enforce this act and may recover: (a) Actual damages; (b) Injunctive relief; (c) Court costs and reasonable attorney fees.
(8) Whistleblower Protection.
Individuals who report violations of this act shall be protected from retaliation.
Any retaliation shall constitute a separate violation subject to additional penalties.
Section 10.
Effective Date This act shall take effect upon becoming a law.
