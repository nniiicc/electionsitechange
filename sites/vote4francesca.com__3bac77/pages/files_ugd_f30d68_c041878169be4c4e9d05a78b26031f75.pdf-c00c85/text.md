Florida Housing and Insurance Emergency Relief Act Developed by Francesca Yabraian, Candidate for Florida House District 1 Lower Costs.
Protect Families.
Fix the System.
A bill to be entitled An act relating to housing affordability, housing stability, property insurance accountability, and protection of affordable housing revenues; creating the Florida Housing and Insurance Emergency Relief Act; declaring a statewide housing affordability emergency; providing for temporary emergency rent stabilization; providing tenant court-access protections; repealing and replacing mandatory court registry prepayment requirements and automatic waiver/default remedies in residential eviction actions; providing tenant stability and fairness protections; providing anti-speculation and housing fairness measures; providing residential real estate ownership transparency; protecting, restoring, and allocating affordable housing trust fund revenues; amending and conforming provisions relating to the William E.
Sadowski Affordable Housing Act, the State Housing Trust Fund, the Local Government Housing Trust Fund, the State Apartment Incentive Loan Program, and documentary stamp tax housing revenues; establishing the Civil Housing Defense Fund as a dedicated housing-stability program and account; providing eligibility standards; establishing an attorney compensation rate; providing for funding, administration, reporting, and enforcement; requiring an independent investigation of the property insurance market; requiring rate correction, restitution, public notice, and consumer claims procedures; providing enforcement authority, penalties, private rights of action, whistleblower protections, rulemaking authority, severability, and effective dates.
Section 1.
Short Title (1) This act may be cited as the "Florida Housing and Insurance Emergency Relief Act." Section 2.
Legislative Findings and Intent (1) The Legislature finds that Florida is experiencing a severe housing affordability crisis driven by rapidly increasing rent, property insurance costs, property taxes, limited affordable housing supply, and speculative property ownership.
(2) Excessive housing costs threaten the health, safety, welfare, and economic security of residents and contribute to displacement, eviction, foreclosure, homelessness, workforce instability, and community disruption.
(3) Rising property insurance premiums, disputed claims practices, and lack of transparency in the property insurance market significantly contribute to increased housing costs for renters, homeowners, condominium owners, mobile-home owners, and small landlords.
(4) Speculative ownership, bulk ownership, prolonged vacancy, concealment of beneficial ownership, and non-local ownership structures can reduce the availability of housing for Florida residents and can distort local housing markets.
(5) Affordable housing revenues, including documentary stamp tax revenues historically dedicated to housing under the William E.
Sadowski Affordable Housing Act, are essential housing-stability revenues and should be used for housing purposes every year.
(6) Access to legal representation is a housing preservation tool.
Preventing unlawful eviction, foreclosure, insurance-related loss of housing, unlawful liens, and unlawful rent increases is less costly and less harmful than addressing displacement after it occurs.
1 (7) Immediate and comprehensive statewide action is necessary to stabilize housing costs, protect residents, preserve primary residences, ensure accountability in the property insurance market, protect affordable housing trust funds, and address the root causes of housing cost increases.
(8) It is the intent of the Legislature to provide immediate relief to renters and homeowners, protect small property owners acting in good faith, discourage harmful speculation, prevent displacement, provide meaningful legal defense in housing-loss proceedings, and ensure that housing dollars are used for housing.
Section 3.
Definitions (1) "Administering agency" means the state agency, public corporation, office, department, or other governmental entity assigned by law, appropriation, rule, or interagency agreement to administer a program, duty, fund, account, investigation, or enforcement function under this act.
(2) "Affordable housing funds" means money deposited in, allocated to, or legally dedicated to the State Housing Trust Fund, the Local Government Housing Trust Fund, local housing assistance trust funds, the State Apartment Incentive Loan Program, the State Housing Initiatives Partnership Program, or any other fund, account, or program established for affordable housing, workforce housing, housing preservation, housing stabilization, foreclosure prevention, eviction prevention, or displacement prevention.
(3) "Bulk owner" means a person, ownership entity, affiliated group, beneficial owner, or related group of entities that owns, controls, manages, or has a beneficial interest in more than five (5) residential properties within this state, whether directly or indirectly.
(4) "Large rental owner" means a landlord, ownership entity, affiliated group, beneficial owner, or related group of entities that owns, controls, manages, or has a beneficial interest in more than fifty (50) residential rental units within this state, whether directly or indirectly.
(5) "Primary residence" means the dwelling that a person occupies as the person's principal home, including a homestead, condominium unit, cooperative unit, mobile home, manufactured home, or other lawful dwelling used as the person's principal place of residence.
(6) "Residential property" means real property or a dwelling unit used, offered, or intended for residential occupancy, including single-family homes, duplexes, multifamily units, condominium units, cooperative units, mobile homes, manufactured homes, and residential rental units.
(7) "Residential rental unit" means a dwelling unit rented, leased, offered for rent, or otherwise made available for residential occupancy by a tenant.
(8) "Unlawful rent increase" means a rent increase, fee, charge, assessment, or other housing cost imposed in violation of this act, a rule adopted under this act, a lease, or any other applicable law.
(9) "Ownership" includes direct ownership, indirect ownership, beneficial ownership, control, management control, ownership through affiliated entities, and ownership through corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, trusts, subsidiaries, parent companies, nominees, holding companies, or any other legal or business arrangement.
Section 4.
Declaration of Statewide Housing Affordability Emergency (1) A statewide housing affordability emergency is declared.
(2) The Legislature determines that the emergency affects residents in every region of the state and requires immediate statewide action.
(3) The Legislature further determines that the protections in this act are necessary to protect the health, safety, welfare, housing stability, and economic security of Florida residents.
2 Section 5.
Emergency Rent Stabilization (1) Effective immediately upon this act becoming law and for a period of eighteen (18) months, a large rental owner may not increase rent for any residential tenancy in this state.
(2) Effective immediately upon this act becoming law and for the same eighteen (18) month period, a landlord or ownership entity that owns or controls fifty (50) or fewer residential rental units within this state may increase rent only if the increase does not exceed one and one-half percent (1.5%) annually.
(3) A landlord may not evade this section by imposing or increasing fees, service charges, utility passthroughs, penalties, mandatory services, month-to-month premiums, lease renewal charges, amenity fees, administrative fees, or any other charge that has the purpose or effect of offsetting a prohibited rent increase.
(4) A landlord may not terminate, refuse to renew, alter material lease terms, reduce housing services, threaten eviction, or take retaliatory action for the purpose of avoiding this section.
(5) The rent stabilization period shall expire eighteen (18) months after the effective date of this act unless extended, modified, or repealed by the Legislature.
(6) This section is a general law of statewide application enacted by the Legislature.
Local governments may receive complaints, conduct reviews, and assist enforcement of this act, but this section does not authorize a local government to adopt rent controls except as expressly authorized by general law.
(7) Any rent increase, fee, charge, lease term, notice, or collection practice imposed in violation of this section is void and unenforceable and is subject to remedies under Section 16.
Section 6.
Repeal and Replacement of Court Registry Prepayment Requirement in Residential Eviction Actions (1) Legislative Intent.
The Legislature finds that conditioning a tenant's ability to answer an eviction, raise a defense, challenge an unlawful rent increase, contest the amount of rent claimed, or seek court review on the prepayment of rent into the court registry creates an unfair barrier to justice and can result in loss of housing without meaningful review of the merits.
(2) Repeal and Replacement.
For residential tenancies under part II of chapter 83, Florida Statutes, the mandatory court registry prepayment requirement and automatic waiver/default remedy in section 83.60(2), Florida Statutes, are repealed and replaced by this section.
(3) Right to Be Heard Without Prepayment.
A tenant may file an answer, assert any legal or equitable defense, raise a counterclaim, file a motion to determine rent, seek dismissal, challenge an unlawful rent increase, contest fees or charges, or assert a claim under this act without first paying or depositing rent into the court registry.
(4) No Automatic Waiver, Default, or Writ of Possession.
A tenant's failure to pay or deposit rent into the court registry within five (5) days after service of process may not constitute an absolute waiver of defenses, may not bar the tenant from raising legal or equitable defenses, and may not by itself entitle the landlord to an immediate default judgment, final judgment for removal, or writ of possession without notice and an opportunity to be heard.
(5) Hearing Required Before Payment Order.
A court may not require a tenant to deposit rent into the court registry, pay rent as a condition of defending the action, or enter a payment order unless the court first provides notice and an opportunity to be heard and makes written findings regarding the lawful rent amount, the undisputed amount if any, payments already made, alleged unlawful rent increases, alleged unlawful fees or charges, habitability defenses, retaliation defenses, counterclaims, hardship, ability to pay, and any other relevant defense or claim.
(6) Limits on Payment Orders.
Any payment order must be limited to the undisputed lawful rent amount determined by the court.
A payment order may not include unlawful fees, prohibited rent increases, disputed 3 charges, amounts subject to a good-faith defense or counterclaim, or amounts that may be reduced or abated due to landlord noncompliance unless the court determines after hearing that the amount is lawful and owed.
(7) Payment Plans and Housing Stability.
If payment is appropriate after hearing, the court may establish a reasonable payment schedule based on the tenant's income, documented hardship, necessary expenses, housing stability, and ability to pay.
The court may consider available rental assistance, Civil Housing Defense Fund support, mediation, settlement, or other housing-stability remedies before ordering removal.
(8) Noncompliance with Payment Order.
If a tenant fails to comply with a payment order issued after hearing, the court may consider appropriate remedies only after notice and an opportunity to cure or be heard.
Noncompliance may not automatically waive defenses or require immediate removal without judicial review.
(9) Landlord Rights Preserved.
Nothing in this section eliminates a landlord's right to pursue lawful rent, possession, damages, or other remedies after notice, hearing, and final adjudication consistent with this act and chapter 83, Florida Statutes.
(10) Conforming Changes.
Sections 83.56 and 83.60, Florida Statutes, and any statutory summons, court form, rule, or procedure inconsistent with this section are amended and shall be construed to conform to this section.
Any summons, notice, or court form may not state that a tenant's defenses are absolutely waived solely because the tenant did not deposit rent before a hearing.
(11) Retaliation Prohibited.
A landlord may not initiate eviction, impose penalties, reduce services, refuse renewal, increase fees, or take retaliatory action because a tenant challenges a rent increase, fee, charge, eviction filing, or court registry demand under this act.
Section 7.
Tenant Stability and Fairness Protections (1) Legislative Intent.
The Legislature finds that housing stability is essential to the health, safety, and economic security of Florida residents and that eviction filings, regardless of outcome, can create long-term barriers to housing.
(2) Right to Request a Hardship Payment Plan.
A tenant facing eviction for nonpayment of rent has the right to request a reasonable hardship payment plan based on current income, documented hardship, necessary expenses, and financial circumstances.
(3) Standard for Payment Plans.
A payment plan under this section must be based on documented income and ability to pay; must be reasonably affordable; must allow repayment over time without creating further hardship; and may allow continued payment of current lawful rent plus partial repayment of arrears where applicable.
(4) Good Faith Compliance Protections.
If a tenant enters into and complies with a payment plan in good faith, eviction proceedings shall be paused, stayed, dismissed, or otherwise resolved as appropriate, and no additional fees, penalties, or charges may be imposed for the purpose of undermining the agreement.
(5) Court Authority.
If the landlord and tenant cannot agree on a payment plan, the court may establish a reasonable and enforceable payment plan based on income, necessary expenses, the amount owed, the tenant's housing stability, and the purposes of this act.
(6) Automatic Sealing of Certain Eviction Records.
Eviction records shall be sealed by the court, without the need for a separate motion by the tenant, when the case is dismissed, the tenant prevails, the parties enter a court-approved resolution, or the tenant complies with a payment plan, settlement, or resolution agreement.
(7) Effect of Sealing.
Once sealed, an eviction record may not be publicly accessible; may not be disclosed to landlords, property managers, homeowners' associations, condominium associations, tenant-screening companies, or consumer reporting agencies except as required by law; and may not be used to deny housing or tenancy.
(8) Pending or Resolved Cases.
An eviction filing that is pending, under active resolution, subject to a payment plan, or resolved without final judgment of eviction may not be used as the sole basis to deny housing.
4 (9) Credit Reporting Limitation.
Eviction filings may not be reported to consumer credit reporting agencies unless a final judgment of eviction is entered and the tenant fails to satisfy court-ordered obligations after notice and opportunity to comply.
(10) Enforcement.
A violation of this section is subject to civil penalties, damages, injunctive relief, restitution, attorney fees, costs, and any other remedy available under Section 16.
Section 8.
Post-Freeze Rent Increase Oversight for Bulk Owners (1) Applicability.
This section applies to all owners classified in the bulk ownership tier under Section 3(3) and Section 9.
(2) Rent Increase Threshold.
After expiration of the emergency rent stabilization period in Section 5, any rent increase by a bulk owner that exceeds a threshold established by rule must be supported by documented justification.
(3) Required Justification.
A bulk owner subject to this section must demonstrate that a qualifying rent increase is reasonably related to documented increases in operating costs, including property taxes, property insurance premiums, necessary repairs, maintenance, utilities, debt service directly tied to the property, or other legitimate costs established by rule.
(4) Review and Audit.
Counties and municipalities may review and audit qualifying rent increases by bulk owners, receive tenant complaints, request supporting documentation, and coordinate with state agencies to enforce this section.
(5) No Retroactive Rent Recovery.
A landlord may not impose or collect retroactive rent increases, fees, or charges for any period during which a rent freeze, rent stabilization limit, or regulated increase was in effect under this act.
(6) Enforcement.
A rent increase imposed in violation of this section is an unlawful practice and is subject to remedies under Section 16.
Section 9.
Anti-Speculation and Housing Fairness Measures (1) Intent.
It is the intent of this section to discourage large-scale speculative ownership practices that reduce housing availability for Florida residents while protecting small property owners and individuals with limited additional property ownership.
(2) Non-Primary Residence Tax Adjustment.
The Legislature shall provide, as authorized by law, increased ad valorem tax treatment, surcharge mechanisms, vacancy fees, registration requirements, or other lawful measures for residential properties that are not the owner's primary residence, with exemptions or reduced impact for individuals owning a limited number of additional properties.
(3) Ownership Tiers. (a) Small Ownership Tier.
A person or ownership entity that owns one (1) or two (2) residential properties beyond the owner's primary residence is not subject to bulk ownership penalties under this section. (b) Moderate Ownership Tier.
Ownership of three (3) to five (5) residential properties beyond the owner's primary residence is not bulk ownership but may be subject to standard reporting requirements established by rule. (c) Bulk Ownership Tier.
Ownership of more than five (5) residential properties is bulk ownership and is subject to additional reporting, tax, fee, disclosure, and regulatory requirements authorized under this act. (d) Enhanced Oversight for Large and Non-Local Ownership.
Corporate entities, institutional investors, out-of-state ownership structures, and affiliated ownership structures within the bulk ownership tier are subject to enhanced disclosure, reporting, monitoring of ownership concentration, and market-impact review.
5 (4) Vacant Property Measures.
The administering agency shall establish criteria to identify residential properties that remain vacant for extended periods of time.
Penalties, fees, registration requirements, or tax adjustments may apply to such properties to encourage lawful occupancy and availability in the housing market.
(5) Small Owner Protection.
Individuals owning one (1) or two (2) additional residential properties are not subject to bulk ownership penalties.
The Legislature intends to protect small landlords and homeowners while targeting large-scale speculative practices.
(6) Small Owner Tax Relief and Tenant Rent Reduction Program. (a) Purpose.
The Legislature intends to reward Florida residents and Florida-based businesses that provide direct and measurable rent relief to tenants while preventing out-of-state entities, speculative investors, and affiliated ownership structures from improperly receiving tax benefits intended for local property owners. (b) Eligibility.
Property tax relief under this subsection may be available only to a natural person whose primary residence is located in Florida or a business entity that is registered in Florida, headquartered in Florida, and actively operating in Florida. (c) Unit Limit.
A property owner that owns fifty (50) or fewer residential rental units may qualify for tax relief under this subsection if all tenant rent reduction requirements are satisfied. (d) Scaled Relief.
Subject to appropriation and implementation by law, owners of one (1) to ten (10) residential rental units may receive up to a thirty percent (30%) property tax reduction; owners of eleven (11) to twenty-five (25) residential rental units may receive up to a twenty-five percent (25%) property tax reduction; and owners of twenty-six (26) to fifty (50) residential rental units may receive up to a twenty percent (20%) property tax reduction. (e) Mandatory Tenant Rent Reduction.
As a condition of receiving tax relief, each tenant must receive a minimum rent reduction of fifty dollars ($50.00) per month for each residential rental unit.
The reduction must be applied directly to the tenant's monthly rent obligation and must be clearly reflected in the lease, renewal notice, rent ledger, or other written rent statement. (f) No Offsets.
A required rent reduction may not be averaged across units, offset, redistributed, reduced, replaced with credits, substituted with rebates or gift cards, recaptured through new or increased fees, or negated by a rent increase before, during, or after receipt of tax relief. (g) Documentation and Tenant Notice.
A property owner receiving tax relief must annually certify compliance and provide documentation showing the amount of tax relief received, the number of residential rental units owned, rent charged before and after the reduction, the amount delivered to each tenant, and all affiliated ownership interests.
Each affected tenant must receive written notice explaining the monthly rent reduction and the tenant's right to report noncompliance.
(7) Unified Ownership and Anti-Circumvention.
Multiple entities shall be treated as a single property owner when they share common ownership, control, management, beneficial ownership, financial interest, business address, registered agent, officers, directors, members, managers, investors, or operational control.
A person may not restructure ownership, transfer title, create affiliated entities, use nominee owners, or divide holdings to qualify for tax benefits, avoid reporting, evade unit thresholds, or circumvent this act.
(8) Disqualification and Restitution.
A property owner that fails to comply with this section shall forfeit eligibility for property tax relief, repay the full value of any tax benefit received, provide restitution to affected tenants for any rent reduction not properly delivered, and be subject to civil penalties and disqualification for a period established by rule.
Section 10.
Out-of-State and Foreign Ownership Transparency and Restrictions (1) Enhanced Disclosure Requirements.
Any individual or entity purchasing residential property in this state that is not a Florida resident, is organized outside Florida, is organized outside the United States, or uses foreign-sourced funds or a complex ownership structure shall disclose: 6 (a) The true beneficial owner of the purchasing entity; (b) The source of funds used in the transaction; (c) The nature of the purchaser's business activities and primary income source; (d) Whether funds originate from foreign financial institutions or foreign accounts; and (e) Any parent company, subsidiary, holding company, trust, nominee, affiliated entity, or other structure used in the transaction.
(2) Financial Institution Responsibility.
A financial institution facilitating a covered residential real estate transaction shall verify ownership disclosures and the source of funds to the extent required by this act, rules adopted under this act, and other applicable law.
(3) Senior Compliance Certification.
A transaction involving foreign-sourced funds, undisclosed beneficial ownership, or complex ownership structures may require certification by a senior compliance officer of the financial institution or closing agent as established by rule.
(4) Prohibition on Concealment.
A person may not use a shell company, layered ownership structure, affiliated entity, nominee owner, or similar arrangement for the purpose of concealing true ownership, source of funds, or market concentration in residential real estate transactions.
(5) Liability.
A person, entity, financial institution, or executive that knowingly or negligently approves, facilitates, or conceals a transaction involving materially false information, undisclosed beneficial ownership, or illicit funds is subject to civil penalties, restitution, disqualification, referral for criminal investigation where applicable, and any other remedy under Section 16.
Section 11.
Protection, Restoration, and Full Allocation of Affordable Housing Funds (1) Legislative Intent.
Affordable housing funds are essential to the health, stability, and economic security of Florida residents and must be protected and used for housing-related purposes.
Housing policy must prioritize both creation of new housing and preservation of existing housing to prevent displacement and instability.
(2) Amendment of Sadowski Housing Laws.
Upon this act becoming law, sections 201.15, 420.0005, 420.5087, 420.9073, 420.9079, Florida Statutes, and related provisions of chapter 420, Florida Statutes, are amended and shall be construed to conform to this act and to restore, protect, and allocate affordable housing revenues under the William E.
Sadowski Affordable Housing Act.
(3) Full Allocation Requirement.
Notwithstanding any other provision of law, one hundred percent (100%) of revenues dedicated to, allocated to, or historically intended for affordable housing purposes under the William E.
Sadowski Affordable Housing Act, chapter 201, chapter 420, or any related law shall be deposited into the State Housing Trust Fund, the Local Government Housing Trust Fund, and any other affordable housing fund, account, or program authorized by law.
(4) Required Housing Trust Fund Deposits.
Affordable housing documentary stamp tax revenues shall be deposited into and used through the following housing funds and programs: (a) The Local Government Housing Trust Fund, including local housing assistance plans, the State Housing Initiatives Partnership Program, housing preservation, home repair, down-payment assistance, rental assistance, eviction prevention, foreclosure prevention, and housing stability programs; (b) The State Housing Trust Fund, including statewide affordable housing programs, affordable housing preservation, housing development, homeowner assistance, rental housing, housing stability programs, and lawful administrative support; (c) The State Apartment Incentive Loan Program, to the extent funds are appropriated or transferred for SAIL purposes; and (d) The Civil Housing Defense Fund established under Section 12 and any other fund, account, or program established by law solely for affordable housing, housing preservation, or displacement prevention.
7 (5) Permitted Uses of Affordable Housing Funds.
Affordable housing funds may be used only for housingrelated purposes, including: (a) Housing preservation and displacement prevention, including rental assistance, housing stabilization, foreclosure prevention, rehabilitation and preservation of existing housing, legal representation through the Civil Housing Defense Fund, and other programs that directly prevent loss of housing; (b) Affordable housing development and supply expansion, including construction, rehabilitation, adaptive reuse, conversion of vacant or underused properties into housing, local housing initiatives, and removal of barriers to housing development; and (c) Housing cost reduction and stability programs that directly benefit Florida residents, renters, homeowners, mobile-home owners, condominium owners, cooperative owners, and lawful occupants.
(6) Prohibition on Diversion, Sweeps, Borrowing, and Executive Override.
Affordable housing funds may not be transferred, swept, reallocated, divided, loaned, borrowed against, pledged, securitized, used as collateral, redirected, offset, substituted, reclassified, or otherwise used for a non-housing purpose, directly or indirectly.
(7) The prohibition in subsection (6) applies to legislative appropriation, budget proviso language, implementing bill language, administrative action, emergency action, executive order, emergency order, formula change, accounting change, fund transfer, cash-flow borrowing, reclassification, or indirect funding mechanism.
(8) No Governor, Cabinet officer, agency head, budget officer, department, state agency, local government, or other public officer may use emergency authority, executive authority, administrative authority, or budget authority to override this section.
(9) Non-Housing Funds.
Affordable housing revenues may not be redirected to the General Revenue Fund, the State Economic Enhancement and Development Trust Fund, the Resilient Florida Trust Fund, the Water Protection and Sustainability Program Trust Fund, the State Transportation Trust Fund, or any other nonhousing fund or program unless the use is directly and primarily tied to affordable housing development, preservation, housing stability, or an expressly permitted housing purpose under this act.
(10) Restoration Requirement.
Affordable housing revenues previously diverted, swept, borrowed, transferred, reallocated, or used for non-housing purposes shall be identified, reported, and restored to the appropriate housing fund, account, or program.
(11) Public Accounting.
Within ninety (90) days after this act becomes law, the Chief Financial Officer, in consultation with the Department of Revenue, the Florida Housing Finance Corporation, the Department of Commerce, and the Office of Economic and Demographic Research, shall prepare a public accounting of affordable housing revenues diverted, swept, transferred, borrowed, or redirected for non-housing purposes since enactment of the William E.
Sadowski Affordable Housing Act.
(12) The public accounting must identify the fiscal year, amount diverted, original source fund, receiving fund or program, legal authority used, and amount required to fully restore the money to affordable housing purposes.
(13) Repayment and Restoration Schedule.
Within one hundred eighty (180) days after this act becomes law, the Legislature shall adopt a restoration schedule to repay identified diverted funds.
Unless the original source fund can be specifically identified, restored funds shall be deposited seventy percent (70%) into the Local Government Housing Trust Fund and thirty percent (30%) into the State Housing Trust Fund.
(14) Non-Reversion.
Affordable housing funds, including documentary stamp tax revenues, loan repayments, penalties, fees, interest, investment earnings, recovered funds, and restored funds, shall remain available for authorized housing purposes and may not revert to the General Revenue Fund or any non-housing fund at the end of a fiscal year.
(15) Enforcement.
A violation of this section is enforceable under Section 16, including injunctive relief, restoration of funds, civil penalties, attorney fees, costs, and any other remedy available by law.
8 Section 12.
Civil Housing Defense Fund (1) Establishment.
The Civil Housing Defense Fund is established as a dedicated housing-stability program and accounting category within the existing affordable housing trust fund structure.
The fund shall provide legal assistance to eligible persons at risk of losing housing.
(2) Trust Fund Construction.
This act does not create a separate state trust fund unless a separate trust fund bill is enacted in the manner required by the Florida Constitution.
Until a separate trust fund is lawfully created, the Civil Housing Defense Fund shall operate as a dedicated program, account, allocation, or accounting category within existing affordable housing funds, including the State Housing Trust Fund and the Local Government Housing Trust Fund.
(3) Use of Funds.
Funds may be used for attorney fees, court costs, filing fees, mediation costs, document costs, qualified legal representation, and other reasonable and necessary litigation expenses in eviction proceedings, foreclosure actions, insurance-related housing disputes, unlawful rent increase challenges, homeowners' association or condominium association lien actions, property tax hardship matters, unlawful fee or assessment disputes, and other housing-related proceedings that threaten displacement or loss of a primary residence.
(4) Eligibility. (a) For purposes of this section, "eligible person" means a Florida resident, tenant, homeowner, condominium unit owner, cooperative owner, mobile-home owner, manufactured-home owner, or other lawful occupant whose primary residence is located in this state and whose housing is at risk due to eviction, foreclosure, insurance-related housing disputes, unlawful rent increases, unlawful fees or assessments, homeowners' association or condominium association lien actions, property tax hardship, denial, delay, or underpayment of insurance benefits, disaster-related hardship, or any other housingrelated legal proceeding, dispute, or condition that may result in displacement or loss of the person's primary residence. (b) A person is eligible if the person is civilly indigent under section 57.082, Florida Statutes, including when the person's income is equal to or below two hundred percent (200%) of the then-current federal poverty guidelines prescribed for the size of the person's household by the United States Department of Health and Human Services and the person lacks sufficient assets to pay court costs or obtain counsel.
In making this determination, the administering agency shall not count the person's homestead or one motor vehicle with a net value not exceeding five thousand dollars ($5,000), and may consider the presumption concerning two thousand five hundred dollars ($2,500) or more in net equity in nonexempt assets consistent with section 57.082, Florida Statutes. (c) A person is also eligible if the person is unable to pay private attorney fees without substantial hardship to the person or the person's family, or if payment of private legal fees would threaten the person's ability to maintain housing, pay necessary living expenses, or protect the person's primary residence. (d) Evidence of eligibility may include documentation of income, assets, liabilities, housing instability, inability to afford legal representation, receipt of an eviction notice, foreclosure notice, insurance denial or underpayment, unlawful rent increase notice, lien notice, property tax hardship, loss of income, disability, fixed income, disaster-related hardship, medical hardship, receipt of Temporary Assistance for Needy Families-Cash Assistance, poverty-related veterans' benefits, Supplemental Security Income (SSI), other means-tested public benefits, or other circumstances established by rule. (e) The administering agency shall adopt rules for determining eligibility, documentation requirements, emergency approval procedures, and priority categories.
Priority may be given to households with children, seniors, persons with disabilities, veterans, survivors of domestic violence, fixed-income residents, and individuals facing imminent loss of housing.
The agency shall publish and annually update income limits based on two hundred percent (200%) of the federal poverty guidelines without the need for further legislative action.
9 (5) Attorney Compensation Rate.
Attorneys and qualified legal service providers representing eligible persons through the Civil Housing Defense Fund shall be paid by the fund at a rate of one hundred fifty dollars ($150.00) per hour for reasonable and necessary legal services approved under this section.
This amount is the attorney compensation rate paid through the Civil Housing Defense Fund and applies to approved legal services unless changed by statute.
(6) Purpose.
The purpose of the fund is to ensure that no individual loses housing solely because the individual cannot afford legal representation.
(7) Funding Source.
Upon this act becoming law, the Civil Housing Defense Fund shall be funded from affordable housing revenues, including documentary stamp tax revenues dedicated to affordable housing, the State Housing Trust Fund, the Local Government Housing Trust Fund, Sadowski Housing Trust Funds, restored affordable housing funds under Section 11, settlement funds, grant funds, penalty funds, restitution funds, private funds, federal funds where permitted, and any other funds legally available for housing stability or legal assistance.
(8) Continuing Allocation.
Beginning in the fiscal year in which this act becomes law, the administering agency shall reserve and allocate recurring money sufficient to operate the fund, subject to available protected affordable housing revenues.
Allocated money is nonlapsing and may not revert to general revenue.
(9) Payment of Approved Claims.
The administering agency shall establish a prompt payment process for approved attorneys and qualified legal service providers.
Approved services shall be paid at the rate established in subsection (5), together with approved court costs, filing fees, mediation costs, document costs, and other reasonable and necessary litigation expenses directly related to preventing displacement or loss of housing.
(10) Administration.
The Department of Commerce, the Florida Housing Finance Corporation, or another administering agency designated by law shall administer the fund and may partner with legal aid organizations, nonprofit housing advocacy organizations, law school clinics, bar associations, and qualified legal service providers.
(11) Provider Approval and Accountability.
The administering agency shall establish provider approval criteria, billing standards, invoice requirements, time-record documentation, conflict-of-interest protections, case outcome reporting, anti-fraud protections, and audit procedures.
(12) Reporting.
The administering agency shall publish annual reports detailing the number of individuals served, case types, geographic distribution, outcomes including prevented evictions or foreclosures, payments made to attorneys and legal service providers, administrative costs, and total expenditures.
(13) No Executive Override.
No executive order, emergency order, administrative directive, budget action, formula change, cash-flow borrowing mechanism, or reclassification may delay, reduce, redirect, or prevent authorized funding or payment of approved claims under this section.
(14) Enforcement.
Failure to fund, administer, or pay approved claims under this section is enforceable under Section 16.
Section 13.
Property Insurance Accountability and Independent Investigation (1) Establishment.
An independent third-party investigating entity shall be designated or contracted to conduct a comprehensive investigation into the residential property insurance market in this state.
(2) Independent Expert Panel.
The investigating entity shall be selected by an independent expert panel composed of individuals with relevant expertise and no material financial conflict of interest.
The panel must include: (a) A representative with expertise in forensic accounting; (b) A licensed actuary with experience in insurance rate-setting; (c) A consumer protection or public interest representative; 10 (d) A member with legal or regulatory experience in insurance law; and (e) A housing affordability or policy representative with experience connecting insurance costs to housing costs.
(3) Appointment Process.
Members of the panel shall be appointed through a combination of legislative leadership, statewide elected officers, and recognized independent professional organizations as established by rule or implementing law, with the purpose of ensuring balanced representation and independence.
(4) Conflict of Interest.
A panel member or selected investigating entity may not have a material financial interest in, current employment relationship with, or current contractual relationship with an insurer or entity subject to investigation.
A material financial or professional interest includes ownership of non-diversified equity interests, recent employment, consulting, advisory, board membership, substantial campaign or organizational financial support, or any relationship that could reasonably create a conflict of interest within the previous five (5) years.
Diversified mutual funds or retirement funds do not create a conflict unless the individual controls the specific investment decision.
(5) Scope of Investigation.
The investigation shall include: (a) Insurance rate increases and justification for such increases; (b) Claims handling practices, including denials, delays, underpayments, dispute outcomes, and litigation outcomes; (c) Financial performance of insurers operating in the state; (d) Loss ratios, premium revenue, reinsurance costs, claims data, reserves, profits, losses, executive compensation, and administrative expenses; (e) Relationships, fee arrangements, and financial transactions between insurers and affiliated entities, including managing general agents, administrative service providers, parent companies, subsidiaries, reinsurers, and related companies; (f) Use of shell companies, layered ownership structures, affiliated entities, or related-party transactions to shift profits, inflate expenses, conceal financial activity, or misrepresent losses; and (g) Any practices that may contribute to increased costs for policyholders, renters, homeowners, condominium owners, mobile-home owners, and small landlords.
(6) Data Submission and Disclosure.
Insurers and affiliated entities shall provide all data requested by the investigating entity that is necessary to complete the investigation, including full disclosure of financial relationships, transactions, fee arrangements, profit and loss statements, affiliate transfers, related-party transactions, shell companies, subsidiaries, ownership structures, and financial flows.
(7) Required Financial Disclosure for Rate Filings.
An insurer seeking a rate increase must submit complete and accurate financial information, including detailed justification for the increase, full financial statements, profit and loss statements, loss ratios, claims data, all affiliate transactions, transfers of funds between related entities, and consolidated and entity-level financials.
(8) Condition for Rate Increase Approval.
No insurer may implement or receive approval for a rate increase unless the insurer has fully complied with the disclosure requirements of this act and applicable rate filing requirements.
(9) Burden of Proof.
The insurer bears the burden of proving that a requested rate increase is justified, actuarially sound, supported by documented risk and losses, and not based on undisclosed affiliate profit shifting or artificial loss creation.
(10) Confidentiality and Privilege.
Required information may not be withheld solely because it is proprietary, confidential, or commercially sensitive.
Confidential, trade secret, or privileged material shall be produced in a manner that allows the investigating entity and enforcement authorities to conduct a complete review while protecting legally protected information as required by law.
Any privilege claim must be identified in a privilege log and is subject to review by the appropriate authority or court.
11 (11) Public Reporting.
The investigating entity shall prepare a written report of findings and recommendations, submit it to the President of the Senate and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, provide it to the Office of Insurance Regulation, and make it publicly available to the greatest extent permitted by law.
(12) Transparency.
Findings, methodologies, data categories, affected companies, recommended rate corrections, and consumer protection recommendations shall be disclosed to the public.
Supporting data may be aggregated, anonymized, redacted, or protected only to the extent required by law.
(13) Timeline.
The independent panel shall be established within thirty (30) days after this act becomes law.
The investigating entity shall be selected promptly thereafter.
The investigation report shall be completed within one hundred eighty (180) days after appointment of the investigating entity unless extended for good cause by the Legislature.
(14) Enforcement.
Failure to comply with this section shall result in denial of a requested rate increase and may subject the insurer or affiliated entity to penalties, restitution, license action, and other remedies under Sections 14 and 16.
Section 14.
Insurance Consumer Protection Review and Restitution (1) Monitoring and Reporting.
The independent investigating entity shall monitor insurance companies for compliance with this act, identify violations, and report violations to the Office of Insurance Regulation, the Department of Financial Services, the Department of Legal Affairs, and any other appropriate enforcement authority.
(2) Mandatory State Action.
Within sixty (60) days after receiving the investigation report, the appropriate enforcement authority shall issue determinations identifying insurers subject to restitution, rate correction, additional review, or enforcement action.
(3) Mandatory Consumer Restitution.
If the investigation or enforcement authority determines that an insurer engaged in unjustified rate increases, excessive fees, improper financial practices, claim underpayments, improper denials, improper delays, affiliate profit shifting, or other practices that resulted in higher costs or financial harm to consumers, the state shall require restitution to affected policyholders.
(4) Rate Reduction Requirement.
Insurers found to have imposed unjustified rate increases shall reduce rates to levels consistent with documented risk, actual losses, and actuarially justified costs.
Rate reductions shall become effective within thirty (30) days after determination unless a shorter period is required by the enforcement authority.
(5) Refund and Compensation Requirement.
Insurers shall provide restitution to policyholders within one hundred twenty (120) days after determination through direct cash refunds, premium credits, corrected billing, claim adjustment payments, or other approved financial compensation mechanisms.
(6) Notice to Policyholders.
Insurers subject to restitution shall provide written notice to all affected current and former policyholders within thirty (30) days after determination.
The notice must explain the reason for the refund or rate adjustment, the amount owed, the method of compensation, the expected payment timeline, and contact information for questions and dispute resolution.
(7) Former Policyholders.
Restitution applies to current and former policyholders who were subject to unjustified rates, charges, claim practices, or financial practices during the applicable review period.
Insurers shall make reasonable efforts to identify and notify former policyholders using last known contact information and other reasonable means.
(8) Unclaimed Restitution.
If a former policyholder cannot be located, the insurer shall maintain the owed funds for not less than three (3) years or transfer the funds as required by applicable unclaimed property law, during which time the individual may claim restitution.
(9) Public Notification Campaign.
An insurer subject to restitution shall conduct a public notification campaign for not less than ninety (90) days to inform current and former policyholders of eligibility for refunds or compensation.
The campaign must include notices on the insurer's website and customer portals, 12 targeted outreach using available contact information, and widely accessible public notices in media or digital platforms as approved by the enforcement authority.
(10) Claims Portal.
An insurer subject to restitution shall establish and maintain a dedicated online claims portal that allows individuals to verify eligibility, submit claims, obtain clear information about refund amounts and timelines, and receive customer service assistance.
The portal must remain available for not less than three (3) years and may not include unnecessary barriers, delays, or burdensome documentation requirements that discourage valid claims.
(11) Public Disclosure.
Findings, affected companies, required refunds, rate adjustments, restitution totals, compliance status, and enforcement actions shall be made publicly available to ensure transparency and accountability.
(12) Penalties for Noncompliance.
Failure to comply with this section may result in fines of up to twenty-five thousand dollars ($25,000) per day per violation, additional penalties based on the number of affected policyholders, enhanced penalties for willful delay or obstruction, interest on delayed restitution, and suspension or revocation of the insurer's certificate of authority or other license as authorized by law.
(13) Automatic Penalties.
Penalties begin immediately upon failure to meet a required timeline, notification requirement, restitution obligation, rate correction, claims portal requirement, or other compliance standard under this section.
(14) Interest.
Delayed restitution or refunds owed to policyholders shall accrue interest, payable to affected policyholders, at a rate established by rule.
(15) Financial Disclosure Enforcement.
The enforcement provisions of this section apply to failure to disclose required financial information; failure to provide complete and accurate profit and loss statements; failure to disclose affiliated entities, shell companies, or ownership structures; misrepresentation of financial condition or operating losses; and noncompliance with rate filing requirements under Section 13.
Section 15.
Housing Supply and Affordability Measures (1) The Department of Commerce, in coordination with the Florida Housing Finance Corporation and local governments, shall identify and reduce barriers to affordable and workforce housing development, preservation, rehabilitation, and adaptive reuse.
(2) The state shall promote and expand programs that incentivize the development, preservation, and rehabilitation of affordable housing and workforce housing.
(3) State and local housing supply measures must be implemented in a manner that complements, and does not replace, eviction prevention, foreclosure prevention, tenant protections, housing preservation, and Civil Housing Defense Fund activities under this act.
(4) The Department of Commerce and Florida Housing Finance Corporation shall prioritize policies that convert vacant or underused property into housing, preserve naturally occurring affordable housing, reduce displacement, and protect residents from housing cost shocks.
Section 16.
Enforcement (1) Enforcement Authority.
The Department of Legal Affairs, the Office of Insurance Regulation, the Department of Financial Services, the Department of Revenue, the Department of Commerce, the Florida Housing Finance Corporation, local governments where authorized, and other appropriate state agencies may enforce this act within their respective jurisdictions.
(2) Investigative Authority.
An enforcement authority may investigate violations, receive complaints, compel production of records, issue subpoenas where authorized by law, conduct audits, order corrective action, and refer matters for civil, administrative, or criminal enforcement.
13 (3) Civil Penalties.
A person or entity that violates this act may be subject to fines of up to ten thousand dollars ($10,000) per day per violation, except where a higher penalty is provided in Section 14; additional penalties based on the number of affected individuals; enhanced penalties for repeated, knowing, willful, or bad-faith violations; and disqualification from programs or benefits under this act.
(4) Restitution and Court Authority.
Courts and enforcement authorities may order full restitution, damages, interest, disgorgement, restoration of funds, refund of unlawful charges, rent credits, premium credits, injunctive relief, declaratory relief, attorney fees, costs, and any other remedy necessary to enforce this act.
(5) License Suspension or Revocation.
The state may suspend, revoke, condition, or decline to renew the authority of an insurer, financial institution, property management company, landlord, ownership entity, or other regulated entity to operate in this state for serious, repeated, knowing, or willful violations as authorized by law.
(6) Fraud and Misrepresentation.
A person or entity that knowingly provides false, misleading, or incomplete information, conceals ownership, conceals source of funds, misrepresents financial condition, or obstructs an investigation is subject to additional civil penalties and referral for criminal investigation where applicable.
(7) Private Right of Action.
An affected individual may bring a civil action to enforce this act and may recover actual damages, statutory damages where authorized, restitution, injunctive relief, declaratory relief, court costs, reasonable attorney fees, and any other remedy available by law.
(8) Whistleblower Protection.
A person who reports a violation, participates in an investigation, refuses to participate in unlawful conduct, or provides information under this act is protected from retaliation.
Retaliation is a separate violation subject to additional penalties, damages, reinstatement where applicable, and other relief.
(9) Cumulative Remedies.
Remedies under this act are cumulative and do not limit any other remedy available under state law, federal law, contract, common law, equity, local ordinance, or administrative rule.
Section 17.
Rulemaking and Implementation (1) The Department of Commerce, the Department of Revenue, the Department of Legal Affairs, the Office of Insurance Regulation, the Department of Financial Services, the Florida Housing Finance Corporation, the Chief Financial Officer, and any other appropriate agency may adopt rules necessary to implement this act.
(2) Emergency rulemaking is authorized to the extent necessary to provide immediate relief, establish complaint and payment processes, protect affordable housing funds, implement the Civil Housing Defense Fund, begin the insurance investigation, and enforce emergency rent stabilization.
(3) Agencies implementing this act shall coordinate data sharing, complaint intake, reporting, enforcement referrals, and public communication to ensure that residents can access relief without unnecessary delay.
(4) The administering agency for the Civil Housing Defense Fund shall begin rulemaking, fund setup, provider approval, eligibility procedures, billing standards, and payment procedures immediately upon this act becoming law.
Section 18.
Severability (1) If any provision of this act or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the invalidity does not affect other provisions or applications of the act that can be given effect without the invalid provision or application.
(2) The Legislature declares that it would have enacted each section, subsection, paragraph, sentence, clause, phrase, and word of this act regardless of whether any one or more provisions are declared invalid.
