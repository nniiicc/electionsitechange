Home Meet Julie Issues Volunteer Contribute District 15 Map Voter Information Contact Home ❭ Issues ❭ Government Accountability Government Accountability Restoring Public Trust and Accountability in Indiana Hoosiers work hard and pay their taxes expecting government to spend that money wisely and honestly.
Yet Indiana hands out contracts without real accountability, cuts services for the families who need them most, and lets officials investigate themselves when something goes wrong.
This is not a partisan issue.
It is a public-trust issue.
Government is supposed to work for the people it serves, and lately it hasn’t felt like Indiana has been doing that.
Here is where I stand, the facts behind it, and exactly what I will do.
The Cost of Unchecked Power: The dangers of an unchecked supermajority are real, and Hoosier families are living with the consequences.
Here's some of what the public record shows Indiana did with your money — much of it while telling families there wasn't enough to go around: Nearly $1 billion — that’s the size of the massive Medicaid forecasting error the state admitted in late 2023.
This was a glaring example of fiscal mismanagement by the Indiana Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA).
Instead of taking responsibility, state leaders rushed to make short-sighted cuts to vital services, balancing the budget on the backs of our most medically vulnerable Hoosiers.
More than $700 million — improper Medicaid overpayments that a federal whistleblower lawsuit claims state officials were pressured to stop recovering between 2015 and 2020.
This includes payments flagged by the state’s own fraud-detection contractor for services supposedly provided to people who had already died.
The allegations suggest heavy influence from hospitals and insurance companies — the same powerful interests that regularly donate to politicians’ campaigns.
Roughly $80 million —The staggering amount of free, federally funded summer food aid the Governor's administration walked away from, stripping grocery benefits from 669,000 Hoosier children.
Indiana opted out of the program to avoid paying just $3.7 million in administrative costs, leaving roughly $80 million in federal funding on the table that would have helped families put food on the table while supporting Indiana grocery stores, farmers, and local businesses. $238 million — payments and tax credits the state's economic-development agency (IEDC) awarded to 46 of the 107 companies that donated to its affiliated private foundation.
A 2025 forensic audit found no documentation showing anyone checked for conflicts of interest and warned the arrangement created the potential for favoritism and misuse of public funds.
Nearly $300,000 — Taxpayer dollars spent on luxury vehicles for statewide officials and security improvements at the Governor's private family residence while Indiana argued it needed to cut or limit funding for essential programs. $308,000+ — the amount of spot bonuses handed out by the Secretary of State’s office, divided across 80 bonuses to 68 staffers , including a family member and his former campaign manager.
Some employees received bonuses as high as $10,000 to $12,000 , far above the State Personnel Department’s general spot-bonus guidance of $100 to $1,000 None of this is partisan.
It's just what happens when the people spending your money are the same people who get to decide whether they did anything wrong.
Lack of Accountability Three major problems allow this system to continue with very little independent oversight.
Lawmakers Police Themselves: While the state has an ethics commission for most public officials, legislators are exempt.
Instead, they investigate ethics complaints through internal committees made up of other lawmakers.
Simply put, they don't answer to the same independent oversight they require of everyone else.
Secret Economic Deals: Some of the state's largest economic development deals happen with very little transparency.
A 2025 state audit found that Indiana's economic development agency did not consistently use competitive bidding and even used a shell company to quietly purchase hundreds of millions of dollars in land without initially revealing the state was the buyer.
Abuse of NDAs: Indiana and its major economic development entities, like the IEDC, regularly utilize strict non-disclosure agreements and issue substantial contracts with minimal independent review.
Restoring trust means measuring results, ending secret deals, and holding the people in power to the same standard as everyone else.
We must implement structural transparency checks so that state spending is judged by its real, audited return to the public.
My Priorities for Honest Government 1.
Real Accountability for Officials Independent Ethics Oversight: Establish a fully independent, non-partisan state ethics commission with sweeping subpoena and enforcement authority over legislators and statewide officials, ending the self-policing culture in state government.
Truthful Testimony Act: Require state officials to answer questions from the General Assembly directly and truthfully.
Evasive or misleading testimony would carry a personal civil fine of up to $10,000, with three violations triggering an automatic ethics investigation.
Automatic Consequences for Misconduct : When wrongdoing is proven, officials must repay any misused taxpayer dollars and can no longer use public money for their personal legal defense.
Focus on Public-Interest Litigation : Direct the state’s legal resources toward protecting consumers, fighting corporate price-gouging, and safeguarding public assets rather than defending official misconduct.
2.
Deep Transparency in Spending Performance Dashboard: Build a public dashboard rating the costs, deadlines, and real outcomes of state projects, with a strict rule to renew no contract without proof that it delivered.
Restrict Economic Development NDAs: Prohibit sweeping, permanent nondisclosure agreements by state economic-development agencies and require public disclosure of incentive agreements.
Local communities and taxpayers deserve to have full visibility into the environmental, economic, and infrastructure costs of large-scale taxpayer funded deals.
Disclose the True Cost Per Job: Require every economic development deal to publish its full public cost per job created.
This includes tax credits, abatements, grants, infrastructure, utility extensions, land discounts, and workforce subsidies.
The information must be presented in plain language within 90 days of signing.
Contractual Accountability & Clawbacks: Require regular audits of major contracts, add automatic “clawback” clauses when a company misses its job or investment targets, and stop paying contractors who repeatedly earn a red rating.
Recover Waste First: Prioritize going after unrecovered overpayments and holding contractors accountable before cutting services or asking Hoosiers to accept less.
3.
Open and Honest Lawmaking True Single-Subject Rule: Replace the vague “germaneness” test with a strict single-subject rule so unrelated measures can’t be slipped into bills at the last minute.
If a bill can’t pass on its own, it shouldn’t hitchhike through on something that can.
Public Notice: Post every version of a bill, including last minute amendments and conference committee deals, publicly online before anyone votes on it.
Community input first: Require public hearings before major changes to voting laws, district maps, or large state contracts.
4.
Restoring the People's Voice Indiana is one of only 24 states that denies citizens the right to place critical issues directly on the ballot.
Currently, one party holds a commanding supermajority in both chambers of the Indiana General Assembly—occupying 40 out of 50 seats in the Senate and 70 out of 100 seats in the House.
When any political party holds this level of unchecked power, the system breaks down.
It becomes too easy for leadership to ignore the pressing concerns of everyday Hoosiers, including those who voted for them and those who didn’t.
Without real accountability, we see closed-door deals, bills that favor special interests, and important issues getting buried because there’s no meaningful check on the majority.
Hoosiers deserve better than a government that only listens to one side.
To restore true representation and ensure every Hoosier has a say in our state’s future, we must enact several fundamental reforms: Establish an Independent Redistricting Commission: We must end political gerrymandering.
Maps should be drawn by an independent commission using strict, nonpartisan rules.
Commissioners must be barred from viewing party registration, past election data, or incumbents’ home addresses until after the district maps are finalized.
Introduce Citizen Ballot Initiatives: Hoosiers deserve the opportunity to put important issues directly before voters without having to ask permission from politicians in Indianapolis.
By adopting a citizen-led ballot initiative process, Indiana can join about half the states that already give citizens a direct voice when the legislature chooses partisan gridlock over the public interest, or simply lacks the courage to act.
Every proposed measure will include a clear, nonpartisan explanation written in plain language so voters understand exactly what it would do before casting their ballot.
Expand Accessible, Secure Voting: Indiana can protect our elections and still make it easier for eligible people to vote.
Today the state stops accepting registrations 29 days out and gives people no way to sign up on Election Day itself.
A Hoosier who moves that month, starts a new job, or simply misses the date loses the ability to vote even though nothing about their eligibility has changed.
That is a paperwork failure, not a security one.
I will work to let eligible citizens with a valid ID and proof of residency register and cast their ballot the same day, keep the polls open later, and let people vote by mail without giving an excuse.
No one should lose their voice over a work shift, a recent move, or a spot on the calendar.
We can keep the rolls accurate and still make sure every eligible voter is heard.
Indiana doesn't have a revenue problem.
It has an accountability problem.
I believe taxpayers deserve honest answers, open government, and leaders who are held to the same standards as everyone else.
Government should earn the public's trust through transparency, accountability, and results.
We cannot keep asking families to sacrifice while government wastes money, hides decisions from the public, and allows those in power to police themselves.
Hoosiers deserve a government that is transparent, responsible, and accountable for every dollar it spends.
Every time government puts political insiders and special interests ahead of the people it serves, Hoosiers pay the price.
I will fight to open the books, expose waste, recover taxpayer dollars, strengthen ethics laws, and give citizens a greater voice in the decisions that shape our state.
