Public Safety & Crime
Protecting Our Neighborhoods: A Smarter Approach to Public Safety
Every Hoosier deserves to feel safe at home, at work, and in their neighborhood.
Public safety isn't about political slogans.
It's about making sure families can walk through their neighborhoods, park their cars, open their businesses, and raise their children without worrying that crime will simply be ignored.
Law enforcement officers work hard every day, but they cannot do the job alone.
They need modern technology, better coordination between agencies, laws that keep pace with organized crime, and enough resources to focus on solving crimes instead of getting buried in paperwork.
However, public safety means more than making arrests.
Many repeat offenders struggle with addiction, mental illness, or other issues that keep them cycling through the justice system.
Holding people accountable and helping them break that cycle are not competing ideas.
Both make our communities safer.
Government's first responsibility is protecting the public.
That means supporting law enforcement, standing with victims, using taxpayer dollars wisely, and making sure our justice system reduces crime instead of simply reacting to it.
Practical Solutions
-
Hold Repeat Offenders Accountable:
Update Indiana law so prosecutors can treat connected thefts committed across multiple counties or over time as part of a single criminal pattern.
Organized criminals shouldn't be able to avoid meaningful consequences simply by spreading crimes across jurisdictional lines.
- Give Law Enforcement Better Tools: Expand secure online reporting for non-emergency property crimes, modernize digital evidence collection, and improve statewide information sharing so investigators can connect related cases more quickly and spend more time preventing crime instead of completing paperwork.
- Support Local Police: Help departments recruit and retain officers, strengthen investigative capacity, and provide the technology and training officers need to solve more crimes while building trust within the communities they serve.
Fort Wayne's challenge isn't simply technology.
Solving more cases also requires enough investigators and patrol officers to do the work.
- Expand Treatment Courts That Work: Support drug courts, mental health courts, veterans courts, and other evidence-based diversion programs that reduce repeat offenses while requiring participants to meet strict accountability standards.
These programs are demanding, but they help reduce future crime and lower long-term costs for taxpayers.
- Focus on Crime Prevention: Strengthen access to addiction treatment, mental healthcare, and reentry programs that reduce repeat offenses while ensuring violent and habitual offenders are held fully accountable.
Smart public safety means preventing tomorrow's victims as well as responding to today's crimes.
- Protect Crime Victims: Make it easier for victims to report crimes, preserve evidence, and navigate the justice system while improving coordination between local agencies so cases don't fall through the cracks.
Public safety isn't about choosing between accountability and compassion.
Strong communities require both.
We should support the officers who protect our neighborhoods, hold repeat offenders accountable, give people struggling with addiction or mental illness a path to turn their lives around, and make sure victims know the justice system is working for them.
When families feel safe in their homes, businesses can thrive, neighborhoods grow stronger, and everyone has a better opportunity to succeed.
That's the kind of Indiana I'll fight to build.
