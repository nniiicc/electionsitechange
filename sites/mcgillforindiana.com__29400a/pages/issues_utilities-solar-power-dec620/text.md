Utilities & Solar Power
Keeping Utility Bills Affordable and Powering Indiana's Future
A strong economy starts with infrastructure that's reliable, affordable, and ready for the future.
Fiscal responsibility isn't just about cutting costs.
It's about making smart choices that actually deliver results for the Hoosiers paying the bills.
Right now, families across Indiana are seeing their electric bills go up faster than their paychecks while Indiana's energy system falls behind.
Rural communities could benefit from better access to reliable power and high-speed internet to attract new jobs and businesses.
These problems hold us back from creating good jobs and keeping our communities growing.
Proposed Solutions
- Restore Fair Net Metering and Expand Solar Access
Homeowners, farmers, and small businesses who invest their own money in solar should receive fair-market credit for the excess electricity they send back to the grid.
Indiana once had a successful rooftop solar program, but changes to state law dramatically reduced those credits, making it much harder for solar to pay for itself.
I will support restoring fair compensation while protecting Indiana's existing sales tax and property tax exemptions for solar equipment.
- Authorize Independent Community Solar
Renters, homeowners with shaded roofs, and others who cannot install their own panels should be able to subscribe to local community solar projects and receive credits on their bills.
Current law effectively limits community solar to utility-owned projects that are not being built at scale.
I will push to enable independent community solar with strong consumer protections so benefits reach working families.
- Protect Ratepayers from Utility Lobbying Costs
Hoosier families are already struggling with higher electric bills.
We shouldn’t make them subsidize utility companies’ lobbying and political activities on top of that.
I support banning utilities from recovering lobbying, advertising, and political expenses in customer rates.
- Make Large Users Pay Their Fair Share
New developments and subdivisions should cover the infrastructure costs they create instead of shifting higher bills onto existing ratepayers.
A single hyperscale data center can demand as much power as 80,000 homes.
- Put a Temporary Moratorium on New Large-scale Data Centers until we have stronger protections in place.
These projects use enormous amounts of electricity and water, put heavy strain on our power grid and water systems, and can impact local farmland and the environment.
We need to make sure they pay 100% of their costs instead of passing higher bills and long-term burdens on to families.
We should pause new approvals until the rules are tightened.
- Support Value-Added Agriculture and Rural Infrastructure
Indiana leads in growing crops, but we export too many raw goods and lose the processing jobs, payroll, and tax revenue that stay behind.
Modern food-processing facilities need reliable power and broadband.
By addressing grid and connectivity bottlenecks, we can attract advanced manufacturing, create high-wage jobs, and keep more economic value in Indiana.
By modernizing Indiana's energy, water, and broadband infrastructure and ensuring those who create new demand help pay their fair share, we can lower long-term costs, create good-paying jobs, strengthen our communities, and build an economy that works for Hoosier families.
