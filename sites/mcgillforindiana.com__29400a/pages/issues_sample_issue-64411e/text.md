Affordability
An Economy Built for Hoosier Families
Everything seems to cost more than it should.
The cost of groceries, housing, healthcare, childcare, utilities, insurance, and everyday necessities keeps climbing while paychecks struggle to keep up.
Some of those increases are unavoidable.
Many aren't.
Often families end up paying more because of policies that reduce competition, shift costs onto taxpayers, or allow hidden fees and unfair pricing to go unchecked.
Affordability isn't about one product or one industry.
It's about making sure the rules work for working families instead of against them.
1.
Look at the whole picture
A government hasn't saved taxpayers money if it simply moves the bill somewhere else.
When affordable childcare disappears, parents leave the workforce, businesses struggle to hire, and the economy loses billions in productivity.
When healthcare coverage is cut, people still get sick, but instead of receiving preventive care, they end up in emergency rooms where hospitals, insured families, and taxpayers ultimately absorb the cost.
When a family loses more in benefits than they gain from a raise, government ends up penalizing self sufficiency instead of rewarding it.
The same principle applies to every major decision.
Before cutting a program or rejecting available funding, Indiana should consider the total long term cost, not just this year's budget.
For example, many Medicaid services are matched by the federal government.
Traditional Medicaid is funded at roughly one third state dollars and two thirds federal dollars, while the Healthy Indiana Plan is funded at a 90 percent federal match.
Walking away from those dollars doesn't make the need disappear.
It simply means Hoosiers continue paying federal taxes while other states receive the benefit.
Real fiscal responsibility isn't about shifting costs from one column to another.
It's about making decisions that lower the total cost for taxpayers, families, businesses, and local communities.
2.
Stop shifting costs onto families
When a big corporation stops paying its share, that cost doesn't vanish.
It lands on you.
Affordability means closing the side doors that quietly push those costs onto working families, because most of this never shows up where you'd think to look for it.
Corporate property tax breaks shift the bill onto homeowners.
Monopoly utilities charge customers for their own lobbying and for projects they only promise to build later.
Landlords stack nonrefundable fees on top of refundable deposits and spring surprise administrative charges on renters at signing.
None of this makes big headlines, but families feel it every month.
We need to close these side doors and stop quietly shifting costs onto the people who can least afford them.
3.
Keep Indiana money in Indiana
Indiana tax dollars should strengthen Indiana's economy.
When taxpayers invest in roads, incentives, or public contracts, that investment should create lasting economic value here at home.
Hoosiers deserve a clear return through good paying jobs, higher wages, and the local tax revenue that helps pay for our schools, roads, police, fire departments, and other essential services instead of seeing those public dollars leave the state.
4.
Protect Hoosiers from unfair and algorithmic pricing
You should pay a fair price for the product itself, not a higher one because a company's software decided you'd put up with it.
More retailers are rolling out electronic shelf tags, artificial intelligence, and automated systems that can change a price in real time.
They already use personal data like your browsing history and location to charge different customers different prices online.
It's only a matter of time before that jumps from the website to the grocery aisle.
This kind of invisible surveillance pricing tilts every transaction in favor of the seller.
Combined with hidden fees and surge pricing, these tools make it harder for families to know whether they're getting a fair deal at all.
Competition can't work when the price itself is hidden.
Hoosiers deserve transparent pricing where the price is clearly displayed, reflects real market conditions, and isn't manipulated by secret algorithms that determine exactly how much a company can extract from your wallet.
5.
Stop emergency price gouging and promote fair pricing
A real crisis is not a license to gouge consumers.
Families understand that storms, supply shortages, and other emergencies can temporarily raise prices.
What they shouldn't have to accept is those temporary spikes quietly becoming permanent without a good reason.
Hoosiers deserve stronger protections against price gouging and more transparency so prices reflect actual market conditions, instead of staying artificially high long after the crisis has passed.
What I’ll Fight For
None of this gets fixed with a single bill.
Here’s where I’ll focus at the Statehouse, and what I’ll push for on your behalf.
- Ban surveillance pricing that uses your personal data or behavior to charge you more than someone else for the same product.
- Require honest, upfront pricing by banning hidden fees, junk surcharges, and surprise charges so the price you see is the price you pay.
- Strengthen protections against price gouging or surge charging on the essentials families can’t skip, like fuel, food, utilities, housing, and medicine.
- Establish an independent state review board that has the authority to stop hospital mergers to prevent monopolies, protect competition, and keep healthcare affordable.
- Hold companies receiving taxpayer incentives accountable by measuring the true cost of every deal, publicly tracking results, and automatically recovering incentives when promised jobs and investments don't happen.
- Close corporate property tax loopholes that shift the cost of schools, roads, and services onto homeowners by forcing them to cover taxes that large corporations no longer pay.
- Eliminate government policies that penalize self-sufficiency by fixing broken benefit cliffs.
We should incentivize and reward work, giving families a clear path out of the system and back on their feet.
- Require every major state decision to account for its true long-term costs and broader impacts instead of letting politicians claim victories while shifting the costs onto families, local communities, and future taxpayers.
- Maximize federal matching dollars that deliver a positive financial return and bring home the tax dollars Hoosiers have already paid instead of sending our money to other states.
Making Indiana more affordable isn't about one law or one industry.
It's about making better decisions across the board.
Every taxpayer dollar should create value.
Every policy should reward work instead of penalizing it.
Every major decision should lower the total cost of living instead of quietly shifting those costs onto families.
That's how we build an economy where hard work goes further, businesses thrive, and Hoosier families can get ahead again.
