Whether you're taking your child to the doctor, managing a chronic illness, caring for an aging parent, or trying to find mental health services, every Hoosier deserves healthcare they can afford and rely on.
Today, the healthcare system is failing families.
Insurance premiums keep rising, hospital bills are unpredictable, mental health services can take months to access, and many seniors and people with disabilities struggle to get the care they need.
At the same time, healthcare providers spend more time fighting paperwork than caring for patients.
It doesn't have to be this way.
Healthcare isn't just an expense.
It's one of Indiana's largest employers and one of our biggest economic engines.
Every healthcare dollar supports nurses, physicians, therapists, pharmacists, home care workers, hospitals, clinics, and local businesses.
It also brings federal funding back to Indiana that strengthens our economy instead of benefiting other states.
Medicaid is one example.
Indiana receives significant federal matching funds that help pay for healthcare services.
Traditional Medicaid: The federal government pays roughly two-thirds of the cost.
Healthy Indiana Plan (HIP): The federal government pays 90 percent of the cost.
Those dollars don't disappear into bureaucracy.
They help keep hospitals open, support healthcare jobs, strengthen local economies, and provide care for working families, children, seniors, and people with disabilities.
Good healthcare policy should improve people's lives while being fiscally responsible.
We can lower costs, improve access, eliminate waste, and hold the healthcare system accountable without taking care away from the people who need it.
My Common-Sense Plan for Indiana Healthcare I am running to bring integrity and fiscal common sense back to how Indiana manages healthcare.
1.
Lower Healthcare Costs for Families Healthcare is one of the largest expenses most families face.
I'll work to lower costs by increasing price transparency, reducing unnecessary administrative expenses, and making sure Hoosiers know what care will cost before they receive it.
Require hospitals and insurance companies to publish clear, upfront prices.
Reduce surprise medical bills and hidden fees.
Increase competition and accountability throughout the healthcare system.
2.
Protect Working Families No one should lose health coverage because of paperwork or because they accepted a raise at work.
Eliminate unnecessary eligibility barriers that cause eligible families to lose coverage over administrative mistakes.
Create a buy-in option that allows working Hoosiers to keep affordable coverage as their income increases instead of falling off a coverage cliff.
3.
Hold Insurance Companies and Hospitals Accountable Government should recover waste and fraud wherever it exists, especially when large corporations are involved.
Aggressively recover improper payments and overcharges before cutting services.
Hold large insurance companies and hospital systems to the same standards expected of local healthcare providers.
Increase oversight and transparency throughout Indiana's Medicaid program.
4.
Expand Access to Care Families shouldn't wait months for care because our system is buried in bureaucracy.
Reduce Medicaid waiver waitlists.
Recruit and retain more mental health professionals.
Reduce unnecessary paperwork that keeps providers away from patients.
Strengthen access to primary care, behavioral health, and specialty services.
5.
Support Seniors, Caregivers, and People with Disabilities Most Hoosiers want to remain safely in their own homes for as long as possible.
Reduce waiver waitlists for home and community-based services.
Expand caregiver tax credits.
Create an independent PathWays for Aging ombudsman to help seniors navigate the system.
Strengthen support for family caregivers.
6.
Strengthen Mental Healthcare Mental healthcare should be available when people need it, not months later.
Expand school-based mental health services.
Increase the number of behavioral health providers.
Invest in regional mobile crisis teams so mental health emergencies receive the right response.
Healthcare should be affordable, easy to navigate, and focused on patients instead of paperwork.
By lowering costs, expanding access, eliminating waste, and holding powerful interests accountable, we can build a healthcare system that works better for families, strengthens our workforce, and uses taxpayer dollars responsibly.
