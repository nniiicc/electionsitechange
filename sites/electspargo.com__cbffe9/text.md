Read more: Affordability in Arizona → 02 Water Security Arizona's future depends on protecting our most precious resource—water.
Rick Spargo has spent months studying Arizona's growing water challenges and believes the state must act now with practical, long-term solutions.
Water security cannot be treated as a partisan issue—it is essential to our families, businesses, agriculture, and future economic prosperity.
Arizona has overcome water challenges before.
In 1903, visionary leaders created the Salt River Project (SRP), laying the foundation for generations of growth.
In the 1960s, the Central Arizona Project (CAP) delivered another bold, long-term solution.
Today, Arizona must once again rise to meet the challenge with the same vision and determination.
Arizona currently has 98 operating data centers, with another 86 already approved.
Collectively, those existing facilities consume nearly 300 million gallons of water every day.
By comparison, the average Arizona household uses about 300 gallons of water per day.
Spargo believes Arizona must carefully evaluate whether approving additional high-water-use facilities is in the best long-term interest of Arizona families and businesses.
Spargo supports responsible water conservation, strategic infrastructure investment, and policies that protect Arizona's limited water supply for future generations.
He believes the state should carefully evaluate new high-water-use developments, including AI facilities and data centers, to ensure Arizona's long-term water security is never compromised.
Today, Arizona has dozens of operating data centers, with many more planned.
A single large data center can consume millions of gallons of water each day while creating relatively few permanent jobs.
Spargo believes economic growth should never come at the expense of Arizona families' access to affordable water and reliable utilities.
Protecting Arizona's water also protects our quality of life.
Strong water policy supports tourism, outdoor recreation, agriculture, local businesses, and the natural beauty that makes our state one of the best places to live and visit.
