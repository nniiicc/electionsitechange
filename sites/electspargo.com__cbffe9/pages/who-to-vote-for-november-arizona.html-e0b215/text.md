The Candidate
Meet Rick Spargo, the Choice for District 3
Spargo is not a career politician.
He is a North Scottsdale small-business owner who built and ran a successful company for 35 years, has lived in the district for two decades, and raised four children here.
He has also served as State President of the Arizona Sons of the American Revolution, a non-partisan civic organization, and raised thousands of dollars for a homeless veterans' shelter.
That mix of business experience and community service is the foundation of his campaign — and it shows up in a platform built around practical results rather than partisan theater.
Spargo isn't running on vague promises.
His campaign is organized around five concrete priorities, each with a clear reason behind it.

One of Spargo's clearest positions is on the legislature itself.
He supports term limits — no more than two terms — because he believes in citizen democracy: people stepping forward to serve, doing the job well, and then making room for new ideas.
It is unusual for a candidate to campaign on limiting his own time in office, and it says something about how he sees the role: a public trust, not a career.
How He Governs
Common Sense Over Ideology
What ties Spargo's platform together is temperament.
He talks openly about reaching across the aisle, finding common ground, and treating neighbors as fellow Arizonans first, before party labels.
His view is that people in the district have far more in common than the political noise suggests, and that real results come from listening and compromise rather than theater.
For voters who want a representative focused on getting things done, that is the case he is making before Election Day.
