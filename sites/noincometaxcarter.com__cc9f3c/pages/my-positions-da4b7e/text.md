Universal Healthcare I support universal health.
The focus of our attention is all wrong.
When someone gets a rash, the question seems to be what expensive medicines can we put on the rash to make it go away in a few months instead of why are you getting a rash?
By examining (asking questions!) and addressing the root causes of health issues, rather than defaulting to the use of expensive pharma to mask symptoms, we can improve the quality of life for EVERY Washington resident.
As a mother, and as your representative, that would continue to be my main focus.
Inevitably, people need health care.
Through grant funding and donations, universities and individual doctors continue to research, refine, and discover new options and solutions for healthcare challenges.
However, insurance and treatment costs continue to skyrocket leaving many of the available healthcare options out of reach.
Catastrophic insurance and allowing interstate competition for insurance decreases the cost of insurance and treatment significantly.
Neither of these options are currently available in Washington.
Currently the “health” or rather “sickness” industry is the 5th largest industry in the USA.
With emphasis on real Health, wait times decrease, as do prices, as people become more healthy.
Why do so many Canadians come to Bellingham for procedures and healthcare?
Because Canadian Universal Healthcare is neither timely nor accessible.
To think that it would be any different in Washington is folly.
Universal healthcare decreases competition, then complacency sets in.
We need high quality healthcare to survive, and we need to keep our competitive health services to do it.

Corporate Healthcare I will advocate for patient choice and research regulations to create more opportunities for private medical practice so individual attention is a possibility for patients.
Those who prefer corporate impersonal--get the medication and get out—can chose corporate medicine, those who prefer more individual attention should also have a choice.
The bill HB 1675 would force the corporations to allow the doctor more time, but would not solve the issue of accessibility, and may actually do more harm than good.

Our state budget has doubled in the last 10 years--50% higher than inflation and immigration!
Individual/Family income has not kept pace, straining many Washingtonians’ budgets.
Still our Legislature continues to ask Washingtonians to pay more rather than trimming that budget.
Ineffective programs failing to do the job they were designed for coupled with zero accountability for allocated funds has left $1.3 billion (yes BILLION...that’s 1,300 millions) unaccounted for in the most recent state audit.
There is no need to increase any taxes in our state.
I will explore ways to decrease existing taxes, give small business a break, and examine how Florida’s model is eliminating taxes to look for solutions that work.

Water Adjudication Process The 40th Legislative District frequently does not have a water shortage problem, but an overabundance of water.
The one size fits all for any given county is not only ineffective, but damaging.
A long time ago, some landlords came up to a king and demanded he sign a paper or lose his “job.” The king did sign the paper and for the first time ever, the natural right of a human to their own life was recognized officially by the Magna Carta.
A natural right is a right that every human around the world desires.
We all desire our own life.
It was later evidenced that unless an individual had property (whether intellectual or physical) and the freedom to use that property, that individual could not sustain their own life.
And so the natural rights to liberty and property were recognized, and put in our WA state Constitution (Art.
1 Sec 3), Declaration of Independence, and US Constitution.
These rights are foundational. (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/44800/44800-h/44800-h.htm) With the state trying to determine the “rights” to water, it is disassociating water from the land.
However, one cannot live on a land without water, and thus it is a necessary part of the land.
Those who buy land that doesn’t have water, have cheaper land and need to come up with a way to access water in another manner.
Some people are also trying to cling to the old ways.
Some of the old ways are good, but others need to be let go.
With our current technologies, which are in many cases helping to improve the old ways dramatically, some of the old ways need to be disposed of.
Water rights that help salmon only in the old style will not help enough to sustain the increase in population of the growing tribes, and growing population in general.
Salmon hatcheries have been very successful sustaining the wild salmon population and need to be encouraged, without decreasing the ever growing need for electricity and food in other areas.
Top Budget Priorities My main priority is to cut the heavy tax burden on working families.
Eliminating the state income tax, the Carbon Emissions Tax, and other programs that are ineffective is a logical starting point.
Reducing the Carbon Emissions tax would decrease the price on gasoline which would affect (decrease!) food prices in the State of Washington and provide immediate relief to struggling families.
Eliminating fraud is also a priority.
The Washington State Audit this year found that “over $1.3 billion in childcare spending could not be fully audited over a four-year period due to missing records, along with $37 million in questionable payments identified in 2025.” https://portal.sao.wa.gov/ReportSearch/Home/ViewReportFile? arn=1039325&isFinding=false&sp=false or https://sao.wa.gov/reports-data/audit-reports for March 30, 2026 In addition, “The audit identified more than $5.6 million in improper charges to Medicaid, along with widespread failures in internal controls meant to ensure funds are spent correctly.” https://www.kvi.com/2026/04/14/fraud-watch-wa-audit-finds-millions-in-improper-medicaid-chargesmajor-oversight-failures-at-health-care-authority/ Eliminating fraud balances out decreases in funding.
Our families and businesses are over taxed.
By improving our businesses climate we can make Washington attractive again for entreprenuers, scientists, and business to come innovate and grow.

To suggest that any citizen is incapable of securing identification—required to bank, receive medical treatment, drive a car, access government assistance, obtain a passport or fly on a commercial airline—is insulting.
The best way to have secure elections is to have in-person voting with ID.
Those who suggest that women, people of color, or indeed any functioning member of society—all of whom require proper identification after the age of 18 to do anything—are incapable of acquiring ID is disingenuous at minimum, or, at worst, seriously stereotyped prejudice.
WA State was ranked 46th out of 50 in the nation in election security recently.
