PFAS chemicals are known to cause serious health challenges including cancers.
Wendy is one of our town residents with a PFAS related cancer - breast cancer.
She uses her story to put a face to the toxic-contamination issue and continues to fight against the Goliath that is Saint-Gobain in order for our town to get remediation costs covered.
As a rep, she has worked to: Protect Public schools Get access to affordable healthcare Promote renewable and efficient energies Protect our environment Opposed the ICE Detention Facility in Merrimack Opposed the Hyperscale Data Center in Nottingham Wendy has a record of fighting for Merrimack town residents.
You can count on her to: Protect our communities from corporate polluters Wendy has led the fight against corporate polluters like Saint-Gobain and Kinder Morgan to ensure safe drinking water and clean air for Merrimack, and will continue to take on the tough fights for us.
Make health care more affordable As a clinical microbiologist, mother and cancer patient, Wendy understands the need for quality, affordable health.
As a legislator, she has repeatedly fought the big insurance companies and their lobbyists to ensure hard working Granite Staters can afford the care we need.
Support our local businesses As a small business owner herself, Wendy Thomas understands the importance of supporting Merrimack's small business and fighting for the resources they need to thrive Wendy sits on the Science, Technology and Energy committee which focuses on energy issues in New Hampshire and she is a State Director for Women in Government.
She has chaired the Environmental Toxins caucus and the Therapeutic Cannabis caucus.
She is also a member of the Hillsborough Executive Council which sets the county's budget.
Wendy is an active member of the Merrimack Citizen Energy Committee, Merrimack Friends and Family, and the Merrimack Historical Society.
Wendy served as the host for the Historical Society's To and Froe video documentary series.
Wendy continues to hold office hours at town hall on the second Tuesday of each month in the Memorial room, Town Hall at 5:00 pm.
