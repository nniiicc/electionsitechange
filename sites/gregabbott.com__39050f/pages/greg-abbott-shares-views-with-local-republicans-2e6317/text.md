“I think he’s done a great job as attorney general,” local attorney Greg Gossett said.
“He’ll make an excellent governor.” Abbott’s speech touched on the future of the Republican Party and his fight against concerns such as voter fraud, the right to bear arms and President Barack Obama’s health care reform.
He said he’s asked what his job entails and explained it’s gotten simplified.
“I go into the office in the morning,” he said, “I sue Barack Obama, and then I go home.” “We’re taking care of business right now,” he said before the dinner at San Angelo Country Club, adding there was a lot of work to be done in the courts.
“We’ll let politics take care of itself later,” he said.
Instead, his speech included statistics on how Texas is leading the nation in job creation.
He cited that 50 percent of the jobs created nationwide in the past five years were created in Texas.
He talked about the party making strides in diversity by citing the election of the first Hispanic U.S. senator, Ted Cruz, and six Hispanics and three African-Americans elected as state legislators.
Abbott asked everyone in the audience to turn on their smartphones, log onto Twitter and start getting involved with the social media he said helped Obama win the election.
The five Republican justices of the 3rd Court of Appeals also took the microphone for quick words of thanks.
Party Chairman Jeff Betty gave introductions, joking that his hardest job was “estimating the size of the room.” About 150 people attended the Presidents Day celebration hosted by the Tom Green County Republican Party — more than double what they expected, Betty said.
At the end of the presentation, Betty said that money raised that evening was the beginning of a building fund for the party so it can host speakers.
He said 10 percent of its goal was reached Tuesday night.
Mike Boyd, president and CEO of First Financial Bank, said he believes Abbott stands for everything the local party does — that the state is strong and a leader in the nation in job creation.
He said that he also likes Abbott’s strong sense of conviction and that he practices it every day.
“He’s not afraid to give his opinion. … And he does his research,” Boyd said.
