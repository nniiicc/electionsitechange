Primary Policies The positions I am going to spend the most time and energy advocating for in my first term.
End Property Taxes Replace property taxes with a fairer system that lowers taxes on workers and rewards not wasting land.
End Property Taxes New Hampshire’s current tax system is awful and needs to be repealed and replaced.
Abolish Property Taxes .
Tax land value rather than the buildings on the land.
This would encourage utilizing valuable land instead of punishing having a house like we do now.
Combine the Land Value Tax with an Offset (OLVT) by giving a $50,000 deduction on a property's taxable land value if the owner lives on the property.
This shifts the tax burden toward out of staters who own 3rd and 4th homes that sit empty, corporate Airbnbs , and wasted land, empty lots, and rotting buildings that could instead be sold to, rebuilt by, and used by local businesses.
80% of OLVT taxes will come from a statewide equal tax rate, distributed to towns to fund schools, and 20% (or more, if the town chooses) from a local rate set by towns.
This ensures poorer towns like Fremont don't continue having to pay absurd amounts of tax while rich towns pay half of what we do per paycheck.
Legalize Marijuana through the NH Liquor and Wine Commission, ensuring it is both tax free ( cheaper than Mass ), and the profits go to lowering residents' taxes .
Rename the commission to the NH Liquor Wine and Weed Commission.
Repeal 2026 HB 155 to make out-of-state corporations pay their fair share again .
Reinstate the Interest and Dividends tax on high-value transactions.
Support a greed fee that applies only to companies where the highest-paid employee’s income is OVER 100 TIMES the other full-time employees' salaries, with a 1:1 penalty above that threshold.
Ban public tax dollars from going to private schools End NH's 2-Party Systems You deserve better than choosing between bad and worse every election.
Democracy Reform Duopoly is bad in business, but it's even worse in politics.
New Hampshire should move toward a system where voters have real choices, and parties have to actually compete to win support.
End New Hampshire’s two-party system by adopting reforms that make representation more proportional and competitive.
Adopt local list proportional representation , preserving local representation while creating a system where voters have real options.
This system works by voters ranking their top few choices of party, then ranking their top few choices of candidate within their first-choice party from their district.
Use Single Transferable Vote for Executive Council and Governor’s races.
This will reduce polarization and make New Hampshire’s government more representative & respond to the people's needs .
Strengthen New Hampshire’s role as a model representative democracy, which will protect our First in the Nation Primary .
Housing New Hampshire has a housing emergency.
We need faster, cheaper, and smarter community construction.
Housing New Hampshire is in a housing emergency.
Housing costs are crushing families, seniors, workers, and local businesses.
If young talent cannot afford to live here, they will move elsewhere, which hurts our economy, weakens our communities, strains school budgets, and raises taxes and costs for everyone.
We must: Adopt statewide pro-housing policy with the goal of making New Hampshire the cheapest state to live in the USA.
Adopt Freedom-First Standardized Zoning , a pro-business policy that allows faster, cheaper construction of mixed-use neighborhoods , built by NH workers, for Granite Staters.
Using a statewide zoning framework, where towns choose what areas are zoned for by choosing from clear state standards, returns property rights to individuals and communities, and reduces development time and cost .
Expand the New Hampshire Housing Finance Authority’s ability to build affordable housing during housing emergencies , including targeting current and future transit corridors for housing expansion.
End street homelessness within 2 years by providing temporary emergency campgrounds on state land with security, safe storage, hygiene products, and a mailing & email address for job applications until stable housing can be secured.
Anything is safer than the streets, and this way, there is a centralized place for distribution of resources to homeless populations, like VA resources for homeless veterans , assistance getting hired, and connecting folks with low income housing opportunities.
Only 1 out of 7 people go into being homless addicted to drugs or alcohol, but over 5 out of 7 will abuse substances while on the streets.
Keeping people off the streets will be a major step towards slowing the spread of drug abuse, which makes this a public safety policy too, not just a housing policy.
Implement upzoning transit-oriented development into zoning standards ensuring housing, jobs, and transportation will grow together .
Create a fund of state money for towns and cities to use for infrastructure improvements to increase the town's population density, if they are doing so in a sustainable way backed up by community planning research.
Secondary Policies Positions I will secondarily advocate for during my first term.
Schools It's time to revive and reinvent NH's public education system.
Pre-K Through University Public Education Require all high schools to allow students to choose to take dual-enrollment CCSNH classes as stand-ins for high school classes.
State funding for two CCSNH classes per semester for public high school dual-enrollment students.
End No Child Left Behind.
School needs to be a challenge, not a time-killer where everyone is moved ahead even when they fail their classes Ensure technology is being used as much as possible in classrooms.
We need to be preparing our children for the future, not preparing them for how the world was #ago.
Protect and expand our special needs programs in schools; this includes recognizing that high-achieving students also need to be specially educationally planned for.
Revamp school funding models as described in my tax policy above.
Ensure that there are enough counselors in every public school.
Mental health is vital to our kids' learning, growing, and becoming successful members of society.
Keep public money in public schools, ending public tax-dollars paying for rich kids' private school tuitions.
Re-fund and modernize the Community College System of New Hampshire (CCSNH) and the University System of New Hampshire.
Reverse the recent department closings at Plymouth State, and require USNH keep Plymouth State open.
Require all research teams to present their work in casual public settings periodically to re-engage the public in science and learning.
Health, PFAS, & Addiction Protect our health, recognize PFAS for the public health emergency it is, and make the polluters pay for the health damage they've caused.
Health, PFAS, and Addiction Recognize the PFAS crisis as a true public health emergency.
Ban PFAS -related chemicals from use, including PFPEs, PTFEs, PVDFs, PFAAs, and PFEAs, including flexible language so the companies can't make minor changes to get around following the law.
Force companies that used these substances to pay into a public health fund for the expensive health costs tied to PFAS.
Part of that will go to a dedicated PFAS environmental impact fund to study and fix long-term environmental effects of these chemicals in New Hampshire communities.
Promote mental health resources for students and adults because mentally healthy communities are safer and more successful.
Mental Health is a top killer of non-elderly Granite Staters.
Additionally, preventative mental health care is fiscally responsible; every $1 in preventative mental healthcare saves $6 in future treatment and societal costs.
Addiction is a disease .
We need a state plan to treat that disease rather than punish non-dealers without treating their condition.
Jail alone will NEVER cure a disease.
Liberties & Community Safety Protect responsible firearm ownership and focus on practical community safety.
Community Safety Protect the rights of law-abiding, trained citizens to safely store and use firearms.
They have the right to defend themselves and their families.
Generally ensure that we are focusing our law enforcement efforts on the crimes that matter.
Marijuana enforcement is a waste of law enforcement's time when they can be hunting down real criminals who are robbing NH citizens.
Treat mental health as a core part of public safety and community success.
This includes having enough counselors in every school.
Consolidate emergency services to the county level, saving money and lowering response times.
Use saved funds on mental health crisis and social service crisis response services so that police can focus on solving real crime , and fire/EMS can focus on their real jobs, letting dedicated experts deal with mental crisis events.
Data Centers AI and data centers should not raise residents’ bills, drain resources, or damage communities.
AI Data Centers AI is an extremely powerful tool with large benefits for domestically controlling it, but AI data centers can consume major amounts of energy, water, land, and infrastructure.
If they come to New Hampshire, they MUST be held to strict standards that protect residents, ratepayers, and communities' natural beauty.
Require AI data centers to produce 200% of their own energy usage in clean energy on-site.
Allow the requirement to fall to 150% if 100% air cooling is used.
Require excess energy to be sold back to the grid at the lowest applicable cost.
Require utility companies to use those savings to offset residents’ energy bills.
This setup will lower your energy bills while advancing the USA's and New England's energy independence.
Require 100% closed-loop cooling systems if liquid cooling is used for any data center (not just AI data centers).
Set statewide noise limits on all data centers, which can be tightened by local communities if needed, in order to protect small-town vibes.
Ban all data centers in high population density areas.
Make data center companies pay 100% of new needed infrastructure costs.
Require minimum 500% assumed liability for violations (minimum of 5x the value of the violation + costs saved by the company), paid by the company itself, not an insurance bailout.
Require third-party expert environmental review before construction ever begins, just like other major infrastructure projects (like new rail lines).
Consumer Protection & Rights Support and expand NH's systems for protecting consumers and seniors from being exploited Consumer Protection and Rights Ban Junk Fees Require that the sticker price be the price you pay after all fees & taxes both in person and online; there should not be last-minute surprise charges or hidden fees.
Common violators of that are Airlines, Hotels, Concerts, Cable & Internet like Comcast.
Increase enforcement and penalties against elder-targeting scams (and all scams).
Require that contracts for essential services include accurate and short plain-language explainations, including terms of service, so that Granite Staters can finally understand what they're agreeing to when hitting "I agree" Transit Support public transit, commuter rail, and development that helps pay for long-term growth.
Transit & Development New Hampshire needs a transportation system that connects people to jobs, housing, schools, and opportunity.
Transit should be paired with smart development so growth helps pay for itself.
Support advancements in all transportation options across New Hampshire.
Support a Route 101 rapid transit line from Manchester to Portsmouth.
Support an I-93 rapid transit corridor from Laconia to Boston.
Commuter rail could also be used if rapid transit is not possible.
Use development around transit, ideally through the New Hampshire Housing Finance Authority, to pay for our transit system, so we can build it all without tax increases.
Support the creation of walkable communities in cities/towns that want it.
Get rid of red tape for those towns to achieve it when necessary.
See housing policy for info on transit-oriented development.
