First and foremost, JJ is a fighter .
From a young age, he knew that if we wanted to see the world change, he had to get out there and do something about it.
In the photo to the left, you can see JJ protesting for individual rights when he was in 6th grade, only 11 years old.
He had to beg his parents to let him go, because when he heard that others were going to be out there working to make their communities a safer place, he refused to stand aside and let others do the hard work without him .
Throughout his entire life, JJ has continued pushing what he believes in and fighting for his friends and neighbors.
The photo to the right, JJ is in 8th grade, telling Pete Broderick, Jim Baker, the rest of the Sanborn School District Board, and former Superintendent Thomas Ambrose that their plan of school consolidation was a horrible idea which saved nearly no money and forced 10 year olds into the same spaces as 19 year olds.
Most of all, this plan was the first steps in their plan to force Fremont to either get kicked out of SRHS or take an awful deal.
JJ recognized that when he was only 14, and called the SRSD Board out for it, because he does not back down when he sees corruption.
JJ later, as a senior at Sanborn, was elected by his peers to be the student representative to the SRSD Board.
In this role, he was the first student in a long time to actually speak up against the board and their decisions, including at board meetings.
The board even attempted to illegally (RSA 194:23-f) kick JJ off of the school board, because they were uncomfortable with their hypocrisy being called out.
He was able to stop several policy changes that would have harmed the school and/or raised taxes needlessly.
In 2025, JJ worked as an AIP Associate at the National Institute of Standards and Technology*, where he supported the mission of the Office of Advanced Manufacturing; including supporting NH's very own BioFabUSA Institute.
In this role, he saw examples of ways that good government can efficiently work to support public good.
We need more of that efficiency in NH's government.
At the same time though, he saw this program struggle because of bad policy created by Congress and state legislatures, and it furthered his drive to do something about the problems he saw.
In university, JJ triple majors, with his primary degree in "Political Theory and Constitutional Democracy", and secondary degrees in "Physics", and "Philosophy".
The focus of his thesis was on constitutional voting system reform, which is part of why he believes so strongly that we need to end the 2 party system, and has a specific proposal on exactly how to do so.
More broadly, his public affairs education taught him how to attack the root causes of issues, including affordability issues like housing and transportation, fair tax systems, energy costs, consumer protection, and health costs and risks.
Today, JJ is running for State Representative, because his unique experiences have inspired him to be the change we NEED to see in NH.
He doesn't believe that we are being treated right, and that our state government is being controlled by pro-2-party system extremists who intend to keep voters like us down; this is unacceptable, and he is proud to be supported by a nonpartisan coalition of citizens who want Fremont and NH to have a better tomorrow.
