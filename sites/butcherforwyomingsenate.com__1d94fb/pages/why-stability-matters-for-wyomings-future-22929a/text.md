Throughout the day, one theme kept surfacing: Wyoming needs stability.
Not because people are afraid of change.
Wyoming has always adapted and evolved.
But families, businesses, and communities make long-term decisions.
They hire employees, build facilities, buy homes, invest in equipment, and raise children based on what they believe Wyoming will look like five, ten, or twenty years from now.
When people lose confidence in the future, investment slows.
Opportunities shrink.
Young people begin looking elsewhere.
Stability is what allows hard work, planning, and investment to pay off.
As I moved from table to table, I heard concerns about workforce shortages, housing, healthcare access, infrastructure, energy development, and the future of our communities.
These issues may seem separate, but they all come back to the same question: Are we creating a Wyoming where people feel confident building their future?
I’ve spent much of my career helping communities work through difficult decisions.
One lesson I’ve learned is that progress doesn’t come from uncertainty.
It comes from trust.
People move forward when they understand where they’re headed and believe the foundation beneath them is solid.
That is one of the reasons I decided to run for the Wyoming Senate.
Good government should create the conditions that allow people to succeed.
That means responsible budgeting, reliable infrastructure, strong schools, accessible healthcare, thriving local communities, and a business climate that encourages investment and job creation.
Wyoming’s future won’t be built through short-term thinking.
It will be built through steady leadership, clear priorities, and decisions that strengthen our state for the long haul.
I appreciate the Wyoming Business Alliance for bringing together so many voices from across the state.
The conversations were thoughtful, candid, and focused on what matters most: ensuring Wyoming remains a place where families, businesses, and communities can thrive for generations to come.
