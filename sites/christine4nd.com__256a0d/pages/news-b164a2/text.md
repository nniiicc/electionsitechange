Preview a Sample Ballot November 3 General Election Please find the link below to the sample ballot for the November 3 General Election.
In addition to the candidates, your ballot may include several important ballot questions.
Please review your sample ballot before Election Day so you have an opportunity to learn about the candidates and measures that will appear on your ballot.
There are several ways to vote: Vote by Mail: Absentee ballots are available now by request.
If you choose to vote by mail, be sure to allow enough time for your completed ballot to be received by the appropriate election office by the November 2 deadline.
Vote Early In Person: North Dakota voters may vote early in person beginning October 27.
Early voting is available at designated locations.
Please check the official voting information for your area before you go.
Vote on Election Day: Election Day is Tuesday, November 3.
Polling places will be open for in-person voting.
Please take a few minutes to make a voting plan, review your ballot, and encourage your family and friends to participate in this important election.
Every eligible voter has an opportunity to make their voice heard.
Thank you for your continued support and for being an engaged member of our community.

14 Aug Friday, 8:58 AM · 2026 Miwest Technology Summit Midwest Technology Summit 8/13/2026 The Summit brought together policymakers, business leaders, and technology experts to discuss Data Centers and how AI is shaping industries across North Dakota.
The focus on policy, practical application, and emerging trends offered many insights.
Leaders in education, business, and agriculture shared how technology has influenced and continues to influence their respective organizations.
North Dakota’s Ad Hoc Legislative Committee on AI and Data Centers, aimed at fostering technology while implementing safeguards to protect citizens from corporate exploitation, digital harm, and unmanaged structural displacement, alongside the Governor’s “Rate Payer Protection” pledge, is instrumental in addressing the concerns of citizens and implementing safeguards.
Technology and learning about the impacts and utility of Data Centers/AI has shown that many of the original anxieties concerning electricity costs, water usage, and other environmental concerns have demonstrated that solutions are possible.
For example, efficient closed-loop cooling systems do not require large quantities of water, resourceful electric capacity responses, fiber-optic connectivity benefits, and zoning issues, etc.
I am looking forward to the second Ad Hoc Legislative Committee report on Data Centers/AI, the developing Federal legislation and policy, and, importantly, what concerns citizens have as Data Centers evolve.
