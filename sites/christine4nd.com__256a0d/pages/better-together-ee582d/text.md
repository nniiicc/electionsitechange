Read this letter as a PDF Better Together Whether real or perceived, it is disheartening to read about the disarray in the NDGOP.
Either way, one must hope that a common purpose will emerge.
One only has to look to the national political arena to observe that, in many instances, bad or non-existent Democrat policies, even preposterous in some cases, are recurrently winning elections at all levels.
Unfortunately, Republicans advocating practical policies are often failing to influence voter turnout.
Poor messaging, ineffective explanations, and party squabbling, among other reasons, are just not attractive to voters, especially Independent voters.
The NDGOP, working together on behalf of the electorate, has generated unprecedented prosperity for our great State.
Nevertheless, factions within the party such as "populist," "conservative, etc., risk overshadowing the many accomplishments.
As a senatorial candidate, be assured, I will work to ensure our laws and programs support all of our residents by enabling each person to build a successful future for themselves, their families, and future generations, regardless of party affiliation.
Historically, the GOP has prided itself on robust party debate, building a big tent, and offering good practical solutions.
The cancellation of dissenting voices or of differing opinions within the party would be a real pity.
As Republicans, we cherish our liberty and seek to protect our Constitutional and inalienable rights.
Yet for Republicans to win this upcoming election, we must also work together to foster harmony, promote the good, and seek peaceful coexistence in our state and districts while holding fast to our core principles.
As we are so often reminded, a house divided against itself rarely, if ever, prevails.
While the NDGOP has a responsibility to effectively communicate its messages so voters can be informed, citizens also have a duty to participate in their government by asking questions and giving input.
Choosing not to vote or remaining silent equates to tacit approval, which does, in the end, mean that the voters have indeed spoken, regardless of turnout, since winners and losers are declared at the conclusion of the election.
My promise to the voters is a family-friendly campaign because I believe in service, the family, the sanctity of life, and that all of us, the young and "mature," deserve a decent shot at the "American Dream." So, whether "populist," "conservative," or somewhere in between, we must work together before the North Dakota Grand Old Party folds in on itself.
See you on the campaign trail.
Dr.
