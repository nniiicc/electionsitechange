VICK: As a son of East Tennessee - born and raised in White Pine and still a resident of Jefferson County - I see our state through the same eyes you do.
Whether I'm commuting to work or heading to the grocery store, I feel the same frustrations and face the same hurdles as every other Tennessean.
From the potholes and congestion of our aging roads to the rising cost of a grocery bag that makes a $100 bill feel like pocket change, our daily lives are being squeezed.
We see "FOR SALE" signs on land our families can no longer afford, and we see our neighbors struggling on the streets, battling mental health crises without a hand to pull them up.
For 40 years, I have served in public education.
I have seen our teachers and students suffocated by politics when they should be empowered by support.
I won't promise to fix every problem, but I will promise this: I will lead with my faith as my compass.
I will make decisions based on what is right for our people, not what is convenient for a political party.
Together, WE can build a Tennessee that lifts us ALL up.

Rural healthcare and hospital closures Living and working in rural East Tennessee, I read and see a heartbreaking trend: the lights are going out in our rural hospitals.
Tennessee has one of the highest rates of rural hospital closures in the country over the last decade.
1 Tennessee has seen more rural hospital closures per capita than almost an other state in the nation.
Today, nearly 30% of our remaining rural hospitals are at immediate risk of closing their doors.
This isn't just a policy failure, it is HEART failure.
When a rural hospital closes, a community loses more than just a building, it loses its safety net.
Expectant mothers are forced to drive over an hour for basic care, emergency responses times skyrocket, and our most vulnerable neighbors, struggling with poverty and mental health crisis, are left with nowhere to turn.
My "Faith First" approach tells me we cannot ignore the suffering of our rural brothers and sisters.
As your governor, I will pursue "NEW RESULTS" for Tennessee by: Protecting At-Risk Facilities: Moving beyond temporary grants to create sustainable, long-term funding models that keep rural ERs and maternity wards open.
Expanding Mental Health Access: Integrating behavioral health into rural primary care so that help is available in the community, not just distant cities.
Investing in the Rural Workforce: Offering aggressive incentives for medical students &nbsp; nurses who commit to serving in our underserved counites.

Mental Health In my four decades as an educator, I have learned an undeniable truth, a child who is hurting cannot learn and a community that struggles with mental health issues cannot thrive.
For too long Tennessee has treated mental health issues as an afterthought.
As your governor, I will make it a primary focus ensuring that "NEW RESULTS" means a healthier, more resilient Tennessee.
My three-point plan for mental wellness in Tennessee.
1.
Transform schools into centers of support.
Over 70% of children who receive mental health care do so at school.
Yet, Tennessee's ratio of school psychologist to students is roughly 1 to 2,400, far below the recommended ratio to 1 to 500.
2.
Breaking the cycle of poverty and crime.
We need to shift our focus from strictly punitive measures and addressing the "root causes" stressors that create negative environments.
3.
Bridging the rural access gap.
Many of our neighbors in rural Tennessee live in "mental health deserts.
Zip codes should never determine whether a Tennessean can access the life-saving care they need.
Cost of Living According to a recent Vanderbilt study, Tennesseans are more and more anixous about the every day cost of living.
87% of those surveyed say that their daily cost of living is "Very" or "Somewhat" expensive. * But what happens when there is a medical emergency or the car breaks down?
Then more and more worry builds up inside our family homes. * https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2025/12/11/vanderbilt-poll-tennesseans-economic-anxiety-surges-transcends-party-lines/ Transportation Infrastructure Challenge Tennessee faces a "structural mismatch" in its transportation funding.
Despite the Transportation Modernization Act of 2023, which provided a massive influx of funds, the state's primary revenue source, the fuel tax, is failing to keep pace with inflation and the rise of fuel-efficient vehicles.
As construction costs have tripled since the early 2000's, the Tennessee Department of Transportation (TDOT) estimates a $30 billion gap to complete all necessary upgrades.
Key pressing issues: 1.
Urban Congestion.
Our major cities, particularly Nashville and Knoxville, are experiencing severe traffic delays.
The state ranks 27th in the nation for urban congestion, with frieght bottlenecks threatening Tennessee's status as a top-tier logistic hub.
2.
Rural Infrastructure Strains: Over 650 miles of rural interstates remain in their original two-lane configuration, despite a projected 54% increase in truck tonnage over the next 25 years.
Furthermore, rural roads remain significantly more dangerous, with fatality rates more than double those of other roads.
3.
The "BRIDGE CLIFF": Approximately 7,900 bridges across the state are currently rated in "fair" or "poor" condition.
Many of these structures are reaching their 50-year design life, creating an impending surge of necessary replacements.
4.
Population Outpacing Pavement: With an influx of hundreds of thousands of new residents, the demand for road capacity is growing faster than projects can be completed, leading to backlog that competes with the need of new construction.
Vouchers As a lifelong educator with 40 years in Tennessee public schools, I believe in the power of education to change lives.
While I absolutely respect a parent's right to choose different options to educate their child(ren) such as homeschool or private schools, I am deeply concerned about the currect path of unversal school vouchers in Tennessee.
The book characters, Robin Hood and his merry men, were know to steal from the rich and give to the poor.
Unfortunately, I can only see this program as a way to make the wealth, wealthier.
In my school district, Cocke County, the state spends an average of $4300 per student, a student voucher is $7500.
That math doesn't add up for the average Tennesseee family.
The "Education Freedom Scholarship" program, as currently enacted, risks leaving our most vulnerable students behind.
Here is why I believe we must refocus our state's priorites. : Rural Tennessee Deserves Better: In many of our rural counties, "school choice" is an empty promise because there are simply no private schools nearby.
Vouchers drain money from the local schools that serve as the heartbeat of these communities.
Support for Students with Disabilites: Private schools are not required to provide the same services or legal protections for students with disabilities that public schol are.
For a family with a special need child, a voucher if often not a "choice" at all, but it's a guarntee of lack of support.
Fiscal Responsibility vs Subsidies: Taxpayer dollars should be used to lift up all students.
Currently, the majority of voucher funds are being used to subsidize tuition for families who were already enolled in private schools.
As mentioned above, this is the Robin Hood mentality in reverse, taking from the public system to bendfit the wealthy.
As your governor, I will advocate for a "PUBLIC FIRST" approach.
We must fully fund our public classrooms, pay our teachers a competitive wage and ensure that every Tennessee student, regardless of their address or abilities, has access to high-quality education.
Faith First, Tennessee Always means investing the the future of all children.
