A Better Oregon.
Oregon doesn't lack resources, ideas, or hardworking people.
What we've lacked is the leadership and accountability to turn those resources into meaningful results.
As a CPA with years of experience managing state budgets, I believe government should be held to the same standards Oregon families face every day: live within your means, prioritize what matters, and take responsibility for the results.
My priorities are straightforward: make Oregon more affordable, put education dollars back into classrooms, address homelessness through treatment and lasting stability, and strengthen public safety.
These aren't partisan ambitions.
They're practical challenges that demand common-sense solutions, responsible stewardship of taxpayer dollars, and a willingness to challenge the status quo.
Oregonians deserve more than promises and bigger budgets.
They deserve a government that works for them.
It's time for new leadership in Salem, and accountability you can count on.

Andrew will fight for responsible budgeting and policies that reduce unnecessary regulatory burdens driving up costs.

We need more of the education dollars we're already spending to reach classrooms.
Read More Homelessness From Managing Homelessness to Restoring Stability Oregon has spent substantial sums and has produced some measurable results.
State figures report thousands of shelter beds created or maintained and thousands of people rehoused, while the 2026 statewide shelter program alone awarded more than $102 million.
At the same time, Oregon's overall homeless population has continued to increase even as unsheltered homelessness declined modestly.
That makes the next question what happens after someone enters the system.
I support a treatment-and-stability model that uses existing shelter and public infrastructure wherever practical, expands addiction and behavioral-health treatment capacity, connects people with established treatment providers, and measures success by whether people become stable enough to remain housed.
State-funded providers should report meaningful outcomes, not simply dollars spent or people contacted.
That means tracking treatment engagement and completion, employment, movement into permanent housing, housing retention after 6, 12 and 24 months, repeat homelessness, and cost per successful outcome.
