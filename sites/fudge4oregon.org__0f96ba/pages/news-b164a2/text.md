Meet Andrew Issues Endorsements News Contribute News 10/16/2026 Fudge Campaign Enters Final Stretch with Broad Coalition, Labor Support, and Expanded Voter Outreach Independent Party cross-nomination, Republican leadership endorsements, Teamsters and SMART support, and Oregon Right to Life PAC recommendation highlight campaign developments as ballots arrive.
WEST SALEM, Ore. — With ballots arriving in mailboxes across Oregon House District 20, Republican and Independent Party nominee Andrew Fudge is entering the final weeks of the 2026 election with an expanding coalition of supporters, an energized volunteer operation, and a comprehensive campaign focused on bringing new leadership and accountability to Salem.
Since securing the Republican nomination with nearly two-thirds of the vote in a contested primary, Fudge has earned the cross-nomination of the Independent Party of Oregon, formal endorsements from prominent Republican leaders and organized labor, and a recommendation from Oregon Right to Life PAC.
"Our campaign has come a long way since the primary," said Fudge.
"We set out to build a coalition that goes beyond political labels, and that's exactly what we've done.
Republicans, Independents, working families, and Oregonians who are frustrated with the direction of our state are coming together around a common message: it's time for accountability and results." Building a Coalition Beyond Party Lines Fudge's nomination by the Independent Party of Oregon represents a significant milestone in a district where independent and nonaffiliated voters play a major role in determining election outcomes.
The campaign has also secured support from Republican legislative leaders and elected officials, including State Representative Ed Diehl and Angela Plowhead, Polk County Commissioners Craig Pope and Lyle Mordhorst, and Commissioner-elect John Swanson.
Fudge has additionally been formally recommended by Oregon Right to Life PAC and is included in the organization's 2026 digital voter guide.
Beyond partisan politics, the campaign has earned the backing of organized labor, including Teamsters and SMART transportation representatives, reflecting shared priorities involving workplace safety, transportation infrastructure, and protecting Oregon workers.
"Having the support of both Republican leaders and organized labor speaks to what this campaign is about," Fudge said.
"We don't have to agree on every issue to work together on the things that matter.
Safe workplaces, responsible government, good schools, and an affordable future aren't exclusively Republican or Democratic priorities.
They're Oregon priorities." Rail Safety Demonstrates Common Ground with Organized Labor One example of the campaign's commitment to practical, bipartisan solutions is Fudge's support for legislation requiring two trained crew members aboard freight trains.
Senate Bill 723 would have established a two-person crew requirement in Oregon, subject to applicable exceptions.
Similar safety requirements have been enacted in several neighboring Western states.
The legislation was supported by rail workers concerned about the safety of train operations, emergency response, and the movement of hazardous materials through Oregon communities.
Despite these concerns, the measure failed to advance through the Oregon Legislature.
Fudge has publicly supported the legislation, arguing that having two trained crew members available provides additional safeguards for rail workers, first responders, and communities along Oregon's freight corridors.
"When something goes wrong on a train carrying hazardous materials, having a second trained crew member available can make a meaningful difference," Fudge said.
"This is about protecting the workers operating those trains and the communities they travel through.
It's exactly the kind of common-sense public safety legislation that deserves bipartisan support." Fudge has emphasized that his approach to public safety extends beyond traditional law enforcement to include transportation safety, emergency preparedness, and preventing avoidable tragedies.
Campaign Expands Outreach as Ballots Arrive With support from Oregon House Minority Leadership, the Fudge campaign has launched an expanded social media and digital communications effort, including professionally produced videos, issue-focused advertising, and direct voter outreach.
These efforts complement an ambitious grassroots operation reaching voters throughout West Salem, Independence, Monmouth, and surrounding communities.
Volunteers are distributing campaign materials, engaging residents at their doorsteps, and mobilizing for the final weeks of voting.
The campaign's distinctive brick-inspired "Building Awesome Together" imagery has also become a recognizable part of its community outreach.
Fudge has continued developing relationships with local businesses and community organizations, including joining the Monmouth-Independence Chamber of Commerce.
On Saturday, October 17, volunteers and supporters will gather for The Final Push for Fudge , a campaign organizing and canvassing event at Roth's Event Center in West Salem, from 9:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m.
"We're not taking a single vote for granted," Fudge said.
"While our digital outreach is reaching more voters than ever, nothing replaces a conversation on someone's doorstep.
We're working hard to earn the trust of voters throughout the district." A Clear Choice Following Recent Developments The final weeks of the election have also brought renewed attention to incumbent Representative Paul Evans, who was arrested October 4 following an incident in Independence and subsequently charged with driving under the influence of intoxicants, reckless driving, and failing to stop after causing property damage.
Evans has pleaded not guilty.
His attorney has stated that he entered a residential treatment program in Arizona, with an anticipated stay extending through Election Day.
His campaign has indicated he intends to remain in the race.
Fudge emphasized the importance of accountability while recognizing the seriousness of the circumstances.
"I sincerely hope Representative Evans receives the care and support he needs," Fudge said.
"But the residents of House District 20 also deserve a representative who is present, engaged, and prepared to serve.
This election is about the future of our communities, and voters deserve a serious conversation about leadership, judgment, and accountability." The campaign continues to emphasize Fudge's professional qualifications as a Certified Public Accountant and experienced state government budget manager, as well as his four central priorities: improving affordability, strengthening education, pursuing effective solutions to housing and homelessness, and protecting public safety.
Looking Toward November 3 With fewer than three weeks remaining before Election Day, the Fudge campaign is focused on its volunteer operation, voter outreach, and communicating its priorities to the residents of House District 20.
"Eight years working inside state government have taught me that good intentions aren't enough.
We need clear priorities, responsible spending, and leaders willing to measure results," Fudge said.
"Back in June, I promised we would build a broad coalition and take our case directly to voters.
We've delivered on that promise.
Now we're asking the people of House District 20 to help us take the next step." "It's time for a change in Salem.
And I'm ready to deliver accountability you can count on." About Andrew Fudge Andrew Fudge is a native Oregonian, husband, father, Certified Public Accountant, and state government budget professional with eight years of experience in public service.
He is the Republican and Independent Party of Oregon nominee for House District 20, representing West Salem, Independence, Monmouth, and surrounding communities.
For more information, volunteer opportunities, or campaign updates, visit www.fudge4oregon.org .
