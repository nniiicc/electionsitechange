RED ROCK, AZ – In case you missed it, JoAnna Mendoza joined Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes to meet with two mothers who lost their children to overdoses and discuss keeping Southern Arizona’s families safe from drug trafficking in our communities.
A retired Marine who served several tours in Iraq and Afghanistan keeping our country safe abroad, Mendoza has consistently emphasized the importance of securing the border here at home.
“As a mom, I think about Arizona kids’ safety every day.
In Congress, I’ll fight to stop the illegal flow of fentanyl and other illicit drugs across our southern border, while making sure Arizonans suffering from addiction can get the help they need,” Mendoza said.
Meeting ahead of Fentanyl Awareness Day, Mendoza and Mayes highlighted the importance of local addiction treatment facilities that are “going to be in a world of hurt” from Juan Ciscomani’s health care cuts.
Mendoza recounted a meeting with a rehab center that said the cuts are “devastating” to Arizonans struggling with addiction.

Tucson Sentinel: In Pima County, AG Mayes joins congressional hopeful Mendoza to talk about opioids
Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes and Democratic congressional candidate JoAnna Mendoza met Tuesday with two Arizona mothers who lost their children to fentanyl overdoses.
Theresa Guerrero’s son, Jacob Guerrero, died six years ago after using cocaine that was laced with the opioid drug.
Tuesday would have been his 38th birthday; his mother sat next to a photo of him labeled “Forever 31” and a bouquet of birthday balloons.
Angelique Tadeo’s daughter Tara Tadeo, a mother of four, likewise died in 2020 after she used cocaine that had been laced with fentanyl.
Tadeo said after she couldn’t reach Tara, she drove to her apartment, where she found her 36-year-old child dead.

[Mayes] told reporters that Mexican drug cartels were becoming more sophisticated, including using drones to smuggle drugs across the border.
Mayes said more than half the fentanyl seized in the United States over the last three years had been in Arizona, where authorities had seized what she called “a record” 27 million fentanyl pills.
“We do that normally in partnership with our federal partners like DEA, the FBI, the ATF,” Mayes said.
“Unfortunately, across multiple administrations, actually both Democratic and Republican, we have not seen the kind of DEA agents brought to Arizona that we need.
We need more federal prosecutors to join me here to combat the general crisis and to prevent these deaths.”
Mayes said she had recently visited a rehab center and “they said that the cuts to Medicaid are going to be absolutely devastating to folks who are on AHCCCS and are willingly going in and want rehabilitation and to get treatment for these things.”
Mendoza, a Democrat who is facing Republican incumbent Juan Ciscomani in what’s expected to be one of the tightest congressional races in the nation, reminded reporters that Ciscomani voted in favor of the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill that included cuts to Medicaid and other social services.
“That’s a result of the One Big Beautiful Bill that Rep.
Ciscomani voted for,” she said.
“These treatment facilities are going to be in a world of hurt.”
Ciscomani’s campaign consultant, Daniel Scarpinato, did not respond to an email asking for a comment.
