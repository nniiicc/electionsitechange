Oz Claim He Brought “$550 Billion” to Medicaid – He Actually Voted for $1 Trillion in Cuts May 10, 2026 FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE May 10, 2026 Contact : [email protected] The man appointed to oversee Medicaid cuts is now helping Ciscomani lie about them RED ROCK, AZ – Today, JoAnna Mendoza is calling out Rep.
Juan Ciscomani for downplaying his health care cuts alongside Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator Dr.
Mehmet Oz, who falsely claims in a video that Ciscomani “managed to get $550 billion more money for Medicaid over the next five years.” That claim is flatly false.
Ciscomani voted for the largest cuts to Medicaid in American history — roughly $1 trillion — and roughly $500 million in cuts to Medicare.
Let’s be clear about what Ciscomani actually did: He voted for sweeping Medicaid cuts expected to remove 300,000 Arizonans from their health care and cost Southern Arizona hospitals roughly $110 million every single year.
Arizona Mirror He did so after promising constituents he “ cannot and will not ” cut Medicaid and warning that the Big, Ugly Bill “ threatens access to coverage ” and “ jeopardizes the stability of our hospitals and providers. ” The so-called rural health “fix” Ciscomani now points to as cover failed Arizona.
Arizona received just $167 million — the sixth-lowest allocation in the country — even though it is the sixth-largest state by land area, with seven of its 15 counties classified as entirely rural.
Meanwhile, the Medicaid cuts Ciscomani voted for will cost Arizona more than a billion dollars annually once fully in effect.
And the damage extends beyond Medicaid and Medicare.
Approximately 70,000 fewer Arizonans enrolled in ACA coverage this year after Ciscomani let enhanced tax credits expire — despite Ciscomani repeatedly warning the expiration would cause “ massive ” consequences and “ real harm .” He then voted against extending them.
The uninsured rate in Arizona is now rising for the first time in years, erasing a decade of progress.
“Juan Ciscomani and Dr.
Oz can try to spin the truth but families in Southern Arizona are already feeling the consequences of Ciscomani’s votes.
Arizonan’s have watched their premiums rise, rural hospitals pushed to the brink, and hardworking families lose the healthcare they depend on.
He sold us out to protect tax breaks for billionaires and voters know it,” said JoAnna Mendoza .
Widely seen as one of the most vulnerable incumbents in Congress, Ciscomani has received nearly $1 million from national Republican Party fundraising committees — the same party bosses whose priorities he put ahead of Arizona’s when the votes were cast.
Juan Ciscomani voted to cut Medicaid.
He voted against the ACA.
He failed to deliver rural health funding.
And now he’s recruited the architect of those cuts to tell Arizona voters it never happened.
