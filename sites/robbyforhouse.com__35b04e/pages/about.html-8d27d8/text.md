Raised on Service
Robby Robbins is from here.
Reevesville, Walterboro, St.
George — these are not campaign backdrops.
They are the towns that raised him.
His parents, Doug and Nancy Robbins, taught him that you owe something to the place you live in.
His grandfathers showed him what that looks like in practice: Billy Moorer spent years as a magistrate, and Jack Robbins served as St.
George's police chief for decades.
Robby grew up watching both of them answer the phone when a neighbor needed something.
He earned his political science degree from the University of South Carolina in 1984 and his law degree there in 1987.
Plenty of classmates headed for firms in bigger cities.
Robby came home and opened a practice in St.
George with attorney Jim Bell, representing families and small businesses across the Lowcountry.
Then came the prosecutor's office, where he spent seven years trying cases — including capital cases — as Deputy Solicitor for Dorchester County and later for the entire First Judicial Circuit, managing the office's day-to-day operations.
In 2004, Governor Mark Sanford appointed him First Circuit Solicitor.
The transportation work started almost by accident and turned into a decade and a half: first the Dorchester County Sales Tax Transportation Authority, overseeing the local projects voters had taxed themselves to build, then the South Carolina Department of Transportation Commission, where his colleagues elected him chairman.
It is the reason he knows exactly how a road gets funded, designed, and finally built — and exactly why it sometimes takes too long.
He and his wife, Mary Bryce LaRoche Robbins of Johns Island, married in 1991 and raised two children, Bryce and Lena, both born in St.
George.
Robby practices law today as a partner at Thurmond, Kirchner & Timbes in Summerville.
