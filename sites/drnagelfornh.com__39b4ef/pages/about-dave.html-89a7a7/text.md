Home About Dave Priorities Accomplishments Upcoming Events/Get Involved Latest News The Missing Chapter Contact About Dave Who I Am In short, I am a husband, father, physician, advocate, author, legislator, and Ironman triathlete.
I have lived in New Hampshire with my wife and two sons for the past 37 years and Gilmanton for the past 31.
Having moved here from a small town in Upstate New York and having worked for the Indian Health Service in Sitka, Alaska, I had fully embraced “Live Free or Die” long before I got here.
What I love about New Hampshire is that it is so easy to get involved and make a difference.
That is what I have done my whole career.
I have always been inspired by my mother.
She was physically very disabled, but mentally very able.
She never let her limitations get in her way.
She had a finger and she had phone, and using the two, she could make a difference.
She was always there to help others in need, something I have come to call “kitchen table advocacy.” I have also been inspired by the lessons learned from my hometown of Orchard Park, New York.
Orchard Park began as a Quaker, Abolitionist farm town in the early 1800's.
I am proud that our town was one of the last stops on the Underground Railroad before the short trip to Canada, which may be why my childhood hero was Frederick Douglas.
Orchard Park welcomed new immigrants from Poland, Ireland, Italy, Germany, and other places after World War II, creating a diverse, true melting pot.
In that environment, I grew to embrace the values of diversity, inclusion, and opportunity guaranteed by our Declaration of Independence, our Federal Constitution, and our Statue of Liberty.
Armed with those lessons, I came to New Hampshire determined to make a difference.
In my medical practice of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, I have served the physically, mentally, and psychologically disabled, first serving as medical director of Easter Seals, then at Concord Orthopaedics where I have been a partner since 1990.
My focus at first was geriatric rehabilitation and brain injury.
I quickly came to see that pain was a part of all the disabilities I encountered, and I became aware of how we, as a culture, stigmatize those who suffer, adding unnecessarily to the burden these patients faced.
I also learned of the disparities that exist in how we treat those who suffer; people discriminated against not just because of their pain and their inability, but also because of their skin color, their sex, their sexual orientation.
I was determined to change the way pain and addiction are perceived, judged, and treated.
I am honored to have done that on local, state, regional, national, and international basis.
I learned that the world of the clinic was artificial.
95% of health care occurs outside a doctor's office.
I felt a need to get out into the community to make a difference.
In doing so, I created my own model of advocacy which I entitled the “John the Baptist Model,” a voice crying in the wilderness.
I learned that too often those in power did not want to hear what I said, but I never let that stop me.
We need to be listened to, and I tolerate nothing less, which is why I switched political parties earlier this year.
Republican leadership in the House was not listening to me and actively trying to silence me.
While that behavior did not exist beyond the House Chambers, it was effectively impairing my ability to do the job you elected me to do.
So, a change was necessary.

I have also endorsed the core belief of the Greatest Commandment: Love your neighbor as yourself .
I try very hard to put these into practice every day.
However, in my work as a physician and advocate, a Christian, and a legislator, I have also come to understand that as H.L.
Mencken said: ​ “For every complex human problem there is solution that is neat, simple, and wrong.” The complex social problems we face require complex solutions that can only be achieved through thoughtful discussion.
All opinions have value and should be respected and listened to.
Sometimes solutions come from surprising sources.
I have also come to understand that when some voices are not heard, the outcome of policy decisions can be disastrous…sometimes even deadly.
I am very proud that in all my advocacy and legislative work I have been able to create a bi-partisan, often non-partisan, multi-stakeholder groups to address and solve these complex problems in lasting, meaningful ways.
It is my hope that is what you want in a legislator.
If so, I would ask you vote for me.
I promise I will never let you down.
