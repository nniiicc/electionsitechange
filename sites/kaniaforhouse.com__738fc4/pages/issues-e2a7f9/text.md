As a candidate for Minnesota House District 3A, Aaron has spent his time meeting with voters and listening to their concerns.
This is his focus based on what he has heard: Focused on What Matters Issues & Priorities Transforming our Economy The foundation of the Northland's economy is our land.
I will support forestry, tourism, and the responsible use of minerals, while ensuring these industries never threaten our forests, waters, and wilderness.
I believe the Northland is uniquely positioned to grow new industries built on our abundant natural resources and our skilled workforce.
From green steel, e-waste processing, biomass, and other renewable energy, there's a real opportunity to build American industries, run by American companies, that keep wealth circulating locally.
Aaron's Plan Grow value-added markets for existing resources rather than shipping raw material out of state: green steel made from Iron Range ore and forest products beyond pulp — biomass from small-diameter or charred wood, and sustainable aviation fuel.
Invest in the infrastructure — broadband internet, health care, and education — along with business opportunities and outdoor recreation needed to attract retirees, remote workers, and new residents who grow the tax base and strengthen the local economy.
Fully staff the Central Iron Range Brownfield Coalition efforts — a partnership of public, private, and non-profit organizations funded through EPA revolving loans — to redevelop underused industrial sites.
Lowering Healthcare Costs and Improving Access I believe health care is a fundamental human right and Northland residents should have access to high-quality, affordable health care.
Rural hospitals are the backbone of our communities — they're among the largest employers, offering high-paying jobs that keep families rooted here.
Yet rural Minnesotans have struggled for decades to access the care they need.
Losing a hospital or clinic doesn't just mean losing care — it could mean losing a town.
Protecting rural health care is protecting rural Minnesota itself.
Aaron's Plan Push for a permanent cap on prescription drug prices (permanent extension of 340B) to limit the amount families pay out-of-pocket for needed medications each year.
Reclassify ambulances as medical services rather than transportation to align funding, staffing, and operational priorities with the clinical and public health roles emergency medical services (EMS) actually perform, and support consolidating rural EMS with local fire departments to unlock grant funding, create economies of scale, and fund full-time dedicated staff and advanced life support programs in the Northland.
Expand access to mental health care across rural communities through a combination of telehealth infrastructure, workforce incentives, integrated primary care, and community-based crisis networks.
Stopping Yearly Tax Increases For too long, the Northland has sent its wealth to the metro and out of state — our timber, our ore, our seasonal property taxes — while getting little back beyond grant applications we have to compete for.
We need to keep more of what we generate right here at home.
While the proposed constitutional amendment to increase the annual payout rate of the Minnesota Public School Fund (PSF) will provide critical budgetary relief across rural Minnesota, sparsely populated school districts still face higher per-student operational overhead costs compared to densely packed metro districts.
Additional funding is needed to cover these costs, without increasing taxes for local residents .
District 3A is a popular destination for recreational property owners, yet taxes generated by those seasonal homes flow straight into the state’s General Fund instead of staying in the district.
I believe more of those dollars should fund the infrastructure here in the Northland that full-time residents, second homeowners, and tourists all depend on.
Aaron's Plan Review the PSF per-pupil distribution system so more of the land and mineral revenue generated in northern Minnesota stays in the region.
Advocate for keeping seasonal property tax revenue within District 3A to fund local infrastructure, rather than routing it to the state general fund.
Push for Local Government Aid (LGA) and County Program Aid (CPA) to be adjusted for inflation to keep up with rising costs.
Without adjusting for inflation, this funding stays flat, forcing local property taxes to climb to cover the gap.
Fight to restructure Minnesota's road funding formula — gas tax, tab fees, vehicle sales tax — rather than continually increasing fees to cover the shortfall created as vehicles become more fuel-efficient and heavier.
Improving Access to Affordable Childcare I will support high quality, affordable childcare that allows children to learn and grow.
Minnesota has some of the most expensive childcare in the country.
I will work to ensure children can get the best start they deserve, and parents have the opportunity to pursue their careers by providing affordable, accessible childcare and early learning opportunities.
A young family sometimes can't take a job because childcare doesn't exist; state support for creating childcare programs and incentives can ease that bottleneck and help the broader economy grow.
Aaron's Plan Support private-sector efforts that offer or subsidize childcare — from dedicated space to direct wage support — to help businesses retain current employees and attract new ones.
Fight for low-cost or no-cost universal childcare for all Minnesota families with children ages zero to five, removing the biggest barrier keeping parents out of the workforce.
Raise childcare workers’ wages to ensure the field can attract and retain the qualified workforce Minnesota families depend on.
Establish new quality standards to raise program integrity statewide, so every family — regardless of zip code — can trust the care their child receives.
Expanding Affordable Housing Opportunities The deepest fix for housing is still jobs — the best way to attract residential development is local job growth.
I believe regulatory policy should work to keep housing available and affordable for everyone in northern Minnesota — not create barriers to it.
That means building and maintaining a wider variety of housing options to meet the needs of college students, seasonal workers, and full-time residents alike.
I support a statewide framework that sets clear standards while still allowing room for local creativity — giving communities the flexibility to implement solutions that fit their needs within consistent state-level guardrails that discourage discrimination and protect fair access to housing.
Aaron's Plan Streamline residential zoning processes to make development more efficient and less burdensome.
The University of Minnesota Extension Ely housing study confirms what residents already know: jobs go unfilled because there's nowhere to live, even as houses sit empty roughly 300 nights a year.
Support local caps and waiting lists on short-term rentals like Airbnb and VRBO, giving individual communities the authority to regulate and manage the conversion of homestead properties into short-term rentals.
Push to expand funding for Minnesota's existing Weatherization Assistance Program — one of the most cost-effective ways to lower housing costs in this climate, and it creates good union jobs.
Back Senator Klobuchar's housing plan, including making the State Housing Tax Credit permanent to incentivize private investment in housing and rental buildings, converting vacant commercial space into housing with renovation incentives, and removing burdensome rules that effectively ban high-quality manufactured housing.
Funding for Quality Education I am committed to maintaining Minnesota's reputation for high-quality education right here in the Northland.
That starts with keeping more of our local revenue, so our elementary and secondary schools are fully funded.
It also means valuing the people who make our schools work — dedicated teachers and educational professionals deserve living wages and real support, not just recognition.
While the proposed constitutional amendment to increase the annual payout rate of the Minnesota Public School Fund (PSF) will provide critical budgetary relief across Greater Minnesota, sparsely populated rural districts still face higher per-student operational overhead costs compared to densely packed metro districts.
Aaron's Plan Support the November 2026 ballot question to increase the Minnesota Permanent School Fund (PSF) distribution rate from an annual payout from 2.5% of the fund’s value to 4.5% of the fund’s three-year rolling average net value.
Review the PSF per pupil distribution system so more of the land and mineral revenue generated in northern Minnesota actually stays in the region.
Advocate for expanding scholarship and pathway programs that keep students engaged with opportunities in the Northland, rather than drawing them exclusively toward the Twin Cities.
Pushing Back Against Divisive Partisan Politics We need a bi-partisan representative in the Capitol.
I have decades of experience bringing people together across parties and communities to fight natural disasters, keep residents safe, and deliver real results.
This election is about trust.
The voters have lost trust in their elected officials.
They want to know that a candidate will follow through with promises and work hard for them in an open and transparent way.
Every day I am showing that I am willing to do the hard work.
My record of building trust is well known.
People respect that they can talk with me and know that I am listening.
Aaron's Plan Lead as someone who listens — entering every conversation, including with those who disagree, willing to be persuaded and to say so.
Work within Minnesota's Legislature in a bi-partisan manner.
Weighing the merits of legislation based on how they would help or hurt District 3A.
Support efforts such as the Minnesota Industrial Transformation Initiative (MITI).
Learn more about MITI .
Draw on my proven record of bringing opposing sides together — efforts such as uniting trail groups in Tower to unlock grant funding, coordinating federal, state, county, and volunteer responders during the Greenwood Fire, and successful transparent environmental review of mineral exploration permits.
Improving Tribal Consultation to Reinforce Tribal Rights I have direct experience with tribal consultation processes and a deep respect for treaty rights.
I understand these aren't just legal formalities — they're federal obligations that Minnesota has a responsibility to honor fully, not just symbolically.
Too often in the past, "consultation" meant checking a box rather than genuinely inviting tribal nations into the decision-making process.
In my role as District Ranger, I worked on a Memorandum of Understanding with the Bands of the Lake Superior Chippewa regarding the activities taking place on the Superior National Forest.
This is what real engagement looks like — an ongoing relationship built through monthly meetings with tribal specialists and quarterly meetings with tribal leadership across a range of issues.
Aaron's Plan Ensure treaty obligations are fully respected and honored across all state and federal activities, including mining, forestry, fishing, hunting and foraging rights.
Strengthen cooperation between tribal and non-tribal communities by expanding shared use of services such as tribal Head Start programs, health clinics, and commercial spaces.
Require meaningful tribal involvement alongside public input in all environmental reviews of mineral exploration and development permits, not just a formality, to build the trust that allows projects to move forward and hold up over time.
Responsible Mining and Mineral Extraction Our elected officials need to look beyond taconite and towards the future.
Supporting mining does not mean just defending mining.
We need to have leaders with vision to bring people together to solve the problems that will unlock our wealth.
Politicians have been pro-mining for a hundred years, during that time we have lost tens of thousands of jobs.
We need leaders who are looking out for workers.
Let’s actually make more jobs!!
Since we are natural resource based, and I have a long history in the field of natural resources, this would be a focus.
Forestry, responsible minerals, and tourism are fields in which I’m comfortable as a leader.
This will require bringing people together to support industries that are compatible with our values and workforce and find ways to retain our wealth and jobs in Minnesota.
Previously, I have worked in Washington, D.C. for the Bureau of Land Management (BLM), which is the agency that oversees minerals under the Federal Land Policy and Management Act of 1976 to balance resource extraction with environmental protection.
I worked at the highest levels of many mining projects across the country including copper nickel in Minnesota.
As the District Ranger for the Kawishiwi District of the Superior National Forest, I supervised the environmental review of numerous permits for mineral exploration and development.
I approved permits for helium and copper nickel exploration for several companies, including Twin Metals, NewRange and Pulsar Helium.
I know that in order to move forward public trust must be the top concern.
This requires a transparent process, with public and tribal involvement, and flexibility on the part of industry.
This is a policy area in which I'm well-versed and exceedingly capable.
The residents of District 3A and the entire state of Minnesota need leaders who can speak with all interested parties, listen to what they have to say, and bring them together in agreement to move the right projects forward in a sustainable way.
Aaron's Plan Protect the Boundary Waters as a non-negotiable priority, while fighting to bring back permanent, year-round jobs for laid-off miners — not just seasonal work — and holding mining companies to high standards instead of letting them cut corners.
Reform the permit process to make it transparent and efficient, so projects can move forward with public confidence rather than getting stalled or challenged after the fact.
Work with all interested parties to craft protections that make sense and hold up over time.
Ensure any legislation expanding protections in the Rainy River watershed is limited in scope, includes tribal consultation, holds the federal government accountable, sets specific metrics, and includes a massive economic development package to offset losses.
Build a broad and lasting coalition for real investment, rather than relying on single-vote resolutions or executive orders that can be reversed with the next election, even if a transparent, inclusive process takes longer to build.
Reducing Fraud Fraud at all levels of government is unacceptable — period.
I spent twenty-three years as a police officer arresting people who thought they were above the law, and I'll bring that same experience and resolve to the State House.
When elected, I will work to protect taxpayer dollars, hold fraudsters accountable, and put responsible oversight systems in place that stop fraud before it happens, rather than chasing it after the damage is done.
Together, we can eliminate fraud in the first place.
Aaron's Plan Build fraud prevention into government-funded programs from day one — conducting background checks and site visits before any money goes out, and using modern technology to automatically catch duplicate or suspicious billing.
Hold managed care organizations and third-party administrators of government programs strictly accountable to state and federal rules and regulations, with real consequences for violations.
Ensure state agencies are properly staffed and government workers are well trained, so taxpayer-funded programs are effectively delivered and rigorously monitored from the start.
Strengthen whistleblower protections and reporting channels so employees and citizens can safely report suspected fraud without fear of retaliation.
Ensure the new independent Office of Inspector General is properly funded and adequately staffed with real subpoena power.
Guard against overreactions such as pausing entire programs like childcare assistance or SNAP due to fraud discovered elsewhere.
This ends up punishing families and children who did nothing wrong.
Promoting Legal Immigration Immigration has become a hot topic in Minnesota as a result of the Department of Homeland Security’s large-scale immigration enforcement campaign, Operation Metro Surge, in late 2025 and early 2026.
I have voiced my concern about federal overreach during these recent events and believe that constitutional rights must be upheld for everyone in the United States.
District 3A shares an international border with Canada.
Our communities have a direct stake in ensuring our borders are secure and properly protected.
At the same time, our hospitality, caregiving, and construction industries depend on immigrant and foreign workers.
The Northland's history and prosperity were built on the backs of immigrant labor.
I believe we can secure our borders and uphold the rule of law while still honoring the vital contributions immigrants have made — and continue to make — to our communities and our economy.
Aaron's Plan Ensure Minnesota’s state and local resources — data systems, jails, and officers — are reserved for enforcing Minnesota law.
Support continued cooperation between local law enforcement and federal partners to remove violent offenders and traffickers from communities.
Push back against federal overreach that infringes on local and state authority, while working with federal partners on legitimate border security needs.
Require independent investigation and accountability when any officer — regardless of agency — violates a person's constitutional rights, while rejecting the stereotyping of entire communities over the actions of a few.
