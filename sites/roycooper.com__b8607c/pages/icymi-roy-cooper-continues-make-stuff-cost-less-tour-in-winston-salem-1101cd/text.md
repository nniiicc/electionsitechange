Senate to make life more affordable for North Carolina families.
WFMY: Roy Cooper Encourages North Carolinians to Vote During “Make Stuff Cost Less” Event in Winston-Salem Roy Cooper: “I’m encouraging people to make sure you go out and vote.
Vote all the way up and down the ballot.
This is not a race to sit out because there is too much at stake .” Winston-Salem Journal: Leading in polls, Roy Cooper tells supporters Senate race is far from over Richard Craver Cooper relied on his characteristic homespun humor mixed with his economic and healthcare campaign priorities, and how they represent the opposite of Whatley’s agenda.
Cooper said he chose to run for the U.S.
Senate to be part “of fixing what’s wrong in Washington.” “As hardworking families in Winston-Salem and around the state pay more at the gas pump and on their energy bills, Washington, D.C., insider Michael Whatley profits off his oil and utility stocks,” Cooper said.
Cooper said he plans to bring “North Carolina commonsense” to Congress and welcomes the opportunity to work with Republican counterparts in what would be the final two years of the second Trump administration.
“I am running to make stuff cost less and will work with anyone in Washington to deliver for North Carolina families.” “This is not a ballot to sit out,” Cooper said.
“There’s too much at stake.” WXII: Roy Cooper Continues “Make Stuff Cost Less” Tour in Winston-Salem Reporter: “Today, Democratic candidate for U.S.
Senate Roy Cooper was in Winston-Salem as part of his “Make Stuff Cost Less” tour, his focus on this campaign, as the name suggests, is to make life more affordable for families in North Carolina.” Roy Cooper: “We’ve seen a lot of times where Democrats have been ahead in October polling, only to lose in November.” Elon News Network: Roy Cooper discusses rising costs, key priorities at “Make Stuff Cost Less” event Miles Hayford Cooper discussed his recent conversations with supporters and the issues they are facing.
He said many people are facing rising costs and are now struggling to buy a home.
At the event, Cooper advocated for public education and criticized federal cuts to the Department of Education.
Some of Cooper’s biggest points of emphasis included his desire to roll back tariffs, end the war in Iran and restore healthcare subsidies. “[Michael Whatley] has worked to pass legislation that allows them to raise prices and raise rates,” Cooper said in his speech.
“As he cheers on the war in Iran, the oil stock goes up.
And guess what?
He still owns the stock.
So that’s money out of people’s pockets and into his.” “I’ve had a Republican legislature for eight years when I was governor, and yes, we disagreed on things, and yes, I vetoed legislation, but we worked together in many ways,” Cooper told Elon News Network.
“I think I got some receipts that I can bring to Washington to show that you can achieve consensus, and that’s what people want.
People just want government to work.” WFDD: Democratic Senate nominee Roy Cooper visits Winston-Salem Rachel Moody “I’m talking with people who are in the middle class and who are barely hanging on,” Cooper said.
“They need someone who will represent them, not the billionaires, not the big corporations that are already doing okay.
Represent everyday people who are being left behind by this broken, chaotic Washington.” For Cooper, bringing prices down includes ending the war in Iran, which has raised the cost of gas and fertilizer.
