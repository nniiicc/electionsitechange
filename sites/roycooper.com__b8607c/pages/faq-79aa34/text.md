Did Roy Cooper really release 3,500 criminals?
No, Roy Cooper did not release 3,500 prisoners.
A court ordered during COVID that the state reduce the prison population due to prison conditions, with advocates asking for roughly 18,000 prisoners to be released ( The Assembly, July 2026 ; FactCheck.org, August 2026 ).
After Gov.
Cooper’s administration fought against this in court, advocates filed a lawsuit and law enforcement and parole officials made decisions on how to carry out the court order to release prisoners using similar criteria that the Trump administration used in the release of about 13,000 federal prisoners during COVID ( N&O, April 2026 ; PoliticsNC, August 2026 ).
The advocates appeared headed to victory after a judge ruled that they were likely to win if the case went to trial, leading to a settlement instead ( WRAL, April 2026 ) to avoid potentially 18,000 people being released .
Roy’s opponent Michael Whatley actually attacked Roy in 2020 for resisting the prisoner release, saying Roy was not doing enough to protect prisoners during COVID ( NCGOP Communications YouTube, July 2020; WSOC-TV, July 2026 ), and now attacks Roy for the opposite.

What’s true?
Senate Leadership Fund (or SLF), the official Republican super PAC for Senate races and Michael Whatley’s largest supporters, put out two advertisements that knowingly misled voters.
The first ad featured Ronald Hayes, a gun violence prevention advocate whose son was tragically murdered.
The ad attempted to connect the offender to Roy Cooper, but fact checks showed that the killer had no prior record and no connection to Cooper ( The NC Beat, August 2026 ).
After the ad aired, Hayes told press that he felt “swindled” and “deceived” by the group, who told him he was participating in a gun violence prevention ad, and that he was a supporter of Cooper ( Mediaite, August 2026 ; NC Newsline, August 2026 ; WRAL, September 2026 ).
The second ad featured the father of Logan Federico, who was tragically murdered in South Carolina by a South Carolina criminal.
SLF admitted to the News & Observer that this murder had nothing to do with Cooper, saying “[i]t is not specifically that Cooper did it” ( N&O, September 2026 ).

Charlotte Observer, August 2026 American Journal News, April 2026 Charlotte Observer, April 2026 Did Roy Cooper really let 16,000 rape kits sit on the shelf untested?
No, Roy Cooper cleared the state’s rape kit backlog and did not let 16,000 rape kits sit on the shelf.
It’s actually impossible for this statistic to be correct.
The 16,000 rape kits this question refers to was a tally of kits sitting in local law enforcement offices across the state that were discovered over a year after Roy had left the Attorney General’s office.
No state office had knowledge of where they all were, possession or control of them and no statewide count of all untested rape kits in the possession of local law enforcement even existed until after Roy was elected Governor.
As Attorney General, Roy’s crime lab discovered and cleared the “no-suspect” rape kit backlog which were kits with no suspects that needed to be tested against the State Crime Lab’s DNA database, testing over 6,000 rape kits that were “collecting dust” in the state’s possession ( Greensboro News & Record, April 2005 ; WFMY, April 2005 ).
After Roy was elected Governor, new state law required local law enforcement to undertake an inventory of untested rape kits and report them to the state, though the count did not give the state possession or control over any of the kits ( WUNC, June 2017 ).
After the 16,000 additional rape kits were discovered, Roy signed a bill clearing the backlog and requiring ALL untested rape kits be turned over to a lab for testing ( WFMY, September 2019 ).
Governor Cooper left the Attorney General office before anyone, including him, could have known how many rape kits were sitting untested in local law enforcement agencies statewide.
He could not have “let” a number “sit on the shelf” that would not exist for another thirteen months.
The math makes this claim impossible.
The claim is false.
