As a proud graduate of a Maine public school, the mom of two children attending a small rural public school, and a former school board chair, I have seen both the promise and the challenges facing Maine’s schools up close.
I’ve also chaired my local school board and that experience reinforced something I carry everywhere: Maine people care deeply about their schools.
Parents and educators across Maine want the same thing — to give every child the opportunity to succeed.
As a parent, I’ve asked the same questions families ask every day.
Is my child learning what they need to succeed?
Will they be prepared for the future?
How do we help students who are struggling?
And how do we prepare our children for a world being transformed by technology and artificial intelligence?
Every child deserves a great public education, regardless of their ZIP code.
Every community deserves strong schools.
And every educator deserves the support and respect necessary to help students succeed.
That is why strengthening Maine’s public schools will be one of my highest priorities as Governor.
Our goal should be simple and ambitious: make Maine a national leader in educational excellence.
We should strive to be among the top states in literacy, mathematics, critical thinking, workforce readiness, and student engagement.
Strong schools require great teachers, educational technicians, bus drivers, school nurses, counselors, and support staff.
Maine must adequately fund public education — and do so in a way that stops forcing communities to choose between school quality and rising property taxes.
State funding and local tax relief should be complementary, not competing, priorities.
At the same time, we must prepare students for the world they will inherit.
Maine graduates roughly 12,000 students each year.
Every one of them should leave school ready for success—whether that means college, an apprenticeship, career training, or entering the workforce.
That requires a renewed focus on what matters most: strong reading and math instruction, hands-on learning, career and technical education, critical thinking, problem solving, and real-world skills.
It also requires confronting a challenge unlike any previous generation has faced.
Our children are growing up in a world of constant digital distraction and rapidly advancing artificial intelligence.
Technology can be a powerful tool, but it should never replace deep learning, human relationships, skilled teaching, or independent thinking.
Maine should lead the nation in developing a human-centered approach to education in the age of AI.
We should limit unnecessary classroom distractions, evaluate the impact of excessive screen use, teach students how to use AI responsibly, protect student privacy, and ensure technology serves learning—not the other way around.
And as we navigate these changes, teachers must have a seat at the table.
The educators working with students every day should help guide how technology and artificial intelligence are used in our classrooms, ensuring these tools support learning rather than dictate it.
Most importantly, we must keep students engaged.
When students are healthy, supported, challenged, and connected to their schools, they succeed.
That’s why we will continue expanding access to school-based health services, strengthen mental health supports, and create more opportunities for experiential and hands-on learning, career exploration, and meaningful community engagement.
Together, we can build an education system that supports teachers, respects taxpayers, strengthens communities, and prepares every Maine child for a bright future in a changing world.
The future of Maine is sitting in our classrooms right now.
Our job is to make sure they’re ready for the future—and that Maine is ready for them.
Supporting Educators at the Core of Student Success
Supporting teachers and school staff is essential to student success.
Today’s teachers are educating, mentoring, and supporting students’ mental health—often without the resources they need—while also facing an affordability crisis that makes it harder to live in the communities they serve.
We must implement policies to recruit and retain public school teachers and address the growing teacher shortage.
- Expedite the increase in teacher pay to a minimum starting salary of $50,000
- Support more young people to enter the field by helping students interested in teaching to navigate the teaching certification process and boost tuition assistance and loan repayment opportunities.
Create more opportunities for students and aspiring teachers to gain meaningful classroom experience and exposure through registered teacher apprenticeships, substitute teaching opportunities, internships, and other hands-on opportunities.
- Identify opportunities to accelerate processes for trained professionals to become teachers.
Continue to simplify and accelerate the process for school ed techs and long term substitutes, with bachelor’s degrees, to become credentialed teachers.
Provide an accelerated pathway for professionals with degrees in math, science, foreign language and other areas of high needs to become certified teachers.
- Increase support for younger teachers to stay in the field through targeted professional development and mentoring from experienced teachers, helping them build skills, confidence, and long-term commitment to the profession and expand high-quality, relevant professional development that reflects the realities educators are facing and supports their growth over time.
- Ensure collective bargaining rights by protecting and expanding the rights educators and school staff to collectively bargain.
- Implement policies that will help teachers to live and work in Maine: Along with improving pay for teachers, I am committed to lowering costs for teachers and all Mainers, increasing access to affordable child care, lowering energy prices, and building more housing that’s affordable so teachers can live in the communities where they work.
A Strong Start for Every Child
Quality early childhood education is where we begin building the skills that determine whether a child becomes a confident reader, a strong problem-solver, and a lifelong learner.
That means intentionally strengthening early literacy and numeracy from the start—grounded in the science of reading, rich language development, early math exposure, and a strong culture of reading and curiosity in every early learning setting.
Together, my affordability and education plans will ensure every Maine family has access to dependable, affordable, high-quality child care that prepares children for success in kindergarten and beyond.
That means expanding access to affordable child care, strengthening high-quality early learning programs across Maine, and supporting the early childhood workforce with the training, stability, and compensation they need to help children thrive—especially in early reading, language, and math development.
By investing early, we ensure children enter school ready to learn, ready to read, and ready to succeed.
We will:
- Implement universal public Pre-K: Make free, full-day Pre-K available to every 4-year-old in Maine whose family wants it, delivered through school districts, Head Start programs, child care centers, and family child care providers.
- Strengthen early identification and early intervention services for young children to ensure that all children are prepared to succeed when they enter kindergarten.
- Ensure child care is affordable for working families: Expand the Child Care Affordability Program to eliminate waitlists and increase access for income-eligible families, with a focus on infant and toddler care.
- Strengthen the early childhood workforce: Sustain and expand wage supports, apprenticeships, and training pathways to recruit and retain early childhood educators.
- Expand access to high-quality care: Support providers in expanding capacity, including infant and toddler care and extended-hour options for working families.
Provide incentives to employers to support quality childcare for workers.
- Invest in professional development: Ensure early childhood educators have ongoing training grounded in evidence-based early literacy, numeracy, and child development practices.
Accelerating Literacy and Math Outcomes
Early proficiency in reading and math is one of the strongest predictors of long-term student success.
Maine has seen declines in both literacy and numeracy, and while progress has been made, too many students are still not where they need to be.
As Governor, I will work in partnership with educators, school leaders, and communities to accelerate academic recovery and ensure every child has a strong foundation in core skills.
We know what works, but we need to ensure every teacher and student has access to the interventions that work.
That means sustained investment in high-quality professional development, instructional coaching, and collaboration time for teachers—designed with educators and grounded in what works in the classroom.
We will also strengthen the use of meaningful student data so schools can identify where students are falling behind, understand what is working, and continuously improve instruction.
This is not about more bureaucracy—it is about giving educators the best tools to support their students.
To strengthen literacy, math, and a culture of learning, we will support educators and schools to:
- Implement the science of reading and strong early math instruction across early grades: Ensure Pre-K through 3rd grade instruction is grounded in evidence-based literacy practices—including phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension—alongside strong foundational math instruction focused on number sense, problem-solving, and conceptual understanding.
- Build a strong culture of reading and learning in every elementary school: Expand daily reading time, classroom libraries, read-alouds, and family literacy engagement so every child is immersed in language-rich environments that build both skills and a lifelong love of reading.
- Strengthen early identification and targeted intervention: Expand screening systems and evidence-based supports so struggling readers and students behind in math are identified early and receive timely, targeted help before they fall further behind.
- Support teachers with high-quality training and instructional coaching: Invest in ongoing professional development, collaboration time, and coaching focused on literacy, math, and effective instructional practice grounded in research.
- Align teacher preparation with evidence-based instruction: Ensure state university teacher training programs reflect current research in cognitive development, language acquisition, mathematical reasoning, and effective classroom practice.
- Expand high-quality instructional supports and interventions: Strengthen access to tutoring, after-school programming, and targeted supports for English language learners and other students who need additional academic support.
Expand Experiential and Work-Based Learning to Ensure Every Student Has Path to Their Future
I will ensure that every young Mainer has access by age 20 to a meaningful, hands-on work experience—whether through an internship, paid job placement, for-credit work experience, pre-apprenticeship, apprenticeship, or similar opportunity.
No young person should reach adulthood without a clear pathway into the workforce and a real sense of direction for their future.
These experiences matter beyond job skills — they keep students engaged, especially in middle and high school when futures start taking shape.
When learning feels relevant, students stay motivated and finish school with a plan.
We will:
- Expand paid internships and work-based learning in partnership with Maine employers, unions, nonprofits, and higher education institutions so students gain real-world experience before graduation.
- Strengthen and expand Maine service and opportunity programs that place young people in meaningful roles serving their communities, including in local government, education, health care, housing, and heritage industries.
- Expand and modernize career and technical education (CTE) starting in middle school, giving students early exposure to hands-on learning, career pathways, and real-world skills that connect directly to high school programs, apprenticeships, and future employment.
- Ensure middle and high school students have structured opportunities to explore careers early and often, so every student graduates with a clear understanding of their options—whether college, training, military service, or entering the workforce.
Strengthen Special Education Services
As Governor, I will work to build a stronger, more responsive special education system—one that supports educators, partners closely with families, and ensures students receive the services they deserve.
By investing in students, educators, and early support systems, we can create schools where every child—regardless of background, ability, or language—has the opportunity to learn, grow, and succeed.
- Maintain state funding for the transition of early childhood special education services into our public schools.
By expanding access to early identification and intervention services, we can better prepare young children with special needs for success in the classroom and address learning challenges at the earliest stages, when support can have the greatest long-term impact.
- Support schools to meet the needs of all children through multi-tiered systems of support by championing a strong, evidence-based system that ensures every student receives the academic, behavioral, and individualized assistance they need to succeed.
By strengthening early intervention, special education services, and personalized learning supports, Maine schools can better meet students where they are and help every child reach their full potential.
We will ensure that these supports are built into the school funding formula and accessible to all Maine schools.
- Strengthen and support Maine’s special education workforce through a comprehensive recruitment and retention strategy.
That includes expanding tuition assistance and student loan forgiveness programs for special education teachers, and building stronger workforce pipelines for critical related service providers, including occupational therapists, physical therapists, and speech-language pathologists.
Support Student Mental Health
As the mother of two teenagers, I see firsthand the pressures young people are facing today and those challenges are clearly reflected in the data.
Over the past two decades, the Maine Integrated Youth Health Survey has shown troubling trends in student mental health, engagement, and overall well-being.
Too many young people are struggling with anxiety, depression, loneliness, and disconnection, and schools are often where these challenges first emerge and where support is most urgently needed.
These issues affect not only students’ emotional well-being, but also their ability to learn, build healthy relationships, and succeed over the long term.
As Governor, I will invest in programs and supports that strengthen student mental health while also improving students’ sense of belonging, connection, and engagement in school.
That includes expanding access to school-based mental health services, strengthening partnerships with community providers, increasing opportunities for students to connect through extracurricular activities and mentorship programs, and ensuring educators have the training and tools to recognize and respond to students in need.
We will:
- Increase the number of school-based mental health professionals to reduce student-to-counselor ratios, strengthen trusted adult relationships, and ensure timely support for students in need.
- Expand school-based health centers that provide integrated physical and mental health services during the school day, reducing barriers related to transportation, cost, and missed work for parents.
- Increase access to telehealth services in rural schools to connect students with mental health care where local providers are limited or unavailable.
- Invest in after-school and youth development programs that provide academic support and build strong connections with caring adults and peers, which research shows can significantly improve student outcomes and aspirations.
This includes combatting pay-to-play trends in middle and high school sports, ensuring every student who wants to play a sport or learn to play music has an opportunity, regardless of income.
Access to Training and Post-Secondary Education
Post-secondary education should be within reach for every graduating Maine student—whether they choose to pursue the trades, technical training, or a four-year college degree.
Maine’s free community college program has been a game changer for students who might not otherwise have continued their education beyond high school.
It expands access to high-quality workforce training in the trades and provides a more affordable pathway to a bachelor’s degree.
- Ensure Maine’s free Community College program continues and work to build a pathway to extend similar tuition-free access for the first two years of Maine’s public university system for middle- and low-income students.
- Strengthen student loan repayment and retention incentives for graduates who choose to live and work in Maine, helping reduce debt while supporting long-term career opportunities here at home.
- Increase investment in the Maine State Grant Program to make higher education more affordable and accessible, helping more Maine students enroll in and complete college without taking on unmanageable debt.
Invest in School Infrastructure: Strong Schools, Healthy Buildings
You cannot deliver a great education in buildings that are cold, leaking, or unsafe.
Students deserve to learn in safe, modern, energy-efficient, and fully accessible schools.
Right now, Maine’s school construction system is not working as it should.
Each year, only a small number of schools receive funding for new construction or major renovation, while many others—often in smaller and rural communities—are left behind because of formula limitations.
The result is a system that is uneven, reactive, and too often leaves communities waiting far too long for needed repairs.
- Develop a statewide school infrastructure plan to assess and address construction and renovation needs across Maine, ensuring every school is safe, sound, and capable of supporting high-quality learning.
- Pass a $100 million revolving loan fund, supported by a bond, for school construction and critical repairs, with a focus on helping rural and smaller districts address urgent infrastructure needs.
This fund will allow schools to fix roofs, upgrade heating systems, improve insulation and air quality, remove mold, and modernize aging facilities—preventing small problems from becoming major capital crises.
- Expand and strengthen Maine’s Green Schools program to support energy efficiency, weatherization, and clean energy upgrades that lower operating costs while improving the health and sustainability of school buildings.
By investing strategically in school facilities, we can ensure that every Maine child learns in a safe, healthy, and modern environment and that communities are not left waiting years for the support their schools need.
Engaging Our Kids: Balanced Technology Use and AI Readiness in Schools
We must take a responsible, balanced approach to technology in our classrooms—one that protects student focus, strengthens learning, and prepares students for a rapidly changing digital world.
The implementation of Maine’s bell-to-bell cell phone policy is an important step toward reducing distraction and improving engagement.
But it cannot be the end of the conversation.
We also need a broader strategy for how technology is used in instruction, how it supports learning, and how we ensure it does not replace deep thinking, creativity, and human connection.
Students need strong foundations in reading, writing, math, critical thinking, and problem-solving.
They must also be prepared to navigate a world shaped by artificial intelligence and digital tools.
That means teaching students not just how to use technology, but how to question it, understand it, and use it responsibly.
As Governor, I will ensure Maine leads with a thoughtful, educator-driven approach to classroom technology and AI.
- Establish a Teacher-Led Technology and AI Task Force: Bring together Maine educators, school leaders, parents, pediatric experts, and child development specialists to guide state policy on classroom technology.
This group will help distinguish between high-quality instructional tools and low-value screen time, ensuring policy reflects real classroom experience—not just technology trends.
- Review and reduce unnecessary screen exposure in early school grades: Conduct a statewide review of screen use in Pre-K through 5th grade, with clear guidance that prioritizes face-to-face instruction, hands-on learning, and early literacy development over passive or excessive device use and develop balanced standards for use in Maine’s middle schools.
- Strengthen digital literacy and AI education for older students: Expand instruction in digital citizenship, computer science, and AI literacy so students understand how these systems work, including issues of bias, privacy, and responsible use.
The goal is not passive consumption, but informed, critical engagement.
- Protect student data and academic integrity: Establish clear statewide standards for the use of AI and digital tools in schools, including safeguards for student privacy and guidance to ensure that generative AI supports learning without replacing authentic writing, problem-solving, and critical thinking.
- Rebalance technology with hands-on learning: Invest in instructional materials, textbooks, project-based learning, and experiential education so that technology is one tool among many—not the center of the classroom experience.
- Prepare students for the AI-driven economy: Work with higher education and industry partners to ensure students are developing both technical fluency and durable human skills—creativity, communication, collaboration, ethical reasoning, and applied problem-solving—that will remain essential in every field.
