MAINE — According to a new survey from the Maine Department of Energy Resources, heating fuel costs are climbing sharply heading into winter.
The agency’s weekly fuel survey found the statewide average price of heating oil hit $5.77 per gallon on September 14 — a 74% jump from a year earlier — with kerosene averaging $6.57 per gallon.
Filling a standard 275-gallon tank now costs nearly $675 more than it did last year.
STATEMENT FROM HANNAH PINGREE:
“Maine is the most heating oil reliant state in the country and we know our homes are often old and leaky.
With heating oil nearly $6 a gallon on average, many Mainers are worried about how they’ll afford to stay warm this winter.
“I am urging Congress and the Trump Administration to double Maine’s LIHEAP allocation to meet this unprecedented need.
Doubling our LIHEAP funding will ensure our programs can keep pace.
“As governor, I’ll also seek to provide additional urgent relief so folks can stay safe and warm in their homes this winter with energy relief support.
I’ll also fight to lower energy costs, and do more to help Mainers tighten up their houses with weatherization programs and investments in efficient technologies like heat pumps to help save money and keep people warm in their homes.
“Providing relief this winter will be urgent and with this war continuing to push prices, the state and federal government needs to step up.
Mainers never give up on their neighbors and they show up by supporting wood banks and fuel funds.
As governor, I will do the same.”
