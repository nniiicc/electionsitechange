Standing with the Miles Delivers Action Coalition, Democratic nominee for Governor lays out plan to protect rural maternity care in Maine
DAMARISCOTTA, MAINE — Hannah Pingree, Democratic nominee for Governor of Maine, was joined today by members of the Miles Delivers Action Coalition (MDAC) to call on the MaineHealth Board to keep labor and delivery services open at Lincoln Hospital’s Miles Campus, and to announce her call for a statewide moratorium on labor and delivery closures until the next governor takes office.
“I had my own kids in rural Maine.
I know what it means to count on a hospital being there when you need it, and I know what a community loses when it isn’t,” said Hannah.
“This isn’t a hard call.
MaineHealth should keep Lincoln’s doors open.
This has to be about the health of our moms and babies and providing the basic services families need to stay in our communities.
We cannot keep on losing these services with no plan for how to keep these deliveries safe.
Statewide, we need to hit pause for a year until the next governor takes office and has time to act.
We cannot lose one more labor and delivery unit in the interim.”
The event came as the MaineHealth Board hosts a series of votes — yesterday July 23, July 30, and August 6 — that will determine the future of labor and delivery services at Lincoln Hospital.
Twelve Maine hospitals have closed their labor and delivery units since 2009, nine of them in just the past few years, leaving rural families driving 45 minutes on average to reach a hospital that still delivers babies, the longest such drive time in New England.
“What started as a handful of neighbors asking questions became a movement,” said Rosie Richards, Miles Delivers Action Coalition.
“More than 10,000 people have interacted and stood with us.
MaineHealth clinicians have asked for a pause.
MaineHealth donors have asked for a pause.
Elected officials, community leaders, and families from across the state have joined this effort because they believe we have a responsibility to do everything we can before Maine loses another maternity unit.” A full statement from the MDAC can be found below.
Hannah outlined a set of concrete steps she will take as governor to strengthen rural maternity care across Maine:
- A statewide moratorium on labor and delivery closures for a full year, giving the state time to put real alternatives in place before another community loses its birthing unit.
- Pressure on the federal government to lift the cap on the Rural Health Transformation Fund, which currently limits direct payments to hospitals and providers to just 15% of the funds each state receives, and to direct new flexibility toward keeping rural maternity units staffed.
- A maternity care summit, convened by the Governor’s office, bringing together hospital and health system leadership, OB-GYNs, family medicine providers, midwives, emergency medicine physicians, state officials, legislators, and the public to reach agreement on solutions — including alternative models of care, staff recruitment, and financial support for units that remain open.
These commitments build on Hannah’s broader Health Care Plan for Maine, which calls for a public health insurance option, expanded access to primary, maternity, and reproductive care, and stronger investment in the basic needs — like housing, food security, and public health — that keep Maine communities healthy.
Hannah Pingree’s campaign for governor has record-breaking fundraising, innovative policy announcements, packed events and Town Halls across Maine, and countless endorsements— alongside three public polls showing her with a commanding lead heading into November.
These milestones highlighted the growing excitement behind her candidacy and her vision to make Maine more affordable, expand opportunities for families, and deliver results that strengthen communities across the state.

FULL STATEMENT FROM THE MILES DELIVERS ACTION COALITION:
Three months ago, none of us imagined we’d be here.
We weren’t looking for a fight.
We were simply parents, patients, healthcare workers, business owners, and neighbors trying to understand why our community was about to lose something so fundamental.
MaineHealth has proposed closing Labor and Delivery at Lincoln Hospital’s Miles Campus this December.
If that happens, Lincoln County will lose hospital-based labor and delivery care, and Miles will become the fifth maternity unit to close in Maine in just two years.
Like many closures before it, we’ve been told this is mainly about workforce shortages.
We know those challenges are real.
But we also believe that before Maine loses another maternity unit, we have a responsibility to make absolutely certain we’ve explored every possible solution.
Because where there is a workforce problem, there is a workforce solution.
So instead of simply protesting, we got to work.
Over the past three months, we’ve spent hundreds of hours meeting with MaineHealth leadership, physicians, nurses, midwives, hospital leaders, researchers, legislators, and rural health experts from across Maine and around the country.
We’ve worked alongside clinicians to develop alternative staffing models, researched recruitment strategies and funding opportunities, and built a vision for strengthening rural maternity care instead of shrinking it.
Along the way, something remarkable happened.
What started as a handful of neighbors asking questions became a movement.
More than 10,000 people have interacted and stood with us.
MaineHealth clinicians have asked for a pause.
MaineHealth donors have asked for a pause.
Elected officials, community leaders, and families from across the state have joined this effort because they believe we have a responsibility to do everything we can before Maine loses another maternity unit.
We haven’t asked anyone to ignore the challenges.
We’ve asked that those challenges be met with the same determination, creativity, and collaboration that built this healthcare system in the first place.
Our work is far from over.
In the weeks ahead, we’ll continue bringing people together, advancing solutions, and asking the MaineHealth Board of Trustees to choose innovation before elimination.
We still believe a different future is possible, and we hope Mainers from every corner of the state will help us build it by hearing what we have to say at the March for Maine Maternity on August 1st, 11am, at Portland City Hall.
