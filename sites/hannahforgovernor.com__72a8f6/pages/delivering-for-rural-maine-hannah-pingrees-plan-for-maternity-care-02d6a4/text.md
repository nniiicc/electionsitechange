The state has lost twelve hospital-based birthing units, nine of them in just the past few years, including units at St.
Mary’s in Lewiston, Waldo Hospital in Belfast, and Houlton Regional Hospital.
Rural Mainers whose closest hospital does not have a birthing unit now drive more than 45 minutes on average to reach the nearest hospital that delivers babies, the longest average drive time in New England.
Distance from maternity care for families impacts both the safety and health outcomes for moms and babies.
This isn’t just a health care problem, it’s also an economic and community challenge.
Young families don’t want to move to a town where the nearest safe place to deliver a baby is an hour away, and few businesses will locate where it’s hard to recruit younger workers for the very same reason.
As a mom who has delivered two kids in a rural part of Maine, Hannah knows what it means to count on a hospital being close by — and what it costs a town when those services disappear.
Maine doesn’t have to accept this as inevitable.
It’s a policy choice, and Hannah is ready to make different ones.
Tell MaineHealth: Don’t close Lincoln’s doors Hannah is calling on the MaineHealth board today to delay any decision on the closure of labor and delivery services at Lincoln Hospital in Damariscotta.
No family in Lincoln County should be left without a safe place to give birth close to home without a statewide assessment of the problem and a greater effort to collaborate on solutions.
Call for a statewide moratorium on labor and delivery closures for one year after the next governor takes office Hannah is calling for a full moratorium on all labor and delivery closures across Maine for a full year after the next governor takes office, giving communities and legislators time to enact solutions.
That time isn’t just a pause, it’s room to do this right.
For example: to explore the shared-staffing models local doctors and nurses have proposed, develop innovation solutions using new federal and state resources can do, and build on the kind of innovative models that are working in other states so that no community loses its birthing unit before real alternatives and a statewide plan are on the table.
Push the federal government to lift the cap on rural health dollars — and put the savings into maternity care While the federal government has touted a $50 billion investment in rural health, the program limits direct provider payments to 15% of each state’s award, with the rest required to support broader transformation efforts, including technology, infrastructure, workforce development and new care models.
Hannah will press Maine’s congressional delegation and federal health officials to lift that cap, and make the case directly that this urgently needed flexibility would allow Maine to support the doctors, midwives, and nurses hospitals actually need to keep Maine’s struggling rural birthing units operating and staffed.
Convene a statewide maternity care summit Hannah has spent her career getting people who don’t normally agree into the same room and pushing them toward a real plan, and she’ll bring that same urgency to maternity care.
As governor, she will convene a summit bringing together hospital and health system leadership, practicing OB/GYNs, family medicine providers, midwives, emergency medicine physicians, state officials, legislators, community leaders and the public.
The goal is not another study.
It’s agreement to: Get honest answers on why units are closing — whether decisions are driven by safety, staffing or by economics alone — and what that means for the next hospital facing the same choice.
Explore and propose solutions to allow communities to retain rural units and creative alternatives to full birthing units , including new state reimbursement models, mobile health units, and expanded midwifery care, telemedicine monitoring, shared primary and OB-GYN care, and require a clear plan for how hospitals without OB-GYN coverage will handle emergency deliveries and ensure the safety of moms and babies.
Ensure support to the units that have stayed open , so today’s survivors aren’t tomorrow’s closures.
This is already part of Hannah’s plan for health care in Maine Hannah’s full Health Care Plan for Maine already commits to strengthening rural health care and expanding access to maternity and reproductive care.
What’s happening at Lincoln Hospital is exactly the kind of test her rural health commitments were written for and exactly why she’s ready to act on day one.
