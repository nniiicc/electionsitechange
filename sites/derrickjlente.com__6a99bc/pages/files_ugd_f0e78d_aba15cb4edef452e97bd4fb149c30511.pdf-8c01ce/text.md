The Supreme Court acknowledged the state is in the midst of a public health crisis and that voting by mail is the safest option.
But justices nonetheless ruled unanimously that the state’s high court does not have the authority to change state law and allow ballots to be sent automatically to all voters eligible to participate in the primary.
Instead, justices have ordered the Secretary of State’s Office or county clerks to send an application for an absentee ballot to every registered primary voter in the state.
Voters will have to complete those ballot applications and send them in to their county clerk’s office in order to receive an absentee ballot in the mail.
In-person voting will still be an option.
The justices ordered all in-person voting, early voting or on election day, must comply with the state’s ongoing public health orders.
In short, your votes matter and they will be counted.
What is important is we all be conscious about our personal safety and the safety of our families.
If absentee voting is an option, please exercise that option.
