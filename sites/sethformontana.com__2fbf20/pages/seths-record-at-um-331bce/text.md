He reversed a decade-long decline and delivered real results for real Montana families.
For eight years, he led the state’s flagship top-tier research institution and served over 16,000 students across UM’s affiliated campuses.
During his tenure, he: Turned around a decade of enrollment decline and grew it to a place of health.
Delivered record-high retention and graduation rates.
Nearly doubled research funding and earned UM's first-ever R1 research status.
Secured UM’s recognition as the #1 university in the nation for community and national service.
Earned UM’s ranking as the #1 military-friendly university in America.
Led the largest fundraising campaign in university history.
Completed the largest campus infrastructure renewal in a generation.
Supported the promotion of women into leadership.
Established wider doors for low-income, first-generation, Native American, and military families.

SETH'S LEADERSHIP FOCUS AT UM: INCLUSIVE PROSPERITY FOR ALL MONTANANS Bondar’s efforts to drive progress at the University of Montana were all in the service of a larger goal as president: to promote inclusive prosperity for all Montanans through opportunities for all to reach their full, unique potential.
This is demonstrated in his success expanding the university's graduate school, dual-enrollment programs, and workforce training initiatives.
These efforts aimed to ensure every Montanan would have access to the education and training needed to secure a well-paying job and meaningful career.
Bodnar prioritized several efforts to support this goal, including: Native American Students — UM recorded the largest number of Native American students in the university's history and increased enrollment of Native American students by more than 40% from 2018.
Veterans and Military-Connected Students — UM was ranked the #1 "Military Friendly" college in the country among tier-1 research institutions in 2025, serving roughly 1,000 military-affiliated students.
First-Generation College Students — Under Bodnar's leadership, first-generation students grew to 33% of the student body by fall 2025.
Workforce Training — Under Bodnar's leadership, short-term workforce training was a priority.
UM served 10,387 individuals through noncredit workforce training programs during the 2024-25 school year alone.
