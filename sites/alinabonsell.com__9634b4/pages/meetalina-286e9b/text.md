WHY I'M RUNNING
I’m tired of the excuses coming out of Albany while our neighborhood pays the price.
I’m tired of career politicians with no term limits falling in line with a far-left, DSA-driven agenda and endorsing someone for mayor whom nearly two-thirds of their own constituents rejected.
They look the other way as antisemitism rises, our streets and subways become less safe, homeless encampments become more prevalent, and more shelters open in our neighborhoods without addressing mental illness or the underlying problems they are supposed to solve.
I’m tired of unfair taxes, struggling small businesses, and a broken family court system where cases drag on for years, hurting parents and children.
This campaign is personal because neglect has been personal for me and for so many people across District 28.
After 24 years of neglect under the same state senator, it’s time for a change.
My Story
ALINA BONSELL
My family came to America as Jewish refugees from Soviet Ukraine and settled in Brooklyn with almost nothing.
I later lived in Staten Island and Long Island before making Manhattan’s East Side my home more than 20 years ago.
I graduated from Hofstra University, built a career in healthcare and pharmaceuticals, started my own business, and completed an entrepreneurship program at Wharton.
I married young, raised two sons, and learned that life rarely goes according to plan.
Like so many New Yorkers, I’ve gone through divorce, fought for my children in a broken family court system, faced housing struggles, and dealt with the rising cost of living in this city.
Neglect isn’t an abstract issue to me.
I know what it feels like to fight for your family in a courtroom, struggle to afford the place you call home, and face systems that make people feel powerless while expecting them to accept less.
Today, I live in Yorkville and am a businesswoman, entrepreneur, and mother of two.
I don’t come from political connections or inherited privilege.
What I have, I’ve built.
I believe government should make people’s lives easier, not harder, by providing accountability, opportunity, public safety, support for strong families, and fewer bureaucratic barriers standing in the way.
