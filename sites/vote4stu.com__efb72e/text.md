Wednesday night (2/11/2026) was amazing.
After waiting four and half hours, my witnesses—Marylanders from across our state and national voting rights advocates spoke powerfully in support of my bill, HB496, that calls for open primaries.
One veteran flew 7,000 miles from Doha where he’s working to advocate for his voting rights in his home state.
One million Marylanders are now registered as independents, about one in four voters.
Just last year, Maryland added 50,000 more independent voters while the two major parties lost nearly 40,000 voters.
This reflects a national trend with nearly half of Americans now identifying as independent and the number is accelerating.
As both parties struggle with shrinking bases, it's past time to modernize our primary elections to make them more inclusive, like the rest 2/3 of the states with open primaries.
Many independents are party-averse immigrants (especially Latinos and Asians), young people (over half of millennials and GenZs as independents), and, as I learned recently, our veterans, who fought for our country and our rights, but now find their voting rights impeded by our closed primary elections.
Primary elections are funded by tax dollars and administered by the government, not political parties, and therefore should be open to all registered voters.
Forcing people to join a political party to make their votes count is undemocratic and flies in the face of our progressive values and demographic reality.
Voting rights are civil rights.
Making our elections more democratic is fundamental to our rights as Americans and should be the new frontier of our civil rights movement.
My special appreciation to Delegate Stuart Schmidt for his bipartisan bill and to all who came, including former Montgomery County councilmember Phil Andrews, the students, and Marylanders across our state.
You touched hearts and changed minds.
I’m honored to be in this fight with you.

Delegate Schmidt has filed legislation to allow unaffiliated voters to participate in primary elections.
The bill lets voters who are not registered with a political party choose which party’s primary ballot they want to vote in—without being forced to join that party.
It also ensures voters do not automatically become affiliated with a political party simply because they voted in a primary.

Delegate Stu Schmidt has filed legislation to ensure individuals can practice their faith without unnecessary government interference.
