The library is the first place I remember walking to by myself, unless you count the time I ran away on my tricycle to the only other place I knew—the grocery store—where a neighbor lady found me chatting up the butcher and asking for a free popsicle.
I was four.
The great librarian Linn Wilson turned me onto Louise Fitzhugh’s novel about a nosey truth-teller who carried a notebook wherever she went.
On the block of factory-worker houses where we lived, I used to hide out in a wide stand of lilacs, eavesdropping on passersby and taking notes on their clothing and conversations a la Harriet.
