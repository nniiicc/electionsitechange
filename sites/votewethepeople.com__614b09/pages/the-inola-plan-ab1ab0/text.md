Home → The Inola Plan The Inola Plan American ownership.
Environmental accountability.
Oklahoma’s money working for Oklahomans.
I support producing aluminum in America and creating good-paying American jobs.
But economic development must protect our communities and give taxpayers a fair return.
My proposal is straightforward: investigate the existing Inola smelter deal, protect the people’s water and farmland, and pursue an American-owned cooperative at a location that independent environmental studies show is suitable.
1.
Demand a Full, Independent Investigation Oklahomans deserve to know how this deal was negotiated, who benefits, and what taxpayers have been promised in return.
I will call for an independent investigation and public accounting of: State incentives, federal grants, tax exemptions, land agreements, and infrastructure commitments.
The project’s ultimate owners and everyone receiving fees or financial benefits.
Lobbying contracts, political contributions, conflicts of interest, and communications surrounding the deal.
Any evidence of improper payments, undisclosed financial interests, or offshore arrangements connected to public officials.
I have serious concerns about this deal.
Those concerns demand records and investigation.
If evidence shows wrongdoing, it should be referred to the appropriate authorities.
The public deserves findings supported by evidence.
2.
Protect Water, Farmland, and Public Health Before supporting a smelter, I will demand independent review of its effects on air, water, agriculture, livestock, nearby residents, and electricity costs.
My requirements will include: Publicly available environmental studies and a comparison of alternative locations.
Baseline testing before construction and ongoing independent monitoring.
Enforceable limits on emissions, water use, wastewater, and hazardous waste.
Financial guarantees for cleanup, closure, and damage caused by the operation.
Public reporting, meaningful penalties, and enforcement when requirements are violated.
Moving a project does not automatically make it safe.
Any alternative location must meet these standards without simply transferring the burden to another community.
3.
Pursue American Ownership Through a Cooperative My policy is no foreign ownership of the proposed taxpayer-supported cooperative smelter.
I will pursue an ownership model inspired by Oklahoma’s rural electric cooperatives, with Oklahomans having a meaningful opportunity to become members and owners.
The proposal would include: American ownership, with safeguards against hidden foreign control.
A democratically elected member board and qualified industrial management.
Independent audits, transparent finances, and strict conflict-of-interest rules.
A plan for members to share in available earnings after operating costs, debt, maintenance, and reserves.
Strong worker protections and opportunities for Oklahoma workers and businesses.
Public investment should help build ownership and lasting wealth for our people.
4.
Put Public Money to Work for Oklahomans Why should Oklahoma prioritize subsidies for a foreign-controlled enterprise while families struggle to afford food, housing, and basic necessities?
I will seek to stop additional state support for the existing foreign-controlled proposal through lawful means and work with the Legislature to redirect legally available funds toward an American-owned alternative.
That money is not a blank check.
Before committing it, we must publish a feasibility study covering construction costs, financing, electricity, environmental protections, market demand, and taxpayer risk.
Existing state commitments must be reviewed before money can be recovered or redirected.
Federal funding requires separate federal approval.
A cooperative must demonstrate that it can operate responsibly and compete.
If the proposal cannot meet those tests, we should not spend the money on it.
5.
Give Affected Communities a Real Voice Inola residents deserve a meaningful say in their community’s future.
Any alternative host community deserves the same respect.
I will support public hearings, accessible project documents, and lawful local votes.
I will also put the cooperative proposal and its financing options before the people through the People’s App before seeking legislation.
Public participation must come before the final commitment.
My Commitment We can pursue American manufacturing while protecting Oklahoma’s water, farmland, and families.
We can demand jobs and environmental accountability.
And we can insist that public investment creates a fair return for the people providing it.
Investigate the deal.
Protect the community.
Build American ownership.
Put Oklahoma’s money to work for Oklahomans.
This is a proposed policy requiring independent studies, legislative action, financing, and applicable approvals.
