THE FARMER SERVANT PLAN TO PREVENT AND END HOMELESSNESS IN OKLAHOMA
Every Oklahoman Deserves a Path Home
A lost job, an illness, a rent increase, domestic violence, or a family crisis can put someone at risk of homelessness.
Others need help with addiction or serious mental illness.
There is no single answer for every person, but every person deserves to be heard and offered a real path forward.
As governor, I will work to prevent homelessness before it starts and help people who are homeless now reach safe, lasting housing.
Moving someone from one sidewalk to another does not solve the problem.
1.
KEEP PEOPLE HOUSED
Bring housing providers, courts, utilities, employers, and community organizations together to reach families before an eviction becomes homelessness.
I will ask the Legislature to support targeted, short-term help with rent, deposits, utilities, and transportation when it can keep someone housed.
No one should lose their home because agencies failed to work together.
2.
CREATE A PATH FROM THE STREET TO A HOME
Support locally led outreach that connects people with safe shelter, food, hygiene, identification, and a housing caseworker.
Expand the options that fit different needs:
- Short-term rental assistance
- Rapid rehousing programs
- Transitional housing
- Recovery housing
- Housing with ongoing support services
Different people face different challenges.
Housing solutions should reflect that reality.
3.
OFFER TREATMENT BEFORE A CRISIS BECOMES A CRIME
Improve access to voluntary mental-health care, addiction treatment, trauma support, and recovery services.
Coordinate those services with Oklahoma’s existing homeless outreach and crisis-response programs.
For people experiencing a crisis, we should use qualified mental-health professionals and crisis-response teams whenever appropriate.
A jail cell should not be our default mental-health or housing plan.
4.
BUILD A PATH TO WORK AND INDEPENDENCE
Partner with CareerTech, employers, workforce programs, and community organizations to create pathways back to self-sufficiency.
Support:
- Paid work opportunities
- Job training and retraining
- Apprenticeships
- Transportation assistance
- Help obtaining identification and work documents
Some people can return to work quickly.
Others need treatment, disability support, or stable housing first.
We will meet people where they are and help them move forward.
5.
PROTECT CHILDREN, FAMILIES, VETERANS, AND RURAL COMMUNITIES
Help families stay together whenever it is safe to do so.
Support young people leaving foster care as they transition into adulthood.
Connect veterans with the benefits and services they earned.
Include rural communities where homelessness is often less visible and may take the form of living in a vehicle, moving between relatives’ homes, or unstable temporary housing.
Every county matters.
Every community matters.
6.
RESPECT LOCAL CONTROL AND PUBLIC SAFETY
Cities, counties, tribes, service providers, and people with lived experience should help shape local solutions.
Public spaces should be safe and accessible for everyone.
When an encampment presents a genuine hazard, outreach efforts should provide notice when feasible, offer a place to go, and help protect essential belongings.
Violence and exploitation must be addressed.
A person’s lack of housing should not erase their dignity.
7.
SHOW THE PEOPLE THE RESULTS
In my first 100 days, I will bring together relevant agencies, local governments, tribes, housing providers, nonprofits, schools, law enforcement, and community partners to map existing services, funding, and gaps across Oklahoma.
I will propose a coordinated budget to the Legislature and publish results through the People’s Table.
The public should be able to see:
- People served
- Housing placements
- Housing retention rates
- Repeat homelessness rates
- Program wait times
- Program spending
Personal medical and housing information will remain protected.
Programs that work should grow.
Programs that fail should change.
OUR PROMISE
I will ask a simple question of every policy:
Does it give an Oklahoman a genuine chance to get back on their feet?
Compassion means offering real help.
Accountability means measuring whether that help led to lasting housing and a better life.
We must care for Oklahomans who are homeless today while preventing more of our neighbors from joining them.
It’s a WE thing, not a ME thing.
Robert E.
Brooks
