Our founders knew that the only way to ensure the long-term survival of the democratic self-government they envisioned was to build a robust culture of informed and engaged citizens.
250 years later, it’s never been easier to stay informed about the issues of the day, but the concept of citizen engagement as a guiding principle has lost the power and influence it once held in American society.
This lack of engagement is both a symptom and a cause of our current crisis.
In response to this crisis, I feel called to do my part as an American citizen and work to ensure my daughters grow up with the same rights and opportunities I did.
Political engagement for me will be running for public office and along the way I hope to encourage and inspire others to find their own form of citizen engagement.
American democracy has bestowed a generous array of gifts upon me throughout my life.
It has given me the opportunity to build a family and acquire home, security, and happiness in abundant measure.
My hope is that public service can be a way to show gratitude for the life these gifts have enabled me to live.
Bio Derek Warriner I am a father, a community leader, and for the last 15 years an executive in the healthcare industry.
While I’ve demonstrated consistent and sustained success in the business world over that time, I also know what it’s like to live paycheck to paycheck.
Before my opportunity in the healthcare industry came along, I worked an array of jobs that span an idiosyncratic spectrum of professional experience: server, bartender, actor, reality TV show contestant, fundraiser, and newspaper ad salesman.
I take great pride in my role as the provider for my family and I understand the urgent drive of a parent to ensure a good life for their children.
I also know what it’s like to work for tips.
I’ve experienced the stress and anxiety of daily life without any health insurance.
I know firsthand the soul-crushing indignity of filing for unemployment.
Representing constituents in a democracy necessarily involves representing the interests of a wide range of citizens and their interests.
Rich and poor.
Healthy and sick.
Young and old.
Republican and Democrat.
And I believe the range and diversity of my own lived experiences has endowed me with the qualities we should all expect from our public servants: understanding, empathy, and integrity.

What North Carolinians are struggling with is not defined by a political label.
Corporate Money out of our Elections The influence of corporate money in our elections is pervasive and hurts our communities by undermining democracy, raising the cost of living for everyone, and fostering systemic corruption.
The 2010 Supreme Court decision in Citizens United, which removed limits on corporate political spending, is the root of the problem.
However, the United States Constitution gives states the right to determine how commerce is conducted within their borders.
By adding a simple regulation pertaining to how corporations conduct themselves in order to have the right to do business in North Carolina, we can eliminate the influence of their money and their control over our state government.
This is the idea: any corporation that wants to do business with the people of North Carolina will be prohibited from using any of its money for politics.
No more donations to Super PACs or any other political entity.
It's that simple.
We just need leaders brave enough to take a principled stand and put people over corporations.
If we ever want to return to that hallowed ideal of a government by the people, and one where the people actually trust their elected officials, we must begin by passing this law.
Regardless of which political party you belong to, corporate control of our government is incredibly destructive.
My commitment to the people of District 68 is that on day one in the North Carolina General Assembly, I will fight for this law and do everything I can to end the power of anonymous corporate money that has taken over our politics and threatened the very fabric of our Democracy.
Democracy I believe in the America envisioned by our founding fathers: a country by the people , for the peopl e, where everyone is entitled to Life , Liberty , and the Pursuit of Happiness .
Right now, we have leaders that aren't concerned with the needs of the people.
They continue to advocate exclusively for massive corporations and the ultra-wealthy at the expense of everyone else.
And they are manipulating election maps to stay in power.
As your Representative, I will fight for the general welfare of our entire community and work to build a fair and transparent government.
Affordability Every North Carolinian should be able to afford the essentials: groceries, healthcare, electricity, housing, and childcare.
This requires leaders who put people, not profits, first.
We can lower the cost of living by standing up to corporate bad actors, cutting red tape, and investing in our community.
Healthcare No one should ever have to worry that if a medical emergency were to happen to their family member or child, they would fall into debt.
North Carolinians need affordable healthcare for both yearly checkups and for emergencies.
As a Healthcare Executive, I believe in competition to foster innovative approaches to quality healthcare, and I also believe in a strong Medicaid to take care of our vulnerable populations: particularly children and seniors.
Education North Carolina ranks dead last in the nation for public school spending.
We're ranked 43rd in the country in teacher pay and we're consistently losing the best teaching candidates to neighboring states.
We can do better.
We owe it to our children to do better.
Our public schools are the pride of District 68, and I’m proud to send my daughters to public school.
If we want to keep it that way, we need to invest in our teachers and stop siphoning away dollars to wealthy private schools.
