Priorities Education Indiana’s public schools are the backbone of our communities and every child is welcome, regardless of background, income, or ability.
That’s what makes them the most essential and equitable investment we can make with our tax dollars.
When public money is diverted to private schools that can pick and choose who they accept, we undermine that promise of equal opportunity.
In the 2024-2025 school year alone, over $490,000,000 was taken from public schools to fund private institutions.
Hoosier families deserve a system where every child has a fair shot, and that starts with fully funded public schools that serve all of us, not just the select few.
Working Class Hoosiers Indiana was built by workers.
It's the people who keep our factories running, care for our families, grow our food, and work to keep our communities going strong.
Meanwhile, productivity has gone up, yet wages in Indiana haven’t kept pace with the forever rising cost of housing, healthcare, childcare, groceries, and utilities.
Families are working harder than ever and still falling behind, not because they lack effort, but because the system no longer rewards their labor fairly.
When workers earn enough to live, they spend locally, strengthen small businesses, and keep dollars circulating in our communities.
When wages become stagnate, everything else stalls with them.
Affordable healthcare isn’t a luxury, it’s a lifeline.
In Indiana, too many families are forced to choose between paying medical bills and putting food on the table.
Rising costs don’t just hurt individual households; they weaken entire communities.
When people can access affordable care, they stay healthy, stay working, and stay contributing to Indiana’s economy.
Rural medical facilities are especially vital.
For many Hoosiers, the nearest hospital or clinic is far away, and closures have turned simple health concerns into emergencies.
Keeping rural healthcare centers open means faster treatment, healthier families, and stronger small towns.
Healthcare Housing Affordable housing in Indiana starts with acknowledging the simple truth; too many Hoosiers are being priced out of their own communities.
Rising home values and unpredictable property tax assessments have pushed families, seniors, and first-time buyers to the edge.
Assessment reform and tax reform is essential so that property taxes reflect real, livable home values, not speculative spikes in the market that punish people for staying in their homes.
Indiana also needs to realign incentives in the housing market.
Right now, the system favors large, high-margin developments while smaller, starter homes are increasingly rare.
We should encourage builders to construct modest, energy-efficient homes designed for first-time buyers by offering them targeted incentives, streamlined permitting, and infrastructure support, without sacrificing local control or community character.
Just as important, first-time buyer homes should be protected from large-scale corporate buyouts that distort prices and lock families out of ownership.
Qualified protections, such as purchase limitations, priority access for owner-occupants, or time-limited resale restrictions, can deter speculative acquisitions while still respecting private property rights and a healthy market.
Affordable housing isn’t about lowering standards or overbuilding.
It’s about creating real pathways to home-ownership, stabilizing neighborhoods, and ensuring that working families can live near their jobs, schools, and support networks.
A smart housing policy strengthens communities, grows the middle class, and keeps Indiana a place where the next generation can put down roots and thrive.
Tax Reform Ryan believes a fair tax system should reflect the simple idea that we all do our part to support the communities we love.
His plan eases the burden on working and middle-class families by embracing a truly marginal income tax structure, ensuring lower-income Hoosiers keep more of what they earn while contributions grow responsibly as income rises.
At the same time, Indiana currently offers extensive corporate incentives, including tax credits, abatements, and other subsidies that can amount to billions in lost revenue.
One example: multi-billion-dollar exemptions tied to large projects like data centers and other mega-deals that erode the base for schools, roads, and healthcare without guaranteed benefits for everyday families.
By better regulating corporate welfare and closing loopholes that allow corporations to sidestep their fair share, Ryan’s approach shifts the tax burden away from families and toward those who benefit most from our economy.
Hoosiers can no longer rely on philanthropy for the essentials every community needs.
He also supports housing affordability through regulated property assessments, ensuring homeowners and renters aren’t priced out by runaway valuations disconnected from real income.
It’s not about targeting anyone, it’s about building a system that works.
By creating a balanced tax structure that strengthens schools, improves healthcare access, invests in local communities, and keeps housing within reach, Ryan’s plan puts hope back within reach for families who want a fair shot and a brighter future for their children.
Solar & Data Center Infrastructure Solar energy could be very beneficial to Indiana, but we need to use it wisely instead of seizing valuable property.
We should instead integrate solar into our existing structure.
We'll get the benefit of new energy, keep land available for farming, create shade for people and vehicles alike.
Now let's talk about data centers.
What are they and why are so many popping up?
Data centers can differ on functionality and operation.
Some are used to store bank and medical information, while some are for generative AI, and others for AI surveillance.
Ryan isn't trying to stop an entire industry, but rather mold it to safeguard our communities.
Ryan wants to put laws on the books for where and how these facilities are built, and to end AI tracking of citizens.
