Hometown Hero 2017​ Brian Schimmel: Service is his passion after lesson from son A phrase in the Bible’s Book of Isaiah, “And a child shall lead them,” might explain a mid-life change of career for Intel manager Brian Schimmel.
Three years ago, Schimmel’s son, Zach, chose as his Forest Grove High School senior project an assignment with Sonrise Church’s meals for homeless families program.
Zach found the weekly Sunday meals so meaningful that he said, “Dad, you ought to come with me,” Schimmel recalled.
Zach and his father soon immersed themselves into that project, as well as Meals on wheels, and the father began to notice a change in his life.
“Seeing how some seniors live could feel crushing – but when I would carry a meal inside their house and see their face light up, I felt I was experiencing God,” the soft-spoken Schimmel said.
Forest Grove is an urban metropolis compared to the dairy farm Schimmel grew up on in the remote coast range highlands, “out in the sticks” 20 miles south of Raining, Oregon.
Excellent grades earned him a full Navy ROTC scholarship to Oregon State University, where he showed leadership becoming the unit’s public affairs officer and led the conversion of the NROTC quarterly publication to a digital format A neck injury precluded hs serving active duty after graduating from OSU, and he went to work for Intel for 19 years, rising to become a global business enablement and acquisition manager.
But the community service that his son began role modeling spoke to him.
Schimmel has since contributed hundreds of hours coordinating a meals program, volunteering for Habitat for Humanity and the Forest Grove Senior and Community Center and chairing - and championing the city’s Sustainability Commission.
Schimmel, in 2016, decided to accept an employee buyout from Intel two months later and feels his service passion is just beginning.
And perhaps he is now the one role modeling for another family member.
Brian and Barb Schimmel’s youngest child, Zoe, began volunteering for Meals on Wheels herself.
“She would be with me every week delivering meals, and seniors would call her by name,” Brian said proudly, “and miss her on those days she couldn’t make it.”
