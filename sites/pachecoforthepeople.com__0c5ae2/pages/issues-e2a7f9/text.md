Immigration and ICE (updated 9/25/26)
THE LAW MATTERS.
SO DOES HOW WE ENFORCE IT.
The recent ICE activity across the South Coast deserves a clear and measured response.

We can protect public safety without racial profiling.

As your State Representative, I will stand for equal treatment under the law, due process, public safety, and basic human dignity.
Law and compassion are not opposites.
We should demand both.

House Bill 1811 Clean Slate Bill
I support the "Clean Slate Bill".
The Massachusetts Clean Slate bill (H.1811) modernizes the state’s criminal justice system by automating the sealing of eligible criminal and juvenile records once individuals have completed required waiting periods.
Instead of requiring people to navigate a complicated petition process, the bill directs the state to seal records automatically, removing unnecessary barriers to second chances.
Key Positives & Benefits
1.
Expands Access to Opportunity
By sealing records automatically, the bill helps individuals more easily access jobs, housing, and education, removing long-standing barriers caused by old records.
2.
Eliminates Bureaucratic Delays
The current system requires manual petitions and can take months or longer.
H.1811 replaces this with an efficient, automated process, reducing backlogs and ensuring timely relief.
3.
Promotes Economic Growth
When more people can work and participate fully in the economy, employers gain access to a broader workforce and communities benefit from increased economic activity and stability.
4.
Strengthens Families and Communities
The bill supports individuals who have already fulfilled their obligations by giving them a fair chance to rebuild their lives—leading to stronger families and safer, more stable communities.
5.
Advances Fairness and Equity
Many people eligible for record sealing never complete the process due to complexity.
Automation ensures that relief is applied fairly and consistently, rather than only benefiting those who can navigate the system.
6.
Aligns Massachusetts with Other States
Over a dozen states have already implemented similar “clean slate” policies with positive outcomes.
This bill allows Massachusetts to adopt a proven, modern approach to criminal justice reform.
Crimes That Are Excluded (Not Eligible for Sealing)
The bill keeps existing limits, meaning serious offenses are not eligible, including:
- Certain sex offenses (especially for Level 2 or 3 offenders)
- Firearms-related crimes (e.g., illegal sale or possession)
- Public corruption crimes (like bribery or perjury)
- Witness intimidation or obstruction of justice
- In general, serious violent offenses or crimes involving harm to others are restricted under Massachusetts law
Bottom Line
H.1811 is a commonsense reform that removes red tape, promotes second chances, and strengthens the workforce—while maintaining existing eligibility standards.
It shifts the system from one that requires people to ask for relief to one that delivers it automatically when it’s earned.
I can stand by that.

Data Centers
Why we should be Opposing the Development of Large-Scale Data Centers in the 9th Bristol District, Now!
The rapid expansion of large-scale data centers across the United States presents significant concerns for communities such as Dartmouth, New Bedford, and the broader 9th Bristol District.
While some would argue these facilities support technological growth and generate tax revenue, which in the current challenging fiscal times may be appealing for communities like ours, the long-term environmental, infrastructure, and community impacts far outweigh the very limited local benefits they provide.
Data centers are massive industrial facilities that house thousands of computer servers responsible for cloud computing, A.I., internet storage, and digital communications.
These facilities operate around the clock and consume massive amounts of electricity and water to power and cool their systems.
In many cases, a single large data center can consume as much electricity as tens of thousands of homes.
In Dartmouth, we already have significant challenges with overuse of water and with the potential for 100’s of additional 40B housing being built, it will not get any better.
Clean water is not an infinite resource!
For communities already facing infrastructure challenges, the addition of a data center would place severe strain on local electrical grids, water systems, and municipal resources.
Our Fire Departments would potentially not have the resources to handle an large scale issue in one of these, given current budget constraints, staffing, etc.
The 9th Bristol District is already grappling with critical wastewater and water infrastructure needs, particularly in Dartmouth.
Diverting substantial water and energy resources to support private technology infrastructure risks placing additional burdens on the taxpayers and ratepayers while offering limited direct community benefit.
Environmental concerns are equally significant.
Data centers contribute to increased greenhouse gas emissions through huge energy demands and reliance on backup diesel generators.
They generate continuous noise from cooling systems and industrial equipment and often require large-scale land clearing that threatens open space, farmland, and environmentally sensitive areas.
For our communities that value coastal preservation, farmland protection, and responsible growth, these developments are inconsistent with long-term sustainability goals.
Economically, the promises surrounding data centers are often overpromised and under delivered.
While construction creates temporary jobs, these facilities typically employ very few permanent workers once operational.
Many developers also seek substantial tax incentives and utility accommodation, meaning communities may absorb environmental and infrastructure costs without receiving proportional economic return.
The South Coast should prioritize economic development that creates meaningful long-term employment, strengthens local businesses, preserves environmental resources, and improves quality of life for residents.
Industrial-scale data centers do not align with those priorities.
As representatives and community leaders consider the future of the 9th Bristol District, it is essential to advocate for responsible development that protects local resources, preserves community character, and ensures that growth benefits residents — not just large multinational technology corporations.

Supporting Individuals with Disabilities
As someone who oversees transportation services for individuals with disabilities, I strongly support the enactment of H. 4358.
In my professional role, I work every day with individuals who rely on wheelchairs not simply as mobility devices, but as an essential part of their independence, employment, healthcare, and quality of life.
When a wheelchair requires repairs, the consequences are immediate and profound.
Missed medical appointments, lost opportunities to participate in community programs, inability to get to work, and increased isolation become daily realities.
I've seen firsthand how lengthy repair delays place unnecessary burdens on individuals, families, and service providers, while also increasing costs to the Commonwealth.
H. 4358 would help ensure that people who depend on wheelchairs have timely access to repairs, allowing them to remain active, independent, and engaged in their communities.
Supporting this legislation is about recognizing that mobility is not a luxury—it is a necessity, and no one should be forced to put their life on hold while waiting for the equipment they depend on every day.
