Here is each vote, with the meeting it came from.
In 2019 the Salem City Council moved to create a tax on wages earned inside the city, paid by employees whether or not they lived in Salem.
The council referred it to the May 2020 ballot, then withdrew it that March.
Salem voters never got to vote on Andersen’s payroll tax — but when a later version came up, it was defeated overwhelmingly.

When people finally had a say Tom Andersen left the City Council before 2023.
He did not vote on the 2023 Salem payroll-tax ordinance.
Salem voters rejected that later version, 81.73% No to 18.27% Yes, in November 2023.
The 2019 proposal exempted minimum-wage earners, and the council voted to refer it to the voters.
The point is who it would have taxed: people who work in Salem, whether or not they live here.
Some people would have been taxed but have no vote in city elections.
