Remembering Alok Mehta and Fred Morrone on September 11
Today, I lit two candles at my church in memory of Alok Kumar Mehta and Ferdinand V.
“Fred” Morrone—two men I never had the opportunity to know, but whose lives and legacies touched mine in different ways.
Alok was a brother of my Triangle Fraternity chapter at Colorado State University.
He was killed at the World Trade Center on September 11, 2001, before my time in the chapter.
Years later, as an undergraduate, another chapter brother and I had the privilege of helping present a memorial honoring Alok to our national fraternity.
Just before this anniversary, after wondering what had become of it, Triangle sent me a photograph and confirmed that Alok’s memorial still hangs at fraternity headquarters more than two decades later.
Seeing it again meant a great deal to me.
Fred Morrone served as Superintendent of Police for the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey and was killed at the World Trade Center.
I never knew Fred either, but his family later honored his memory through a scholarship that helped support my graduate education.
Two men I never had the opportunity to meet, yet their lives—and the people who chose to preserve their memories—touched mine.
Today, I remember Alok and Fred, their families, and all those who were killed or whose lives were forever changed on September 11, 2001.
May their memories be a blessing.
We will not forget.
