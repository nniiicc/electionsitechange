The problem Families in NJ-03 are getting squeezed—groceries, utilities, childcare, and insurance costs keep rising while wages and opportunity don’t always keep pace.
Too often, Washington talks about the economy like it’s a stock ticker.
Most people experience it as monthly bills and unexpected emergencies.
My approach Focus on policies that lower everyday costs without gimmicks—competition, transparency, and enforcement where markets are failing people.
Support targeted tax relief for working families and small businesses, paired with responsible budgeting and oversight of waste.

Clear positions on stock trading/ethics rules so voters can trust decisions aren’t influenced by personal gain.
Labor, Unions, and Workers’ Rights Ryan Michael Kelly for Congress campaign literature, union-printed in 2026.
The problem Too many people work hard and still lack a living wage, predictable benefits, safe working conditions, or a meaningful voice in decisions that affect their jobs.
Workers should not have to face retaliation or political barriers simply because they want to organize collectively.
My approach I support the right of workers to organize and bargain collectively.
Elected officials should stand with working people not only when asking for endorsements, but when workers are organizing and fighting for better wages, benefits, and working conditions.
I oppose so-called Right to Work laws and support stronger federal protections for organizing, a living wage, universal paid parental leave, and policies modeled on the PRO Act.
My campaign also tries to put those values into practice.
We choose union printing for campaign literature whenever practicable, and our current campaign literature is union-printed and carries the union bug.

The American healthcare system is fragmented, expensive, and burdened by private insurance middlemen whose financial incentives can conflict with what patients and providers actually need.
My approach I support universal, single-payer healthcare.
Healthcare should be treated as a public good and a basic guarantee, not something that depends on your employer, income, age, or ability to navigate an insurance bureaucracy.
Universal coverage should include preventive and routine care, emergency care, mental healthcare, prescriptions, reproductive healthcare, prenatal and postnatal care, fertility care, and medically necessary care for every person.
My goal is straightforward: every American should have healthcare at least as good as the healthcare members of Congress provide for themselves.

We do not have enough genuinely affordable housing, and homelessness cannot be solved by simply moving people from one place to another.
My approach We need substantially more affordable housing, including public and social housing, while making sure new development is supported by appropriate infrastructure and thoughtful planning.
I support broadly applicable rent stabilization that permits reasonable increases tied to legitimate costs while protecting tenants from excessive increases.
I support Housing First approaches and supportive services.
Clearing encampments without a safe and realistic housing alternative does not solve homelessness, and hostile architecture simply makes life harder for people who already have nowhere to go.

The problem Education is supposed to expand opportunity, but for too many families it has become a long-term financial burden that delays homeownership, family formation, and economic stability.
Tuition has risen faster than wages, while students are asked to take on large, long-term debt to access public and nonprofit education that should be broadly affordable.
At the same time, federal priorities have too often placed enormous resources into areas that most Americans never meaningfully consented to, while leaving students and families to shoulder rising costs on their own.
My approach I believe education is a public good.
Public colleges, universities, and nonprofit institutions should be affordable, predictable in cost, and not require decades of debt to access.
Student loan forgiveness—especially for public servants, educators, healthcare workers, and nonprofit employees—is not a handout.
It is a recognition of service and a practical investment in communities.
Right now, students carry nearly all the financial risk—even when programs don’t deliver, circumstances change, or life intervenes.
I believe risk should be shared more fairly between students, institutions, and the public.
When students leave school without a degree, or graduate into a labor market that doesn’t support repayment, the answer shouldn’t be lifelong debt and penalties.
Education should open doors—not trap people financially when the system fails them.
Long term, we should reduce the need for loans altogether by controlling tuition growth, increasing public investment in education, and aligning costs with real economic outcomes.

Working people should not be forced to choose between good jobs and clean air, clean water, affordable energy, and a livable climate.
My approach I support a transition toward clean energy that creates good union jobs and protects workers whose existing jobs are affected through replacement employment, training, income support, and strong labor protections.
I support greater public ownership and democratic accountability of essential utilities, major investment in clean energy including offshore wind and responsible nuclear power, and moving public investment away from fossil fuels.
Corporations should bear the true costs they impose on communities rather than shifting those costs to residents and ratepayers.

Mass incarceration, wealth-based detention, unnecessary criminalization, and perpetual surveillance can destabilize people and communities without making anyone safer.
My approach I support shifting resources toward social workers, mental-health professionals, violence prevention, treatment, and community services while focusing law enforcement resources on serious threats and violent crime.
I support ending cash bail and wealth-based pretrial detention.
Limited temporary detention may still be necessary where individualized evidence shows a serious and immediate threat, subject to due process and regular review.
I support decriminalizing personal drug consumption, expanding harm reduction and treatment, decriminalizing consensual sex work, fair and timely parole, and reducing unnecessary incarceration.
Police and jails should not be our default mental-health system.
Prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, and successful reentry should be central parts of public safety.

At the same time, millions of law-abiding Americans responsibly own firearms, and the Second Amendment is a constitutional right.
Too much of the gun debate treats those realities as mutually exclusive.
I believe we can protect constitutional rights while pursuing practical, evidence-based measures that reduce violence and promote responsible firearm ownership.
My approach I strongly support the Second Amendment and the right of law-abiding Americans to own firearms.
I have been a firearm owner and a life member of the NRA, and I understand the importance many Americans place on responsible gun ownership.
I also support expanded background checks, secure firearm storage, community-based violence prevention, suicide prevention, and Extreme Risk Protection Orders with strong due-process protections.
I do not support a broad ban on commonly owned firearms, and I do not support reinstating the federal assault-weapons ban.
I believe policy should focus on preventing dangerous people and dangerous situations from becoming tragedies while respecting the rights of responsible gun owners.
My 2026 Moms Demand Action Gun Sense Candidate distinction reflects my willingness to govern with gun safety in mind.
It is not an endorsement by Moms Demand Action or Everytown for Gun Safety Action Fund.

The problem Civil rights mean little if people cannot actually exercise them.
Discrimination and unequal access still affect housing, employment, education, healthcare, public services, and participation in civic life.
Women, LGBTQ+ people, people with disabilities, racial and ethnic minorities, and other communities continue to face legal, economic, and institutional barriers.
My approach I spent more than a decade working in federal civil-rights and civil-liberties oversight.
I support strong enforcement, independent oversight, meaningful complaint processes, and proactive examination of disparities in government programs.
I support strong federal protections for reproductive freedom, including abortion access, contraception, fertility care, medication abortion, maternal healthcare, and prenatal and postnatal care.
I support LGBTQ+ equality, including protections against discrimination in employment, housing, education, healthcare, and public services.
Transgender people deserve the same freedom, dignity, healthcare, and equal protection as everyone else.
I support stronger ADA and Section 504 enforcement and reforms that allow people with disabilities to work, marry, raise families, receive healthcare, learn, and live independently without being penalized by outdated benefit rules.

Government can interfere with people’s ability to worship and practice their faith, but it can also undermine religious liberty by favoring certain traditions, deciding which beliefs are sufficiently legitimate, or using government power in ways that make people afraid to enter a house of worship.
Those concerns arise in civilian life and in the military.
Service members should not have their faith reduced to an inaccurate government category, lose access to appropriate spiritual care because their tradition is administratively inconvenient, or be treated differently because their religion is unfamiliar or unpopular.
My approach I support a strong separation of church and state because it protects religious liberty.
Government should neither establish nor favor a religion, and every person should be free to practice their faith — or no faith — without unnecessary government interference.
My concern about religious liberty is not new.
I have spoken with religious leaders over the years about military religious accommodation, chaplaincy, religious identification, and the government’s treatment of different faith traditions.
Those conversations reinforced a principle I still hold: government should protect people’s ability to practice their religion, not decide which sincere faiths are sufficiently legitimate to count.
My own religious journey has crossed different traditions, and I am Catholic today, but religious liberty means very little if we defend it only for people who believe as we do.
I will defend the freedom of people of every faith, people whose beliefs do not fit neatly into government categories, and people who practice no religion.
That principle should apply to military service as well.
Service members should be able to accurately identify their religious preference, seek reasonable religious accommodations, receive appropriate spiritual support, and practice their faith consistent with legitimate military requirements — without government officials ranking religions according to theological legitimacy.
Houses of worship also deserve strong protection from routine government intrusion, immigration enforcement, and surveillance.
Genuine emergencies and properly authorized investigations can present different circumstances, but churches, synagogues, mosques, temples, and other sacred spaces should not become ordinary enforcement or intelligence venues.
Religious liberty and equal treatment under the law are both fundamental.
Protecting freedom of worship and conscience should go hand in hand with protecting people from discrimination and ensuring that civil rights and access to public life do not depend on another person’s religious beliefs.

Whether someone is undocumented, seeking asylum, a refugee, protected by TPS, or navigating the immigration system as a military family member, government must respect due process, dignity, and constitutional rights.
My approach I support abolishing ICE and fundamentally restructuring federal immigration enforcement around due process, civil rights, legitimate public-safety needs, and meaningful independent oversight.
I support sanctuary policies that prevent local police from automatically functioning as federal civil-immigration officers.
People should be able to report crimes, serve as witnesses, and seek help without fearing that ordinary interaction with local government will become an immigration-enforcement action.
I would restore and strengthen DHS civil-rights and oversight offices and protect them by statute from political dismantling.
I support stronger statutory protections for asylum seekers, refugees, TPS recipients, immigrants, and military families so basic rights cannot simply disappear whenever administrations change.

The problem The United States has spent enormous resources on wars and military interventions while Congress has repeatedly surrendered its constitutional responsibility over war powers.
U.S. foreign policy should not provide blank checks for governments or military actions that violate international law, cause widespread civilian suffering, or undermine long-term peace.
My approach I am anti-war.
Military force should be reserved for the actual defense of our country and people, and Congress must reclaim its constitutional authority over war.
I support a permanent ceasefire in Gaza, an end to U.S. military aid that enables violations of international and humanitarian law, and diplomatic, economic, and political pressure to end the occupation and protect Palestinian rights.
I support the use of peaceful Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions as protected political expression and oppose laws that punish people merely for participating in BDS.
I believe the Israeli government’s actions in Gaza constitute genocide.
I also unequivocally oppose antisemitism and believe Jewish people must be protected from hatred, discrimination, threats, and violence.
Criticism of a government and advocacy for Palestinian rights should not automatically be treated as hatred toward Jewish people.
Palestinians and Israelis both deserve to live in peace and security.
U.S. policy should prioritize human rights, accountability, diplomacy, humanitarian assistance, and a lasting peace rather than occupation, collective punishment, displacement, or endless warfare.

The problem Public trust erodes when accountability appears selective or nonexistent.
When leaders or individual officials misuse authority, violate the law, or infringe civil rights without consequence, it undermines democracy itself.
A democracy cannot function if authority is treated as immunity.
My approach The rule of law applies to everyone—elected officials, political appointees, and individual government employees alike.
Accountability must be lawful, evidence-based, and impartial.
That means independent investigations, enforcement of civil-rights protections, and consequences through established legal process.
Holding individuals accountable for misconduct is not anti-government.
It is pro-democracy.
Lawful authority depends on public trust, and that trust is earned when power is exercised within constitutional bounds—and corrected when it is not.
When warranted by evidence and law, Congress should use constitutional remedies—including impeachment and removal—as safeguards, not partisan weapons.

The problem Washington approves enormous spending packages while public trust declines.
Too often, the details are opaque, oversight is weak, and outcomes are unclear.
When Congress fails at oversight, people reasonably ask: who is this for—and why aren’t urgent needs at home getting the same seriousness?
My approach I believe Congress should apply rigorous oversight to large federal expenditures—especially in defense and security—before approving billions of dollars.
Oversight means accountability, measurable outcomes, transparency, and consequences for waste or abuse.
It also means being honest about tradeoffs and priorities.
This isn’t about turning inward.
It’s about governing responsibly: meeting humanitarian obligations where we can, while also delivering for NJ-03 on affordability, healthcare, infrastructure, and resilience.
