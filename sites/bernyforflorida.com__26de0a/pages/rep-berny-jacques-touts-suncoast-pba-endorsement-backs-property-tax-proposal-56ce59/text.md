TAMPA, Fla. — State Rep.
Berny Jacques defended Florida’s law enforcement policies, highlighted a recent endorsement from a local police organization and argued that local governments should prioritize public safety spending if voters approve a proposed property tax overhaul.
Appearing on Florida’s Voice Radio with Drew Steele, the Republican lawmaker discussed his campaign for House District 59, recent legislative efforts supporting law enforcement and an upcoming campaign event ahead of the November general election.
Jacques said his campaign recently held a “Super Saturday” door-knocking event and announced that he had received the endorsement of the Suncoast Police Benevolent Association.
Jacques, a former state prosecutor, said the endorsement reflected both his legal background and his legislative record on public safety.
“I worked with all of these agencies that make up the Suncoast PBA and enforcing our laws in the trenches, in the courtrooms, to make sure the bad guys that they’re apprehending on the streets are put away for a very long time,” Jacques said.
“I’ve hit the ground running on advancing policies that make their job easier, and that make our community safer.”
Jacques highlighted Florida’s $5,000 signing bonus program for new law enforcement officers and said the state also provides up to $1,000 to help certified officers from other states cover recertification and training costs required to become certified in Florida.
The interview also touched on a proposed constitutional amendment that would reduce and eventually eliminate property taxes.
Jacques said local governments should begin prioritizing core services, particularly public safety, rather than discretionary spending.
“If there’s any reason for government to exist, it’s to keep the people safe,” Jacques said.
“That should be first and foremost.”
Jacques also promoted his next campaign “Super Saturday” canvassing event on Aug. 29, which follows Florida’s Aug. 18 primary election.
Although he does not face a Republican primary opponent, Jacques said the event will focus on preparing for the general election and helping Republicans maintain their legislative majorities in November.
