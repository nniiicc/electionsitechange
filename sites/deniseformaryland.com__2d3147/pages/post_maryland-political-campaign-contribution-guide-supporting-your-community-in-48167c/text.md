Who Can Contribute to a Maryland Campaign?
Eligibility for making a contribution is straightforward but strict to ensure election integrity.
You must be a U.S. citizen or a permanent resident, often referred to as a Green Card holder.
All contributions must come from your own funds.
It's illegal to be reimbursed by another person or entity for your donation.
While there isn't a specific minimum age set by the state for donors, you must have the legal capacity to make a gift and use your own money.
Foreign nationals are prohibited from contributing to any election.
Certain government-regulated industries may also face specific restrictions, so check your status if you hold major state contracts.
Maryland Contribution Limits and Regulations for 2026 The current four-year election cycle concludes in 2026.
During this period, established Maryland campaign finance regulations dictate exactly how much financial support a candidate can receive from a single source.
For the cycle ending in 2026, the maximum Maryland political campaign contribution an individual can make to a single candidate committee is $6,000.
This limit applies to the entire four-year duration.
This means any gifts given in 2023, 2024, or 2025 count toward your 2026 total.
Beyond cash donations, the state also regulates "in-kind" contributions.
These are gifts of professional services, office space, or materials provided at no cost.
If you host a fundraiser at a rented venue or provide printing services for campaign flyers, the fair market value of those items is treated as a contribution.
It's essential to track these values carefully.
They also count toward the $6,000 cycle limit.
While individuals have a $6,000 cap per candidate, Maryland does not currently enforce a total aggregate limit on how much a single donor can give across all different campaigns in a cycle.
This allows you to support multiple community leaders as long as you respect the individual committee limits.
Individual vs.
Entity Contribution Limits Maryland law maintains a level playing field by applying the $6,000 limit to most donors.
This includes individuals, corporations, unions, and political action committees (PACs).
Each entity is treated as a separate donor.
For families, spouses are considered individual contributors.
This means a husband and wife can each contribute up to $6,000 from their own funds, totaling $12,000 per cycle for the household.

Frequently Asked Questions?
Is my Maryland political campaign contribution tax-deductible?
No, your Maryland political campaign contribution is not tax-deductible.
The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) distinguishes between charitable donations to 501(c)(3) non-profits and gifts made to political committees.
While your support is a vital civic investment in the future of District 25, it does not qualify for a deduction on your federal or state income tax returns.
What is the maximum amount I can donate to a Maryland delegate in 2026?
The maximum amount an individual or entity can contribute is $6,000 per candidate for the current four-year election cycle.
This cycle concludes with the 2026 general election.
This limit is cumulative, meaning it includes all monetary and in-kind gifts provided since the cycle began in early 2023.
Once you reach this $6,000 threshold, you cannot make additional contributions to the same committee until the next cycle begins.
Can a business or corporation make a political contribution in Maryland?
Yes, businesses and corporations can legally contribute to campaigns in Maryland.
These entities are subject to the same $6,000 cycle limit as individual donors.
It's important for business owners to track these gifts carefully to ensure compliance with state regulations.
If your entity holds a single state or local government contract of $200,000 or more, you may have additional disclosure requirements under Maryland law.
Will my name and address be made public if I donate to a campaign?
Yes, Maryland law requires the public disclosure of a donor's name, residential address, and employer information for every contribution.
This data is recorded in the Maryland Campaign Reporting Information System (MD CRIS) to maintain transparency in our elections.
This standard procedure ensures all campaigns remain accountable to the public and helps prevent the influence of anonymous interests in our legislative process.
Do I have to be a resident of District 25 to donate to Denise Roberts?
No, you don't have to live within District 25 to support the campaign.
Any U.S. citizen or permanent resident can make a Maryland political campaign contribution to Delegate Denise Roberts.
While our primary mission is focused on the needs of Prince George's County, we value the support of any individual who believes in our vision for legislative equity and community progress.
What is an in-kind contribution and how does it count toward the limit?
An in-kind contribution is a gift of goods or services provided to the campaign at no cost.
Common examples include donating office supplies, providing professional printing, or allowing the campaign to use a venue you own for a community forum.
The fair market value of these services must be reported and counts toward your $6,000 individual contribution limit for the 2026 election cycle.
How do I know if my online donation was processed securely?
We use ActBlue to ensure all online transactions meet the highest security standards.
This platform utilizes advanced encryption and maintains strict PCI compliance to protect your sensitive financial information.
You'll receive an immediate email receipt confirming your transaction, which serves as a reliable record of your support for the Delegate's legislative mission.
Can I donate to multiple Maryland candidates at the same time?
Yes, you can support as many Maryland candidates as you choose.
The state does not currently enforce an aggregate limit on the total amount an individual can give across different campaigns.
As long as you don't exceed the $6,000 limit for any single candidate committee, you're free to invest in multiple leaders who you believe will best serve our state's interests.
