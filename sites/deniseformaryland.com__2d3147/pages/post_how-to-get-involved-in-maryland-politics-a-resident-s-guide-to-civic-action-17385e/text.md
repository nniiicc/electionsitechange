Serving as an Election Judge
Election judges are the backbone of the polling place.
To serve, you must be a registered voter in Maryland and at least 16 years old.
The process begins with a formal application through the Prince George’s County Board of Elections, followed by mandatory training sessions that cover equipment operation and voter check-in procedures.
On Election Day, such as the upcoming General Election on November 3, 2026, judges manage the flow of voters and resolve technical issues.
State of Maryland employees often receive administrative leave to serve in this capacity, allowing them to fulfill their civic duty without sacrificing personal time.
In Prince George’s County, there is a critical need for bilingual judges to assist our diverse population.
This role is paid, but the true reward is the professional pride in facilitating a fair election.

Tracking Bills and Legislative Sessions
The 2026 legislative session is a fast-paced 90-day window, convening on January 14 and adjourning on April 13.
This final day is known as "Sine Die." To stay informed without feeling overwhelmed, residents should create a "MyMGA" account on the official legislative website.
This tool allows you to build tracking lists for specific bills, such as those related to the Maryland Voting Rights Act of 2026.
You'll receive automated email updates as these bills move through the various stages of Maryland's legislative process.
Understanding the rhythm of the session is vital for effective timing.
A bill’s "First Reading" marks its introduction and assignment to a committee.
"Crossover" is the critical deadline by which a bill must pass one chamber to be considered by the other.
Monitoring these milestones helps you time your emails and phone calls for the moments when they're most likely to influence a legislator’s vote.

How to Give Effective Testimony
Public hearings are your opportunity to speak directly to the committees deciding a bill’s fate.
You can testify in person in Annapolis or virtually via Zoom.
Each committee has strict sign-up deadlines, often requiring registration through the MGA website 24 to 48 hours before the hearing.
It's a professional environment, so being prepared is essential.
Oral testimony is usually limited to two minutes.
Structure your comments by stating your name, your district, and whether you support or oppose the bill.
Focus on one or two personal reasons why the legislation matters to you.
Because oral time is short, your written testimony is equally important.
It becomes a permanent part of the public record and allows you to provide the detailed data or personal stories that don't fit into your two-minute speech.
Beyond formal testimony, consider writing a "Letter to the Editor" in local Prince George’s County publications.
Concise, professional emails to your representatives also keep local issues in the spotlight.
Joining an advocacy group or a legislative committee allows you to pool resources with other residents, making your collective voice much harder to ignore.
This collaborative approach is a cornerstone of how to get involved in Maryland politics effectively.

The Maryland Legislative Page Program
High school seniors have a unique opportunity to serve as Pages during the annual legislative session.
To be eligible, students must reside in Maryland and demonstrate strong academic standing.
Pages work directly on the floor of the House of Delegates or the Senate in Annapolis.
Their duties include distributing legislative materials, assisting presiding officers, and delivering messages between members.
Because the selection process is competitive, interested seniors must apply by the fall deadline to serve during the session that begins on January 13, 2027.
This role provides an unparalleled view of how laws are negotiated in real time.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need to be a registered voter to get involved in Maryland politics?
You don't need to be a registered voter to participate in many forms of civic action, such as testifying on bills or joining community advocacy groups.
However, registration is required to cast a ballot or serve as a Maryland Election Judge.
Residents who aren't yet registered can still influence policy by contacting their representatives or supporting candidates through campaign contributions.
It's a welcoming process for everyone.
What is the difference between a State Delegate and a State Senator in Maryland?
Maryland's General Assembly consists of two chambers: the Senate and the House of Delegates.
Each of the state's 47 legislative districts is represented by one Senator and three Delegates.
While both roles involve drafting and voting on legislation, the Senate is a smaller body with 47 members, whereas the House of Delegates has 141 members.
Both chambers must approve a bill before it becomes law.
How do I find out which legislative district I live in?
You can identify your legislative district by using the "Lookup" tool on the Maryland General Assembly website.
Simply enter your home address to see a list of your specific state and federal representatives.
Knowing your district is a vital first step for anyone learning how to get involved in Maryland politics, as it ensures you are contacting the officials who directly represent your neighborhood.
Can high school students earn service-learning hours through political involvement?
Yes, high school students often earn Student Service-Learning (SSL) hours through non-partisan political activities.
Opportunities include serving as a student election judge or participating in recognized community advocacy programs.
Students should verify specific requirements with their school's SSL coordinator before beginning their service.
These roles offer a practical way to build a resume while fulfilling graduation requirements and serving the public good.
What is the Maryland General Assembly session, and when does it happen?
The Maryland General Assembly session is a 90-day period where state legislators meet to pass the budget and debate new laws.
The 2027 session begins on January 13 and concludes on April 12.
Because the session is relatively short, most legislative activity happens quickly.
Staying organized and tracking bill deadlines during these three months is essential for anyone who wants to provide timely feedback on pending legislation.
How can I support a specific bill that is currently being debated in Annapolis?
You can support a bill by submitting written testimony or signing up to speak at a committee hearing via the MGA website.
This is a practical way of how to get involved in Maryland politics during the session.
Contacting your district's representatives through professional emails also makes a difference.
Personal stories help legislators understand the real-world impact of their decisions on families in District 25.
Are there non-partisan ways to get involved in Maryland elections?
Yes, serving as an election judge or a non-partisan voter registration volunteer are excellent ways to support the democratic process without a party affiliation.
You can also participate as a neutral election observer to help ensure transparency at polling places.
These roles focus on protecting the integrity of the vote and ensuring that every eligible citizen has the opportunity to cast their ballot safely and accurately.
How do I apply for a legislative scholarship in District 25?
To apply for a District 25 Legislative Scholarship, you must contact your local Delegate's office directly for an application.
These scholarships are available to residents attending Maryland institutions of higher education or certain vocational schools.
Applications are typically reviewed in the spring; it's best to reach out early in the year to ensure you meet all submission deadlines and requirements for the upcoming academic cycle.
