On the 27th of the 100 Conversations Tour, residents revealed stunning perspectives that keep me up at night 50% RULE & BEYOND One conversation at a time, each voice reflects our community voice toward grounded solutions The Reality Behind the 50% Rule The reality today is this: Many residents are either one emergency away from financial crisis, or are already living in one.
A car repair.
A medical bill.
A rent increase.
A heating bill in winter.
Any one event can push a working family into instability.
The 50% Rule is meant to establish a real affordability benchmark – or affordability stress index – at the state level, which is similar to how we already measure housing affordability.
If basic living costs consume more than half of a household’s income, then government should recognize that residents are under financial pressure and automatically trigger relief measures and policy responses.
That could include: tax relief, energy relief, housing support, transportation affordability measures, and local cost-of-living assessments Government already tracks economic indicators.
But those numbers often fail to reflect what residents actually experience in daily life.
Therefore, 50% Rule says affordability must become measurable, visible, and actionable.

A Mindshift from Spending to Investing There is a fundamental difference between spending and investing.
If you cut a slice of a pizza and it’s simply gone forever, that’s spending.
But if you cut a slice and it helps create a larger pizza in return, that’s investment.

Invest & Grow: People, Infrastructure, and Local Economy • Investing in People We need stronger pathways into stable careers.
That includes: jobready skillsets, skilled trades programs, technology and vocational partnerships, workforce training.
Bottom line: not every student needs a four-year degree.
But every resident deserves a pathway to economic stability. • Investing in Infrastructure Local communities understand their own needs better than distant mandates.
Therefore, infrastructure decisions, including housing, transportation, and development priorities, should involve stronger local-level assessment and accountability, not simply one-size-fitsall state directives we face today.
Because growth works best where local communities have both support and a voice. • Investing in Local Economy Strong local businesses create strong communities.
Based on my listening tour by far, strong local businesses create strong local communities by focusing on the following: small business growth, healthy local environment for local investment, expansion opportunities long term, and grant permission cycles.
A healthy economy is not built only through government programs, but when local communities and businesses can work together with confidence.
Why Political Balance Matters Democracy is described as “two steps forward, one step back.” But that one step can carry real consequences for families, businesses, and communities.
Policies affect affordability, public trust, education, infrastructure, and longterm economy.
That is why healthy political balance matters.
Both democratic and republican party leaders acknowledge that, when one party dominates government for too long, there is a risk that accountability and local perspectives begin to weaken, and that polices can move too far in cone direction without enough challenge or adjustment.
One-party rule can produce major progress when decisions are right, but it can also produce major setbacks when decisions are wrong.
