A Message from Senator Francis Heyliger Born on St.
Thomas, Raised as a Virgin Islander I was born on the island of St.
Thomas, and like so many of us who grew up here, my earliest understanding of home was shaped by the view from our hills, the harbor below, the sea stretching out to the horizon, and somewhere beyond that water, the sister islands that completed us.
I knew from a young age that we had something unique in the Virgin Islands: three distinct islands, each with its own personality, its own rhythm, its own story, and yet, unmistakably, irrevocably, one people.
I was raised to know and believe that our sisters and brothers, no matter which island they call home, are bound to us by something deeper than geography.
We share a history that was written in struggle and survival.
We share a culture that was forged in the crucible of the Caribbean.
We share a future that can only be built together.
The people of St.
Croix are not distant relatives, they are family.
The people of St.
John are not neighbors across the water, they are us.
This is not just a sentiment I carry in my heart.
It is a conviction that guides every vote I cast, every bill I sponsor, every fight I take to the floor of the Legislature.
When I stood up for water safety and ratepayer relief, I was fighting for St.
Croix.
When I championed the unclaimed property database, I was fighting for every Virgin Islander on every island.
When I push for government accountability and economic opportunity, I am pushing for all of us, because in a unicameral legislature, every decision I make touches every corner of this territory.
Our Shared History A Territory Forged in Resilience and Pride The United States Virgin Islands carries one of the most layered and remarkable histories in the entire Western Hemisphere.
Our islands were home to the Ciboney and Arawak peoples long before European contact.
The Taino cultivated these lands and fished these waters for generations before the arrival of Columbus in 1493 changed the course of Caribbean history forever.
Under Danish colonial rule, our islands became a crossroads of the Atlantic world, a hub of trade, sugar production, and, most painfully, the transatlantic slave trade.
The enslaved Africans who were brought to these islands did not merely survive; they built a civilization.
They brought their languages, their music, their spiritual traditions, and their extraordinary resilience to these shores, and in doing so, they created the foundation of the Virgin Islands culture that we celebrate and carry forward today.
The 1848 emancipation on St.
Croix, led by the legendary Moses Gottlieb, known to history as General Buddhoe, stands as one of the most significant acts of liberation in Caribbean history.
It was a declaration, made on our soil, that the people of these islands would not be defined by their bondage but by their freedom.
That spirit of determined self-determination has never left us.
In 1917, the United States purchased our islands from Denmark for $25 million in gold, and in 1927, Virgin Islanders were granted American citizenship.
We have navigated the complex reality of being an unincorporated territory ever since, American in many ways, yet distinctly Caribbean in our soul, our culture, and our identity.
That duality is not a contradiction, it is our strength.
Today, the Virgin Islands stands at a crossroads of its own.
The challenges of infrastructure, economic development, climate resilience, and self-determination demand that we face them together, not as three separate islands, but as one unified people with a shared stake in our collective future.
Our Islands Three Islands, One Heartbeat St.
Thomas The Gateway to Paradise St.
Thomas is the beating commercial heart of the Virgin Islands, a place where the world comes to meet us, and where we have always stood proud.
Charlotte Amalie, our capital city, is one of the most naturally beautiful harbors in the entire Caribbean, a port that has welcomed traders, travelers, and dreamers for centuries.
But St.
Thomas is far more than a harbor.
It is a community of resilient people who have built something extraordinary on a small island, a culture that blends African heritage, Danish colonial history, Caribbean rhythm, and American ambition into something entirely our own.
From the hills of Frenchtown to the shores of Magens Bay, St.
Thomas carries a spirit that is impossible to replicate anywhere else on earth.
St.
John The Jewel of the Caribbean St.
John is nature's masterpiece, a place where two-thirds of the land is protected as a national park, where the turquoise waters of Trunk Bay have been called among the most beautiful in the world, and where the pace of life reminds you what truly matters.
St.
John is not simply a quiet island, it is a deeply intentional community.
The people of St.
John have made a conscious choice to preserve their natural environment, their close-knit neighborhoods, and their unhurried way of life.
Cruz Bay pulses with warmth and creativity, and the trails of the national park tell the story of the Taino people, the enslaved Africans who rose in rebellion at Estate Annaberg, and the generations who have tended this land with love and determination.
St.
Croix The Garden of the Antilles St.
Croix is the largest of our islands and perhaps the most richly layered in history, culture, and agricultural tradition.
Known as the Garden of the Antilles, St.
Croix has long been the breadbasket of the Virgin Islands, with its rolling hills, fertile valleys, and the iconic windmills that still stand as monuments to the resilience of those who worked this land.
Christiansted and Frederiksted are two of the most architecturally significant towns in the entire Caribbean, their colorful colonial buildings lining streets that have witnessed centuries of struggle, liberation, and reinvention.
The people of St.
Croix carry a distinct pride, a deep-rooted identity forged through hardship and triumph, expressed through their food, their music, their festivals, and their unshakeable sense of self.

There is no St.
Thomas vote and St.
Croix vote.
There is only a Virgin Islands vote.
I travel to St.
Croix.
I listen to the people of St.
John.
I carry the concerns of every community into that chamber with me, because I was not raised to think in terms of islands, I was raised to think in terms of people.
