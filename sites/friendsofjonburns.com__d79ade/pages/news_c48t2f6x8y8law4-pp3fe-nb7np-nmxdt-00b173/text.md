State of the Ports On Wednesday, I gathered alongside state leaders and hardworking men and women from across the state to celebrate another record-breaking year for the Georgia Ports Authority.
From Coastal Georgia at the Ports of Savannah and Brunswick to inland facilities as far north as Chatsworth and Gainesville, Georgia’s ports and transportation infrastructure are driving our economy, creating good-paying jobs, and increasing opportunities for hardworking Georgians.
In fact, Georgia’s ports support more than 605,000 jobs across the state.
Whether it’s manufacturers in North Georgia, farmers in South Georgia, small businesses in our rural communities, or the hardworking men and women who deliver those products to their final destination, our ports have created opportunity and delivered an incredible economic impact across Georgia The House looks forward to continuing to work alongside our partners at the Capitol and the Ports Authority to build on our accomplishments and keep our infrastructure strong, reliable, and efficient for years to come.
Speaking at the 2026 State of the Ports in Savannah Georgia Training Center for Industrial Operations Southeast Georgia has attracted thousands of good-paying jobs and new economic opportunities over the last several years.
While we’re proud of the opportunities coming to our region, we also recognize the importance of ensuring our workforce has the skills and training needed to pursue successful careers.
That’s why the Georgia House was proud to invest nearly $30 million in Ogeechee Technical College’s new Training Center for Industrial Operations, which will equip students with the hands-on training and technical skills they need to succeed in today’s advanced manufacturing industry.
