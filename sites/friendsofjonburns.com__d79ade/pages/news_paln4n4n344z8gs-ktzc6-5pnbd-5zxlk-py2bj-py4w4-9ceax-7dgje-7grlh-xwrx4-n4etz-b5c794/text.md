Wildfire + Georgia Forestry Association Jul 31 Written By Emma Nunez Last Thursday, a wildfire broke out in Statesboro that burned nearly 400 acres.
Thanks to the swift and coordinated response of local first responders, firefighters, and state resources from the Georgia Forestry Commission, the fire is now 90 percent contained.
Thankfully, no homes were damaged, and no injuries have been reported.
Extremely hot and dry conditions across Southeast Georgia, combined with debris left behind by Hurricane Helene, have significantly increased the risk of wildfires.
Please remain vigilant, avoid unnecessary outdoor burning, and follow all burn restrictions and safety guidance issued by your local officials.
I want to thank the men and women who worked around the clock to contain this wildfire, the local officials who remained in constant communication with our state emergency management teams, and every business, church, and individual who came together to help their neighbors in need.
Their willingness to step up during a time of crisis is a testament to the character and resilience of our Southeast Georgia communities.
Georgia Forestry Commission crews working to contain the Bulloch Co. wildfire Georgia Forestry Association Conference I was excited to join the Georgia Forestry Association over the weekend for its annual conference, where more than 700 foresters, landowners, manufacturers, and their families gathered to hear from industry and state leaders about the future of Georgia's forestry sector—an industry that has helped shape our state's economy and supported countless communities for generations.
Changes in forest product markets, mill closures, and natural disasters have placed unprecedented strain on the forestry industry, not only here in Georgia, but across the Southeast.
Despite these challenges, we are blessed with dedicated leaders, businesses, and advocates who have worked tirelessly at both the state and federal levels to deliver meaningful support through trade negotiations, favorable tax policies, disaster relief, and strategic investments in the long-term health of Georgia's working forests and forestry industry.
Forestry has long been one of the cornerstones of rural economies, supporting thousands of jobs and strengthening communities across our state.
The House remains committed to working alongside our industry partners to ensure Georgia's forestry industry remains strong and competitive for generations to come.
Speaking to attendees at the 2026 Georgia Forestry Association Conference The House is continuing our work throughout the interim, holding study committee meetings and hearing directly from Georgians about the issues, policies, and priorities that matter most to our communities.
As always, I look forward to hearing from each of you as we continue working together to build an even brighter future for House District 159 and our great state.
Have a great weekend!
