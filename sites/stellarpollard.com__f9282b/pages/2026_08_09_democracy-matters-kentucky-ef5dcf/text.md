Begin their message:
| If you think Democrats are severely outnumbered in the Kentucky State House, the State Senate presents an even steeper hill to climb.
Right now, the numbers in the Senate stand at a staggering 32 to 6.
When a single party holds that level of supermajority control, the legislative process essentially happens behind closed doors.Part of what makes the Senate such a difficult body to flip is how the chamber is structured.
Unlike the House, where every single representative faces reelection every two years, State Senators serve staggered, four-year terms.
Because only half the map is up for a vote in any given election cycle, shifting the balance of power takes a much longer, more deliberate effort.But if we want Democrats to truly take back Frankfort, we have to work on the Senate, too.
These races have been neglected far too long, letting the opposition gain unchallenged power.True balance requires accountability in both chambers, which means showing up for candidates willing to take on these crucial four-year races.
Supporting a Democrat running for State Senate forces the opposition to actually defend their territory instead of just coasting into office.
It’s how we expand the map, break the supermajority, and build power that lasts.As part of our commitment to keeping you informed about the candidates we’re supporting in November, we are highlighting Stella Pollard, who is running for Kentucky’s 20th Senatorial District.
We reached out to her to find out more about her background, her vision, and how she plans to fight for our families in Frankfort. |

| What is an experience from your personal or professional life that shapes how you view public service? |
| I’ve called Franklin County home for over 12 years, but my roots started in Perry County, Kentucky.
Growing up in the mountains was a true blessing, even though my family faced real poverty.
We lived in a home passed down through generations, without central heating or air conditioning, relying on well water and hard work just to keep food on the table.
Public education changed everything for me.
It opened a door of opportunity that altered the course of my life.
I became a first-generation high school graduate, serving as the Valedictorian of Buckhorn High School, and went on to become a first-generation college graduate at HCTCS and Morehead State.
Inspired to pay that gift forward, I became a teacher.
Public education has a powerful ripple effect, and serving our community as an educator has been the greatest honor of my life. |
| What is an urgent issue facing your district, and what is your plan to tackle it, if elected? |
| I am proud of where I come from.
As the daughter of a coal miner and the wife of a third-generation coal miner, I know exactly what it looks like when a massive industry extracts what it wants from a community and vanishes, leaving families to pick up the pieces.
My husband and I had to leave our home just to find a path to success.Today, data centers represent that same familiar threat.
While my opponent actively advocates for data centers and the automation that eliminates local jobs, he is simultaneously writing legislation to sell out our locally owned power plants, water districts, and the infrastructure of Kentucky Wired.
His sudden return to politics after decades of absence seems explicitly tied to these corporate interests.We need a leader who protects our local utilities and economies.
My legislative priority is simple: give our Kentucky towns zoning rights and moratorium authorities at the state level.
Right now, the state legislators are putting all of that pressure on our local officials, which isn’t appropriate, fair, or right for Kentuckians. |
| Frankfort is a tough legislative environment for Democrats right now.
What is your strategy or your track record for fighting for your constituents when the odds are stacked against you? |
| Collaborating is key- we don’t get back to normalcy by being stubborn.
My opponent is already telling people that I wouldn’t be able to work with leadership.
However, after being re-elected for the past four years he’s had only one bill to become law.
My strategy is to work hard, listen to my constituents, and vote to follow their needs.
I’ll earn the trust of my colleagues. |
| What is the main goal your campaign needs to achieve between now and Election Day, and how can we help you achieve it? |
| Volunteers are always welcome.
But helping me meet key stakeholders in any of the towns would be great.
I would love to find a southern Boone Countian to host a fundraiser! |
