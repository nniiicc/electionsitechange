Let's keep it that way.
Connecticut is a "Home-Rule State" and, as such, our state's Constitution guarantees local control under most circumstances when it comes to Zoning, Education, and general Governance.
Like most Newtown residents, I like it that way.
I believe a community thrives when allowed to; and Newtown has largely controlled its own destiny by its own collective definition for the "Character" of our community for more than 300-years.
Lately, "self-determination" has been under attack from the majority party in our own state government.
It's become a core priority of the majority party to proliferate high-density, "affordable" housing to counter decades of slow economic and residential growth.
Majority leadership flexes its muscle to pressure "rank & file" to vote for a turn-over of zoning authority to the state.
In 2025, with a veto-proof Supermajority, legislative Democrats bullied a HB-5002 through the House and Senate, without any consideration for local zoning preeminence, creating a streamlined process for developers to take advantage of the state's "8-30g Affordable Housing Statute" to reduce the burden of proof to favor developers over municipalities in decisions having to do with local zoning.
Having been assured passage, they hurried the bill to Governor Lamont's desk.
Instead of signing the bill, he vetoed it, infuriating Democrat leadership as session ended in early June.
Determined to force high-density into localities' single family, commercial, and even historic district zoned-plots, The supermajority worked behind closed doors with the Governor, and forced a pre-Thanksgiving special-session, reintroducing the concept in a new, more invasive bill, HB-8002, without public hearings, and without disclosing the draft bill to minority Republicans until minutes before the gavel opened special session.
The new bill further diminished the ability of localities to contest developers' plans by referring disputes to a Hartford-appointed Housing Board, denying locality the right to bring suit.
Despite two days of deliberation and attempts to amend the bill, the more intrusive bill passed on party-lines, and Governor Lamont signed it.
Undoing "Local Control" will sidestep the public-process by which we currently determine what we want Newtown to be.
Silencing our community's voice, and undoing over 300-years of give & take will NEVER get my vote.
I can assure you I have and will always vote 'Nay" to state control of local zoning.
I will continue to aggressively defend us from the outside influencers' efforts and majority party's pressure to change the character of single-family-zoned towns across CT, Newtown included.
