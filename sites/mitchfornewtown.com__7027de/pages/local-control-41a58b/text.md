Let's keep it that way.
Connecticut is a "Home-Rule State" and, as such, our state's Constitution guarantees local control under most circumstances when it comes to Zoning, Education, and general Governance.
Like most Newtown residents, I like it that way.
I believe a community thrives when allowed to; and Newtown has largely controlled its own destiny by its own collective definition for the "Character" of our community for more than 300-years.
Lately, "self-determination" has been under attack from the Democrat supermajority in our own state government.
It's become a core priority of the Lamont Administration and the legislature's majority party in the House & Senate has been under pressure to proliferate development of high-density, "affordable" housing to counter decades of slow economic and residential growth.
Majority leadership has tried since 2019 to flex its muscles and pressure "rank & file" majority legislators to vote for various initiatives undermine, or bypass local Planning & Zoning resistance to high-density in single-family zonedto turn-over zoning authority to the state.
After years of not making that happen, It's become a core priority of the majority party to proliferate high-density, "affordable" housing to counter decades of slow economic and residential growth.
In earlier sessions, majority leadership has flexed its muscles to pressure "rank & file" to vote for a turn-over of local zoning authority to the state, without success.
In 2025, with a veto-proof Supermajority, legislative Democrat leaders bullied a heavy-handed affordable housing bill, HB-5002, through the House and Senate, clearing the way to undermine local zoning preeminence with a streamlined process for developers to take advantage of the state's existing "8-30g Affordable Housing Statute" to enabling developers to more quickly and easily take a municipality to court to contest permit denials, favoring developers, shifting burden of proof to municipalities.
Having attained passage, they hurried the bill to Governor Lamont's desk where, instead of signing the bill, he vetoed it, after heavy pushback from municipal leaders.
Furious, majority leadership vowed to rerun, as session ended in early June.
Determined to force high-density into localities' single family, commercial, and even historic-district zones, the supermajority worked behind closed doors with the Governor, eventually forcing a pre-Thanksgiving special-session, to reintroduce the failed concept in a new, more invasive bill, HB-8002, without public hearings, and without disclosing the draft bill to minority Republicans until minutes before the gavel opened special session.
The new bill further diminished the ability of localities to contest developers' plans by referring disputes to a Hartford-appointed board, denying localities the right to bring suit.
Despite two days of deliberation, fillerbustering, and attempts to amend, the more intrusive bill passed on party-line votes in the House and Senate and, this time, the Governor signed it.
Undoing "Local Control" will sidestep the public-process by which we currently determine what we want Newtown to be.
Silencing our community's voice, and undoing over 300-years of give & take will NEVER get my vote.
I can assure you I have and will always vote 'Nay" to state control of local zoning.
I will continue to aggressively defend us from the outside influencers' efforts and majority party's pressure to change the character of single-family-zoned towns across CT, Newtown included.
