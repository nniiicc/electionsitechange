Summary Senator Michael Webber secured millions of dollars in state funding to improve the quality of life for residents in his district.
To help future projects avoid the same challenges he introduced thoughtful reforms to simplify the process.
Background: Why the Community House Matters The Rochester Community House has long been a cherished part of the neighborhood, serving individuals and organizations throughout the Greater Rochester Area.
Over the decades, it has supported thousands of residents, hundreds of charities, and hosted countless events, both private and public.
The Need for Modernization The aging building needed modernization and improvements to improve accessibility for people with disabilities and add more gathering space.
To fund these updates, the city of Rochester applied in 2023 for a federal grant from the Department of Labor and Economic Opportunity, with letters of support from state legislators.
The application was ultimately unsuccessful, so the city had to find other avenues.
How Funding Was Secured Without outside support, the city faced the prospect of burdening residents by bonding for the renovations.
Senator Webber advocated for the facility at the state level, highlighting its role in civic development and local engagement.
He emphasized its important contribution as a key resource and a vital support system for many nonprofits in the area. ​ Senator Webber secured $2 million in the 2025 state budget for the renovation project, and city officials began initial planning.
With the promise of state funds, the city was finally empowered to transform the venue into a vibrant hub that's more open, accessible, and welcoming to more residents.
A Setback: Funding Threatened After more than a year of planning and preparation for the overhaul, city officials were blindsided by actions at the state level that threatened the funding.
The House Committee on Appropriations had clawed back hundreds of millions of dollars designated for previously authorized projects, including funds committed to the Rochester Community House.
The decision threatened to derail the renovation project, which was already well into the planning stage, casting uncertainty over its future.
The Challenge ​ Effectively navigate state government obstacles to preserve the promised funding A New Chapter for a Cherished Institution Senator Webber remained committed to delivering results for his district.
As a longtime Rochester area resident, he understood that the building was more than a line item in a budget.
It is a home for the local organizations that make Rochester a great place to live.
A strong community depends on strong organizations, which rely on dedicated volunteers, generous donations of time and resources, and a place to coordinate vital services for residents.
These organizations are the heart of civic life and make a difference to the well-being of residents.
Taking away their home weakens the very fabric of the community.
Advocacy and Action Webber immediately reached out to his Senate colleagues to restore the funding.
He encouraged them to work together as legislators to create effective statewide fiscal policy.
While they may disagree with some spending items authorized in previous budgets, unilateral cuts undermine trust in the legislative process.
Senator Webber joined other legislators to vote on a supplemental appropriations bill.
The bill brought back funding for various state and local projects and initiatives that had been negotiated and approved in previous state budgets.
The Senate passed the bill with bipartisan support, and he continued advocating for its passage with House colleagues. ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ ​ "He was a key player in securing million-dollar grant funding for the renovation of this beautiful place.
It will be a treasure for generations to come. " Lynn Oates, Board Member Rochester Community House Securing the Future When it came time to approve the final state budget, he proudly cast his vote for a balanced, bipartisan spending plan that included $1.4 million for the Rochester Community House expansion and renovation.
The building’s future is now secure, and its legacy of service to the area will endure.
The unwavering dedication of the civic leaders who cultivated this region into a thriving community will continue to be honored.
What’s Next for the Community House In September 2026, construction crews officially broke ground on the site.
By March 2027, the bigger, better, more functional building will reopen its doors, ready to offer expanded programming and services.
This begins an exciting new chapter filled with promise for a beloved local landmark.
