Summary Senator Michael Webber secured millions of dollars in state funding to improve the quality of life for residents in his district.
To help future projects avoid the same challenges he introduced thoughtful reforms to simplify the process.
Background: Unintended Consequences The I-75 Modernization Project rebuilt a significant length of the interstate highway.
The intended goal was to modernize infrastructure and improve traffic flow.
An unintended consequence was increased road noise from the new paving materials, along with fewer natural noise barriers after the removal of trees that had previously blocked freeway noise.
"When the announcement came out that I-75 was going to go through a big remodel, what we didn’t realize was there was going to be clear-cutting of the trees and other vegetation." Ray Watts, Troy resident Many nearby residents had to endure higher noise pollution in their homes and neighborhoods.
New sound walls were badly needed to replace the natural sound barriers and effectively reduce road noise. ​ Officials from the Michigan Department of Transportation (MDOT) agreed that two segments of I-75 met the criteria for sound abatement; however, MDOT explained that the previously appropriated state funds still fell $10 million short of the $14 million needed to complete the sound walls.
The Challenge ​ The I-75 rebuild increased noise and removed natural sound barriers Residents pushed for more sound walls but faced a $10M funding gap.
The Solution: Results for Residents Recognizing the detrimental effects of noise pollution on residents' quality of life and property values, Senator Webber took action to advocate for his community’s well-being.
Since sound walls can only be funded through direct appropriation from the state legislature, he negotiated across party lines to secure funding in the FY 2024 state budget.
By collaborating with colleagues from both parties, he successfully secured $10 million in new funding, helping to bridge the gap to reach the $14 million needed to build the two sound walls.
Construction on the sound walls started in summer 2026, bringing residents closer to relief from noise pollution.
"He listened to a grassroots group of citizens who brought to his attention that this modernization of I-75 brought noises that nobody expected.
Mike put together a bipartisan effort to get the job done." Ken Shack, Troy resident Lasting Impact for a Better Michigan Senator Webber recognized that this ad hoc budget process was unsustainable; he sought a way to ease the frustrations residents initially faced.
One challenge is that sound wall projects often depend on federal funding, which can take many years to secure.
Michigan law currently lacks a reliable mechanism to fund sound wall construction at the state level.
To address this gap, Webber introduced Senate Bill 643, which would establish a dedicated state fund managed by MDOT to support noise reduction projects.
The bill would enable MDOT to work directly with cities to identify and fund sound wall projects, eliminating reliance on complex federal guidelines.
If enacted, this solution, modeled after an existing law in Minnesota, would streamline funding for future projects, reduce reliance on federal programs, and empower local communities.
A permanent funding system would provide lasting relief from highway noise for people all across Michigan.
Senator Michael Webber’s readiness to collaborate on meaningful solutions highlights his dedication to improving quality of life for his constituents.
By introducing legislation to create a sustainable funding mechanism for future noise-reduction projects, he’s transforming government for the better by reducing reliance on federal funding and empowering local communities.
His proactive approach not only tackles current challenges but also lays the groundwork for lasting solutions, helping to create more livable environments for all Michigan residents.
