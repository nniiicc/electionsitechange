PLATFORM AND PRIORITIES
As an educator, I have seen firsthand the struggles Louisiana families face every day — rising costs, stagnant wages, unaffordable childcare, and underfunded schools.
Too often, Washington prioritizes politics and special interests over the needs of the people.
That needs to change.
As your candidate for Congress in Louisiana’s 4th district, I will fight to put people first by focusing on policies that strengthen working families, support small businesses, and invest in our children’s future.
Affordability
We must make living affordable again.
This means lowering housing costs, lowering the cost of groceries, bringing down gas prices, lowering utility costs, and reducing the cost of childcare.
Supporting Toddlers And Readiness for Tomorrow (START)
Every child deserves a strong START.
The average cost of childcare in America is over $13,000 per year - per child!
But instead of putting that financial burden on families, what if we invested that money right here at home?
High-quality childhood education is one of the strongest economic investments we can make.
Research shows that every $1 invested in early learning returns $7 to $10 through reduced government spending on education and criminal justice, increased tax revenue, and a more productive workforce.
Through the START program, we can invest in our children and young families.
That means $13,000 per year stays in your pocket, your children receive instruction from licensed educators, and you can go back into the workforce sooner.
The Small Business Wage Bridge Act
Give workers what they are owed.
Louisiana is one of only 5 states that does not have a state minimum wage.
The minimum wage in the United States has been $7.25 an hour since 2009.Mike Johnson has opposed raising the minimum wage both in 2019 and 2021 – while he makes $223,500 a year.
Through the Small Business Wage Bridge Act, the federal minimum wage is increased to $15 per hour, but small businesses are protected.
Instead of subsidizing billion-dollar corporations, those subsidies are transferred to small business owner.
That way, the small business owners do not suffer from the increase in minimum wages.
This has been done before in 2007, when Congress increased the minimum wage and paired it with the Small Business Tax Relief Act of 2007.
This Act would build on the structure of the 2007 Act.
Reinvesting in Teachers and Schools
There are serious issues with our education system.
Teachers are overworked and underpaid, Parents are locked out and ignored.
Students have lost all hope that their schooling will help them.
If we want this country to thrive we must change how we fund our schools and teachers AND we must give more power to the community and ensure equal education.
Students in Shreveport should have all the same access and opportunities as students in NYC or LA have.
Our schools must be funded, our teachers must be paid, and our communities must have a say if we want to save education in this country.
No Student Goes Hungry
There are 989 billionaires in America. 989!
In a country with so much wealth, students should not have to go to school hungry.
By strengthening and expanding existing school meal programs, this policy supports student performance, improves long-term outcomes, and provides immediate cost of living relief for working parents.
Research shows that students who have consistent access to school meals perform better academically, attend school more regularly, and are more likely to succeed long term.
Through working with churches, schools, and community groups, our government can ensure that all students get three meals a day with dignity no matter the time of year without having to worry about debt at the age of 8 years old.
Programs like this help to keep kids in school and off the streets, while ensuring they are set up to get a proper education just like everyone else.
Cut Contractors, Support Service Men and Women
Our country has a paradox: we have the largest military budget on the planet, yet our service men and women live in some of the worst conditions on and off their bases here in the states, why is that?
Unfortunately, our government uses that budget to line the pockets of the contractors.
It is time we end exorbitant spending.
We must take away these enormous military contractor budgets, and use the money to support our troops.
Our military should not be ruled by private companies.
We must ensure that families who serve our country are provided or can afford safe housing, access essential services or resources, and maintain financial stability, because military readiness starts at home.
Safety
For far too long, the people of Louisiana have been forced to accept that we aren’t owed safety, that for some reason our voices mean less that big companies and their dollars.
We live in the state that is home to cancer alley.
In Shreveport, Louisiana’s third-largest city, the level of dangerous chemical run off in the river is some of the highest in the country.
I refuse to stand by while our people live in these unsafe conditions.
Safe Water, Reliable Infrastructure
Invest in modernizing water systems, strengthening oversight, and ensuring consistent access to safe reliable drinking water, without shifting the cost onto residents through rising utility bills.
In Northwest Louisiana, water infrastructure failures are not theoretical.
Shreveport’s systems have received failing grades, while residents face rising fees to maintain basic services.
Since 2017, the state’s Safe Drinking Water fee has nearly quadrupled, yet many communities are still dealing with aging and failing systems, reliability issues, and ongoing public health concern due to constant water boil advisories.
Now, with Data Centers and Carbon Capture coming to Louisiana, our water and utilities are in even more danger.
We must fund basic infrastructure at the federal level by balancing the budge and closing the funding gap.
We also must regulate these utility companies who hold monopolies on our water, gas, and electricity.
Finally, we must call for moratoriums on data centers and carbon capture companies until we can guarantee that they will not cause additional harm to our families, our children, our seniors, our land, and our wildlife.
Common Sense Healthcare
Our healthcare system makes no sense.
We have some of the worst coverage on the planet, while we pay more per person than any other country.
Our elders are having to pick between life-saving medicine and groceries.
Parents are having to choose between eating or getting an inhaler for their kids.
We must put a stop to this and create a healthcare system that makes sense.
What does that look like?
It looks like healthcare that is free at the point of service that anyone in this country, no matter how rural, can access without worry.
It looks like the state taking over these big pharma companies to stop them from bleeding us dry.
If we did that, not only would more people have full healthcare access than they do now, we would all be paying less while seeing the doctor more.
We need a healthcare system that protects everyone, but especially our most vulnerable populations (children, seniors, veterans, people with disabilities).
We must address disability support and elderly facility costs, while ensuring healthcare is never tied to employment.
Additionally, bodily autonomy and gender-affirming care must be protected as fundamental rights.
Safe Birth Access
Over the past several decades, hospital closures, staffing shortages, and financial pressures have led to the loss of labor-and-delivery units across rural America.
In Louisiana, more than a quarter of parishes lack adequate maternity care, forcing women, especially in rural areas and around military installations, like Fort Polk, to travel long distances for basic prenatal and delivery services.
We must expand access to OB-GYN and birthing care in rural and underserved areas by increasing provider availability, supporting mobile prenatal services, and creating targeted incentives for healthcare professionals to serve high-need communities.
This ensures that every mother, regardless of location, income, or background has access to safe, timely care before, during, and after childbirth.
When we provide safe birth access, we allow for all families in Louisiana to flourish.
Freedom
In the land of the free, far too many are ignored.
Our government no longer listens to the working class.
Instead, large companies rule our day-to-day lives.
From data centers to big pharma, we are ignored and little by little our freedom is taken away.
It is time we fought back.
We can no longer allow hand shake deals and insider trading in our halls of government.
We can no longer allow rich executives paying us as little as they can.
We can no longer allow the will of the people to be taken away.
This is our government and its high time we took it back from these elites.
Overturn Citizens United
Money has taken over politics.
The 2026 Texas Senate race is projected to cost $446 MILLION in total advertising spending alone!
That is an insane amount of money.
Imagine what else we could do with the money that currently is used in political races.
It doesn’t have to be like this.
In fact, it wasn’t like this before Citizens United Supreme Court decision in 2010 (Citizens United v.
FEC).
We must overturn Citizens United if we want to get dark money out of politics and have politics start working for the people again, as intended.
Establish Term Limits for Members of Congress
Serving in Congress was never meant to be a career.
People serving in Congress should do so to help their communities.
But by having no term limits, we have seen that members of Congress stay in their roles for decades.
Not having term limits allows these party insiders (on both sides) to get into office, build up enough power to stay there, the use the office as a career.
This must stop.
Ban Individual Stock Trading
It should not be possible for members of Congress to get rich off their position.
In addition to overturning Citizens United and establishing term limits, we must also ban member of Congress from trading individual stocks while in office.
Additionally, we need to implement fines and punishments for those who are caught breaking finance laws.
My vision is simple: government should work for the people — not the other way around.
By putting families, children, and communities first, we can build a stronger Louisiana and a stronger America.
