Media Contact: Lenore Goldman Lenore@christian4michigan.com Christian4Michigan.com July 27, 2026 FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE STURGIS TOWN HALL ADVISORY: US HOUSE CANDIDATE VUKASOVICH TO LEAD PUBLIC FORUM ON RURAL HOSPITAL CLOSURE STURGIS, Mich. — As concerns rise about slashed rural healthcare access, Congressional candidate Christian Vukasovich will host a Public Town Hall on the Sturgis Hospital Closing on Tuesday, July 28, at 6:30 p.m. at the Sturges-Young Center for the Arts, 201 N.
Nottawa St.
The forum will connect residents and healthcare workers to discuss the impact of the hospital closure, healthcare cuts, and what can be done, with an open question-and-answer session moderated by Emmy Award-winning television journalist Janet Davies.
Christian Vukasovich is demanding answers and action following the closure of Sturgis Hospital on June 19 after 101 years of service, with 72 hours’ notice.
"We cannot allow Sturgis to become the blueprint for the future of rural Michigan," Vukasovich said.
"Keeping rural hospitals open is not a luxury.
This is a public health and safety crisis.
Every minute matters during a heart attack, stroke, farm accident, or other medical emergency." Current Rep.
Tim Walberg claimed rural hospital closures are a “necessary step” to get the federal budget back on track - a budget off-track due to his vote for deep tax cuts for the wealthiest at the expense of hardworking families, seniors, people with disabilities, and veterans.
Walberg further voted to throw 34,000 residents in his own district off health insurance this January - and over 21 million small business owners, employees, and families off Medicaid and another 12 more off the ACA nationally.
His massive cuts to Medicaid and Affordable Care Act reimbursements are what rural hospitals also rely on.
The closure eliminated all emergency, surgical and outpatient services for the Sturgis community, leaving more than 11,000 residents without a local hospital.
When time is of the essence in seeking medical care, patients must now travel 20 to 30 minutes to reach the nearest emergency room.
“I have Parkinson's.
The idea of having to drive who knows how many miles to get to some other hospital in an emergency is frightening,” said Coral Fry, a native of Sturgis.
“Sturgis Hospital personnel were up to date, kind, and very competent.
Rural hospitals closing is a real travesty in this country.” Sturgis Hospital had already downsized in 2023, converting to a Rural Emergency Hospital model that eliminated inpatient care in exchange for enhanced Medicare payments and a $3.2 million federal facility fee.
Even those measures failed to prevent closure with big cuts ahead.
The consequences extend beyond healthcare.
Sturgis Hospital was one of the community's largest employers leaving 300 community members and neighbors unemployed.
The closure will also impact local businesses that relied on hospital employees and visitors for their livelihood.
A community’s strength depends on a healthy workforce, and in Sturgis, more than 32% of residents rely on some form of government-supported healthcare, such as Medicare, Medicaid, or veterans' healthcare benefits.
50% of Sturgis’ children under 18 are covered by Medicaid, and more than 10% of Sturgis’ residents are uninsured.
High gas prices alone will deter many residents from driving 30 minutes each way for care.
LaGrange, Indiana, is closest to Sturgis, but Medicaid is not transferable out of state, except for extreme emergencies.
In addition, Medicaid cutbacks go into effect in January 2027 as Affordable Care Act rates keep rising for the uninsured.
Finding a wider solution to American healthcare is essential to Sturgis’ survival, in addition to its long term growth.
The closure also places new strain on resources of neighboring hospitals and emergency responders while simultaneously creating transportation barriers for many residents.
Regional transit services often require advance scheduling or do not cross county lines.
Many patients will have few options but higher cost ambulance transportation to facilities 20-30 minutes away.
The impact on patient outcomes can be a matter of life and death.
Sturgis is the fourth rural hospital closure in the United States this year, the fifth in Michigan since 2005.
Vukasovich warned that other rural hospitals across southern Michigan face similar threats as healthcare funding comes under increasing pressure.
"The lesson from Sturgis is clear: when healthcare funding is cut, rural communities pay the price," Vukasovich said.
"These aren't numbers on a spreadsheet.
They are emergency rooms, healthcare workers, and families suddenly forced to travel farther for lifesaving care, not to mention the impact on a small town economy.
Together we can and must change this.” ### Christian Vukasovich Running for U.S.
