About Me I was raised in North Pomfret, Vermont, the only place I’ve ever called home.
I attended The Pomfret School, and I graduated from Woodstock Union High School.
Later, I obtained a bachelor’s degree in Political Science from Norwich University.
After working for a couple years, I returned to school and obtained a master’s degree in climate and environmental policy from Vermont Law and Graduate School.
The affordability crisis is affecting me personally, as many of my friends and classmates have moved away from Vermont; either due to the absence of well paying jobs, or the high cost of living, or both.
Some of my friends, like many young people, who have chosen to stay in Vermont still cannot afford to purchase a house of their own.
This is not a sign of success for the next generation of Vermonters.
Young people, and Vermonters of all ages, by birth, or by choice, cannot afford to stay here.
I will fight for a Vermont which is affordable for young people, farmers, working families, and retirees alike, to stay, and to thrive in this state we love and call home.
My educational focus was in state and local government and policy.
I understand the importance of grassroots politics, and a functional state government which works for all working people.
This includes farmers, and agricultural workers, who are too often forgotten, and who must have a seat at the table.
Agriculture is an essential sector of our rural economy, and must be protected for our state economy to thrive.
I am committed to public service.
I was recently appointed to fill a vacancy on Pomfret’s Selectboard.
I have been a member of Pomfret’s Zoning Board of Adjustment since 2023.
My profession is Planning and Zoning.
I have worked in the field for the past two years.
Our housing market is in crisis, and not from a lack of construction.
Most new houses being constructed right now are second homes, one-bedroom apartments, or short term rentals.
In addition to this, more housing units are being converted into short term rentals, than are being constructed year-to-year.
Housing is no longer easily available to, or affordable for Vermonters.
This is my fifth season as a coach for the Woodstock 5th and 6th grade mountain bike team, and I am a member of the Woodstock Area Mountain Bike Association (WAMBA).
I am honored to be able to volunteer my time to teach the next generation of mountain bikers, and to help this three-season sport and hobby grow.
We must ensure access for three-season outdoor recreation to offset shorter winter seasons.
This will sustain and support local businesses, and will expand, and diversify Vermont’s rural economy.
