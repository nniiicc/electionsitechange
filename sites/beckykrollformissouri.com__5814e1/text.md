Access to healthcare and healthy food isn't a luxury.
It's essential for keeping families and communities strong.
Supporting rural healthcare, protecting access to local food, and making sure people can meet their basic needs helps build a healthier, more stable Missouri for everyone.
Access to healthcare is one of the most basic needs a community can have.
No matter where someone lives, what job they hold, or how much money they make, people should be able to see a doctor when they need one.
Healthcare should not feel like a luxury or something people have to delay because they are worried about the cost.
Healthy families build strong communities, and strong communities build a stronger Missouri.
But healthcare doesn't begin and end at the doctor's office.
Access to affordable, healthy food is also a basic part of keeping people healthy.
Across Missouri, particularly in rural communities, grocery stores and healthcare services are getting farther away.
Hospitals have closed, specialists are scarce, and communities have lost local places to buy groceries.
For someone with reliable transportation, traveling to another town may be an inconvenience.
For an older adult who no longer drives, a person with a disability, or a family already struggling with transportation costs, those miles can become a serious barrier.
This is especially important as Missourians grow older.
We should be helping people remain healthy and independent in their own homes for as long as possible.
When someone can't easily reach a grocery store, a pharmacy, their doctor, or other basic services, staying in the home and community they have known for decades becomes much harder.
Lack of access can mean poorer health, less independence, and a shorter, less comfortable life.
Protecting healthcare and food access is also about protecting our communities.
When a hospital, clinic, pharmacy, or grocery store disappears, we don't just lose a business.
We lose jobs, services, and another reason for families to remain in that community.
I believe Missouri should focus on practical solutions that keep essential services within reach.
That means supporting rural hospitals and healthcare providers, improving maternal and infant health outcomes, strengthening preventive and mental healthcare, and finding ways to support grocery stores and other sources of affordable, nutritious food in communities that are losing them.
Missouri's future depends on healthy people who are able to work, raise families, grow older with dignity, and contribute to the communities they call home.
Your ZIP code shouldn't determine whether you can see a doctor, buy healthy food, or live a long and healthy life.
