You don’t need a peer-reviewed study to know that.

Water is the lifeblood of local farmers, and I see it as the foundation of our community.
Without clean, sustainable water, families and businesses can’t thrive in Madison County.
We must protect this critical resource, and I promise to do everything possible to safeguard our community’s water.
Part of that protection means treating water as a resource to serve Idahoans.
To do that, we must keep EPA overreach out of Idaho.
That means working closely with our local Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ).
We’ve passed legislation that ensures our state agency handles national programs involving Idaho’s water.
Working with DEQ allows us to keep oversight of Idaho’s water where it belongs—in the state.
It means Idahoans work with each other and not some bureaucrat from Seattle telling us what to do.
