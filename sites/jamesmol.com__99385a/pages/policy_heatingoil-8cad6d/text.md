In one Minute Connecticut heating oil averaged $5.88 a gallon in the state’s September 28 survey.
The same week last year it was $3.23.
One 200-gallon delivery now costs about $530 more.
Federal heating aid runs on a stopgap budget through December 11, and the final amount for this winter is decided after the election.
Connecticut’s plan assumes roughly level federal aid while oil rose more than 80 percent. ​ There is no state tax on home heating oil to cut.
It is already exempt.
What there is: two programs that cover oil, and rights as a customer that most people do not know they have.
Get Help Now Energy Assistance (CEAP) For households up to $48,714 for one person, $63,703 for two, $78,692 for three, $93,681 for four, $108,669 for five. ​ Pays $355 to $705 straight to your fuel dealer, plus up to three emergency deliveries of $430 if you run low.
Dealers are paid a set price below retail and cannot bill you the difference.
Program deliveries start November 2. ​ Open now through May 31, 2027.
You have to reapply every year. ​ Apply for Energy Assistance Call 860-560-5800 Generation Power CT Formerly Operation Fuel.
For households just above the CEAP limit, up to 75 percent of state median income. ​ Up to $# once a year toward oil, gas or electric.
Applications are online only, and only during two windows: December 1 to 15, 2026, and March 1 to 15, 2027 ​ ​ ​ ​ Generation Power CT Call 860-243-2345 Your rights as an oil customer Ask for 100 gallons if a full fill is out of reach.
A dealer cannot require a regular customer to take more than 100 gallons, or 75 percent of the tank if that is less, and cannot add a small-delivery fee at 100 gallons. ​ No fee on automatic delivery.
A dealer cannot add a delivery fee to a delivery the dealer schedules. ​ Shop, and order at a quarter tank.

What I will do in Hartford Make sure relief reaches oil customers.
If you heat with gas or electricity and qualify for hardship protection, your heat cannot be shut off from November to May.
If you heat with oil and cannot pay, the truck simply does not come.
Relief should reach people who buy fuel by the delivery, including families just above the limits, where a $500 grant no longer covers a 100-gallon delivery Budget for this winter’s prices, not last winter’s.
