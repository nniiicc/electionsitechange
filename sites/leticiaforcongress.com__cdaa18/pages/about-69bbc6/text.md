Skip navigation menu About Priorities News Endorsements Events Volunteer Donate running for Congress - district 9 Leticia Gutierrez About Priorities News Endorsements Events Volunteer Donate running for Congress - district 9 Leticia Gutierrez Leticia's Story Leticia Gutierrez has spent her life in service to her family, her faith, and her community.
For 45 years, she has called this district home.
It's where she went to school, raised her children, and built a career connecting working families with the leaders who make decisions about their lives.
Her values were forged early.
Leticia's parents worked as farm laborers, and they gave everything so their children could have a better life.
Leticia watched them work from sunup to sundown, and she learned that faith, family, and hard work aren't slogans.
They're how you take care of the people you love.
Leticia graduated from Milby High School and earned a degree in finance from the University of St.
Thomas.
When she became a mother, she set out to raise her two children to walk the same path.
As a single mom, she taught them what her parents taught her: work hard, take care of your family, and never forget your duty of service.
When her oldest son chose to serve our country in the U.S.
Army, she knew those lessons had taken root.
That same son sparked one of her longest-running causes.
When he developed asthma as a child, Leticia learned firsthand how hard it can be for working families to get the care their kids need, and she became a determined advocate for children's health.
Today, Leticia works alongside elected officials and government agencies to deliver results for her community.
She brings decision-makers to the table with the families their choices affect, and she works across party lines to find solutions that help the most people.
She measures success by what gets done, not by who gets the credit.
Leticia doesn't believe life should be easy and that effort is part of success.
But providing for your family shouldn't be this hard.
Across Texas, people are doing everything right.
They work long hours, take second jobs, and play by the rules, yet they still fall further behind.
Paychecks shrink at the checkout line, bills keep climbing, and the ladder of success keeps getting pulled out of reach.
That isn't a failure of effort.
It's a rigged system, and Leticia is running to fix it.
In her Texas, if you put in the work, you get ahead.
