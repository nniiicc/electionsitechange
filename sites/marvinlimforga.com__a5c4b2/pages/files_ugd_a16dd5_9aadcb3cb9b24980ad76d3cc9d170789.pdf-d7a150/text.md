HB 1105 – Georgia Criminal Alien Track and Report Act of 2024 Amends O.C.G.A. §17-5-100 HB 1105, Summary • • WHAT is HB 1105?
HB 1105, titled the "Georgia Criminal Alien Track and Report Act of 2024," amends O.C.G.A. §17-5-100 to enhance enforcement of federal immigration laws. o The bill MANDATES immigration status verification upon an arrest for certain offenses and provides a list of acceptable documents for verification. o Local law enforcement agencies are required to seek agreements to assist federal agencies with immigration laws. o Sanctions are put in place for local governments implementing sanctuary policies. o The bill also mandates the reporting of inmate immigration status in county jails and requires the honoring of immigration detainer notices.
Application of HB 1105 • WHEN an officer MUST verify immigration status?
1.
During the course of any investigation (including traffic offenses) of a criminal suspect by a peace officer, when such officer has probable cause to believe the suspect has committed a criminal violation, the officer SHALL seek to verify the suspect’s immigration status. • This does not apply to victims, witnesses, bystanders, or anyone else who is not a suspect.
Application of HB 1105 • HOW can an officer VERIFY immigration status?
1.
A document issued by a state or federal government of the United States. a.
An original or certified birth certificate, b.
A certification of report of birth issued by the US Dept. of State, c.
A certification of birth abroad issued by the US Dept. of State, or d.
A consular report of birth issued by the US Dept. of State.
2.
A valid Georgia’s driver’s license, 3.
A valid Georgia identification card issued by the Department of Driver Services, 4.
A valid driver’s license from the suspect’s home foreign country (but the officer is to also review said suspect’s visa or passport to verify the validity of license), 5.
A Form 1-94 Arrival/Departure Record issued by US Dept. of Homeland Security, Application of HB 1105 • HOW can an officer VERIFY immigration status?

Questions to Ask Yourself • WHAT SHOULD I DO IF I VERIFY THAT THE SUSPECT IS AN ILLEGAL ALIEN OR I AM UNABLE TO VERIFY STATUS, AND I PLAN ON ARRESTING HIM/HER FOR THE CRIME THEY COMMITTED?
1.
If you have determined that the suspect has committed a crime that requires them to be booked in, then transport the suspect to the Gwinnett County Jail and follow through with the appropriate charges.
Questions to Ask Yourself • WHAT SHOULD I DO IF I VERIFY THAT THE SUSPECT IS AN ILLEGAL ALIEN, BUT I WAS ONLY GOING TO GIVE THEM A CITATION? • The officer MAY take any action authorized by state and federal law, including, but not limited to: 1.
Notify the US Department of Homeland Security, OR 2.
Detain the suspected illegal alien, OR 3.
Securely transport the suspect to any authorized federal or state detention facility. * You shall transport the suspect to the Gwinnett County Detention Center if you receive confirmation that there is an outstanding warrant regarding an immigration violation.
A Note on the Issuance of Certain Citations • A law enforcement officer may arrest a person 18 years of age or older accused of any misdemeanor violation of the below code sections, by the issuance of citation, only after the arresting officer reviews the accused’s criminal record on file with NCIC/GCIC, ensure the accused fingerprints are obtained and shall seek to verify the immigration status of the suspect: o O.C.G.A. § 16-7-21 (Criminal Trespass) o O.C.G.A. § 16-8-14 (Theft by Shoplifting) o O.C.G.A. § 16-8-14.1 (Theft by Refund Fraud) o O.C.G.A. § 16-13-30 (Possession, Manufacturing, etc., of certain Controlled Substances and Marijuana) Questions to Ask Yourself • DOES THIS MEAN I CAN WALK UP TO ANY RANDOM PERSON AND DEMAND VERIFICATION OF THEIR IMMIGRATION STATUS? • NO.
Only those persons who have been identified as a suspect in a criminal investigation and you determine you are going to make an arrest or issue a citation should have their immigration status verified.
HB 1105, In Summary • If you are investigating a crime, • And you believe that a suspect has committed a crime, • And you are going to either arrest them or write them a citation, • Then, you will need to seek to verify the suspect’s immigration status.
Scenario 1 • You detain someone for misdemeanor shoplifting.
The suspect is unable to provide a GA license but provides an international license as identification. o You determine that the individual is in fact the person on the international license, so you know his/her identity. • You determine you are going to issue citations for the shoplifting and plan to release on citation. o You then attempt to verify his/her immigration status, and nothing returns.
You try the Rapid ID fingerprint reader, and no violations return. • Can you still release the suspect on a citation?
Answer • • • • YES.
It is up to the officer's discretion to determine whether to release on citation for an appropriate offense.
As long as there is no indication of an outstanding immigration violation warrant, you may proceed as you would with any other individual. * note: in this case, officers also must review the accused’s criminal record on file with NCIC/GCIC, and ensure the accused fingerprints are on file or are obtained.
Scenario 2 • You arrive on the scene of a domestic.
You determine the wife is going to be charged with Family Violence Battery. o You must seek to verify her immigration status.
She cannot provide a DL or any form of ID. o You attempt to run her on the Rapid ID fingerprint machine.
You still get nothing. o You determine her name and DOB based on what she and others on the scene tell you.
You run her and nothing comes back to assist you further. • What do you do?
Answer • You mark her arrest report as “unable to verify” and you proceed with her arrest and transport her to GCDC.
Scenario 3 • You arrive on the scene of a neighbor dispute.
You investigate and determine that probable cause exists to charge a suspect with criminal trespass, however, everyone has calmed down and the neighbor who originally called the police does not want to prosecute. o You decide you are not making any arrests or issuing any citations. • Are you required to seek to verify immigration status?
Answer • NO. • You are not making an arrest or issuing a citation.
Complete an incident report as you would normally.
