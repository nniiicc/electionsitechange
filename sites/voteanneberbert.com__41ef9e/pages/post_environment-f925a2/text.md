The courage of “&” in environmental stewardship
Utah’s environmental challenges are urgent and tangible.
Prolonged drought, dwindling water supplies, escalating air pollution, and the alarming decline of the Great Salt Lake threaten public health, economic stability, and overall quality of life.
The courage of “&” means recognizing the true costs of environmental action versus the far greater costs of inaction; it emphasizes that responsible stewardship is essential for Utah’s future.
Of particular concern are the underhanded, backdoor MIDA deals that recently occurred with the proposed Stratos data center.
I deplore corruption and value transparency and will be fully transparent: I am currently working to build a business, LightWrk, that aims to use human-reasoning to train robotic models.
But I vehemently oppose the building of this data center in Utah, and that will not change.
Intergrity is doing what is right even if you may benefit from doing what is wrong.
I believe that if humans are intelligent enough to innovate and train AI models, they are intelligent enough to innovate how to build data centers that won't wreck havoc on water, pollution, and energy consumption.
Building technology responsibly must be more important than building it quickly, and the health of humans and the planet must be the foundation on which any technology is advanced.
I am not anti AI when it is used in ways that improve human outcomes.
I have used it in my campaign, my business, and to study for my MBA.
But I will always stand against irresponsible technological advancement that prioritizes greed over safety.
Financial security
Environmental degradation imposes significant economic burdens on Utahns.
The shrinking Great Salt Lake jeopardizes industries reliant on mineral extraction, agriculture, tourism, and outdoor recreation, while escalating public health costs linked to dust pollution and toxic exposure, including arsenic from exposed lakebed sediments, are a pressing concern.
Poor air quality exacerbated by winter inversions leads to increased healthcare spending, decreased productivity, and lower workforce participation.
Addressing these challenges demands immediate investment; delaying action only shifts greater costs to families, businesses, and future generations.
Additionally, the more the lake dries, the more toxic chemicals such as arsenic are released into the atmosphere, impacting not just health and well-being but adjacent home values.
Human dignity
Clean air, safe water, and a healthy environment are fundamental to human dignity.
Communities exposed to toxic dust, polluted air, and unsafe water suffer disproportionately, particularly children, seniors, low-income families, and those with chronic health conditions.
Utahns should never have to choose between economic opportunity and their health or between staying in their communities and breathing clean air.
Protecting dignity means ensuring that environmental risks are not unfairly borne by those least able to mitigate them.
Real solutions
- Restore and stabilize the Great Salt Lake: Commit to measurable water-saving targets prioritizing inflows to the lake, recognizing its critical role in public health, climate moderation, and economic stability.
- Modernize water use across sectors: Update agricultural, municipal, and industrial water practices through incentives, pricing reforms, and infrastructure improvements that reflect Utah’s arid reality.
- Invest in drought resilience and long-term planning: Align growth, land use, and water availability to ensure development decisions remain sustainable and do not exceed resource capabilities.
- Reduce air pollution and mitigate inversion impacts: Strengthen emissions controls, expand clean transportation options, and implement policies to lower particulate pollution during inversion events, protecting public health year-round.
- Proactively address environmental health risks: Monitor and mitigate toxic dust exposure from lakebeds and industrial sites, prioritizing early intervention to prevent long-term health and economic harm.
