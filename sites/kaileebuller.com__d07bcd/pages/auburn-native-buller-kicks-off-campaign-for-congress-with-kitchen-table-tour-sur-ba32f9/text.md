Kitchen Table Solutions Tour Media Blog Issues Volunteer Yard Sign Request Newsletter Sign Up Donate Auburn native Buller kicks off campaign for Congress with ‘kitchen table tour,’ survey Mar 23, 2026 Back to Blog Kailee Buller, an Auburn native who is challenging U.S.
Rep.
John Mannion in the 22nd Congressional District, will kick off her campaign with a “kitchen table tour” in central New York and a survey for voters.
Buller, a Republican, will use the kitchen table tour to hear from voters about their economic challenges.
She will travel across the district, which includes Onondaga and Madison counties, along with parts of Cayuga, Cortland and Oneida counties.
Residents who want to host an event for Buller can fill out a form at kaileebuller.com/tour .
Buller is also asking voters to fill out a survey at kaileebuller.com/affordability .
The survey seeks information about affordability challenges facing 22nd district residents, and their ideas for addressing those economic woes.
“A common thread that weaves us together is the desire to ensure our communities have their basic needs met a roof over our heads, food on the table and the ability to keep our families warm in the winter without breaking the bank,” Buller said.
She added, “These are the issues dominating kitchen table conversations across central New York and ones John Mannion hasn’t listened to or done anything to fix.
That’s why I’m launching the kitchen table tour and focusing this campaign on bipartisan, common-sense issues like affordability and job creation that can move New York back in the right direction.” Buller announced her kitchen table tour on the same day Mannion, D-Geddes, held a town hall meeting in Auburn.
Although an RSVP was required, about 80 people attended the event.
He took questions submitted on index cards, as well as direct questions from the audience.
Among the topics covered at the town hall meeting were utility costs, the war in Iran, immigration and the Republicans’ election legislation.
Buller, a 2007 Auburn High School graduate, resigned from her job as chief of staff to U.S.
Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins to move back to central New York and run for Congress.
She has worked in the Washington, D.C. area for the last 15 years, including jobs with the U.S.
Department of Agriculture in both of President Donald Trump’s administrations.
