Kitchen Table Solutions Tour Media Blog Issues Volunteer Yard Sign Request Newsletter Sign Up Donate New Congressional Testimony: Mannion Policy Makes Housing Crisis Worse Sep 10, 2026 Back to Blog Mannion admitted Albany’s gas ban would raise housing costs, then voted for it anyway September 10, 2026 Auburn, NY — Kailee Buller, candidate for New York’s 22nd Congressional District, is calling out Congressman John Mannion after new testimony before Congress warned that New York’s restrictions on natural gas in new construction are making an already severe housing affordability crisis worse.
Michael Fazio, executive director of the New York State Builders Association, testified that fully electrifying a new home in colder climates can add more than $15,000 in construction costs, pricing families out of qualifying for a mortgage.
John Mannion knew that would happen.
In 2023, while serving in the State Senate, Mannion warned that New York’s push to ban natural gas in new buildings would increase housing costs.
“People need access to propane and natural gas to keep warm through Central New York winters, and banning gas stoves and gas heating, even if only in new buildings, will drive up costs during a housing crisis,” Mannion said at the time.
Then Mannion voted for for A.3006C / S.4006C , legislation that contained the very policy he warned would raise costs.
The legislation prohibited fossil-fuel equipment and building systems in most new construction, including systems used to supply or deliver fossil fuels.
“John Mannion knew this policy would make housing more expensive.
He said it himself, then turned around and voted for it anyway,” said Buller.
“Now New York home builders are telling Congress exactly what families are paying for that mistake.
It’s why when I’m elected I’ll cosponsor the bipartisan Energy Choice Act, which home builders are advocating for, to protect energy choice and lower costs.” Background: Kailee Buller is an Auburn native, working mother of three, a former Chief Executive Officer, and most recently served as Chief of Staff at the U.S.
Department of Agriculture, where she helped lead a department of more than 100,000 employees and a budget exceeding $200 billion.
She is running for Congress to lower costs, support working families and farmers, and bring accountability and common sense back to Washington.
