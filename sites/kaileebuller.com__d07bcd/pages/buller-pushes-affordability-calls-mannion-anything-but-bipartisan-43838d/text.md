Kitchen Table Solutions Tour Media Blog Issues Volunteer Yard Sign Request Newsletter Sign Up Donate Buller pushes affordability, calls Mannion ‘anything but bipartisan’ Mar 23, 2026 Back to Blog Kailee Buller has announced her candidacy for New York’s 22nd Congressional District, challenging Rep.
John Mannion and criticizing him as overly partisan while promoting affordability for farmers and bipartisanship.
Buller is running with the backing of Republican and Conservative Party County Chairs, but says she intends to run a bipartisan campaign and play a bipartisan role if elected into this position.
Born and raised in Auburn, Buller says she pursued a career out in Washington D.C. due to the poor job market after her graduation from SUNY Buffalo in 2011.
That decision led her to a career in federal food and agriculture policy, working in high positions at the U.S.
Department of Agriculture under both Trump administrations.
She says she’s taking the experience she has in that industry to fight for affordability for Central New York farmers and families.
“This district is a very middle- to low-income district,” Buller said.
“There’s a lot of farmers in this district and I feel like the experience I have, has aligned me to be the best advocate for this district.
I understand how D.C. works, understand farmers and understand how some of these welfare programs work.” Within the issue of affordability, Buller said the solar power issue being debated across New York is one of the reasons utilities have gotten so high for the average resident.
According to Buller, subsidization of solar panels and other forms of renewable energy help the production and parent companies in places like China and Denmark, not American industry.
“John Mannion was in when a lot of these terrible climate policies got passed,” Buller said.
“These aren’t even American companies, so we’re using New York taxpayer dollars to subsidize windmills and solar panels that are predominantly made overseas.” As a major component of her campaign, Buller is embarking on her Kitchen Table Tour around NY-22 to meet with the residents affected by these issues and federal policies.
She’s emphasizing her plan to be bipartisan in this role, something she critiques about her opponent.
“Mannion has been everything but bipartisan,” Buller said.
“He’s been a party line voter and he hasn’t even voted for his own earmarks in the past.
He’s not what Central New York needs.” Buller will be using her Kitchen Table Tour and campaign to meet the people she plans to represent, taking the time and interest in their concerns about the district and the state.
“I want to meet with people who want to tell me their story and what they would propose for solutions,” Buller said.
“These are complicated challenges and we all have to work together on them, but I want to start with the people that live those challenges.” Buller will also be present at the CNY Maple Festival in Marathon April 18 and 19 to meet her potential constituents in Cortland County.
