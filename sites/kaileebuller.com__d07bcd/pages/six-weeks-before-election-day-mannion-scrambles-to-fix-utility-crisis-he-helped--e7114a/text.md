Politico called energy costs the biggest issue in the race for New York’s 22nd Congressional District.
Mannion’s record explains why: He voted for Albany’s gas ban.
In 2023, Mannion warned that banning gas in new buildings would drive up costs.
Then he voted for it anyway .
He defends the climate law.
Mannion has praised the law’s “lofty goals” even as a state memo projects it will add $4,000 a year to household energy bills.
He looks to China.
Just last week, Mannion pointed to China’s solar buildout as the model for America.
Instead of fixing the problem, he spent taxpayer dollars on a survey.
Mannion spent at least $10,000 in taxpayer-funded ads asking constituents how much their bills went up.
He never released the results.
In April, Buller released a five-point plan to lower energy costs.
The plan calls for full transparency into National Grid and NYSEG’s foreign ties, repeal of Albany’s climate law, an end to the fracking and gas bans, fast-tracked nuclear, and permitting reform to build pipelines like Constitution.
Kailee Buller said, “John Mannion helped create this utility crisis in Albany.
Now, six weeks before Election Day and on his way out of town, he’s scrambling to fix the problem he created.
I’m the only person in this race with an actual plan to bring costs down and clean up John Mannion’s mess.” About Kailee Buller: Kailee Buller is an Auburn native, working mother of three, a former Chief Executive Officer, and most recently served as Chief of Staff at the U.S.
Department of Agriculture, where she helped lead a department of more than 100,000 employees and a budget exceeding $200 billion.
She is running for Congress to lower costs, support working families and farmers, and bring accountability and common sense back to Washington.
