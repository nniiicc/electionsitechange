Providing Oversight to Ensure Public Dollars Are Spent Wisely on Infrastructure
Minnesota invests billions in roads, bridges, clean water treatment, and transit systems.
However, funding projects is only half the battle; ensuring those dollars are spent efficiently, transparently, and without costly contractor loopholes is what truly protects taxpayers.
Moving from short-sighted political budgeting to industry-standard engineering lifecycle asset management is essential for our state’s fiscal health.
As a licensed Professional Engineer (PE) who founded and ran a civil engineering firm for nearly three decades, Senator Ann Johnson Stewart brings hands-on construction management experience to the Minnesota Senate.
She understands blueprints, bidding schedules, lifecycle costs, and material specifications—applying accepted civil engineering standards to ensure state investments deliver maximum durability for every tax dollar spent.
Industry-Standard Lifecycle Cost Analysis (LCCA)
When government only looks at the initial price tag of a project, taxpayers end up paying the price for decades.
According to the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA), engineering benchmarks demonstrate that every $1 spent on routine asset preservation saves $6 to $10 in future emergency reconstruction costs.
To protect public dollars, Senator Johnson Stewart advocates for the American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE) Grand Challenge standards, which mandate the use of Life-Cycle Cost Analysis (LCCA) across all public works.
- Auditing the “Below-the-Surface” Costs: Using LCCA to account for 30-to-50-year operational, maintenance, and rehabilitation costs before approving capital appropriations.
- Closing Change-Order Loopholes: Vetting technical specifications upfront to eliminate ambiguous project scopes that trigger expensive contractor change-orders mid-construction.

Fiduciary Accountability on the Capital Investment Committee
The Minnesota Section of the ASCE gives Minnesota roads a D+ and our overall infrastructure a C, emphasizing that stop-and-start, unpredictable funding cycles cause public assets to deteriorate faster than they can be repaired.
Serving on the Senate Capital Investment (Bonding) Committee, Senator Johnson Stewart applies formal Value Engineering (VE) principles to analyze project functions and eliminate unnecessary capital expenditures without sacrificing safety or performance.
- Vetting True Shovel-Readiness: Auditing engineering readiness, environmental reviews, and matching fund commitments before allocating state bond proceeds to local projects, as mandated by the Minnesota Management and Budget (MMB) Capital Budget Instructions.
- Prioritizing High-Risk Deficiencies: Directing capital investments to the state’s most vulnerable systems—including bridges operating beyond their 50-year design life and mandatory pipe replacements required by the Statewide Lead Pipe and Service Line Replacement Mandate.
- Maximizing Federal Return on Investment (ROI): Structuring state bonding to aggressively secure federal matching funds through the MnDOT IIJA Discretionary Match Program, ensuring Minnesotans get their federal tax dollars back.
Commercial & Industrial Development Accountability
Infrastructure oversight also means ensuring that massive corporate developments do not overwhelm our local grids and municipal pipelines.
ASCE and Minnesota Department of Natural Resources (DNR) hydrological permitting standards mandate that heavy commercial users demonstrate zero negative impact on local resources before receiving high-capacity utility connections.
- Data Center & Utility Oversight: Enforcing technical standards so high-demand facilities invest in dedicated grid infrastructure and separate groundwater permits, rather than shifting peak-demand electric costs or water shortages onto local homeowners.
- Climate Resilience & Public Drainage: Requiring municipal drainage networks to meet updated NOAA Atlas 14 Precipitation Frequency Estimates to prevent washouts and downstream stormwater damage.
- Skilled Labor & Construction Quality: Partnering with union apprenticeships and enforcing prevailing wage standards to ensure public projects meet strict engineering tolerances, preventing premature structural failures.
