FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE Official Statement from Rob Rush, candidate in the Georgia 14th Congressional District Election.The United States is now involved in a war with Iran.
And Congress still has not authorized it.
Rising Fawn, Georgia March 5th, 2026 On March 4th, the United States Senate voted on a War Powers Resolution that would have required congressional approval before the United States could continue this unauthorized war with Iran.
That resolution failed.
The vote fell largely along party lines, leaving the President free to continue military operations even though Congress has not declared war.
That should concern every American.
The Constitution is clear: the power to declare war belongs to Congress — not the President.
The Founders placed that authority in the legislative branch specifically to prevent any single leader from dragging the nation into war without the consent of the American people.
Congress had an opportunity to assert that authority.
It failed.
Instead of rising above party politics to defend the Constitution, Washington once again demonstrated how dysfunctional both the Democratic and Republican parties have become when it comes to one of the most serious responsibilities government has — deciding when Americans go to war.
The administration has said the U.S. strike was carried out after Israel launched its own pre-emptive attack on Iran, and that American action was taken partly out of concern that Iran might retaliate against U.S. forces or interests.
However, according to multiple media reports citing officials briefed on U.S. intelligence, lawmakers were told that intelligence agencies had no indication Iran was preparing a pre-emptive attack on the United States before the strikes occurred.
Decisions that could lead to war should be based on verified intelligence and debated openly in Congress — not speculation or assumptions.
Americans have seen what happens when intelligence surrounding war is rushed or misrepresented.
In 2003, the United States was led into the Iraq War based on claims that Saddam Hussein possessed weapons of mass destruction.
Those weapons were never found.
The result was a war that cost trillions of dollars, destabilized an entire region, and cost thousands of American lives.
That history should make every American cautious when new wars are justified without clear evidence and without Congress fully debating the facts.
Sending American troops into combat should never be treated as a partisan issue.
It is a constitutional duty.
If American lives are going to be placed at risk and billions of taxpayer dollars committed to another Middle East war, Congress must debate it openly and vote on it.
And there is another reality Americans need to understand.
Wars like this do not just cost lives overseas.
They cost trillions of dollars.
The Iraq War alone ultimately cost American taxpayers trillions of dollars.
Imagine what that kind of investment could have meant for communities across Northwest Georgia and the 14th District.
Better infrastructure.
Lower energy costs.
Support for farmers and small businesses.
More resources for veterans.
Lower taxes for working families.
Instead, when wars escalate, families here often feel the impact immediately.
Instability in the Middle East drives up oil prices.
Higher oil prices lead to higher gasoline prices.
Higher fuel costs increase transportation costs.
Transportation costs drive up the price of food, goods, and services.
That means working families, farmers, truck drivers, and small businesses in Northwest Georgia often end up paying the price.
Congress attempted to act on March 4th — and failed.
That cannot be the end of the story.
Congress must continue pushing to reclaim its constitutional authority under the War Powers Resolution.
The American people deserve transparency, accountability, and a voice through their elected representatives before our nation commits to another costly war.
No President — Republican or Democrat — should have the power to wage war without Congress.
The Constitution must come before politics.

Ruszkowski cited growing warnings from leading AI executives that advanced AI systems may outpace human control — combined with mounting strain on America’s already fragile infrastructure.
“Humanity is about to be handed almost unimaginable power, and it is deeply unclear whether our social, political, and technological systems possess the maturity to wield it,” warned Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei.
Rob Rush says that statement alone demands immediate action.
“When the very CEOs building this technology admit we may not be mature enough to control it, that’s not innovation — that’s Russian roulette with civilization.” A One-in-Six Risk Is Not Acceptable Multiple AI researchers have publicly acknowledged measurable probabilities of catastrophic outcomes from uncontrolled AGI systems.
“If credible experts are discussing even a one-in-six chance of civilizational collapse, that risk is unacceptable,” Ruszkowski said.
“We regulate nuclear weapons.
We regulate biological weapons.
AGI belongs in that same category of oversight.” AGI refers to systems capable of reasoning, strategizing, and acting autonomously at or beyond human-level intelligence.
Once operational, such systems may operate beyond meaningful human oversight.
Infrastructure Crisis: Power, Water, and Cost of Living Rob Rush emphasized that beyond existential concerns, AI expansion is already imposing real-world burdens on American communities.
Massive AI data centers require extraordinary amounts of electricity and water for cooling and operation.
Many are being constructed in regions with aging power grids and limited infrastructure capacity.
“Our power grid is antiquated.
It is already strained.
We are seeing brownouts, grid instability, and deferred maintenance across the country,” Ruszkowski said.
“Adding energy-intensive AI centers without modernizing infrastructure first is reckless.” He continued: “Energy demand from these facilities is driving up electricity costs for working families.
People are struggling to pay their power bills while billion-dollar AI campuses consume the equivalent electricity of small cities.” Rob Rush is calling for: • A temporary federal pause on new AI data center construction • A national infrastructure impact assessment of existing AI facilities • Public transparency regarding water usage and grid strain • Protections to prevent AI expansion from inflating residential energy costs • Infrastructure modernization before any large-scale AI expansion proceeds “No American family should see their electric bill rise so a handful of corporations can chase technological supremacy,” he said.
Not Anti-AI — Anti-Oligarchy Rob Rush made clear he is not calling for a shutdown of current AI tools used productively.
“I support responsible AI that assists medicine, research, logistics, and productivity.
But crossing into Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI) and autonomous agents capable of independent strategic action is fundamentally different.” “This technology was built on humanity’s collective knowledge.
It does not belong to a small group of billionaires racing each other for dominance.” If It’s National Security — Why Is It Private?
National leaders have acknowledged that advanced AI is a matter of national security.
“If this technology has existential and national security implications, why is it controlled by private corporations?” Rob Rush asked.
“Why are Unelected Executives determining the Future of Human Civilization?” He is calling for: • An immediate federal pause on AGI/ASI development • A halt to autonomous AI agents capable of independent strategic action • Congressional hearings under oath with AI executives • A national security review of AGI/ASI - level research • Development of a democratic federal regulatory framework • Serious consideration of public or national oversight of AGI/ASI research A Democratic Future — Not a Machine-Dominated One “This is not science fiction,” Rob Rush said.
“The builders of this technology are warning us themselves.” “The decision to create something potentially more intelligent than humanity must not be made in private boardrooms.
It must be made democratically — or not at all.” “We will not gamble the human race.
