Source Georgia continues to debate how state elections are conducted and every year there is new legislation on this topic.
What changes to our current election system do you support, if any?
Jeff Newlin Georgia’s elections are run by our 159 counties and overseen by the Secretary of State.
The legislature’s job is clear rules and the resources to follow them.
SB 202 moved us forward.
The next step is the one the General Assembly set in June’s special session: replace QR-code ballots with hand-marked paper ballots by 2028.
I support that.
Nearly 70% of Americans already vote this way.
The voter fixes any error at the scanner before leaving the precinct, instead of a poll worker guessing intent after the polls close.
The state should cover the counties’ equipment costs so it does not burden the local taxpayer.
I would keep absentee ballots for those who need them: the elderly, the disabled, and military and overseas voters.
We have nearly three weeks of early voting, including weekends.
So the legislature should review how broadly mail-in ballots go to everyone else, and keep SB 202’s ID requirement for every mail-in ballot.
Voters should want to cast their ballot where they can correct it on the spot.
A mailed-in ballot is final once sent.
I fully support proof of citizenship at voter registration and putting citizens-only voting in our constitution, the amendment the Senate let die in June.
Our current senator voted against SB 202, the QR-code ban, and this year’s paper-ballot law.
Let’s get the process into law and expect every official to follow it.
Candidates need to accept the certified results and bring an end to result litigation.
Source Sen.
Kim Jackson It is important for readers to understand that Georgia's current election system is both secure and accurate.
People across the state have access to the ballot on weekends, weekdays, and by absentee ballot.
That said, I deeply understand that there are people throughout Georgia who have misgivings about the presence of QR codes on their ballots.
Therefore, I do support that most recent change that will remove QR codes in future elections, and I look forward to working with the new Secretary of State as we further refine our elections processes in the future.

Pharmacists and doctors who follow the rules would be protected from lawsuits and discipline.
