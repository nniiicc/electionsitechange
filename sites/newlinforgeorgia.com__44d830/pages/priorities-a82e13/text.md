Cap property tax increases and expand homestead exemptions for seniors, veterans, and those disabled.
Get every Georgian into the workforce with good-paying jobs, and for those already working in dead-end jobs, provide avenues to get training for higher-paying jobs such as technology, healthcare, and the trades.

Georgia reached its 4.99% income tax target this year, 3 years early.
The next cuts should follow the state's revenue safeguards on the road to eliminating the income tax.
I will vote for every responsible income tax cut that reaches the Senate floor.
Until the income tax is fully eliminated, I will help those who are working but struggling to pay high property taxes by indexing family income to property taxes to provide income tax deductions.
Our senator has voted NO on every income tax cut that reached the floor: in 2024, in 2025, and this year's cut to 4.99%.
She led the opposition to SB 476, the bill to eliminate the state income tax on the first $50,000 for working Georgians, and called it "a scam" on the Senate floor.
In her own op-ed she proposed a graduated income tax instead, with a 6% rate on top earners, above today's 4.99% flat rate.
That is the choice in this race.
I will vote to let you keep more of each paycheck.
She has voted to take more of it.
On affordability, we need to start with the bills families cannot escape Start with the bill families cannot escape: the property tax.
DeKalb’s tax digest grew 50% in five years while wages grew about 19%.
About half of the bill goes to a school system with 18,000 empty desks.
I will fight for property tax caps, larger homestead exemptions for seniors and veterans, and a freeze for homeowners on fixed incomes.
Beyond property taxes, I’ll support every responsible income tax cut that reaches the floor so working families keep more of each paycheck.
I’ll work to stop legislation that adds to the cost of living.
I’ll keep data center power costs off residential bills.
I will oppose any PSC rate structure that makes families subsidize industry.
Insurance and housing costs need the same scrutiny: more supply, fewer mandates that drive up premiums and rents.
Lastly, let people work.
Georgia is the top state for business 12 years running.
Yet a nurse, barber, plumber or contractor could wait months for a license because the old software failed and boards moved slowly.
This year’s HB 1254 started fixing that.
Finish it, fully honor licenses from other states, and drop license requirements that no longer protect anyone.
A growing paycheck with less taken by the state and local government is the best affordability program there is.
Every dollar government spends should be tied to an outcome families can see, not a press release.
If we focus more on jobs and prosperity and less on affordability, we’ll eliminate the affordability issue.
We have a multi-generational problem to solve, and we need to start now Affordability is the most urgent challenge facing Georgia families.
Property taxes and the cost of living are pushing working families and seniors out of communities they have lived in for decades.
In DeKalb County the tax digest grew 50% in 5 years while wages grew about 19%.
If the state does not act on meaningful tax relief, income tax reductions, homestead exemptions, and property tax freezes for seniors, we will lose the middle class families that hold our communities together.
Education and workforce development are the challenge that will define the next decade and beyond.
Georgia is attracting major employers in manufacturing, technology, and logistics, but we are not producing enough skilled workers to fill those jobs.
If we cannot supply those workers, businesses will look to other states that can.
We need to get our schools back to the basics so that students enter high school proficient in reading and math and understand personal and family budgeting.
We need to invest in trades training, technical education, and apprenticeship programs so that every young Georgian has a clear path to a good career, whether or not they pursue a 4-year degree.
Without well-paying jobs with a career path, affordability will never be solved.
Individual and family prosperity is the best solution to the affordability problem.
Public safety and infrastructure round out the picture.
Communities that do not feel safe do not grow.
We need a plan for roads, public transportation, rapid transit, water infrastructure, and a power grid that can handle the demands of a strong, growing economy without adding more costs to Georgia families.
We need to be good stewards of your money Balanced budgets are how families and well-run businesses operate, and Georgia’s agencies can too.
Georgia entered this budget year with $14.6 billion in reserves after years of surpluses.
Those reserves matter: they paid for Hurricane Helene recovery and this spring’s $35 million wildfire fight, covered almost $5 billion in extra road funding as GDOT’s construction costs jumped 70%, and fill gaps no one can plan for.
The problem is not too little revenue; it is spending measured by inputs instead of results.
When this year’s income tax cut opened a $1.3 billion gap, the governor trimmed $300 million in new spending and told agencies to hold the line.
That is the right approach; make it routine.
My plan is to eliminate waste and fund core programs: public safety, roads, schools that perform, and the caseworkers and nurses the state cannot do without.
Hold the rest to zero-based review every budget cycle.
Use one-time surplus money for one-time needs such as the paper ballot system due by 2028 and school capital, never for recurring programs.
Finish the HB 1254 licensing overhaul so Georgians get to work faster with less paperwork.
Post every agency’s results online for taxpayers.
Georgia reached its 4.99% income tax target this year, three years early.
The next cuts should follow the state’s revenue safeguards on the road to eliminating the income tax, and a leaner state’s savings should go to property tax relief, where District 41 homeowners feel the squeeze most.
I will not support tax breaks for corporations that leave Georgia families paying more.
Data centers and other privately funded projects will not get water or power cost breaks that end up on your bill.
Jobs and Education Support parents' ability to choose their child's school.
Measure results and put administrators of failing schools on notice to improve or they will be replaced.
Expand career and technical education and partner with employers to shape courses for the workforce they need and offer work-study jobs to prepare students for jobs immediately after graduation.
Make the skilled trades a first choice, not a fallback.
Read my full position on Jobs and Education Support parents' ability to choose their child's school Parents know their children best.
I support school choice: a parent should be able to choose the public, charter, private, or home school that fits their child, and the money should follow the student.
Georgia took a real step in 2024 with the Georgia Promise Scholarship Act, SB 233.
It gives families zoned for a school in the lowest-performing 25% of Georgia's public schools $6,500 a year for tuition, fees, books, and tutoring, with priority for lower-income families.
It started with the 2025-26 school year.
Our senator voted against it, twice.
I will protect and expand the Promise Scholarship, back public charter schools, and keep the influence over our schools with Georgia parents, not the associations and lobbyists who fight every choice program.
We need to make our schools accountable for educating our kids During my 37 years in business, I never saw a company tolerate leaders who kept missing their defined objectives, and employees who could not meet the expectations of their job were replaced.
Our schools should be no different.
Set high standards and measure who is meeting them.
Here is what I would do instead.
Set Georgia-wide performance standards, and tie meeting those standards to every administrator and every teacher.
Identify failing schools by name.
Put their administrators on a Georgia Department of Education remediation plan with deadlines and measures, and if the school fails to come up to standards, replace the administrators.
Georgia already formally reviews each teacher annually.
We need to act on the TKES results: make them public, put a teacher who ranks low on a GDOE-defined performance improvement plan, and remove any teacher who does not improve under that plan.
DeKalb County spends $1.6 billion a year and graduates students below the state average from eight of the ten high schools serving District 41.
The problem is not the map.
It is that no one is held accountable for the results.
And the influence on our schools should come from Georgia parents, not from the teacher and administrator associations and their lobbyists.
Expand career and technical education Career and technical education works.
Georgia's CTAE students graduate at 98%, well above the state average.
Students choose from more than 150 career pathways, earn industry credentials, and get real work experience through internships and apprenticeships while still in high school.
DeKalb already has 23 industry-certified programs at 13 high schools, including Tucker, Stone Mountain, Redan, Chamblee, and Dunwoody.
That is a start, not a finish.
Georgia has only 65 available workers for every 100 open jobs, and for every 5 tradespeople who leave the trades, only 2 are coming in.
Electricians, plumbers, welders, and HVAC technicians are in short supply.
Those careers pay well, come with health coverage, and will not be replaced by AI.
I will expand these programs to every high school serving District 41, bring employers in to shape the courses and offer work-study jobs, and make the skilled trades a first choice for our students, not a fallback.
Every student deserves a real path to a career, college or not.
Data centers create jobs today and build careers for the future By reading this, you are benefiting from a data center somewhere.
Every click, swipe, and show you stream requires a data center.
Are you ready to give this up?
As a former executive at the largest technology provider in building materials, now leading Georgia’s ready mixed concrete association, I have worked where technology meets construction every day.
The discussion should not be Yes or No to data centers, but how best to build out the computing power America needs to thrive and compete.
Data centers matter.
Georgia can lead the nation.
But growth can’t come at the expense of families paying higher power bills and property taxes.
Families should not foot the bill for billion-dollar tech companies.
Private money is there.
Let these companies pay their own way and pay a premium for power and water consumption; subsidies are not necessary.
Building data centers creates high-paying jobs for Georgia’s trades workers: electricians, pipefitters, ironworkers, plumbers, concrete crews.
We should pair this growth with training programs that benefit Georgia workers and create career paths well beyond the building of a data center.
On where they’re built: local governments should keep zoning control.
Communities like Ellenwood deserve a real voice before projects are approved.
Reviews of water, energy, noise, and property values should be required, not optional.
Don’t block data centers.
Build them right, with private companies paying for the impacts they create.
Healthy, Safe, and Strong Communities Build a foundation for strong families through careers that begin immediately following high school.
Make healthcare easier to access and afford, mental health included, with a safety net for those who need it and a path to a career with coverage.
Support law enforcement at every level of government and respect their sworn duty to prevent, detect, investigate, and apprehend those who violate state and federal criminal law.
Read my full position on Healthy, Safe, and Strong Communities Strong families are the key to strong communities We are not talking enough about the problems caused by the erosion of the family and of individual responsibility.
Too many young men are not working.
In DeKalb County, 12% of men aged 20 to 24 are unemployed.
That is more than 2 times the rate for young women in DeKalb, and well above the 8% rate for men statewide.
1 in 4 of these young men is not working at all.
There is not enough focus on this, and it contributes greatly to the problems we spend so much time debating: crime, affordability, healthcare costs, and Medicaid expansion.
The answer is not another program.
It is a path.
The college path is well understood, but out of reach for many because of the runaway cost of college and uncertain job prospects unless you graduate with a sought-after degree.
As a result more kids need to be looking at other career and life paths.
That path is actually already established, but needs to be stronger.
Georgia's career and technical education students graduate at 98%, and DeKalb already has 23 industry-certified programs at 13 high schools, including Tucker, Stone Mountain, and Redan.
I will expand those pathways, bring employers in to shape the curriculum and offer work-study jobs, and make the skilled trades a first choice for young men in our district, not a fallback.
A 22-year-old with a career supports a family, stays out of trouble, pays taxes, and is a productive member of the community.
Educated men and women, with well-paying jobs, in stable families, supported by smart tax, health care, and housing policy, can put DeKalb County, Georgia, and the US back on track.
Healthcare must become easier to access and afford with government programs as a safety net not a permanent solution I believe in a safety net for Georgians who need it.
I support the Georgia Pathways program, which provides coverage while encouraging work, education, or community service.
Help people who are helping themselves.
I also support expanding community health centers and telemedicine access, meeting people where they are.
But here’s the question we’re not asking enough: why do so many Georgians lack access to good jobs with benefits?
The number of uninsured is a symptom.
The root cause is that too many of our neighbors lack the skills and training to get careers with employer-provided health coverage.
That’s why I tie healthcare directly to workforce development.
Georgia has a massive shortage of skilled tradespeople: electricians, plumbers, welders, HVAC technicians.
These careers pay well and typically include health insurance.
They’re also careers AI will not eliminate.
Rather than expanding a government program funded by federal dollars that may not be there tomorrow, I’d invest in getting Georgians into jobs that provide both the dignity of work and the security of coverage.
Too often, mental health is left out of the healthcare discussion.
Mental health is personal for me.
I have watched family members and close friends struggle with addiction, anxiety, abuse, suicide, and violent crime.
Help that comes too late costs families everything, and the burden lands on schools, emergency rooms, courtrooms, and jails that were never built to be the front line of mental health care.
Mental health is health.
In the Georgia Senate I will work to keep mental health part of the healthcare discussion.
I'll seek ways to reach young people early with parent involvement, get people in crisis into treatment instead of a jail cell, and grow the mental health workforce so help exists in every county to support every Georgian in need.
This includes faith-based providers who do so much of this work already and must never be shut out of state-funded care because of their faith.
Respect law enforcement at every level of government Safe communities are the foundation of everything else.
I back the men and women of law enforcement: local police, sheriff's deputies, state troopers, and federal agents alike.
I oppose every effort to defund the police or to impede their ability to do the job they are duly sworn to perform.
Respect for the law starts with respect for the people who enforce it, at every level of government.
I will make sure officers have the tools, training, and pay to keep our neighborhoods safe.
Even with the best officers, there are times when we must be able to protect ourselves and our families.
I will defend your right to legally own a firearm, oppose new restrictions on law-abiding gun owners, and keep the focus of enforcement on the criminals who use guns.
