Fifth generation.
Same district.
"I have blood in the district." — Kevin Martin Kevin is a fifth-generation native of Cobb County.
His mother grew up off Bolton Road, his father in Center Hill, out along Bankhead Highway.
His family has been in this stretch of Georgia for generations, and he has lived here his whole life.
His grandfather had a third-grade education and came to Atlanta on the WPA in the 1930s, the son of a sharecropper with nothing behind him.
He built his own business anyway, hired people out of his own community, and kept a garden that neighbors could work when they needed to eat.
Kevin's father spent 42 years at Atlanta Gaslight as a crew foreman.
His mother spent 35 years at BellSouth and worked her way up to first-level supervisor.
The four of them lived in a two-bedroom, one-bath house under 1,500 square feet.
His parents gave him and his brother what they needed rather than what they wanted, and told them the way out was a better education than they had gotten.
Kevin started cutting grass at nine years old, because if he wanted a bicycle he was going to buy the bicycle.
That is the whole reason he takes the question of who gets a fair shot here personally.
He has watched this district from the inside for his entire life, and he is not asking anyone to take his word for where he comes from.
What 30 years of auditing actually teaches you At one company, payroll kept climbing.
So did the number of people getting checks.
Nobody could say why.
Kevin went through it department by department, headcount by headcount.
The payroll officer was adding their own family members to the payroll.
The fraud stopped, and the person responsible was gone, because somebody finally asked a question nobody else was asking.

The system isn't just broken.
It's completely fixed… …and you are not the one that benefits.
Ask Kevin what is actually wrong here and he doesn't reach for a culture-war answer.
He walks you through the math, one step at a time.
Our schools stopped being the same school district.
People point at Cobb County's overall numbers and call it good.
Go school by school instead, out toward South Cobb and South Fulton, and you are looking at a different district entirely.
So young families stopped moving in.
Nobody moves their kids somewhere the schools are struggling.
Kids who grow up here finish school and leave.
The ones under forty who stay are largely the ones who can't afford to move out of their parents' house.
And the people left behind carry the whole bill.
An aging district with a shrinking tax base has one lever left, so property values get pushed up and the tax bill follows.
Seniors who paid their homes off decades ago get taxed out of them.
Congress is doing nothing but bickering over what headlines and what sound bites will make it easiest for them to win re-election.
The right and the left agree on one thing: the system is not built for you to win.

Where Kevin Stands Kevin is a controller, so he starts every one of these the same way: what is actually going wrong, what Congress can actually do about it, and what he would change.
Pick the one that matters to your household.
Schools Send education money back to Georgia without the federal strings, and let this district raise up its lowest-performing schools rather than pulling down its best.
Where he stands → Jobs and the Trades Federal student aid was built around the four-year degree.
Make it work for shorter technical programs, and fund career and technical education like it matters.
Where he stands → The Federal Budget Pass an actual budget through regular order, fund the core obligations first, and make every other program justify itself before it gets another appropriation.
Where he stands → Housing Open unused federal land for people to build on, give mortgage terms a real hearing, and keep lending standards honest so we do not repeat the 2000s.
Where he stands → Wages and Working Families A full-time wage should clear the cost of living and keep pace with inflation, without compressing the ladder that makes taking on more responsibility worth it.
Where he stands → Prescription Drug Costs Stop paying more than every other developed country for the same medication, bring critical drug manufacturing home, and break up the insurer-doctor-pharmacy arrangement.
Where he stands → Community Hospitals Make federal payment reflect what low-volume community care actually costs, and enforce antitrust against the regional medical monopolies closing these hospitals.
Where he stands → Crime and Policing Fund law enforcement properly, fix the federal side of the pretrial release problem, and appoint judges who read the law as written rather than legislate from the bench.
Where he stands → The Second Amendment Keep federal law focused on people who have actually done something, no national gun registry, and apply the Constitution consistently across every right it protects.
Where he stands → Defense Start with the inventory, not the check.
Set a real readiness benchmark, require replenishment when we fall below it, and audit defense like every other line.
