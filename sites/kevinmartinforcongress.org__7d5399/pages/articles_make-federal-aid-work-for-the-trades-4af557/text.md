It Stopped About Ten Weeks Short of the Trades.
Workforce Pell took effect July 1, 2026 and covers short-term training.
Its fifteen-week ceiling still leaves out most of the licensed trades this district cannot hire.
Kevin Martin on what Congress should change.
Try getting a plumber out to your house this month.
Then an electrician.
Then an HVAC tech before the heat breaks.
You will wait, and you will pay whatever they ask, and it is not because anyone is gouging you.
There are not enough of them.
Economics 101 - Supply and Demand, low supply, high demand, higher prices.
For about forty years we told every kid in this district the same thing: get into a good college or get left behind.
A lot of them listened.
They got their degrees, but they took on the debt, and a lot of them did not land where they were promised.
Meanwhile the work that actually keeps a community running cannot find people to do it.
That is not an accident of the market.
Federal policy put its thumb on the scale, and Congress is what put it there.
What changed on July 1 Something real happened this summer, and most people have not heard about it.
As of July 1, 2026, the Workforce Pell Grant program is in effect.
For the first time, a student can use a federal Pell Grant for a short-term training program instead of only a traditional degree.
Programs have to lead to a recognized credential, connect to an academic pathway, and hit hard outcome thresholds: a 70% completion rate and a 70% verified job placement rate measured 180 days after finishing.
Those outcome requirements are the right instinct.
I have spent my career as a controller, and that is exactly the test I would apply.
If the federal government is going to put money behind a program, the program should have to prove that people finish it and get hired.
That is not a partisan idea.
It is how you would look at it if it were your own money, which it is.
Give credit where it is due.
This was a genuine step, and it moved in the direction this district needs.
The part that still does not work Here is the catch, and it is a specific one.
To qualify for Workforce Pell, a program has to run between 150 and 599 clock hours, lasting at least eight weeks but fewer than fifteen .
Fifteen weeks is not very long.
It is one semester.
It covers a certificate program, and it genuinely helps somebody get into a starting role in healthcare, IT, advanced manufacturing, or transportation.
What it does not cover is most of the licensed trades.
The work people in this district actually cannot hire — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, line work — is licensed work, and licensed work takes longer than fifteen weeks to train for.
That is the entire point of licensing it.
We require more training precisely because the consequences of doing it badly are somebody's house burning down.
Apprenticeship programs usually require 8,000 on the job training hours (this is four years minimum) AND 576 classroom hours.
A full-time student in Georgia colleges takes 16 - 20 hours per semester, so this would equate to nearly 30 semesters which is more than a 2 year and 4-year degree.
These hours are the same for Electrical, Plumbing, HVAC, and Line Work. (source - Wirehours.com ) So, the federal aid system now has a door for the eight-to-fifteen-week programs and a door for the four-year degree, and a gap in the middle where a good share of the skilled trades live.
"We need to develop more trade schools to provide an education for those who are not able to afford the more expensive colleges and universities but for them to learn a skill set, a trade." — Kevin Martin What Congress actually controls here This is one of the places where the answer is not "somebody should do something." Congress writes the rules for the entire federal student aid system.
Who gets a loan.
What a Pell grant can be spent on.
Which institutions qualify.
How long a program has to run to be eligible.
That fifteen-week ceiling is not a law of nature or a budget constraint.
It is a number Congress chose and wrote down, and Congress can choose a different one.
Congress also funds the federal workforce and career-technical education programs, and sets the rules for federally registered apprenticeships.
That matters, because it means this is not a complaint.
It is a specific thing a member of Congress can go do, and it is one of the reasons I am running.
What needs to change Raise the ceiling to match the trades.
The eight-to-fifteen-week window was drawn around short certificates.
Extend eligibility to the longer technical and licensure programs that lead directly into the licensed trades, and keep the completion and placement thresholds attached to it.
The accountability is the good part of this law.
The narrow definition is the part that needs work.
Keep the outcome requirements.
Seventy percent completion and seventy percent placement is a real bar.
Any expansion should carry it.
If a program cannot get people through and hired, federal money should not be behind it, whether it runs eight weeks or four years.
Then watch what it does.
The honest answer about how well Workforce Pell works is that nobody knows yet.
It has only been in effect since July 1, 2026.
Anyone who tells you they know how it is going is guessing.
The right posture is to fund it, measure it, and adjust it based on what the numbers say a year from now.
Why this one matters here This is the most direct route to a better living for the largest number of people in this district, and it does not require taking on debt to get there.
There are people here who would be excellent at that work, make a good living doing it, and own a home years before their friends finish paying off student loans.
They are not waiting on a speech.
They are waiting on whether the program they want to enroll in counts.
It is also how you bring business back.
Companies go where the skilled workforce is.
Educate and build the workforce and the jobs follow, which gives young people who grew up here a reason to stay instead of leaving for somewhere they can start.
A young person here should have two respectable paths, not one.
As of July, we are closer to that than we were.
We are not there.
But, again, keep asking the question, WHY.
Why did Congress not do something before now and why have the citizens of the district not demanded more from the congressional leader?
The old saying, give a man a fish, he will eat for a day.
Teach him how to fish, and he will eat for a lifetime.
Let's help train more fishermen and women.
