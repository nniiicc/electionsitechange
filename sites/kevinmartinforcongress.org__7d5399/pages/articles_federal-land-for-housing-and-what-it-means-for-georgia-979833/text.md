Almost None of It Is in Georgia.
The federal government is already moving to open public land for housing.
Kevin Martin on why that helps the West more than Georgia, and what would actually move the needle in GA-06.
Ask anyone under thirty-five around here about buying a house and you get the same three answers.
The down payment is out of reach.
The rate makes the monthly payment impossible.
And nobody ever explained how any of it works.
I have said for a while that one of the most direct things the federal government could do is make land available to people who want to build on it.
We did exactly that in the 1800s.
The government had land, people were willing to go build something on it, and it worked.
Here is the honest update: that is now happening.
And I need to tell you where it is happening, because it is not here.
What is actually underway In March 2025, the Interior Department and HUD signed an agreement to identify federal land that can be transferred for housing and to streamline how it moves to state and local governments and nonprofit builders.
The Bureau of Land Management has identified around 535,000 acres it manages within four miles of towns with at least 5,000 people.
In Nevada, BLM has proposed selling about 19 acres to the City of Henderson for affordable housing at $100 an acre, deliberately priced below market to get homes built.
That is a real policy, moving now.
It is also almost entirely a Western policy.
More than 99 percent of BLM land is in the eleven Western states and Alaska.
In its most recent count, the Congressional Research Service found no BLM land in Georgia at all.
What federal land we have around here is mostly parkland along the Chattahoochee River, not land waiting to be opened up for houses.
So if I stood here and told you that opening federal land was going to get your daughter into a house in Douglas County, I would be selling you something.
It is a good national policy.
It is not a GA-06 housing plan.
I would rather tell you that than have you find out later.
What actually constrains housing here The problem in this district is not that the federal government is sitting on land outside Douglasville.
It is that not enough gets built, and the growth we did get went lopsided.
North and northeast of Atlanta boomed.
South and southwest did not keep pace, and those are precisely the areas with land, lower costs, and room to grow.
That imbalance is not a federal land question.
It is an infrastructure, financing, and local zoning question, and the federal role in it is narrower than people assume but not zero.
What Congress actually controls Being precise about this matters, because a candidate who overstates federal power on housing gets caught.
Congress does not zone your county.
It does not decide what gets built in Austell.
Those are local decisions and they should stay local.
What Congress does control is significant anyway.
Federal policy sets the terms of nearly every mortgage in the country, because the federal government backs, guarantees, or insures the majority of home loans, and federal rules determine who qualifies and on what terms.
Federal law governs how credit is reported and scored.
Federal infrastructure money shapes where growth is even possible, because you cannot put housing where there is no water, sewer, or road capacity.
And federal borrowing feeds the interest rate environment that sets the monthly payment.
That last one ties straight back to the budget, which is not a coincidence.
Most of these threads run back to the same place.
What needs to change Support the federal land transfers where they apply, honestly.
It is good policy for the states that have the land.
I would vote for it.
I am not going to claim credit for it here.
Put federal infrastructure money where the growth capacity actually is.
The south and southwest of this region have land and room.
What they often do not have is the water, sewer, and road capacity to support building at scale.
That is a legitimate federal role and it is the version of the supply argument that applies to GA-06.
Stop making the monthly payment worse from Washington.
Federal borrowing is a material input into rates.
Every conversation about housing affordability that ignores the deficit is only half a conversation.
Be honest about the timeline.
Nothing in here puts somebody in a house next spring.
Supply takes years.
Anyone promising faster is guessing.
Why it matters Homeownership is still how most families in this country build anything worth passing on.
Lock a generation out of it and you get young people who cannot start, and who eventually leave for somewhere they can.
That is already happening here.
Kids who grow up in this district finish school and go start their lives somewhere more affordable.
A young person who grew up here should be able to buy here.
I would rather give you a smaller answer that is true than a bigger one that is not.
