The Federal Minimum Wage Has Not Moved Since 2009.
I Am a Republican and I Think That Is Indefensible.
The federal minimum wage has been $7.25 since 2009.
The tipped wage has been $2.13 since 1991.
Kevin Martin on why a Republican is saying that number has to move.
Let me put the numbers down first, because most people do not know them.
The federal minimum wage is $7.25 an hour.
It has been $7.25 since 2009.
That is seventeen years.
The federal tipped minimum wage is $2.13 an hour.
That has not moved since 1991.
Not seventeen years.
Thirty-five.
Now think about what a gallon of milk cost in 2009.
Think about rent in this district in 2009.
Think about what a used car cost.
I am a controller.
I spend my working life watching costs move.
There is no version of the arithmetic where the cost side of a household budget rises for seventeen years and the floor under the income side does not move at all and that is fine.
I know what I am supposed to say here
I am a Republican, and the expected position is that the minimum wage is a job killer and the market sorts it out.
I am not going to say that, because I do not believe it as stated, and because I think people can tell when someone is reciting.
Here is what I actually think.
The market does a lot of things well.
What it does not do is set a floor.
A floor is a decision somebody makes.
We made one in 2009 and then stopped thinking about it for seventeen years, and the result is a number that has quietly become meaningless.
Most workers in Georgia already earn well above $7.25, because the labor market moved past it on its own.
That is sometimes offered as proof the number does not matter.
I read it the opposite way.
If the market has already moved past the floor, then raising the floor to something real costs less than people fear and does more for the workers still pinned under it, which is disproportionately tipped workers living on $2.13 plus whatever comes in that night.
What Congress actually controls
This one is simple.
Congress sets it.
Directly, in statute.
There is no agency to blame, no court blocking it, no state standing in the way.
Congress has not raised it because Congress has not voted to raise it.
Worth knowing: the federal government already applies a higher floor to some of its own work.
Under an older executive order, the minimum wage on certain federal contracts signed between 2015 and early 2022 rose to $13.65 an hour as of May 11, 2026, with $9.55 for tipped employees on those contracts.
A later order that set a higher floor on newer contracts was revoked in March 2025.
So the federal government has already decided, on some of its own contracts, that $7.25 is not an adequate wage.
It just has not extended that judgment to everybody else.
What needs to change
Raise the floor to something that clears the cost of living.
I am not going to pick a number in a blog post and pretend I arrived at it rigorously.
The number should come out of actual cost-of-living data, and it should be defensible line by line.
Then index it, so we never do this again.
This is the part I care about most as a numbers person.
The real failure was not the 2009 number.
It was building a system where the floor only moves when Congress musters a vote, which means it moves once a decade and then sits.
Tie it to a published measure and let it adjust on its own.
Deal with $2.13 honestly.
A tipped wage frozen since 1991 is not a policy anymore.
Whatever the right structure is, that is not it.
Keep the ladder intact.
This is a real concern and I am not dismissing it.
If you raise the bottom and compress everything above it, you punish the person who took on more responsibility for an extra dollar an hour.
Any increase has to be phased so the rungs above it have room to move too.
That is a design problem, not a reason to do nothing.
Why I am willing to say this
Because I have watched people in this district work full time and not clear their own cost of living, and I do not think the answer is to explain to them why the market is right and their experience is wrong.
The frame I keep coming back to is this.
Most people are not sitting around waiting for a handout.
They are working, and the arithmetic stopped working, and nobody in Washington has adjusted the floor since before their kids were born.
You should be able to work a full week and afford to live in the place where you work.
If that sentence is controversial in my own party, I would rather be on the wrong side of my party than the wrong side of that sentence.
