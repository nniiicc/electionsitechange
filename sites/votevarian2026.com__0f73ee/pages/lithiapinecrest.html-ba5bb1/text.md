Is there any way to infuse cash into the community and collect HUD grant after the fact?
Yes, there is a legal mechanism to infuse cash into West Tampa immediately and collect HUD grant funds after the fact, but it requires highly precise structuring.
Under federal guidelines, you cannot simply spend random local money and ask HUD for a retroactive check.
However, HUD explicitly allows for "Pre-Award Costs" and "Section 108 Loan Guarantees," which serve as the perfect legislative vehicles for your "Immediate Cash Infusion" platform. [1, 2]
A strategic multi-step playbook allows you to deploy capital immediately in 2027 while securing federal reimbursement later:
1.
The Pre-Award Cost Strategy (The Short-Term Cash Drop)
Under HUD federal regulations (24 CFR § 92.212), a local municipality or housing authority can begin spending money on a project before the federal grant is officially awarded. [1]
The Action: You will direct the Tampa Housing Authority (THA) to formally amend its consolidated action plan to include the West Tampa Transformation Project. [1]
The Cash Infusion: Once the plan is amended, the city can legally front up to 25% of its anticipated federal allocation using local municipal reserves or short-term bridge financing.
This cash can immediately hit West Tampa in early 2027 to start land acquisition, architectural drafting, and community program rollouts. [1]
The Retroactive Collection: When HUD officially approves the multi-million dollar Choice Neighborhoods or CDBG grant later that year, the federal funds are used to retroactively reimburse the local city accounts that fronted the cash. [1, 2]
2.
The HUD Section 108 Loan Guarantee (The Mass-Scale Shield)
If a $10 million pre-award drop isn't big enough to combat the stadium's immediate real estate pressure, you can use HUD's most powerful financing tool: The Section 108 Loan Guarantee Program. [1, 2]
How it works: Section 108 allows a city like Tampa to borrow up to five times its annual CDBG allocation from private investors, using its future federal HUD grants as collateral. [1]
The Cash Infusion: Instead of waiting years for annual grant cycles, this program infuses a massive lump sum of $30 million to $50 million in cash directly into West Tampa in 2027.
This capital is used to aggressively buy up local property, build affordable housing units, and issue commercial grants before land values skyrocket from the stadium. [1, 2]
The After-the-Fact Payoff: Over the next 20 years, the City of Tampa repays the loan directly out of its annual federal HUD allocations.
The community gets the protective cash shield today, and the federal government pays the bill tomorrow. [1]
