Social Security The Varian Plan: Protecting the Promise I.
The Crisis of Inaction For decades, Washington has treated Social Security as a political football.
Democrats use it to stoke fear; Republicans use it to talk about "entitlement reform." Meanwhile, the 2026 Social Security Trustees Report confirms that without intervention, the trust fund faces exhaustion by 2032.
As an Independent, I am the only candidate in this race who can sit across from both sides to demand a solution that doesn't involve cutting benefits for our seniors.
II.
The "Fair Share" Solution The current system is regressive.
In 2026, the Social Security tax cap sits at $184,500.
This means a CEO in Sarasota stops paying into the system by February, while a nurse or teacher pays in all year.
My Proposal : I support lifting the cap on earnings over $400,000.
This preserves the "donut hole" to protect middle-class earners while ensuring the wealthiest Americans contribute to the solvency of the fund for the next 75 years.
III.
Ending the "Invisible Tax" on Seniors The Social Security Fairness Act is a non-partisan necessity.
We must repeal the Windfall Elimination Provision (WEP) and the Government Pension Offset (GPO).
It is an insult to our public servants—teachers, police, and firefighters—that their hard-earned Social Security benefits are docked simply because they also have a pension.
IV.
Real Inflation Protection The 2.8% COLA increase for 2026 is a drop in the bucket compared to Florida’s rising property insurance and healthcare costs.
I advocate for switching the COLA calculation to the CPI-E (Consumer Price Index for the Elderly), which accurately weighs the costs that seniors actually face, rather than a general index that includes things retirees aren't buying.
V.
Capping health insurance premium to COLA rate To mandate that annual increases for Medicare premiums, state Medicaid cost-sharing, and private health insurance premiums shall not exceed the percentage increase of the Social Security Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA) for that fiscal year.
If seniors' income only goes up by the COLA rate, their healthcare costs shouldn't go up any faster.

Modernizing Benefits for Today’s Economy Inflation hits seniors the hardest.

The Outlandish Choice: A National "Sovereign Wealth Fund" T he Concept: Create a massive, state-owned investment fund funded by new alternative revenue streams to permanently bankroll Social Security.
How It Works: The government could seed this fund using modern revenue sources unrelated to wages, such as: A federal tax on automated AI workflows and corporate robots replacing human labor.
Nationalizing a percentage of revenue from federal land resource extraction (similar to how Alaska's Permanent Fund pays citizens from oil revenues).
A micro-tax on all high-frequency algorithmic financial transactions on Wall Street.
The Impact: This removes the burden entirely from personal paychecks and links retirement security to overall national productivity and technological growth.
A national Sovereign Wealth Fund (SWF) is the most transformative option because it shifts the entire burden of Social Security away from worker paychecks.
Instead of taxing labor, the government builds a massive investment portfolio to fund public retirement.
How It Works in Practice To fund a Social Security SWF without standard taxes, the government would capture revenue from national assets, corporate growth, and emerging technologies: The AI and Robot Tax: As automated systems replace human jobs, the payroll tax base shrinks.
A micro-tax on corporate AI workflows and robotic automation would capture the wealth generated by machines and direct it into the fund.
Federal Asset Monetization: A portion of revenues from oil, natural gas, minerals, and wind/solar leases on federal lands would be permanently funneled into the portfolio.
This mirrors the Alaska Permanent Fund , which uses state oil wealth to pay annual dividends to residents.
Financial Transaction Tax (FTT): Imposing a tiny fraction-of-a-cent tax on high-frequency, algorithmic Wall Street stock trades would generate immense revenue from institutional trading volume without impacting standard retail investors.
Real-World Precedents This concept is highly successful on a global scale.
Norway's Government Pension Fund Global: Norway funnels its state oil revenues into a massive SWF worth over $1.6 trillion.
It invests in global stocks, bonds, and real estate, effectively securing the country's long-term public welfare and retirement needs.
Singapore's GIC and Temasek: Singapore uses state-backed investment funds to grow national reserves and support public infrastructure and social spending.
The Trade-offs The Good: It solves the demographic crisis (fewer workers supporting more retirees) by linking Social Security to technological productivity and market gains rather than human population growth.
The Bad: It requires immense upfront capital to start, and the federal government would become a massive shareholder in private global corporations, creating unprecedented debates over state-owned market influence.
