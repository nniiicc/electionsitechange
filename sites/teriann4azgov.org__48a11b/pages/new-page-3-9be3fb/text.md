That does not solve Arizona’s biggest concerns.

Transparency Over Corporate Influence Teri Ann Hourihan for AZ Governor.
In Arizona, billion-dollar corporations and data center companies routinely contribute money to elected officials and governors.
Whether legal or not, it creates the appearance of a conflict of interest and leaves many Arizonans questioning whose interests are being put first.
As Governor, I will not accept money from these corporations because my loyalty belongs to the people of Arizona—not special interests.
For too long, large corporations have had a louder voice than the families, small businesses, and communities impacted by their decisions.
We need to have an honest conversation about how this system operates and the influence it can have on public policy.
Arizona's leaders should be accountable to the people they serve, not to the corporations funding their campaigns.
I will put Arizonans first.
That means transparency, accountability, and decisions based on what is best for our state—not what is best for billion-dollar donors.

Arizona families should not be left carrying the burden while corporations receive the benefits.

It’s time for new leadership.

Arizona Must Put People Before Surveillance The Navajo Nation is expanding the use of Flock license plate reader cameras to support law enforcement and improve public safety.
Public safety matters, but so does ensuring that families have their basic needs met.
When millions of dollars are available for surveillance technology while many Native families continue to struggle with food insecurity, housing, healthcare, and economic opportunity, it’s fair to ask whether our priorities are balanced.
As Governor, I will work to ensure that public safety investments are paired with investments in people.
Safe communities are built not only through technology, but by ensuring families have access to food, housing, jobs, healthcare, and hope.
Arizona should never lose sight of the people behind the policies.
Protecting Arizona’s Privacy & Safety License-plate reader technology can help law enforcement locate stolen vehicles and address serious crimes, but public safety cannot come at the expense of our constitutional rights or the security of our personal information.
Recent concerns surrounding Flock Safety and automated license-plate readers highlight why government must establish strong safeguards around surveillance technology, including how information is collected, stored, accessed, and shared.
As Governor, I will support technology that legitimately helps keep Arizonans safe while demanding strict accountability.
I will push for strong cybersecurity and data-breach protections, limits on unnecessary retention and sharing of personal data, transparency and auditing of government surveillance systems, and safeguards against abuse or unconstitutional searches.
We should never have to choose between public safety and constitutional liberty.
Arizona can protect both.
