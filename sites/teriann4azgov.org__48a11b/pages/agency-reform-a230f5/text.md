The more I examine what has happened in Arizona’s behavioral health system, the more concerned I become that there may be patterns deserving independent investigation.
I want to be clear: I am not stating this as fact, because these are just me putting things together and creating questions.
Of which, I believe there are serious questions that deserve answers.
When behavioral health providers are shut down following complaints, who ultimately benefits?
Are complaints always legitimate, or are some being weaponized by competitors?
Are large providers with substantial state contracts disproportionately positioned to absorb clients after smaller providers close?
Are state agencies investigating every complaint with the same level of scrutiny and due process?
The situation involving NewFound Hope has raised additional questions for me.
Public reporting documented that the provider was investigated, its operations at a Ramada hotel were scrutinized, and clients were later relocated after the facility ceased operating.
Those events are matters of public record.
What concerns me is whether there is a broader pattern across Arizona’s behavioral health system that deserves independent review.
If providers, nonprofit organizations, government officials, or anyone else are coordinating to eliminate competitors rather than simply protecting patients, that would represent a profound failure of public trust.
I am committed to continuing to ask questions, follow the evidence wherever it leads, and advocate for transparency.
