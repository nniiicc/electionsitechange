Harriet Holman has spent her life in service: more than two decades in uniform, on Dorchester County Council, and now fighting for District 102 in Columbia.
With S.C.
Attorney General Alan Wilson With Jenny Costa Honeycutt The Army came first Harriet was born in Mobile, Alabama, the daughter of the late Major General Abraham Holman.
When you are raised in a military family, you learn about discipline and service early.
She spent more than two decades in the United States Army.
She retired as a Lieutenant Colonel in the Adjutant General Corps, earning five Meritorious Service Medals during her career, including the Defense Meritorious Service Medal.
She kept going to school the whole time.
An associate degree from Central Texas College was followed by a bachelor's in English from Jackson State University, and she later graduated from the U.S.
Army Command and General Staff College.
In 2018, she earned a master's degree in criminal justice from Claflin University.
Then Dorchester County When she took the uniform off, she brought the same discipline home to Dorchester County.
Governor Henry McMaster appointed Harriet to Dorchester County Council in 2017.
Voters elected her in 2018 and again in 2020, and she served her neighbors until 2024.
Now the State House In November 2024, District 102 elected Harriet to the South Carolina House of Representatives.
When she was sworn in on November 11, she became the first Black Republican woman ever to serve in the South Carolina General Assembly.
She has never treated that as the headline.
She treats it as the job.
She serves on the Medical, Military, Public and Municipal Affairs Committee, which handles the military and veterans issues she knows from the inside, and on the Interstate Cooperation Committee.
Home and community Harriet and her husband Herman, who married in 2009, live in Ridgeville.
Between them they have three children: Chineka, BJ and Tonie.
She is a past president of the St.
George Rotary Club and a Paul Harris Fellow, a board member of the Dorchester County Heritage Center, a past board member of the St.
George Rosenwald School, a past member of Leadership Dorchester, and a member of the Tri-County Chamber of Commerce.
She belongs to the Disabled American Veterans, the American Legion and the VFW.
Faith, family and love of country are the principles she was raised on, and they are the ones she takes to every vote.
