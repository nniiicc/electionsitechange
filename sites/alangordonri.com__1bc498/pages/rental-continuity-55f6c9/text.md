Fair risk pricing
The problem is not only rent.
It is risk.
Landlords price expected damage, missed rent, long vacancies, legal bills and emergency repairs into the cost of housing.
Tenants pay for that uncertainty even when they are responsible and never cause a loss.
Rental Continuity replaces part of that uncertainty with defined coverage, early intervention and verified performance.
The goal is simple: protect both sides well enough that good housing no longer needs such a large risk cushion.

Protection in both directions
The tenant, landlord and property are evaluated together.
Good performance on either side should be recognized—not erased by a one-sided screening system.

- Use approved handypersons for covered repairs
- Protect belongings against fire and theft
- Receive limited rent continuity after qualifying hardship
- Build a positive record from verified on-time payments
- Cover qualifying tenant-caused property damage
- Dispatch vetted contractors and document the work

A better risk record
The plan uses verified performance and property conditions to price risk more accurately.
A mere eviction filing should not automatically stain a tenant's record, and a landlord should not receive the same price regardless of repair history.
Tenant: payment and tenancy history
Landlord: repair response and claim history
Property: condition, systems and prior losses

No.
Ordinary renters insurance remains part of the protection, but Rental Continuity coordinates it with small-repair service, rent-continuity coverage, landlord damage protection and defined legal coverage.
No plan can dictate every market rent.
This proposal targets the losses and uncertainty landlords build into rent, verifies the savings, and creates incentives for participating landlords to share those savings through lower insured-unit rents, smaller increases or renewal credits.
The strongest model uses an approved repair network and a documented dispatch process.
That protects tenants from delay while giving landlords and insurers control over quality, authorization and price.
Fair underwriting is central to the plan.
Verified payment history, responsible property care, prompt repairs and safer building conditions should earn better terms.
Repeated preventable losses should affect the party responsible for them.
The practical route is a partnership with an established licensed insurer.
The carrier would issue and regulate the coverage while the program coordinates underwriting data, contractors and prevention services.
