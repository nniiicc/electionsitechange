Ask Hope would help Rhode Islanders find the right office, understand official information, organize their facts, and prepare clear requests—at any hour and without needing an insider.
This page describes a proposed public-service pilot.
Ask Hope is not yet a live government service.
See how it would work Review the safeguards Right office Official source Clear draft One clear front door to Rhode Island government.
Every answer should show its source and say when a person needs to take over.
What Ask Hope would do Help people move from confusion to a usable next step.
The tool would work from verified government sources, structured intake questions, and subject-matter review for each area included in the pilot.
It would not invent an office, a deadline, or a legal conclusion.
01 ⌖ Find the office Identify the state, local, or federal office that handles the issue and link to its official contact page, form, or procedure.
02 § Explain the process Translate published instructions, forms, rules, and statutes into plain language while preserving links to the original sources.
03 ✎ Prepare the request Ask focused questions and produce a first draft of a complaint, APRA request, appeal, assistance request, or supporting-document list.
04 ⇧ Escalate responsibly Recognize urgent, dangerous, or highly individualized matters and direct the resident to a trained person or emergency resource.

Residents should be able to stop, save their work, review every source, and decide whether to submit the resulting document.
1 Describe the problem Use ordinary language.
No agency name or legal vocabulary is required.
2 Answer focused questions Hope asks for the facts needed to distinguish one office or process from another.
3 Review the sources The answer links to the official page, procedure, form, statute, rule, or public guidance relied upon.
4 Receive a usable result The resident gets a checklist, organized fact summary, or editable first draft.
5 Reach a person when needed The system provides the correct human contact and carries forward only the information the resident chooses to share.
Useful from the first pilot Help people comply with the law—not bury them in paperwork.
Ask Hope would begin with bounded tasks whose official sources and outputs can be tested before the pilot expands.
Agency navigation Find the correct department, division, municipal office, or federal referral.
Permits and registrations Map published steps for licenses, tax registrations, renewals, and required forms.
Complaint preparation Organize dates, parties, events, prior contacts, requested relief, and supporting documents.
Public-records requests Help residents direct a narrow request to the public body that maintains the records.
Service questions Locate official eligibility information and the proper application or assistance channel.
Plain-language access Explain public instructions in accessible language and support translated interfaces.
Public-service safeguards The system must be useful without pretending to be infallible.
Every design choice should make it easier to verify, correct, and challenge the system’s output.
Sources Show the authority Answers display the official sources used, their dates when available, and a direct link for independent review.
Limits Say what Hope cannot decide The tool does not determine guilt, liability, eligibility, legal rights, or how an agency will rule.
Privacy Collect less Ask for the minimum information needed, warn before sensitive material is entered, and provide clear retention and deletion controls.
Review Bring in a person Urgent, disputed, uncertain, or high-consequence matters receive a clear route to trained staff or outside help.
Audit Publish performance Test referral accuracy, source freshness, accessibility, privacy, and unequal error patterns, then publish the results.
Corrections Keep a visible record Residents can report a bad answer, and material corrections appear in a public change history.
Nonpartisan by design The same official answer for every Rhode Islander.
Ask Hope would not promote a party, a candidate, or an officeholder.
Responses would be based on published law, official procedures, and identified records.
Political affiliation would not be requested or used to shape an answer.
An independent review process should test whether similarly situated users receive materially consistent guidance.
Build it carefully Begin with a limited pilot that can be tested in public.
The public should be able to see what the pilot covers, how often it succeeds, where it fails, and what it costs.
Phase I Verified directory Connect official agency directories, contact pages, forms, and published procedures to a searchable Rhode Island service map.
Phase II Drafting tools Add structured help for APRA requests, complaints, referrals, and basic government correspondence.
Phase III Limited public pilot Test with real users, trained reviewers, accessibility specialists, and adversarial scenarios before wider release.
Phase IV Public evaluation Publish accuracy, referral, privacy, accessibility, correction, and cost results before deciding what to add.
The Attorney General’s role Build a dependable intake system and tell the truth about its limits.
The Attorney General can establish the tool for the office’s own services, use public sources to improve referral and complaint intake, require privacy and accuracy standards in procurement, and invite other agencies to participate.
The Attorney General cannot direct every state agency.
Broader integration would require agency cooperation and, where necessary, action by the General Assembly.
A foundation already exists Ask Hope would organize official resources—not replace them.
