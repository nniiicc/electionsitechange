Meet the candidate
Alan Gordon brings rigorous legal study, more than a decade of constitutional advocacy, and a personal understanding of how government power affects ordinary people.
Why he is running
For years, Alan studied public law and constitutional rights.
Then he experienced the criminal justice system personally—representing himself through prosecutions that he and Campaign Manager Rev.
Dr.
Anne Armstrong describe as politically motivated and retaliatory.
Between 2015 and 2026, the criminal cases brought against them during that period ultimately concluded in their favor.
The experience convinced Alan that people without legal training, money, or political connections need an Attorney General who will insist upon one standard of justice.
The office should never be a weapon for one party, one institution, or one favored class.

Independent by choice
Alan’s decision was deliberate.
He wanted to preserve his independence to challenge and seek reform of even the powerful institutions that regulate the legal profession.
What Rhode Island requires
Membership in the Rhode Island Bar is not required to run for or hold this office.
The Rhode Island Constitution requires a civil officeholder to be a qualified elector.
The Secretary of State’s published eligibility requirements for Attorney General list age, residency, and citizenship—and do not add Bar membership as a qualification.
He does not represent himself as an attorney admitted to practice in Rhode Island.
His candidacy instead rests on his legal education, advocacy, firsthand courtroom experience, and his belief that public authority must remain answerable to the people.
A different kind of legal service

Provided private ecclesiastical legal guidance to the Secretary of the historic Bermuda lodge.
Serves as a private ecclesiastical legal advisor to the Matriarch.
