How the proposal works Protected capital with a purpose.
The account is an investment trust, not ordinary spending money.
Legislation would define eligibility, administration, permitted uses, and safeguards.
01 Open an account at birth Every eligible child receives an individually tracked Childhood Capital Account.
02 Use a sliding public contribution The state fully funds up to $5,200 annually for family income through $40,000, then phases its share to zero at $200,000.
03 Invest for eighteen years Family, employer, private, philanthropic, federal, and public sources may all contribute under the model.
04 Protect the principal Withdrawals are limited to approved uses that create security, skills, property, or lasting earning capacity.
The central policy choice Women own their accounts—and the choices those assets make possible.
A woman keeps her Childhood Capital Account whether or not she becomes a mother.
Approved uses could include: Buying a home Starting a business Education or occupational training Transportation needed for work Qualifying health needs Support during early motherhood Retirement Responsibility at fatherhood The man’s account follows a different rule.
If he never fathers a child, his account remains invested for retirement.
When he fathers his first child, the full account balance transfers to the mother when she gives birth.
The transfer belongs to the mother.
It is distinct from child support and recognizes the physical burden of pregnancy, childbirth, and recovery.
His protected account → Birth of first child → Property in her name RI Rhode Island already has a starting point The Baby Bond Trust establishes the basic machinery.
Rhode Island enacted the Baby Bond Trust in 2025.
Once fully funded, it provides a one-time $# trust allocation for qualifying children whose families receive RI Works during the child’s first year.
The Treasurer would invest the funds for approved wealth-building uses.
The Women’s Wealth Building Initiative is much larger: annual deposits for eighteen years, broader funding, and a deliberate focus on property controlled by women.
