Rhode Island • Public Fact-Finding Project Tell Us About Your Experience With DCYF and Family Court We are gathering experiences and verifiable information to determine whether recurring or widespread problems exist in Rhode Island's child-welfare system.
This project does not assume that misconduct or constitutional violations occurred.
Its purpose is to identify patterns that can be tested against records, law, court decisions, and statewide data.
What We Are Trying to Learn We want to determine whether unrelated families report recurring patterns involving possible due-process violations; delays in reunification services or evaluations; delays later relied upon in changing permanency goals or seeking termination of parental rights; visitation problems; inaccurate or disputed information presented to Family Court; failures to make reasonable reunification efforts; or abuse, neglect, unsafe placement, exploitation, injury, inappropriate restraint, or children becoming missing while in state care.
Why dates matter: Similar documented timelines across unrelated cases can reveal a pattern.
We are especially interested in when removal occurred, when services were ordered, when they actually became available, when visitation changed, and when the permanency goal or TPR petition changed the case.
A Possible Comprehensive Review In March 2026, the Rhode Island Attorney General published a comprehensive report following a years-long investigation into child sexual abuse in the Diocese of Providence and the institution's response.
That investigation reviewed more than 250,000 pages of records and included survivor and witness interviews.
A review involving DCYF and Family Court would involve different laws, records, agencies, privacy protections, and investigative authority.
The comparison is the method: listen to affected people, gather records lawfully, follow the evidence, identify recurring patterns, and recommend reforms where the evidence supports them.
Information submitted through this project can help identify issues deserving further examination.
When Alan Gordon takes office as Attorney General, these reports could help inform the establishment of an official, legally authorized review or investigation into systemic misconduct or failures involving DCYF, consistent with the powers and responsibilities of that office.
No particular outcome is promised.
A submission does not mean that a violation will be found, an investigation or prosecution will be opened, or an individual Family Court case will be reopened.
Protect Children's Privacy Do not submit unredacted confidential Family Court or DCYF records through this public form.
Do not enter a child's full name, date of birth, Social Security number, medical details, home or foster-home address, or other identifying information.
Submitting information does not create an attorney-client relationship and does not substitute for an appeal, court filing, police report, complaint to the Office of the Child Advocate, judicial-misconduct complaint, or other legal process.
You may indicate that supporting documents exist; a secure process can be arranged separately if documents are later requested.
