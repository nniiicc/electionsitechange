The minority keeps an independent voice.
A small, independently functioning office inside the Attorney General's structure would participate before major decisions are made, place dissent in the official record, hear grievances about the AG's office, and bring serious integrity concerns to the courts when appropriate.

The problem One elected office can speak with enormous legal power.
The Rhode Island Attorney General is elected statewide and exercises substantial authority over criminal prosecutions, state litigation, investigations and major questions of public law.
Elections properly decide who leads that office.
But electoral victory does not make the constitutional concerns of everyone who voted the other way disappear.
The proposal does not create a second Attorney General and does not give the minority a veto.
It creates a disciplined way for a materially different constitutional, factual or policy analysis to be heard, preserved and—when necessary—put before a court.
A check without a veto The Attorney General keeps the power of the office.
The Minority Constitutional Advocate gets access, a voice, an investigative channel and a permanent record of dissent—but cannot take over prosecutions, direct prosecutors or bind the State.
How it works Eight practical functions The pilot is designed to operate before, during and after major decisions—not merely criticize them after the fact.
1 Seat at major policy meetings The Advocate receives timely access to major policy and litigation-policy discussions and the materials needed to evaluate them.
2 Minority Reports become part of the record When the Advocate materially disagrees, a written Minority Report accompanies the final decision and becomes part of the permanent official record.
It may not be edited or suppressed by the Attorney General.
3 Independent complaint channel People who believe they were treated unfairly or improperly by the Attorney General's Office have somewhere outside the ordinary chain of command to bring the complaint.
4 Prosecutorial-integrity review The office can investigate credible allegations involving withheld evidence, discovery failures, Brady/Giglio-type issues, false or misleading evidence, retaliation, abuse of process, conflicts or comparable misconduct.
5 Reports to the court When an investigation concerns a pending or completed case, retained counsel may seek permission to place factual findings and relevant evidence before the court through lawful procedures.
The judge retains all adjudicative power.
6 Amicus and intervention role Where a substantial nonfrivolous competing constitutional position exists, retained counsel may seek amicus participation or intervention where applicable rules permit it.
7 Systemic oversight Patterns across complaints and investigations can be identified, anonymized when appropriate, and addressed through recommendations and public annual reporting.
8 Taxpayer Savings Ledger Every claimed fiscal benefit is tracked separately as documented savings, recoveries or estimated avoided costs so the pilot can be judged by results instead of rhetoric.
Guardrails What the Advocate cannot do Cannot initiate or dismiss a criminal prosecution.
Cannot enter a nolle prosequi or direct charging decisions.
Cannot supervise Assistant or Special Assistant Attorneys General or direct police or grand-jury operations.
Cannot bind the State to a settlement or claim to speak for the State.
Cannot veto the Attorney General's lawful decision.
Cannot override a judge.
Courts retain judicial power and decide what remedy, if any, is warranted.
Is not required to oppose the Attorney General merely for the sake of opposition; there must be a material, nonfrivolous basis for a different position.

Implementation Start with a voluntary pilot.
Test it in public.
The proposal contemplates an incoming Attorney General establishing a voluntary internal pilot within existing administrative and budget authority to the extent legally permissible.
The office could be given strong operational independence by written policy while the Attorney General remains the constitutional head of the department.
If the pilot proves useful, Rhode Island could then consider legislation, court-rule changes, or constitutional action as appropriate to make the office durable, guarantee its independence, and define any additional powers.

Dissent on the record.
