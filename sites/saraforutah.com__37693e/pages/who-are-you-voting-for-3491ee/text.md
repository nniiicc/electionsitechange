I've been a young mother, a caregiver, a small business owner, and a transportation professional working on some of Utah's largest infrastructure projects.
Like many families across Tooele, Juab, and Millard Counties, I've experienced the challenges of balancing work, family, healthcare, rising costs, and an uncertain future.
Today, too many Utah families feel like they're doing everything right but still struggling to get ahead.
Housing costs continue to rise.
Healthcare remains difficult to access, especially in rural communities.
Traffic and infrastructure haven't kept pace with growth.
Families want safe communities, good-paying jobs, and leaders who will focus on practical solutions instead of political division.
That's why I'm running for the Utah House District 29 I believe most Utahns are not interested in partisan fights or politics.
They want results.
They want government to be accountable, tax dollars to be spent wisely, and decisions to be made with transparency and common sense.
I am a Blue Dog Democrat because I believe fiscal responsibility and compassionate leadership can—and should—go hand in hand.
We can support working families while demanding accountability.
We can invest in infrastructure, education, and healthcare while being responsible stewards of taxpayer dollars.
We can protect our rural communities while planning for future growth.
My commitment is simple: listen first, tell the truth, and focus on solving problems.
This campaign isn't about politics, I am not a politician.
It's about building a future where our children and grandchildren can afford to live, work, and thrive in the communities they call home.
Together, we can create a Utah that remains affordable, accountable, and accessible for everyone.

Temporary Access Road – Immediate Relief I am actively working with state, local, and private stakeholders to advance a temporary road connection to relieve pressure on SR-36.
This is not a long-term concept—it is a near-term, executable solution designed to: Reduce heavy truck congestion through residential corridors Improve emergency response reliability Provide immediate safety relief for daily commuters This is about getting something built now , not years from now.
Holding Industry Accountable – Not Taxpayers I am pushing for a framework where industrial users and freight operations contribute directly to the infrastructure they impact—so local families are no longer subsidizing system strain.
Coordinating Real Solutions Across Agencies I am already engaging with: UDOT and regional planners Local municipalities and county leadership Industry operators moving freight through the valley The focus is simple: align decision-makers and remove bottlenecks that are preventing action.
