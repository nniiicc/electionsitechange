No More Wars!
As an Afghan American who has traveled to Afghanistan and witnessed the consequences of decades of conflict, I understand how decisions made in Washington can alter the lives of families thousands of miles away for generations.
I have seen what happens when powerful nations treat another country like a battlefield instead of a home.
Families are displaced.
Communities are torn apart.
Children inherit trauma and instability they did not create.
That experience has shaped a simple belief: American foreign policy should make the world more secure, not leave countries more broken than we found them.
For more than two decades, the United States has conducted military operations across Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Libya, Yemen, Pakistan, Somalia, and elsewhere.
The cost cannot be measured only in military spending or territory.
It must also be measured in civilians killed, families forced from their homes, veterans carrying the wounds of repeated deployments, and refugees searching for safety after their countries have been destabilized.
Military force without a clear political solution can deepen the very dangers it is supposed to eliminate.
Civilian casualties and prolonged occupations fuel anger, strengthen extremist recruitment, and undermine trust in the United States.
We cannot bomb our way to lasting peace.
Our Constitution provides a clear safeguard against endless conflict.
Congress has the responsibility to debate and authorize war.
Yet presidents of both parties have relied on outdated authorizations or acted without a direct vote from the representatives of the American people.
No president should have unlimited authority to commit our troops to open-ended military operations.
Congress must reclaim its constitutional responsibility, and the public deserves to know the mission, its cost, and how it will end.
National security spending must also face the same scrutiny as every other part of the federal budget.
In 2018, the Pentagon received approximately $700 billion, while the projected costs of the F-35 fighter jet program and nuclear weapons modernization reached into the trillions.
Washington cannot continue approving enormous military expenditures with little resistance while telling working families that healthcare, housing, education, and economic security are unaffordable.
Changing course does not mean withdrawing from the world.
It means leading with discipline and purpose.
The United States should invest in diplomacy, humanitarian assistance, international partnerships, and economic cooperation before turning to military force.
We should work with our allies and international institutions whenever possible instead of acting alone.
We must end open-ended deployments, stop unauthorized air campaigns, and return service members from conflicts that lack clear goals or a responsible conclusion.
The best way to honor our troops is not to send them into wars that political leaders cannot explain and refuse to end.
America is strongest when our word carries weight, our alliances are trusted, and our military is used only as a true last resort.
A responsible foreign policy can protect our country, reunite military families, rebuild relationships abroad, and prevent another generation from inheriting wars that should have ended long ago.
