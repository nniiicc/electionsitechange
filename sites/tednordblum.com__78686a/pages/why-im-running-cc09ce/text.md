It was forged in personal tragedy.
In November 2021, my family's world was turned upside down when my brother died from a fentanyl overdose.
The grief was immense, but it was followed by a burning question: how could this happen?
As I looked for answers, I saw a state that was failing its most vulnerable.
A broken system where criminals are coddled, and families are left to pick up the pieces.
I saw a government in Sacramento that was more interested in pushing political agendas than in solving the real problems that are devastating our communities.
I knew then that I couldn't stand by and watch it happen.
I had to do something.
I had to fight for a California where no other family has to experience the pain that mine has.
