The Issues Agriculture & Rural Economy Nathan grew up on the farm.
He witnessed farming success and neighbors pulling together to help each other in need.
He witnessed farms go under and the destruction and absorption of small, family run farms by larger and larger corporations.
He sees that family farms have much the same problems and concerns as small businesses and working families everywhere - skyrocketing health insurance costs, corporate consolidation and too much money, resources and power going to too few at the top.
Healthcare support and relief, greater wired broadband access and better crisis supports benefit everyone.
AI Datacenters Artificial Intelligence is no longer the villain in dystopian science fiction stories.
Now, A.I. large language models seem to be trying to become the villain in present day communities, driving up electricity prices and promising benefits to a wealthy few while taking jobs from working people.
It’s OK to ask questions like, “Why do we need this?
Who benefits?
How does this benefit people?” It’s not OK to ignore these questions and make decisions behind closed doors.
Those building these centers should be required to make their proposal to the communities public.
Education The founders of this country knew educated citizens were required for a thriving democracy.
One reason businesses are drawn to Minnesota is our commitment to education and the rewards it brings to our state.
Too often schools have been the first cut when budget projections fall and emergency funds dwindle.
Investment in education is investment in Minnesota’s future.
The returns benefit everyone.
Supporting teachers and education is supporting democracy itself.
Healthcare Access Nathan believes we need universal single-payer healthcare as soon as possible.
People love doctors, nurses and healthcare workers.
Does anyone truly love their corporate health insurance company?
Doctors and Emergency Medical Technicians make their living helping people.
Insurance companies make record profits by denying coverage.
Medical bankruptcy is a thing of the past in countries with universal coverage.
We should move into the future with universal coverage because it’s cost efficient, better for patients, and better for businesses burdened with the search for plans for their employees.
Bonus - it’s the right thing to do.
