About Steve A leader who stays involved.
From the panel-board to the village board, Steve has spent two decades building businesses, families, and a community that takes care of its own.
Steve Wicklund is a three-term Village President of Union Grove.
He runs BSW Electric, an 11-employee shop serving commercial, residential, healthcare, and public-works clients across southeastern Wisconsin.
Before he was elected to lead the village, he served on the Community Development Committee and helped guide land-use, planning, and municipal decisions that shape life in his hometown.
He and Cindy have been married for 20 years.
They are raising three sons (Zak, Cole, and Brody) and have lived in Union Grove for 14 years.

Local Values.
Practical Leadership.
The issues that show up at kitchen tables and on Main Street, not just at the Capitol.
01 Cut Property Taxes Wisconsin homeowners are getting priced out, and the bill keeps landing back on local ballots.
East Troy asked for $4 million a year in April 2026 and voters said no, 61 percent to 39.
Burlington passed $13.8 million over three years in 2025 after two failed attempts.
Steve will push the state to carry more of the school funding load instead of shifting it onto your property tax bill.
02 End Unfunded Mandates The state writes the rules and sends the cost to local government.
PFAS testing, stormwater compliance, juvenile placement.
The daily rate counties pay to house one juvenile offender has climbed from $284 in 2015 to more than $1,200, roughly $460,000 a year for a single placement.
Those costs land on your property tax bill.
Steve will fix it at the source.
03 School Choice & Parental Rights Parents deserve real options and real answers.
Steve will protect the Wisconsin Parental Choice Program, push for full curriculum transparency, and require that 70 percent of school operating dollars reach the classroom rather than administration.
04 Fully Fund Public Safety Backing the men and women who keep our communities safe.
Full funding for law enforcement and first responders, defending truth in sentencing, and holding the line against bail policies that put violent offenders back on the street before trial.
05 Data Centers Pay Their Way Racine County is becoming a data center hub.
That has to mean jobs here, not higher electric bills for everyone else.
In April 2026 the Public Service Commission rewrote We Energies’ rules for its largest power users, requiring contracts of at least 15 years and full payment for the generation built to serve them.
Steve will defend that protection and back the training pipeline that turns these projects into local careers, like Gateway Technical College’s Microsoft Data Center certificate.
06 Support Farmers & Trades Cut the red tape strangling Wisconsin farms, protect farmland from unchecked development, and expand market access.
Skilled work has been undervalued for a generation, so Steve will invest in the apprenticeships, technical colleges, and trades incentives that build the workforce our economy actually needs.

Register to Vote Not registered, or moved since you last voted?
Register online or by mail through October 14.
After that you can register in person at your municipal clerk’s office through October 30 at 5 p.m., or at your polling place on Election Day with proof of residence.
Check your registration at myvote.wi.gov.
Vote Early or Absentee Request a mailed absentee ballot at myvote.wi.gov by October 29 at 5 p.m.
If you would rather vote in person before Election Day, in person absentee voting runs October 20 through November 1.
Hours vary by municipality, so check with your clerk for times and locations.
Vote on Election Day Polls are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3.
Bring a photo ID.
Completed absentee ballots must reach your clerk by 8 p.m. that day.
Find your polling place at myvote.wi.gov.
District 33 covers eastern Walworth County and southwest Racine County, so your clerk and polling place depend on where you live. myvote.wi.gov will point you to the right one.
