As a teacher, parent, and lifelong Southern Indiana resident, I believe state government should focus on what matters most: strong schools, affordable living, accessible healthcare, and real opportunity for local families and businesses.
Public education Fully fund and protect public schools As a former teacher, I know how much students and educators need the resources to succeed.
Public schools are the foundation of our communities — they deserve serious, sustained investment.
Make sure public schools receive the funding students and teachers need Support educators with fair pay and classroom resources Strengthen local schools so families can count on them From Sarah's reporting: When politicians attack a school band program Strong schools build stronger communities — and every child deserves the chance to succeed.
Lower costs Property tax & utility relief Families across District 70 are stretched thin by rising costs while large corporations get tax breaks.
It's time for a government that protects Hoosiers' wallets first.
Deliver real property tax relief for working families Bring transparency to how taxes are calculated Stop utilities from shifting costs onto ratepayers Make sure corporations pay their fair share Hoosiers deserve a government that protects their wallets.
Data centers Hoosiers first, not billion-dollar corporations Why are our cities and counties scrambling to protect us from massive data centers while our state government hands those same companies enormous incentives to come here?
Harrison County passed a one-year moratorium.
So did New Albany.
Madison County went six months.
Boone County and many others are taking action too — as of this summer, roughly a third of Indiana counties have moved to restrict data-center development.
That is not happening by accident.
Local leaders are asking some pretty basic questions: How much water will these facilities use?
What will they do to our electric grid and our utility bills?
Who pays for the infrastructure they require?
What happens to nearby homeowners and farmland?
What about noise, generators, and pollution?
Meanwhile, Indiana has created huge tax incentives for qualifying data centers — including exemptions on equipment and electricity that can last 25 years, and in some cases up to 50 years.
Fifty years.
I use AI.
I use technology.
I am not against progress.
I am against giving billion-dollar corporations the red carpet while Hoosier communities are left trying to figure out how to protect themselves.
In the Indiana State House, I will fight to: Protect families from subsidizing massive corporate electric demand Require transparency about water and electricity use before approval Protect local control and zoning authority Require strong noise, pollution, and environmental safeguards Make data centers pay their fair share for the infrastructure they need Take a hard look at these massive tax breaks and ask one simple question: what are Hoosiers getting in return?
Our local governments should not have to save us from bad state policy.
The Statehouse should be protecting us too.
If a corporation wants to build here, fine — but they should not get unlimited access to our water, our power, and decades of tax breaks without accountability.
