As a citizen, I take citizenship seriously.
Citizenship asks something of all of us.
It asks us to pay attention and understand where our experience can contribute to making our communities better.
Citizenship means recognizing that government is not something other people do to us; it is something we are responsible for doing together.
That’s why I’ve decided to run for office and more specifically to run as an independent.
I’ve never wanted to be a politician.
I believe that when we see harm being done to our communities, we have a responsibility to do more than complain about it.
We need to step up and do our part.
People in Boundary and Bonner counties practice citizenship every day by volunteering, running local businesses, coaching kids, running 4-H clubs, maintaining roads, fighting fires, caring for neighbors, caring for family, and participating in community organizations.
Running for office is how I can do my part.
We need everyone doing their part to make it all work.
Legislators are asked to pledge one oath.
The exact wording of the oath is: “I do solemnly swear that I will support the constitution of the United States and the constitution of the state of Idaho, and that I will faithfully discharge the duties of representative according to the best of my ability.” It’s not an oath to a political party or to the Citizens Alliance of Idaho or the Freedom Caucus or donors or a national think tank or a scorecard.
It’s a promise to uphold the constitution and faithfully represent the people.
Running as an independent makes that promise especially clear.
I will evaluate legislation according to its constitutional limits and the consequences for North Idaho.
I’ll scrutinize and question every decision and use my best judgment.
I won’t follow instructions handed down by a party or an outside organization.
In practical terms, this oath means protecting liberties guaranteed in the U.s.
Constitution including: The freedom to speak, worship, publish, assemble, petition government, and criticize public officials without government punishment Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures Due process before government deprives someone of life, liberty, or property Equal protection under the law for everyone The right to keep and bear arms The supremacy of the U.S.
Constitution and valid federal law when state and federal authority conflict Upholding the Idaho constitution carries these obligations: Public education: Idaho must maintain a general, uniform, and thorough system of public schools.
Citizen lawmaking: Idahoans retain the power to propose laws through initiative and approve or reject laws through referendum.
Government by the people: Political power belongs to the people.
Elected officials exercise borrowed power; they do not own it.
Equal government: Government exists for the common benefit—not to grant special power or advantage to one party, organization, or favored interest.
Local authority: Cities and counties have constitutional authority to adopt local regulations, provided those regulations do not conflict with state law.
The Legislature should not casually change state law simply to strip communities of that authority.
Fiscal restraint: The Constitution places limits on state debt and requires the Legislature to account honestly for how public money is raised and spent.
Water rights: The right to put unappropriated water to beneficial use is foundational to Idaho’s farms, ranches, communities, and way of life.
Endowment lands: State trust lands must be managed faithfully for the long-term benefit of Idaho’s schools and other constitutional beneficiaries.
Hunting, fishing, and trapping: These are constitutionally protected parts of Idaho’s heritage.
Honest lawmaking: Each bill must address one subject, clearly expressed in its title, so unrelated provisions cannot be hidden inside it.
I’m concerned.
These are not abstract promises.
They are guardrails on political power.
Idaho is a small state and outside interests can have big influence for relatively small investments.
Why I am concerned I see troubling trends that challenge the spirit of that oath.
Model legislation arrives from national organizations instead of bills being developed in response to genuine Idaho needs.
There are repeated efforts to make the citizens’ initiative process harder and legislators voice commitment to overturn decisions made by Idaho voters.
The Legislature changes state law to prevent cities and counties from making decisions that should be made locally.
Legislation is passed despite knowing there are serious constitutional concerns.
This leaves taxpayers to pay the cost when those laws are challenged in court.
We are creating a cottage industry of litigation.
I hear repeated calls to “take back” federal public lands without an honest accounting of what state ownership could mean for public access, hunting, fishing, wildfire protection, and the risk of future land sales.
And I see legislators judged by party loyalty and private scorecards instead of by whether their decisions protect their constituents and serve Idaho well.
I am running as an independent because I take my responsibility as a citizen seriously—and because I believe representation should be an act of citizenship, not an act of party obedience.
