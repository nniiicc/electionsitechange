HOW the IFF Kills Bills
Episode 1: Senate Bill 1397, 2026 Session
Each week, the Ledger will analyze one Idaho legislative bill: its purpose, its impact on District 1, and how the IFF influenced its results.
We will be connecting the dots between votes and North Idaho’s strength.
The Problem
Monopoly sewer companies can just decide to hike rates on Idaho property owners in Idaho.
Homeowners just have to pay.
Rural Idaho has many private wastewater/sewer systems.
When they raise rates and property owners have to pay.
A real case: A wastewater system was sold to a large company that immediately doubled rates and put a moratorium on hookups.
Some property owners were forced to pay double the rates and were prevented from building.
Lose – lose.
Purpose of the Bill
- Create certainty for small private wastewater companies with fewer than 100 hookups.
Create clarity about what requires regulation.
- Ensure that large monopolies cannot drive prices up artificially or deny service unexpectedly.
- Protect local drinking water by requiring standards for the treatment of wastewater in large private subdivisions.
IFF Scoring
- IFF prioritized the rights of the monopolies over the property owners and small wastewater systems.
It called sewer systems an open marketplace, as if multiple competing companies might build a wastewater system in the same subdivision.
How IFF Scoring Controlled Legislative Behavior
Behind closed doors, representatives agreed that the bill was good for rural Idaho communities.
Publicly, they chose the good grade over their constituents and the bill died on the floor.
Anticipated Effects on District 1
If the bill had passed,
- It would have protected homeowners from damage to property values and eliminated unexpected costs from larger sewer systems.
- It also protected small sewer companies from unexpected government regulation.
