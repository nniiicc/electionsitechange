Property Tax Elimination is a shiny object being pitched by Idaho politicians.
It’s not new, but it’s gaining momentum.
The idea of no taxes sounds good until you realize that their “plan” leaves Idaho homeowners subsidizing vacation property owners.
Property tax is the only tax paid by people who own here but don’t live here.
If we eliminate that tax, vacation property owners and corporations doing business here will no longer contribute to local services – snow removal, fire protection, police, emergency services, etc.
The people who live District 1 will have to pay the difference.
It’s over a half billion a year, statewide.

The Problem
Property taxes feel unfair.
We built a house years ago and the assessed value has skyrocketed.
We paid our mortgage down to nearly nothing, yet taxes and insurance keep our monthly payments high.
The homeowner’s exemption, brought by a citizens’ initiative, was made ineffective in 2016 when it was capped at a $100K valuation depreciation.
Property taxes are essential to pay for roads, fire, police, ambulance, sewer, schools, and more.
The counties and cities can determine how to deploy those dollars.
The state is advertising low income and property taxes to attract national buyers and it’s working.
People are buying property here, which drives up prices.
Those people often don’t become part of our communities.
Six years ago, the House leadership proposed a bill to eliminate property taxes.
It did not progress to a vote.
What happens if you remove property taxes?
Let’s ask the three questions.
- who pays?
Idaho homeowners foot the bill for all local services
- who benefits?
Vacation property owners and corporations who no longer pay their share of local infrastructure costs
- who decides?
Boise and the think tanks and caucuses funded by dark money
43% of the homes in unincorporated Bonner County are not lived in full time.
These owners currently pay property tax, no Idaho income tax, and barely any sales tax.
Many nonresident property owners want to pay their fair share of local services.
If the IFF is successful in removing property tax, the revenue from that 43% is no longer available to the community.
That 43% is volume.
If we add in the revenue generated from Home Depot, Walmart, and North 40, it grows even more.
Are we, as homeowners, prepared to foot the bill for vacation properties and corporations?
My property valuation went up 69%.
I do believe we need to address the burden on homeowners.
What Property Tax elimination Proposed
- Pretended to ease the burden on Idaho homeowners while shrinking the pool of people paying in to support local services.
- Replaced the lost revenue with an 11% sales tax, including groceries – the highest in the US.
- Took control of from local governments to the statehouse
How the IFF Influenced the outcome
The Freedom Index has an agenda.
You get points for cutting the money.
You lose points for spending it.
The IFF scored HB 359 at +1 for cutting taxes.
Then they took away a point because the bill replaced the lost revenue with sales tax.
The outcome was a rating of zero which allowed legislators to vote either way without penalty.

Anticipated Effects on District 1
The IFF says they’ll make up the revenue generated through property tax through “the natural growth in sales and income taxes at the state level and hand it to local governments instead.” That means increasing sales tax.
Your fire district, your roads, your kids’ school all funded out of the sales tax you pay on groceries every week.
And the man with the house on the lake stops paying for the road that gets plowed to his gate.
Covering his share?
That’s not tax relief.
That’s subsidizing the guy down the block.
You might have heard this:
- Other states have already done this.
No state has.
North Dakota came closest.
It was on the ballot in November 2024 and voters said no — 63.5% against.
All fifty-three counties voted it down.
The opposition was fire chiefs, sheriffs, EMS, school boards, the farmers union and the chamber of commerce.
The man who wrote it was a Republican legislator who called property tax immoral.
North Dakotans agreed it felt unfair and still voted no, because nobody could say what would pay for the ambulance.
- Tourists and out-of-staters will pay it at the register.

They warned that an 11% rate — highest in the country — would push Idahoans across the line to shop in Montana and Oregon, and would cost us tourists.
And do the arithmetic on the lake house.
He’s here six weeks a year.
He pays the sales tax for six weeks.
You pay it for fifty-two.

That math works at 6%.
At 11% the same $620 covers about $5,600.
