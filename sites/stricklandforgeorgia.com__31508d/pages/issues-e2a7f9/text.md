Brian's priorities begin with a straightforward question: who needs protection, and what can the law do to help?
He is not asking Georgians to judge him on promises alone.
He has a record, and it shows where he stands.

The focus will be on coordination, enforcement, and results.
Brian's Record Georgia faces real threats from organized crime and criminal networks that cross city and county lines.
He helped protect the pay of law enforcement officers injured in the line of duty, because an officer who was hurt while protecting the public should not face a financial crisis at home.
He worked on laws to confront gangs and organized crime, including efforts to keep children from being recruited into them.
He supported stronger laws against deadly street narcotics and human trafficking, and he fixed the backlog in competency hearings that had left people waiting in jail far too long.
He has also backed reforms that help people who have served their time find work and stay out of the system, because reducing repeated crime keeps everyone safer.
Fighting for Our Most Vulnerable Support the Campaign VOTE NOV 3, 2026 Click to Watch Brian's Message “ The measure of our state is how we treat the people who need help the most. ” As Attorney General Brian wants Georgia to be the best state in the country for families with special needs and for access to mental health care, and he believes the Attorney General should bring that same commitment to protecting consumers.
That means going after the scams that target seniors, identity theft, price gouging, and businesses that cheat the people they serve.
When Georgians are taken advantage of, the office should act.
Brian's Record Brian has spent years building a record to match those words.
He wrote laws to protect seniors from abuse and to improve care in nursing homes.
He gave families the ability to run background checks on the people they hire to work inside their homes.
He protected pregnant women in the workplace, let workers use their sick leave to care for family members, and strengthened the rights of grandparents raising grandchildren.
He stood up for victims of human trafficking, helped bring national opioid-settlement money home to Georgia communities, and backed strong action against Medicaid fraud so that money meant for people who need care is not stolen.
The clearest example of how Brian works is what he did for Georgia's foster children.
For years, children in state care were sleeping in government offices and hotel rooms, not for a single night, but sometimes for weeks at a time, with no bed, no school, and no one to count on.
Everyone agreed it was wrong, but almost no one believed it could be fixed.
Brian took it on.
He wrote Senate Bill 133 and built the coalition to pass it, sitting down with Governor Kemp, the Department of Human Services, and the veteran juvenile court judges who saw this problem in their courtrooms every day.
The law created a single, uniform process for how a child enters state custody, required courts to exhaust every option to keep a child safely with family first, and made the courts and the child welfare system work together instead of around each other.
It passed the Senate 53 to 1.
He also opened the door to college for young people aging out of foster care, because a child the system has already failed deserves a real shot at a future.
Fixing something this broken takes more than one bill, and Brian has said as much himself.
But a hotel room is no place to raise a child, and he was willing to spend the months it took to move Georgia closer to ending the practice.
That is how he works: he finds the problem, does the work, and keeps at it until it is fixed.
Fairness and Accountability Under the Law Support the Campaign “ The same law should apply to everyone.
No one should receive special treatment. ” As Attorney General The Attorney General holds enormous power, and that power has to be used carefully.
Brian believes the office should base its decisions on the law and the facts, not on someone's party, wealth, influence, or connections.
Brian's Record Brian's dedication to the impartiality of the law runs through his record.
He established nonpartisan elections for probate court judges so that those races stay focused on qualifications and the work of the court.
He guaranteed a fair discovery process in misdemeanor cases, because people facing charges deserve both the information the law entitles them to and a process that treats them fairly.
His experience as a small-town lawyer and as Chairman of the Judiciary Committee has taught him how the system affects victims, defendants, families, law enforcement, and entire communities.
Brian also believes that public office is a public trust.
Government officials should answer to the same law as everyone else, and the Attorney General's office should never be used to reward friends or punish opponents.
Protecting Georgia's Land, Water, and Farms Support the Campaign “ Georgia's growth should not come at the expense of clean water, healthy communities, or working farms. ” As Attorney General Brian will work to protect Georgia's natural resources and shield family farms from unnecessary rules pushed on them from Washington, DC and other states, while still supporting responsible growth.
Brian's Record Georgia is growing fast.
Brian is proud that it remains one of the best states in the country to do business, but that growth should not come at the expense of the communities people call home.
These issues are personal for Brian, whose own family history is rooted in Georgia agriculture and in the parks and coastline that make this state special, including Jekyll Island.
He helped create and fund the Georgia Farmland Conservation Fund to protect the working farms that feed the state and carry its heritage forward.
He drafted legislation to address the release of hazardous substances like ethylene oxide, because communities deserve a serious response when dangerous chemicals threaten public health.
