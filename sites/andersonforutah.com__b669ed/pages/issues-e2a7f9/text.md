We need a real change in our government, and real representation.
THE ISsues
Utah is an amazing place to live and raise a family, but our community faces some serious challenges: The lake is shrinking, the sky is dirty, and our children can’t afford to live here.
- Government and State Representatives should be accountable to the people they represent.
Our rights in a democracy have been under attack from unfair maps, judicial restructuring, and MIDA asserting extra-governmental authority.
Local control has been diminishing as the state takes more power for itself, and those in power desperately try to hold onto power despite the will of the people.
I took an oath to protect and defend our Constitution, and I believe a government by the people, for the people is what makes America great.
- Utah is facing enormous pressure with the high cost of housing.
With unattainable housing, families and small businesses have no place to get started.
The cost of housing is causing negative impacts on the economy, and our quality of life.
The state has a lot of power to change this dynamic.
The first step is changing tax law to benefit owner-occupied homes and discourage private equity acquisition of single-family homes.
Thirty percent of housing units in Salt Lake County are corporate owned.
We must find ways to discourage corporate ownership and allow real people to own the properties they live in.
- We must take bold and dramatic action to save the Great Salt Lake right now.
We have run out of time for small fixes.
We cannot deny that Utah is one of the fastest growing states in the country, and much of that growth is happening on the Wasatch Front.
We are constrained by mountains on one side and the Great Salt Lake on the other.
We need to invest in development policies that use less land, and less water but still allow people to live and flourish in this great state.
Agricultural use of water must be addressed head on.
My background in agricultural economics provides me with unique insights for tackling a tough issue.
- Little Cottonwood Canyon is a unique place that is dear to many of us in Utah.
A massive gondola that will cost nearly a billion dollars is not the right solution to traffic problems that only occur for a few days of the year.
We need to invest our tax payer money in traffic solutions that affect the majority of Utahns during our daily commutes.
- Every day I see the haze in our skies that blocks our mountain views I worry about what consequences my children will face.
It is a challenging problem but one we know how to fix.
First, we have to restore the Great Salt Lake to prevent toxic dust storms.
Second, we must invest in clean public transportation.
Our skies will not be clear until we reduce our reliance on cars.
Traffic congestion continues to get worse.
We must invest more in efficient public transportation and active transportation networks.
- The cornerstone of a strong economy is a healthy and well-educated population.
I want to see public schools with adequate resources for students and teachers.
Teachers are dedicated and skilled professionals that deserve greater respect and support.
- For-profit health insurance companies have proven to be only interested in CEO pay and Wall Street bonuses.
We must act now to protect affordable access to healthcare for all Utahns.
State level action can be taken to enforce anti-trust laws that would break up the big health insurance companies that extract wealth from our communities.
- Tax policy can be adjusted to lower property taxes for home owners.
Let’s start with ending the wasteful use of public money on individual households with the Utah Fits All Scholarship.
We also need to restore Utah’s commitment to funding education.
The legislature has been cutting income taxes which results in the burden of education funding going to property owners and retirees in local communities.
We must restore education funding from income taxes and reduce the burden of property taxes on local owners.
- Data Center build out is occurring at a reckless pace without community support and feedback.
Local communities need transparency, communication, and control of the process when data centers are planned.
They need to time to understand the potential downsides with noise, heat, water, and energy use, as well as how it will affect the tax base and prices.
