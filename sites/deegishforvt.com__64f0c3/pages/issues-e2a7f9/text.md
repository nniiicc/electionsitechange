Information on H.955 and Act 170: Education bill H.955 , and Act 170 An act relating to next steps in transforming Vermont’s education system , are education transformation laws.
From the Secretary of Education website: Act 73 established a process to a transition the public education system featuring larger school districts, a weighted student funding formula, and a statewide tax rate.
Act 170 made significant adjustments, including extending the timeline , pivoting from state-directed redistricting to district-led mergers, mandating participation in cooperative educational service areas (CESAs and formerly known as BOCES), and making enactment of the foundation formula and statewide tax rate contingent upon additional legislation in 2027 and 2028.
Act 73 and Act 170 also pave the way for statewide graduation requirements and a statewide calendar, class size minimums, state aid for school construction, and revised criteria for independent schools to receive public dollars.
Together, these components represent a multi-pronged approach to achieving the twin objectives of boosting educational outcomes while bending the cost curve of funding education in Vermont.
Act 73 and Act 170 are not definitive laws.
While certain components have already gone into effect (such as revised criteria for independent schools, the process to implement statewide graduation requirements, and establishment of CESAs), there are significant decisions in Vermont’s education transformation that will depend on additional legislative action, future reports, and local voter approval.
Further notes: The foundation formula will decide the per pupil cost statewide baseline and will distribute education funds based on the number of students and demographic factors including economically disadvantaged students and English-language learners.
Act 170 calls for school districts to join merger committee meetings beginning this fall to consider possible school mergers.
School mergers are voluntary and the committee process allows for local voices.
Towns will be considering if a school merger is right for them - will a merger keep their community viable?
Currently, the town of Thetford is assigned to school district merger committee Group 10.
Sharon and Strafford are assigned to Group 9 and Norwich is exempt from the merger study group because it is an interstate district.
The state is aiming for districts of at least 2,000 students, with a special track for orphan districts under 750 students.
Voters will decide mergers on Town Meeting Day in 2028.
Mergers are incentivized by school construction aid.
Towns will need to think creatively about grade clusters and configuration merger possibilities that could be helpful in cost reductions and improved student opportunities and outcomes.
I will oppose foundation formula and excess spending penalty models that will defund our communities' schools and penalize local taxpayers.
Housing An estimated 10,000 units of housing are needed in the Upper Valley by 2030 to meet increased demand, and according to the Vermont Housing Needs Assessment , 36,000 primary homes are needed in Vermont.
Meanwhile, the cost of building a 2,000 foot home in Vermont is estimated to be between $500,000 - $800,000.
I will fight to help Vermonters spend less than 30% of their annual income on housing - what is considered as affordable - and will fight for incentives for small scale developers to create affordable and missing middle housing.
I will fight for efforts at the state and local level to incentivize year round occupancy through appropriate tax allocations.
In Sharon and Strafford, for example, the Homestead education tax rate is less than the Non-Homestead rate, while in Norwich and Thetford, the Non-Homestead education tax rate is lower than the Homestead rate.
I will support new tiers for the Non-Homestead rate so that second homes and short-term rentals are separately identified and taxed higher than homesteads.
My husband and I were able to renovate a farmhouse in Sharon village into three energy efficient rental units.
The timing for the project - during the Covid pandemic - made the project possible because we were able to find an excellent general contractor, get a low interest rate, and the price of building materials had not yet ballooned.
I will work to help small scale developers secure low interest loans and purchase building materials.
Many statewide programs exist to help improve the housing shortage.
I support these programs: Homes for All and 802 Homes is a project of the Agency of Commerce and Community Development to help put community tested home designs with streamlined permitting in the hands of small-scale developers to accelerate home building projects.
The Vermont Housing Finance Agency offers Downpayment Assistance Program and First-Generation Homebuyer Grants.
Agency of Commerce and Community Development Downtown and Village Center Tax Credit program.
The Community Housing and Infrastructure Program ( CHIP ) is a state tax increment financing tool for investments in public infrastructure (water, sewer, roads and stormwater systems) to support new housing development.
The Vermont Affordable Housing Coalition is a nonprofit membership organization that advocates for expanding affordable housing and to ensure that all Vermonters have safe, adequate, physically accessible housing.
Members include Habitat for Humanity .
Vermont State Housing Authority provides critical housing assistance for Section 8 vouchers, eviction prevention and manufactured home repairs.
Affordability Vermonter's are struggling to afford rent, groceries, gas, and utilities.
Vermont has the most expensive healthcare premiums in the country and the fifth highest property tax rates.
There is a fundamental global problem: too few people have too much wealth and power.
And far too many people don't have nearly enough.
Our multiple crises of housing, healthcare, education, climate and democracy are tied to this inequality.
As federal support of food assistance, healthcare and education declines, the collective wealth of our communities suffers and more Vermonters struggle to make ends meet.
I will work hard to make tough budget decisions that will minimize cost burdens of our most vulnerable residents and work to find solutions to the challenge of unaffordable healthcare and property taxes.
I will fight for universal primary care to improve overall community wellness and reduce healthcare costs through simplified billing and negotiation of medical costs.
I also favor reference based pricing to tie hospital reimbursement rates to benchmarks based on Medicare rates.
This year, Vermont joined ArrayRx, a multi-state collaborative to help pool purchasing power for prescription drugs, making VT able to negotiate and lower prices.
I will support a similar multi-state collaborative to pool health insurance coverage.

Climate Change Climate change is expensive.
The 2023 and 2024 flood events in Vermont caused $1 billion of destruction.
Humans emit 175 million tons of greenhouse gasses into the atmosphere every day, causing more solar radiation to be trapped and raising temperatures.
The eleven hottest years on record have been these last eleven years.
Climate change is causing more frequent and catastrophic heatwaves, wildfires, droughts and floods and is negatively impacting the economy, infrastructure, ecosystems and tourism here in Vermont.
Climate scientists predict Vermont will have fewer days of snow, more frequent heavy rain events and flooding, longer periods of drought and higher temperatures.
Heavy rain events require expensive road maintenance, adding costs to our town budgets.
Ticks and tick related diseases are increasing in Vermont as well due to climate change.
Ticks like it warm and wet and Vermont's recent wet springs have resulted in an increase in tick bites .
We need to urgently reduce our use of fossil fuels.
I will fight for greater access for all Vermonters to renewable energy, public transportation, electrification of heating and transportation, and local energy solutions such as thermal energy networks, and I will support programs to help Vermont reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
In 2015, as chair of the Sharon Energy Committee, I secured a state Clean Energy Development Fund matching grant for a 12kW solar PV system for the Sharon Elementary School.
I will work for similar state grant opportunities for our schools, businesses and nonprofits.
I will urge the Public Utility Commission to restore more favorable siting charges for small solar projects and to permit, once again, community solar projects, so that more Vermonters can afford to install net-metered solar.
I support legislation that would protect Vermont against AI data center development.
I will defend Act 47, the Vermont Climate Superfund that provides the state can recover costs from fossil fuel companies from climate change impacts here in Vermont.
I support giving legal standing for the rights of nature.
I am pleased that S.202 - Plug-in Solar - makes it easy for homeowners and renters to install qualifying plug-in solar devices to help lower utility costs.
S.202 also adopted all current federal appliance efficiency standards.
I applaud Efficiency Vermont for helping Vermont transition to a more affordable and cleaner future.
Efficiency Vermont helps to save customers money, strengthen Vermont’s economy, and lower carbon emissions and offers rebates on heat pumps, appliances, weatherization, heat pump water heaters, electric vehicles and EV charging, and e-bikes.
