Temporary transfer laws allow law enforcement to petition a judge for the right to temporarily transfer firearms from a person who presents an imminent risk to themselves or another person.
For a judge to agree, law enforcement must meet the high standard of presenting “clear and convincing evidence.” At a hearing before the judge, the owner of the firearms has a right to retain and be represented by counsel.
If the judge orders the temporary transfer, depending on the specifics of the law, the firearms can be held in a law enforcement evidence locker, by a trusted family member or friend, or by a licensed firearms dealer.
When the crisis has passed, those firearms are returned to the owner.
In Tennessee, private firearms sales in person, online, or at a gun show are not required to undergo a background check.
Without a background check, felons, those with mental illness, and domestic abusers can subvert the federally required background check performed by licensed firearms dealers.
Safely storing firearms Ten years after the legislature passed a law allowing firearms to be stored in an unoccupied vehicle, Tennessee ranks first in stolen firearms.
Memphis ranks first among major cities with approximately 546 stolen firearms per 100,000 residents.
A safe storage law would require actually locking the firearm in a separate container and establish incentives for complying with the law.

Consistently, Tennesseans support sensible gun legislation.
The semi-annual Vanderbilt University poll found that 78% support temporary transfer and 69% back safe storage.
Majority support cuts across partisan lines.
But what about the Second Amendment?
I grew up on a farm in Hickman County, and my father and brother were law enforcement officers.
Guns have always been a part of my life.
Owning a gun is a right we enjoy as Americans, but with rights come responsibilities.
Ownership of a firearm should include safeguards for fellow citizens, especially for the most vulnerable.
Not everyone sees it that way, and, unfortunately, society cannot count on some of its fellow citizens to take the necessary steps to protect children from being wounded or worse.
However, the state legislature can, if they have the courage to stand up to the gun lobby.
Laws like temporary transfer, comprehensive background checks, and safe storage are not precursors to government confiscation of firearms or Orwellian-type despotism.
They are reasonable, evidence-based solutions to the number one killer of children in our state, and the will of the overwhelming majority of citizens.
These measured policy approaches are common ground for Republicans, Democrats, and Independents; urban, rural, and suburban residents; and East, Middle, and West Tennesseans.
Most importantly, they are in the best interest of those who rely on us the most -- our children.
