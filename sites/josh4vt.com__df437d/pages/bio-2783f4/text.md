WHY I'M RUNNING
Roots of Service:
The Candidate Biography of
Josh Williams
1.
The Heritage of Action:
Lessons from the Foothills
Josh Williams’ commitment to public service was not born in a political arena, but in the foothills of North Carolina, observing a blueprint for a life dedicated to others.
His foundational work ethic was forged by his grandfather, PawPaw Frank Scott, an Army medic who survived more than a year as a Prisoner of War in Nazi Germany.
Frank returned home not with stories of his trauma, but with a quiet, relentless dedication to his family, his neighbors, and complete strangers.
Josh grew up in the passenger seat of Frank’s old Ford truck, watching his grandfather weave at every passerby and stop to pick up debris from the road simply to save a stranger from the hardship of a flat tire.
By viewing service through the eyes of a man who served "when no one was looking,"
Josh learned that true leadership is an act of stewardship—a silent, heavy responsibility to protect those around you.
"If you only do something right when people are watching, then that was no way to act.
You always act the same even when no one is looking."
This early education in character created a drive in Josh to seek out roles where the stakes were highest.
However, the "silent" trauma of his grandfather’s POW experience also left a mark, eventually informing Josh’s strategic transition from the frontlines to leadership to protect his own family from the same "not knowing" his grandfather’s family had endured.
2.
From the Frontlines to Leadership:
A Career in Public Safety
Josh’s career in public safety provided a visceral, ground-level education in the systems of government.
His background is marked by a rare strategic breadth, having served across the entire spectrum of emergency response.
While many candidates speak of "crisis management" in the abstract, Josh has lived it—from the adrenaline of undercover narcotics work, where he once dyed his hair white to remain unrecognized by his own mother, to the crushing weight of making split-second life-and-death decisions.
The catalyst for his pivot from active duty to systemic leadership came during a drug bust that went "sideways," when a suspect grabbed both a handgun and an infant from a crib.
That moment solidified Josh’s realization: the weight of the badge is immense, and the most sustainable way to protect a community is by fixing the broken systems that put families and responders at risk.
The Professional Scope of Service:
Police Officer & Investigator: Specialized in undercover narcotics and private investigation, navigating complex legal and ethical landscapes.
Firefighter & EMT: Providing direct, high-pressure emergency medical care and fire suppression.
Search and Rescue Coordinator: Managing multi-agency operations to recover the missing in treacherous environments.
Emergency Dispatch: Serving as the critical, calm link between the public’s worst moments and the help they need.
EMS Executive Director: Transitioning to the "So What?" layer of leadership, Josh managed regional budgets and personnel, eventually leading his community’s entire frontline response through the heights of the COVID-19 pandemic.
3.
Choosing Vermont:
Authentic Living and Economic Contribution
Eighteen years ago, Josh chose Vermont as his permanent home, a decision sparked by his arrival in Manchester to open the Land Rover Experience.
He didn't just move here; he invested here.
Josh quickly transitioned into the state’s working landscape as a hospitality owner, purchasing and rebranding a lodge at the base of Magic Mountain.
He didn't just run a business; he built an ecosystem, adopting a "Vermont-only" philosophy that stocked his bar exclusively with local beer, wine, and spirits.
This was a deliberate economic statement: he believed in Vermont’s producers and the state’s capacity for self-reliance.
Today, Josh lives on Red Mountain, where the life he has built is defined by the community’s independent spirit.
In the Bennington District, Josh found a place that mirrors his own core values: Personal Responsibility, Local Involvement, and Independent Spirit.
It is a community that ignores the noise of identity politics and instead values a man’s character, his word, and what he contributes to his neighbors.
He is not a resident who happens to live in Vermont; he is a steward who has earned his place in its working landscape.
4.
Stewardship and Conservation:
The Pinchot Philosophy
Josh’s approach to the environment is rooted in the "Pinchot Philosophy"—the belief that conservation is not about locking the land away, but about active, practical stewardship.
He rejects the "passive preservation" model where the state or outside interests "lock up" Vermont land for carbon credits or water rights.
To Josh, the Vermont landscape is a working resource that requires human involvement to remain healthy.
Central to this vision is a fierce defense of landowner rights, ensuring that those who work the land remain its primary guardians.
Conservation Action Plan:
1.
Protecting Landowner Rights: Ensuring that the families who pay for and maintain the land are not stripped of their authority by distant bureaucrats.
2.
Active Forest Management: Moving beyond the "museum" mentality to ensure forests are productive, resilient, and managed by those who know them best.
3.
The "Win-Win" Funding Model: Educating the public on why purchasing hunting and fishing licenses is a primary act of conservation for all Vermonters.
These fees unlock critical federal matching dollars that fund state conservation for everyone, regardless of whether they ever pick up a rod or a gun.
5.
A Loud Voice for the Bennington District:
The Path Forward
Josh Williams is entering the political arena because the "Vermont Promise" is being broken by a state government that prioritizes its own agenda over the affordability of its citizens' lives.
For Josh, these issues aren't academic.
When he speaks about the healthcare crisis, he speaks as someone who lives with Type 1 Diabetes.
He depends on insulin and medical technology every single day; he knows that when the system fails or costs skyrocket, it isn't a policy debate—it's a threat to survival.
This lived experience is why he refuses to accept the status quo in Montpelier.
His mandate for the Bennington District is clear:
Ending the Healthcare Abstraction: Using his personal reliance on the system and his EMS leadership background to demand measurable results and patient-centered care.
Returning Power to Towns: Leading the charge to reform Act 250 and repeal Act 181, stripping authority from unelected state boards and returning land-use decisions to the local people who live with the consequences.
Fiscal Sanity in Education: Demanding that Montpelier provide local school boards with a fair foundation formula now, allowing for local control, school choice, and property tax relief.
Economic Viability: Cutting the regulatory "overhead" that makes it impossible for young families to stay and for small businesses to grow.
Josh Williams will not be a "silent voice" who blends into the background of the statehouse.
He is a leader who has seen the systems of government from the inside, felt the weight of a life-and-death crisis, and managed the complexities of a small business.
He is ready to be a loud, persistent advocate for the Bennington District.
"I am not running to manage Vermont’s decline.
I am running to change its direction.
I ask for your vote to bring practical leadership and honest accountability back to Montpelier.
Together, we will make Vermont affordable again."
