My plan to modernize government begins with specific legislative reforms.
I will work to strengthen Michigan's Freedom of Information Act by expanding transparency requirements to the Legislature and governor's office, improving public access to government records, and requiring greater disclosure of taxpayer-funded contracts and spending.
I will pursue reforms to the General Law Village Act of 1895 to address overlapping township and village services, improve coordination, and examine opportunities to reduce duplicative taxation without cutting essential services.
I will also advocate for modernizing state permitting, licensing, and grant application systems so residents, small businesses, and local governments can complete applications, track progress, and receive decisions without navigating unnecessary administrative barriers.
Modernizing government also means changing how we invest taxpayer dollars and measure results.
I will support legislation requiring clearer public reporting of state grants, corporate subsidies, infrastructure investments, and program performance so residents can see exactly where their money goes and what it accomplishes.
My office will establish regular meetings with local government leaders, publicly track district funding priorities, and help communities identify and pursue existing state and federal resources.
I will advocate for coordinated, long-term infrastructure planning that prioritizes road repairs, water systems, public utilities, and community development before costly emergencies occur.
Government should not be measured by how much money it spends, but by how effectively it serves the people.
Our goal is straightforward: eliminate waste, update outdated laws, protect taxpayer dollars, and build a government that is transparent, efficient, accessible, and accountable to every Michigan resident.
Strengthen Education and Tax Fairness Michigan’s education funding system was built around Proposal A in 1994, and it is time to modernize that framework to reflect what educating a child actually costs today.
I will work to reform the State School Aid Act by strengthening student-based funding, accounting for inflation, fully addressing special education needs, and creating a more equitable way to finance school buildings, classroom improvements, and technology without placing the entire burden on local property taxpayers.
That means building on Michigan’s weighted funding formula, expanding career and technical education, investing in teacher recruitment and retention, and measuring educational progress through student outcomes rather than simply how much money we spend.
I will also support expanding affordable childcare through Michigan’s existing childcare assistance and early childhood programs, increasing provider capacity, and helping working families access dependable care.
Our schools should have the resources to succeed regardless of a community’s property values, and our children deserve access to quality education from early childhood through graduation.
Tax fairness is just as important as education funding.
I will pursue reforms to Michigan’s property tax system that help families, seniors, and longtime residents remain in the communities they helped build.
That includes reviewing the General Law Village Act and overlapping township-village taxation, expanding eligibility for Michigan’s Homestead Property Tax Credit, and exploring additional protections for seniors on fixed incomes.
I will work to ensure local governments are compensated fairly for the services they provide while identifying opportunities to reduce unnecessary duplication and relieve pressure on homeowners.
We can modernize school funding, protect essential local services, and make property taxes more equitable by changing how we allocate resources, improving accountability, and prioritizing long-term investments.
The goal is simple: stronger schools, affordable childcare, fairer taxes, and communities where families can afford to put down roots and seniors can afford to stay.
Policy Proposal How We Accomplish It 1.
Modernize Proposal A (1994) Reform Michigan’s school funding framework by strengthening weighted student funding, accounting for inflation, and ensuring schools receive adequate resources based on actual student needs rather than local property wealth.
2.
Reform School Infrastructure Funding Amend the State School Aid Act to expand state support for school building repairs, safety improvements, classroom technology, and facility upgrades.
Reduce reliance on local property taxes and bond proposals to fund essential school infrastructure.
3.
Improve Teacher Recruitment and Retention Strengthen teacher and staff compensation and retirement benefits, expand teacher preparation and apprenticeship programs, reduce unnecessary administrative burdens, and invest in special education staffing and support services.
4.
Expand Affordable Childcare Expand eligibility for Michigan’s Child Development and Care assistance program, increase reimbursement rates for childcare providers, support new childcare facilities, and expand early childhood education in underserved rural and suburban communities.
5.
Reform Property Tax Fairness Review the General Law Village Act of 1895 and Michigan’s property tax framework to address overlapping township-village taxation.
Explore more equitable funding arrangements that align taxes with services provided and reduce unnecessary duplication.
6.
Protect Families and Seniors from Displacement Expand Michigan’s Homestead Property Tax Credit, examine additional property tax relief for qualifying seniors and working families, and support housing affordability programs that help longtime residents remain in their homes.
Support Local Control and Responsible Growth Michigan's communities deserve a meaningful voice in how their land is developed, how infrastructure is expanded, and how growth affects the people who already live there.
I will work to modernize the Michigan Zoning Enabling Act of 2006 and the Michigan Planning Enabling Act of 2008 to strengthen local decision-making authority, improve public notification requirements, and require greater transparency throughout the development approval process.
For major industrial developments, including data centers, I will pursue legislation requiring independent third-party environmental and infrastructure impact assessments before approval, with developers responsible for the costs of those reviews.
Communities should have enforceable standards for noise, water consumption, electrical demand, setbacks, traffic, and environmental protection.
I will also support stronger financial guarantees, infrastructure agreements, and decommissioning requirements to ensure corporations cannot leave taxpayers responsible for the long-term costs of private development.
Economic development should never come at the expense of residents' health, property, infrastructure, or quality of life.
Responsible growth also requires better long-term planning and genuine public participation.
I will advocate for reforms that require major development proposals, supporting studies, zoning amendments, and financial agreements to be publicly accessible well before decisions are made.
I will work to strengthen coordination between township and village governments, county road commissions, utility providers, and state agencies so development decisions account for existing infrastructure and future community needs.
My office will actively support local leaders in updating master plans, strengthening zoning ordinances, protecting farmland and open spaces, and identifying appropriate locations for housing, small businesses, and industry.
Property owners deserve fair treatment and the right to make decisions about their land, but those rights must be balanced with the legitimate interests of neighboring residents and the broader community.
Growth should be planned with communities, not imposed upon them.
My goal is to protect local control, preserve the character of our communities, and ensure development delivers lasting benefits to the people who call them home.
Policy proposal How we accomplish it 1.
Modernize the Michigan Zoning Enabling Act (PA 110 of 2006) Strengthen requirements for public notification, development impact studies, and transparency during rezoning and special land-use decisions.
2.
Strengthen local authority over large developments Clarify township and village powers to establish enforceable standards for industrial developments, including data centers, while respecting state law and property rights.
3.
Require independent impact assessments Establish requirements for independent studies of major developments’ effects on roads, water, power infrastructure, noise, and public services, with developers covering review costs under clear statutory authority.
4.
Modernize the Michigan Planning Enabling Act (PA 33 of 2008) Strengthen long-term planning requirements and coordination among townships, villages, counties, road agencies, and utility providers.
5.
Protect taxpayers from development costs Establish clearer rules for developer-funded infrastructure improvements, financial guarantees, and decommissioning plans so communities are not left with unnecessary expenses.
6.
Improve public participation Require earlier public notice, accessible digital planning records, and meaningful opportunities for residents to review development proposals before final decisions.
AI, Data Centers and Technology AI, Data Centers & Technology Guardrails Artificial intelligence and emerging technologies are advancing faster than many of the laws designed to protect the public.
Michigan needs clear, enforceable standards that encourage responsible innovation without sacrificing our communities, natural resources, workers, or personal privacy.
I will work to strengthen Michigan's zoning and environmental laws by establishing statewide minimum standards for large-scale data centers, including mandatory independent third-party impact assessments, enforceable noise and setback limits, water-use reporting, and infrastructure capacity reviews, while preserving local governments' authority to establish stronger protections.
I will pursue reforms to Public Acts 181 and 207 of 2024, which expanded data center tax exemptions, to require stronger public accountability, independently verified economic benefits, and consequences when corporations fail to meet their commitments.
I will also support legislation strengthening Michigan Public Service Commission oversight to ensure data centers pay the full costs of the electricity, substations, transmission upgrades, and other infrastructure required for their operations.
Michigan families and small businesses should never be forced to subsidize the operating costs or profits of multibillion-dollar technology corporations.
Responsible technology policy must also protect the people whose jobs, personal information, and everyday lives are increasingly affected by artificial intelligence.
I will advocate for comprehensive consumer data privacy legislation giving Michigan residents greater control over how corporations collect, sell, store, and use their personal information, including protections against invasive surveillance technologies, biometric tracking, and discriminatory algorithmic pricing.
I will pursue enforceable standards for AI used in employment, housing, insurance, healthcare, education, and government services, including transparency, independent oversight, and the right to challenge consequential automated decisions.
Michigan must also protect workers from unfair algorithmic management, invest in workforce training and apprenticeships, and ensure technological advancements create opportunities for Michigan workers rather than simply replacing them.
My office will support innovation that strengthens our economy while holding corporations accountable for its consequences.
Technology should serve people, not the other way around.
Michigan's future must be built around protecting our communities, our workers, our privacy, and our resources.
Policy proposal How we accomplish it 1.
Establish statewide data center standards Amend Michigan’s zoning and environmental laws to establish minimum requirements for setbacks, noise, water consumption, energy demand, and independent environmental reviews while preserving local authority to adopt stronger protections.
2.
Protect utility ratepayers Require large data centers to cover the infrastructure and energy costs attributable to their operations, including substations and grid upgrades, rather than shifting those expenses onto residential and small-business customers.
3.
Reform corporate tax incentives Reevaluate Michigan’s data center sales and use tax exemptions and condition future incentives on independently verified jobs, infrastructure investment, environmental compliance, and measurable public benefits.
4.
Establish comprehensive digital privacy protections Give Michigan residents enforceable rights to access, correct, delete, and limit the sale or use of personal information, with additional protections against biometric surveillance and discriminatory data practices.
5.
Regulate high-risk artificial intelligence Require transparency, human oversight, and safeguards when AI is used in employment, housing, insurance, healthcare, education, and government decisions.
6.
Protect Michigan workers Establish worker protections against discriminatory algorithmic management, require appropriate notice and oversight of workplace AI, and invest in retraining and apprenticeships for jobs affected by automation.
7.
Strengthen cybersecurity and government accountability Establish safeguards for AI and personal data used by state agencies, including security reviews, public reporting, and clear processes for challenging automated government decisions.
