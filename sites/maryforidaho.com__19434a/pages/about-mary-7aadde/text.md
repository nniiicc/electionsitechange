That belief was shaped by my childhood.
I grew up in a low-income, racially diverse neighborhood on the South Side of Chicago during a time of significant racial tension and social change.
Later, my family moved to Richmond, Virginia, as the community was grappling with the profound changes brought by school desegregation.
Those experiences taught me that while our backgrounds may differ, every person deserves equal dignity, equal opportunity, and equal protection under the law.
They sparked a lifelong commitment to civil rights, fairness, and building communities where everyone has the chance to succeed.
I also learned important lessons about resilience and independence early in life.
When my parents divorced during my teenage years, I watched my mother struggle financially while working to support her family.
Seeing those challenges firsthand motivated me to pursue my education with determination and to build the skills necessary to support myself no matter what obstacles life might bring.
That experience deepened my appreciation for the value of education, economic opportunity, and the security that comes from being able to stand on your own feet.
Growing up near the Chesapeake Bay, I also learned that communities have a responsibility to protect the places they love and to leave them better than they found them.
As a young adult, I knocked on doors and organized with Clean Water Action because I believed ordinary people could make a difference when they came together around a common purpose.
Long before I became a lawyer, I learned that lasting change starts with listening to people, building relationships, and having the courage to act.

Mary Shea After graduating from the College of William & Mary and earning my law degree from the University of Richmond School of Law, I began my legal career in the Virginia Attorney General's Office, where I worked on complex civil rights and public policy litigation.
There, I saw how government decisions affect real people and how important it is that our institutions remain accountable, fair, and responsive to the public they serve.
When I moved to Idaho, I found a community where I could put those values into practice.
For more than twenty-five years, I have represented families, children, grandparents, foster parents, guardians, and individuals facing some of the most difficult challenges of their lives.
As a Certified Child Welfare Law Specialist, I have spent much of my career advocating for the safety, well-being, and dignity of children and families.
But my work has never been limited to individual cases.
Throughout my career, I have looked for opportunities to improve the systems that shape people's lives.
I have taught and mentored future legal professionals.
I have developed educational programs and curriculum.
I have written about complex legal issues and helped improve legal procedures.
I have worked with judges, attorneys, educators, health care leaders, and community members to find practical solutions to difficult problems.
Whether in a courtroom, a classroom, or a boardroom, I have always believed that leadership means bringing people together to make things work better.
Education has been one of the great passions of my life.
During my years at Idaho State University, I had the privilege of teaching, advising, and mentoring students as an instructor, academic advisor, and program coordinator.
Watching students discover their potential, overcome obstacles, and build successful careers reinforced my belief that education remains one of the most powerful tools we have for creating opportunity and strengthening communities.
The role that has shaped me most, however, has been motherhood.
Being a mother teaches you that public policy is never abstract.
Every decision made by government eventually touches a family.
It affects the quality of our schools, access to health care, the cost of living, the safety of our communities, and the opportunities available to our children.
My experiences as a mother have deepened my commitment to strong public schools, accessible health care, and policies that help families thrive rather than merely survive.
Leadership has been a central part of my life's work.
I have led nonprofit organizations, legal organizations, educational programs, and community initiatives.
From serving as President of Idaho Legal Aid Services and the Portneuf Health Care Foundation to leadership roles throughout the Idaho legal community, I have learned how to build consensus, solve problems, and bring people together around shared goals.
President Idaho Legal Aid Services Board President Portneuf Health Care Foundation Specialist Certified Child Welfare Law Educator Idaho State University I have always believed that public service is about more than holding a position.
It is about improving lives.
That belief has guided me as a civil rights attorney, child welfare advocate, educator, author, nonprofit leader, and community volunteer.
It has guided me in my work protecting vulnerable children, expanding access to justice, advocating for reproductive freedom, strengthening educational opportunities, and supporting the health and well-being of Idaho families.
Today, it is why I am running for the Idaho House.
I love Idaho.
I believe in the people of this state.
I believe we can strengthen public education, support working families, expand access to health care, protect individual freedoms, and create opportunities for future generations.
Most importantly, I believe we can do these things by working together.
My life has been dedicated to helping people navigate difficult systems and making those systems work better.
I am ready to bring that same experience, compassion, and determination to the Idaho Legislature.
