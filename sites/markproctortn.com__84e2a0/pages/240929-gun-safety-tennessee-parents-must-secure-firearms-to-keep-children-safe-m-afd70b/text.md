Luckily, there are preventive solutions we can implement that have been proven to save lives.
It’s part of what the THP academy ingrained in us as state troopers.
The first thing I did when I walked in my front door after work – even before I hugged my kids and asked them about their day – was to secure my firearm in my gun safe, making sure to store my ammunition separately.
We know these simple secure storage behaviors are effective in reducing firearm injury and death.
Here's what the data shows: Studies have repeatedly found that the presence of an unlocked firearm in the home is linked to a higher risk of suicide and unintentional firearm injury among children and adolescents.
A 2022 national survey of firearm owners found that 65% reported at least one unlocked firearm.
According to a 2023 Centers for Disease Control (CDC) analysis, 86% of unintentional firearm deaths among children and adolescents (ages 0‑17) happened at a house or apartment, including 56% in their own home.
Nearly half (45%) of the firearms used in these incidents belonged to a parent of the shooter, and the vast majority were loaded (74%) and unlocked (76%), and were most commonly accessed in bedroom settings in nightstands, under a mattress or pillow, or on a bed.
Sadly, children ages 0–5 years accounted for nearly a third (29%) of unintentional firearm injury deaths, followed by those ages 6–10 years (14%).
In addition, unsecured firearms left in vehicles have led to a massive increase in gun thefts from cars.
These stolen guns are falling directly into the hands of criminals for use in violent crimes.
I strongly recommend removing your firearm from your vehicle at night.
