Delivering Results for the 46th District
Policies Jason Ortitay authored that became Pennsylvania law
Jason does not measure success by how many bills carry his name.
He measures it by how many of his ideas actually become law and improve life for families in Washington and Allegheny counties.
Sometimes that means a standalone bill signed by the governor.
More often it means doing the hard work of getting his language adopted into the larger bills that move at the end of a session.
Either way, the result is the same: real policy, enacted, making a difference.
Here is what Jason has delivered.
Protecting Children and Families
Gabby’s Law (Act 65 of 2020)
Jason authored the law guaranteeing that a parent or guardian can get mental health treatment for their child under 18, even when the minor refuses care.
Before this law, some providers were turning parents away.
Named in memory of Gabrielle Bruno, this is one of Jason’s proudest accomplishments and a law that continues to save lives across the Commonwealth.
Naming the Terry D.
Tackett Jr.
Memorial Bridge (Act 63 of 2024)
Jason authored the legislation naming the Southern Beltway bridge over State Route 980 in Cecil Township in honor of Terry “Bubby” Tackett Jr., a Presto volunteer firefighter and union laborer who gave his life serving others.
It is a permanent tribute to a local hero in the community where he grew up.
Modernizing “Child Care” in Pennsylvania Law (enacted in Act 92 of 2015)
In his first term, Jason’s proposal updated state law to replace the outdated term “day care” with “childcare” throughout, reflecting how families and providers actually talk about caring for children.
The change was adopted into the broader childcare licensing reform signed into law in December 2015.
Teaching Every Child to Read
Early literacy has been Jason’s signature cause for years.
Statewide, only about a third of students read proficiently by the end of third grade.
Jason has worked across the aisle to change that.
$10 Million in New Literacy Funding (enacted as part of HB 1505)
Jason secured $10 million in new funding for evidence based literacy efforts, building directly on the
structured reading reforms he authored in Act 135 of 2024.
Sometimes described as a lighter version of HB 2541, this funding continues six years of work to bring proven reading instruction into every
Pennsylvania classroom.
Structured Literacy Reform (Act 135 of 2024)
Jason championed the most significant reading reform in Pennsylvania in years.
It requires evidence-based reading instruction, creates a Reading Leadership Council to maintain approved curriculum and screening tools, provides free teacher training, and ends the failed “three cueing” method.
This was the culmination of nearly five years of Jason’s work.
Structured Literacy Training Program (enacted in Act 55 of 2022)
Jason’s proposal creating a statewide professional development and in classroom coaching program in structured literacy for school personnel, laying the groundwork for the larger 2024 reform.
Stronger, Safer Schools
Eating Disorder Awareness and Prevention (HB 190, enacted as part of HB 1505)
Jason authored the language directing the Departments of Health and Education to develop eating
disorder awareness and prevention materials for schools, parents, and staff, and creating a statewide
advisory committee of counselors, nurses, and physicians to guide the effort.
School Safety and Student Mental Health Grants (enacted in Act 55 of 2022)
Jason secured a historic 100 million dollar investment in school safety, with the flexibility for districts to direct funding toward student mental health needs.
Threat Assessment Teams (enacted in Act 18 of 2019)
Jason’s proposal requiring school districts to create multidisciplinary teams that identify students in distress before a crisis, building on the Safe2Say Something tip line.
Fairer School Safety Grant Funding (enacted in Act 18 of 2019)
Jason’s proposal changing how school safety block grants are distributed so that schools receive a base grant scaled to their size, making the process fairer for every district.
Expanding Educational Opportunity
Testing Alternatives Study (HB 1752, enacted as part of HB 1505)
Jason authored a provision directing the Legislative Budget and Finance Committee to study whether
exams like the SAT, ACT, or Advanced Placement tests could serve as an alternative to the Keystone
Exams, with a report due to the Legislature by June 2027.
Statewide Online Course Clearinghouse (enacted in Act 55 of 2022)
Jason’s proposal directing the state to build a catalog of online K-12 courses available to public schools, nonpublic schools, home education families, and the public.
A Bridge Year for Special Education Students (enacted in Act 55 of 2022)
Jason’s proposal giving an additional year of instruction to special education students who lost critical learning time during COVID-19 before aging out of the system.
Dual Enrollment and Teacher Recruitment (enacted in Act 55 of 2022)
Jason’s proposal strengthening dual enrollment so more students, including charter school students for the first time, can earn college credit in high school, while expanding efforts to recruit and retain teachers.
Disability Inclusion Curriculum (enacted in Act 55 of 2022, extended as part of HB 1505)
Jason’s proposal creating a pilot program teaching students about the contributions of individuals with disabilities.
First piloted at South Fayette Township School District, the program was extended for two additional school years in this year's education code.
Consumer and Gaming Protections
One Helpline for Problem Gambling: 1-800-GAMBLER (enacted in Act 42 of 2017)
Jason introduced the legislation that became Pennsylvania’s major 2017 gaming law, and within it he consolidated the state’s scattered problem gambling resources into a single, easy to remember helpline, 1-800-GAMBLER.
When someone in crisis reaches for help, they now have one number to call.
It remains the statewide lifeline for problem gambling today.
Protecting Pennsylvania's Natural Resources
Studying Wildlife Corridors (House Resolution 87 of 2024)
Jason worked with Rep.
Mary Jo Daley on a bipartisan resolution directing a statewide study of wildlife corridors to identify how to better protect habitat connectivity across Pennsylvania.
The Shapiro Administration is now applying the study’s recommendations.
Leadership Beyond Legislation
Leading the Fight to Stop the Wolf Bridge Tolling Plan
When PennDOT moved to toll interstate bridges across Pennsylvania, including South Fayette Township, Jason led the charge against it.
He rallied his community, supported the legal challenge that municipalities won in Commonwealth Court, and fought the plan at every turn until the tolling scheme was defeated.
The reform that followed became Act 84 of 2022.
A note on the numbers
Counting Jason’s individual enacted policies above, he has delivered sixteen distinct provisions into Pennsylvania law, alongside his leadership on issues like stopping the bridge tolling plan and directing the statewide wildlife corridor study now being implemented by the administration.
Several of these became law as part of larger education and safety packages, which is how the most consequential
legislation often crosses the finish line.
As Jason has said publicly, he has no pride of authorship.
What matters is that the policies became law and the people of the 46th District are better for it.
