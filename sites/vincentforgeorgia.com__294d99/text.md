Why I’m Running
Building a Better Georgia
I’ve spent my career here in this community as a chiropractor, working with families, listening to their concerns, and helping people get back on their feet.
That work has taught me something important: listening is the beginning of solving problems.
Before you can help someone, you have to understand what they’re facing.
You have to ask questions, pay attention, and care enough to follow through.
Being a parent, a healthcare provider, and a member of this community has shaped the way I think about leadership.
To me, real leadership begins with service and genuine concern for the people around you.
This campaign didn’t begin with politics.
It began with a belief that our community deserves a representative who listens, shows up, and puts local people first.
For nearly two decades, our district has had the same representation.
After that much time, it is fair to ask a simple question: is our community better off?
When I talk with people across this district, I hear real concerns.
People want to know the water they give their children is safe.
Families are worried about rising power bills.
Communities want to know that major decisions about development, infrastructure, utilities, and resources are being made with them in mind, not around them.
Too often, people feel like decisions are happening without their voice.
That is why I’m running.
The values that guide this campaign are the values that guide my life every day.
My faith teaches me that we are responsible for one another.
My work has taught me the importance of listening.
And living here has shown me how deeply people care about their neighbors and this community.
To me, public service means treating people with respect, protecting your neighbors, and leaving your community better than you found it.
But those values only matter if they show up in real decisions.
I believe growth and development should strengthen our community, not place hidden costs on the families who live here.
I believe clean water, affordable utilities, and responsible decision-making are not partisan issues.
They are community issues.
At the end of the day, this campaign is about something simple: serving the people, protecting our community, and making sure our voice is heard in Atlanta.
Our district deserves leadership that is honest, accountable, and focused on the people who live here.
