Life in America has gotten too expensive, and too hard, for too many people.
I don’t think it’s the government’s job to make life easy — that was never the promise.
But it is the government’s job to make it a little easier, and to make it fair.
Right now it does neither.
Here’s what isn’t fair.
The biggest companies don’t pay their people enough to keep up with the cost of living — and they don’t pay enough in taxes either.
Then the very workers they underpay turn around and hand far too much of their paycheck to the government.
So the people doing the work carry the load twice : once in wages that don’t stretch, and again in a tax bill that never lets up.
That’s backwards.
We’ve got it upside down.
We tax the wages a family works for and let the profit at the very top skate.
It should be the other way around — we should be taxing what’s created and taken home at the top, not the paycheck someone’s on their feet all day to earn.
Fix that, and we can do something simple and honest: leave more money in the pockets of the people who earned it, and let a working family finally breathe.
Here’s what I’ll do — and why End the federal income tax on the first $150,000 you earn.
For most working and middle-class families in our district, that ends the federal income-tax burden almost entirely.
Not a loophole, not a carve-out you need an accountant to find — a simple, honest cut for the people who do the work.
You’d still pay into Social Security and Medicare, because you earned those and you’re going to collect them.
What changes is that Washington stops reaching into a working paycheck first and asking questions later.
Put up to $10,000 back in the pockets of the families who need it most.
If these companies won’t raise wages or bring prices down, then the least we can do is stop taxing families into the ground and put real money back in their hands.
For the working Americans who need it most, I’ll fight for up to $10,000 in refundable relief — money back to cover what the month actually costs, and the spending power to get a little ahead instead of living one emergency from the edge.
Pay for every dollar of it — with no new debt.
This is the part both parties dodge.
They cut taxes and run up the bill, or they quietly raise your costs somewhere else to cover it.
I won’t do either.
Every dollar of this tax cut is paid for by making PG&E, Big Pharma, and Big Tech finally pay their fair share — the same ones getting away with it right now.
Companies like PG&E, Big Pharma, and some of the largest tech companies on earth book billions in profit and pay little or nothing in federal tax, because they can afford the armies of lobbyists and accountants to work every loophole in the code.
They’re tax cheats, plain and simple.
Close the loopholes, make them pay the same fair rate you already do, go after the cheating, and cut the fraud and waste that bleeds billions of your dollars every year — and the money is there.
Yes, it’s enough.
Your tax cut comes out of their free ride, not out of your services and not out of your children’s future.
Part of fixing that code means ending the rules that quietly reward companies for moving jobs overseas.
Right now our tax rules quietly reward a corporation for moving its profits — and its jobs — overseas, then selling right back to us from abroad.
We are taxing the company that builds here and rewarding the one that packs up.
That is backwards, and it’s a big reason good jobs left towns like ours and never came back.
I’d shut down offshore tax dodging and pair it with a pro-growth corporate plan that backs the companies investing in American workers: lower and simpler if you build it here — and a higher rate if you ship those jobs overseas.
Reward building in America, penalize leaving it, and put what we collect straight into your tax cut.
And this isn’t a one-time gimmick that vanishes next year.
Keep an honest corporate tax code and actually collect what the biggest corporations owe, and this tax cut is something we can sustain — year after year — because it’s funded by a fair system instead of a credit card.
Making the biggest corporations pay their fair share is pro-growth.
This is exactly where the powerful try to scare you.
I’ve built companies.
I want them to win.
There’s nothing wrong with a company earning a profit and pouring it back into workers, wages, and building something real — that’s the growth we want, and a fair tax code should reward it.
What we should stop rewarding is the opposite: giant companies funneling profits into stock buybacks and executive bonuses while paying nothing back into the country that made them possible.
Pro-growth means pro-building, pro-hiring, pro-worker.
It does not mean letting the biggest players skip the check while your paycheck gets taxed on the way in the door.
What this means for Our District More than 4 in 10 families here rent, and most earn well under $150,000 — so this isn’t a cut aimed at people who already have plenty.
It’s aimed at the nurse, the teacher, the electrician, the small-business owner, the family running the numbers at the kitchen table trying to make it all fit.
And it comes down to one question: who is the tax code built for?
Right now it’s built for the corporate donors who fund our politics — the same interests that write the checks get the loopholes, while your wages get taxed on the way in the door.
I don’t take their money, so I’m free to say the plain thing out loud: the people who work should not carry a heavier load than the industries that don’t.
The bottom line For as long as most of us can remember, life has only gotten more expensive — and the same establishment politicians stayed in power while it happened.
I’m running because we need representatives who actually feel what high costs do to a family, who work for you instead of the corporations funding their campaigns, and who are willing to do something differently.
I’m a Democrat because I believe more freedom and more opportunity for more people is worth fighting for.
And I’ll say plainly what our party establishment has failed to: cutting taxes for working people does exactly that.
It’s something Republicans have often been right about, and a lot of Democrats won’t admit it because they refuse to give the other side credit for anything.
That kind of thinking is what keeps this country stuck.
I don’t believe there are Democratic ideas and Republican ideas.
There are just ideas that work and ideas that don’t.
A real tax cut for working families works.
I’ll fight for it no matter which side it came from.
You should be able to work hard and get ahead.
That’s the whole promise.
End the corruption that rigged the code, make the tax cheats pay their fair share, and send people to Washington who answer to you instead of their donors — and a working family in this district finally keeps more of what it earns, for good.
