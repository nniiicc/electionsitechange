State Representative Debra Bazemore was elected to serve the citizens of House District 63 in 2016, which is now District 69.
The district is comprised of residents of Fayette and Fulton counties.
She currently serves on the Economic Development and Tourism, Governmental Affairs, Human Relations & Aging, Natural Resources and Small Business Development committees.
She is the Vice Chair of the Fulton County Delegation, Creator and Chair of the City of South Fulton.
Her leadership roles have included Chief Deputy Whip for the House Minority Caucus, and Regional Chair for the National Black Caucus for State Legislators.
Representative Bazemore is a native of New York, where she served as the Founder and President of Concerned Parents, advocating for students at the state level.
Her commitment to community service was recognized with the prestigious Frederick Douglass Award.
She moved to unincorporated Fulton County, now the City of South Fulton, in 2005.
Serving as President of Old National Area Residents United (ONARU), where she played a vital role in combating blight in the community and labored alongside county leadership to have uninhabitable properties condemned and demolished.
As Chair of South Fulton United, she was a dedicated advocate and supporter of giving South Fulton residents the right to vote to incorporate.
She facilitated and participated in over 100 presentations to educate citizens on the benefits of cityhood.
Representative Bazemore began her work at the state level as the Chief of Staff to a State Senator, working under the Gold Dome and traveling to counties statewide to listen to citizens’ concerns, address statewide issues and develop invaluable partnerships.
Since being elected, she has sponsored and championed legislation to ensure the safety of Georgia citizens in their homes and communities, bring awareness to critical health issues among school-aged children and put an end to predatory practices targeting Georgia citizens on the path to homeownership.
Her achievements include being named a Henry Toll Fellow, earning an Honorary Humanitarian Doctorate, and receiving the Presidential Lifetime Achievement Award.
