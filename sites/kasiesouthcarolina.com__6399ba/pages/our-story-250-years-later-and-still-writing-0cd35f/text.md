It’s the story of rebellion against a tyrant king, suffering through a long military campaign, and ultimately finding at dawn’s early light that our faith and perseverance paid off.
It’s the story of a hot summer’s conference where educated men debated and designed the three branches of government with limited powers, ensuring liberty and freedom for generations to come.
It’s the story of decades of growth and expansion, innovation and ingenuity from the cotton gin to locomotives stretching from sea to shining sea.
It’s brutal, violent, and cruel.
But it’s also generous, grateful, and hopeful.
Ours is a story of resistance that became resilience.
Of prudence that became prosperity.
We have veered wildly, recklessly, like the last cart on a roller coaster’s long train.
Clinging to the handholds, eyes squeezed shut, exhilaration making our hearts pound.
Our future has been uncertain.
Ours is a story of faith.
In God and the promise of our inalienable rights.
In the founding fathers’ wisdom and the institutions they laid out for us.
Their courage is our courage, their faith our faith.
Their story is our story.
The most endearing thing about the social media surrounding the FIFA World Cup soccer tournament in the United States has been the way it has renewed our patriotism.
As if hearing from foreigners that the United States really is as great as we think it is provides validation.
Like we somehow forgot, in all our internet bickering, that we’re the home of the free and the brave.
Whether it’s celebrations of Buccee’s jerky bar, confirmations of the superiority of Texas brisket, or the Scots drinking Boston’s Sam Adams dry, we’ve been reminded that greatness isn’t always in conquest.
Greatness in South Carolina includes Garden City Pier karaoke, the 3.4 mile Watermelon Festival parade from Varnville to Hampton, 1 Million Cups weekly entrepreneurial meet-ups in Columbia, Myrtle Beach, Greenville, and Charleston, St Pat’s at Five Points and the Lexington Blowfish St Patrick’s Parade, Mardis Gras in Shandon and Poultry in Batesburg-Leesville.
Whether it’s the Sun Dial Festival in Barnwell or the Gaffney Peach Festival, South Carolina’s greatness is in communities coming together.
Thank you for welcoming us, Hampton County.
Photo courtesy Jeremy Ginn We celebrate each other, dance and sing and pray together.
We cheer for our teams, protect our children, care for our elderly, build our businesses, and cherish our freedom.
Together.
We’ve been saying for a while that there’s more that unites us than divides us and it’s true.
We have an incredible story to tell.
It’s a story of redemption and resilience.
Courage and compassion.
Luck and love.
The serendipity of July 4, 1776, and the 250 years since, wherein generations of Americans have felt the profound gratitude of life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.
A republic, if we can keep it.
Happy Independence Day and may you and yours be blessed for another 250 years to come.
