One might argue the roots of the revolution are in just those kinds of accusations.
But over the last two weeks while campaigning, I had some interesting conversations with people.
The key takeaways are these: Everyone knows the parties cheat.
Everyone is annoyed/disgusted/frustrated by the cheating on both sides .
The “play the game” crowd is losing ground to the “it doesn’t have to be this way” crowd.
The “It Doesn’t Have to be This Way” Crowd From left to right: Dr.

The System vs.
Cheating Democrats and Republicans have convinced people they are the government.
But they’re not.
They are political parties whose job it is to get people elected.
And they do.
And they make a lot of money doing it.
Then when their people get elected, they change the rules.
The drama of Texas gerrymandering is exhibit A and it’s not new or even particularly creative.
What is new is how open and unapologetic they’re being about wanting to fix the 2026 midterm election to get Republicans five more seats and secure a majority.
I guess if you’re going to have cheating, it’s best that we all call it what it is. ‘The System’ is a broad term used to describe the rules of the game, like the flimsy double-sided printed page that comes with UNO: No Mercy.
What ‘the system’ means here is election laws.
They’re different in every state and that’s a good thing.
Election laws should fit the locality inasmuch as they serve the locality.
South Carolina’s League of Women Voters reports little to no election fraud in South Carolina.
So the cheating must be happening within the rules of the game.
Political cheating is cultural.
We expect politicians to lie – they’ll say anything to get elected.
We expect them to profit – they all get rich on insider information.
And because we expect it, we’re neither shocked nor dismayed when these behaviors occur.
Of course they misbehave.
Of course they sell out.
My Papa told me not to get into politics until I was 50 years old.
By then, he said, I’d be ruined anyway and politics won’t be the thing that did it.
And isn’t it the truth that even the people we think of as good people are corrupted, twisted, and made to say and do things out of character in order to play the game?
The real cheating is in how the parties have organized the work.
The career pathing that goes into party politics is hierarchical.
The endless fundraising and the issue baiting are toxic.
There exists a narrative in political parties that opportunity is earned through loyalty.
Toe the party line, wait your turn, and you’ll be supported when it’s your time.
These rules are evidence of manipulation just like gerrymandering.
In a recent op-ed on FITSNews.com Barbara Arthur accused the SC GOP’s former chair of running the organization like a television crime boss.
On her While I Breathe blog, Katrina Shealy alerted readers to the politics under the sheets in the wake of revelations of more sexual misconduct by Republicans holding office.
In a recent episode, the Frogmore Stew podcast took on gerrymandering to talk about why elections feel rigged (hint: it’s not the voting machines).
Organizing efforts to achieve desired outcomes is how business gets done and the major parties are businesses.
They earn revenue, spend resources, and hire employees.
Their service is regulations and legislation.
They sell government.
And big donors get the rules they want in industry, in trade policy, and in elections.
Political operatives who claim to know the parties’ game have been offering me a lot of advice.
Wear more make-up, grow my hair out.
I was told I need to match my campaign photograph every time I’m seen in public or voters will get confused.
I was told I’ll need to hire a consultant.
I was told running for office isn’t easy and it isn’t fun.
I don’t expect it to be easy but I don’t do anything if it isn’t fun.
The real cheating is in how the parties have turned all of our stomachs sour on politics.
Without our engagement, they can do whatever they want.
Voter turnout is low and they want it that way.
Representative government should be seen as a privilege.
It should be spirited and passionate, but also amiable and congenial.
We’re all citizens of this state and this country.
We all love our state and our country.
What we expect from our government may differ, but we do have some common ground.
I’ve heard on the campaign trail repeatedly that our common ground is in how sick we are of the political theater: the pretending, the faking, the lying, the manipulating.
I’ve heard the two parties are machines.
I’ve heard the two parties care about power and fundraising and nothing else.
On a recent episode of The Afternoon Drive (audio below), I interviewed Lura Forcum of The Independent Center and shared with our audience that a full 43% of Americans view themselves as politically independent.
They may vote team blue or team red because those are the candidates they’re given, but they don’t identify as either Democrat or Republican.
The folks that used to say we should work within the system and play the established game, are now saying the game is what’s wrong with government.
It doesn’t have to be this way.
We need to elect people who plan to do the work of governing.
At all levels of government from City Council to the White House, we need people who will work the job, not perform the party politics.
How will we know those people when we see them?
They won’t cheat on their spouses, or their taxes; they can be trusted to do the right thing.
They will value the checks-and-balances of three branches, not rage against them as obstructionist when they don’t get their way.
They will not abandon their principles for the latest political bandwagon.
They will not exploit their influence for personal gain or petty grudges.
They will not ignore the constituents who didn’t vote for them or gerrymander them out of relevance.
They won’t be building a political career for themselves; they will step forward to serve when they’re ready, not when they’re told.
They will know they’re in office to serve constituents, not party bosses.
They will leave when their time is over.
Maybe they won’t yet be 50 years old, not yet ruined, to my Papa’s estimation.
Maybe they’ll be outsider enough to know the insiders like the game as is.
Maybe they’ll be optimistic enough to think we can win it playing it our way.
I’m proposing some new campaign rules, straight out of the “it doesn’t have to be this way” mindset: Tell the truth.
Always.
Have fun.
Everything else should be according to election laws which you can find here .
It’s not easy to run for statewide office, but it doesn’t have to be so hard.
Just smile, listen, and thank the people for caring enough to share their thoughts with you.
Then go make the right choices – the ones that promote liberty, that represent the will of the people, preserve self-governance, and protect natural rights.
Ready to get in the game?
We could use your help.
