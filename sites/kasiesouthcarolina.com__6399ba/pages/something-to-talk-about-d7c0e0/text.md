I talk for a living and I’m at home on the radio especially with radio entrepreneurs like Banana Jack and James.
A professional bio is the short paragraph listing your title, your accomplishments, and anything else that’s relevant for exactly why the organizers thought you’d be a good pick for presenter or panelist.
It’s awkward because listening to someone describe you in third person makes you feel like you ought not to be there.
They’re talking about you, not to you.
There’s a lot of talking about happening in this election.
They’re not talking about me.
That’s a topic I’ve been vocal about on X and LinkedIn.
The establishment has decided ignoring me is the best way to handle the contradictions between us.
They’re talking about each other.
Let them.
I’m not interested in bratty politics.
It’s theater.
It’s childish.
It doesn’t advance the conversations we need to have.
And, let’s be honest, it’s tacky.
I want to talk about the issues .
I want to talk about the national debt and the ballooning costs of Social Security and Medicare and how those programs MUST be reigned in, sooner rather than later.
I want to talk about how the two parties don’t want to govern, they just want to win.
I want to talk about how South Carolina has a unique and rare opportunity this election to do something radical, to tell the major parties we’ve had enough of the trash.
But I’m being told I have to talk about them .
It’s how they’re fundraising, by bashing each other.
It’s how they’re getting news coverage.
I’m being told if I do the same, I could earn the same coverage.
It’s not who I am to talk badly about others.
That’s just not me.
So, let’s talk about me.
I am a woman, like them .
I have been married to the same man for 25 years and we raised a kid who is currently in college.
Biological female, wife and mom, pronouns she/her woman.
I am a doctor, like one of them .
I have a PhD in Organization and Management.
I earned it in 2014 when I defended my dissertation on the critical factor US companies need to invest in foreign markets when there is a threat of terrorism.
If you’re wondering, that factor is trusting the locals to run the operation.
I work for the state, like one of them .
I work at the University of South Carolina as faculty with a rank of Clinical Assistant Professor which means I teach classes but don’t conduct research.
I’m also a director at the Faber Center for Entrepreneurship.
I own a business.
My consultancy, Clemson Road Creative, is a side hustle I use for summer work and income.
It’s focused on supplementing my work in my primary interests: entrepreneurship and writing.
I am a radio personality.
I co-anchor Mornings with Kasie and Kev weekday mornings from 7 a.m. until 9 a.m. except Thursdays.
I also co-host Write On SC on Saturdays at 9 a.m.
You can listen live from anywhere on makethepointradio.com.
I have two published novels and two published text books and two award-winning short stories.
I also have tattoos.
The butterflies on my right arm represent the six works I just mentioned.
I have been Chair of the South Carolina Libertarian Party and President of the South Carolina Writers Association.
Two organizations that do a lot of good without a lot of resources that trusted me to lead them.
I am a member of Washington Street United Methodist Church currently taking the Disciple Bible Study class.
I have, over many years, built a broad network of business owners, entrepreneurs, economists, and creators.
We all envision a world where government gets out of the way so we can make things better for everyone.
We all imagine we can change the world through business, through art, and with kindness and compassion.
There are a dozen contrasts from me to the other candidates.
The biggest difference, though, is how they have major party, and major donor commitments.
It’s an advantage for them because campaigning costs money.
But it means they will owe those parties and those donors if elected.
Not me.
I am fully independent.
I can do what’s best for South Carolina.
And we need a Senator who can bring resources to our state.
That Senator must have leverage and as junior senators in the war between Republicans and Democrats, they won’t have that leverage.
But I will.
I don’t expect either of them to talk about me, they might not even talk to me when we meet on stage in debates.
We’ll all stand there awkwardly and listen while the host reminds them that I’m highly educated, with real business and organizational experience.
An accomplished somebody.
Who knows stuff about foreign policy, economics, community, and what it takes to thrive.
If you’re wondering, it’s not more government.
Every blog post, every live stream, every interview, every forum, every chance I get to speak with voters, I’ll be talking about what actually matters.
What I have to say about them will have to wait until I can say it to them.
That’s just good manners.
