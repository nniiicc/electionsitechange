We’re working hard to overcome it.
It’s hard to write this post because I certainly don’t want to come off as whining or criticizing people for doing their jobs.
I know people have made some efforts for me.
After I tweeted to Judi Gatson and Greg Adaline, they included me in their election night coverage during the GOP runoff.
Then, weirdly, my part of the show was cut out of the YouTube post.
When questioned, their news director said the live feed went down right then, so YouTube didn’t have the segment.
My campaign manager watched the live feed on YouTube and saw my interview as it aired.
We’re getting the cold shoulder from traditional media.
I reached out to the Post & Courier’s Pee Dee reporter and spent a lovely 30 minutes with her during which she recorded our conversation and said she’d work up an article.
That was August 13th.
Nothing has ever been posted.
Then I had a phone interview with The State’s reporter.
Another lovely, recorded conversation, with the conclusion that an article would be written.
That was September 3rd.
Nothing has ever been posted.
The two young women reporters with whom I spoke are not to blame.
I feel certain it’s their editors who decided not to waste the space on a Libertarian.
Press coverage for Jermaine Johnson at the State House I have been campaigning since March 29, 2025.
Here’s a link to a banger of a speech I delivered on very short notice at the Statehouse in August of last year.
It’s not like we’re not trying.
It’s that they’re ignoring us.
We want to reach as many voters as possible.
We know we can do that with traditional media.
Despite “new” media heralding the end of local news, local television, and all things traditional, turns out they still have a wide reach.
How do I know?
My mother-in-law called me after the WIS mention.
“Kasie!
You were on TV!” Being on TV is still a big deal.
Even if they pronounce your name wrong. ::facepalm:: We have been sending weekly media alerts for months.
Telling all the news outlets where I will be all week long.
No one has shown up.
At the SCETV debate, we knew the Republican and Democrat candidates were going to ignore me.
They ignored me in their social media posts leading up to the event.
Just kept talking about how they were going to debate each other.
Then, on stage, neither ever acknowledged me or mentioned me by name.
They used the constant snipes at one another to tack # seconds on to every response so they kept getting to speak for # seconds and I kept getting just 60.
Their strategy worked, they got significantly more air time than me.
Our strategy worked, too.
I stayed out of the fray, answered the questions, told real stories and gave real solutions.
A few times I referenced what they’d said, but never by name and so when they asked for rebuttal to my remarks, they were denied.
Then we went to the media spin room and I was much more comfortable on equal footing with my peers and delivered a few great moments there.
In the “spin room” after the debate on SCETV Sunday, October 11th; none of the footage has ever been posted.
None of those ever made it to coverage either.
Except one article that took two of my quotes, fact checked them, and wrote a piece making me sound like a liar and Annie Andrews like an expert.
Awesome.
Thing is, when you know a lot about a topic, it’s hard to get the answer out in a minute.
And when I said, “No one will buy our debt,” that was true #ago.
But last Thursday, a bunch of 30-year bonds were gobbled up by foreign investors.
The point of the comment was to illustrate how bad our debt is.
The fact check made it seem like the buying of the bonds proved me wrong.
It doesn’t.
They bought the bonds because yields went up and so now our debt is even more expensive than it was before the bonds were sold.
When you know a lot, it’s hard to give short answers.
When you know nothing, you say basically nothing.
Okay, back to the media thing.
So I wasn’t prepared for the blackout to be so complete, but of the news outlets that covered the debate, many didn’t put me in the story.
Wait, what?
You didn’t tell the story about the “only grown up on the stage” who “gave actual answers to the questions” and “didn’t just sling mud” (those are quotes from Facebook comments).
Apparently the Libertarian looking like the only adult in the room wasn’t the story the traditional media wanted to push.
Check out the headlines and links below for evidence.
The exception was our new friends at FOX Carolina who not only told the whole story but dispatched a camera crew to my meet-and-greet in Greenville on Monday night.
So thank you, FOX Carolina.
In the most egregious post-debate story, this article talked about how the Democrat and Republican spent the entire debate talking about how unqualified the other one was.
And yet, there I was, on stage, imminently qualified, and actually answering the questions.
The article reduced my presence to a “oh, she was there, too.” Infuriating.
See my tweet.
My response to a ridiculous “news” article about the debate The laziest story about Sunday night’s debate was the two of them sniping at each other.
Yet it’s the story every outlet ran.
Come on, journalists, do better.
As I told the outlet linked above, 22 year-old sports writer me could have written a better game story than that.
So what should we do?
My campaign has been relying on independent media and sharing all the links and highlights all over our channels.
And we’re asking folks to re-post, re-tweet, and re-blog everything we put out to extend it into your networks, too.
Maybe the digital landscape will give us some ground we’re not getting from TV coverage.
A lot of the mainstream press is behind a paywall, so that could work to our advantage if we show up on blogs and other free news sources.
But I’m also on the road meeting people in real life.
Not an organized event that’s all about Kasie.
Community forums where I get to listen and learn about you .
Small group meetings where I get to learn what’s keeping you up at night.
What worries you about the future?
What’s hitting your bank account the hardest?
What do you think I should do about it?
Not me on a stage, arriving one minute before the thing starts, speaking scripted talking points into a microphone, accepting applause, and disappearing before the questions start flying.
But me walking the streets in these towns, knocking on business doors and introducing myself.
Visiting the big events they have – Red Rose Festival in Lancaster, Pageland Watermelon Festival, Uniquely Union, Newberry Oktoberfest, Westminster Bigfoot Festival – and enjoying the fun and joy people get from their community coming together.
We’re sharing our pride in South Carolina.
And that’s so powerful to me that I wish I could bottle it, shake it up, and spray it all over the polls and the political machinery that keeps telling ya’ll I can’t possibly win.
The Path to Victory is Simple: We do this Together.
This election is plurality voting.
That means most votes wins.
And you have one vote to spend.
I won’t tell you how to do it.
I think that’s your freedom, liberty, and autonomy.
I will just say that if I earn enough votes, I can win.
And if enough of us believe it, vote it, and trust our fellow South Carolinians to do the same, we’re going to make history.
Again.
And apparently that’s scaring some establishment folks who have ramped up their spending, ramped up their commercials, and started emailing me to drop out or risk electing the wrong bad candidate.
It’s even gotten the media to ignore me harder, if such a thing were possible.
We will stay the course.
I didn’t get into this to play political games.
I got into this to fix the national debt.
You would think that goal was met with the way the debt is in the news.
But it’s popular now because everyone is campaigning and it’s a popular campaign thing to say, “I’m concerned about the debt,” knowing full well that 1) your party did this (both parties are complicit) and 2) you will never, ever get anyone to talk about the debt after November 3rd.
No one except me.
I told ReZsaun Lewis last week that I will stomp all over the Capitol knocking on doors until Congress gets serious about the debt.
I’ve been saying that the skills to campaign are not the same skills to govern.
The second truism here is that spending is not viability.
Money raised, size of the team, ads, signs, all that stuff cannot replace hard work.
And I promise I won’t get outworked.
Not on the campaign trail and not in Washington.
I’ll be working when the cameras aren’t on.
When the social media cheerleaders aren’t cheering.
When things get hard, and they will, I’ll keep working.
I don’t need media witnesses.
I have all of you as my witness.
So thanks for showing up and thanks for being in this with me.
Ready to get in the game?
We could use your help.
