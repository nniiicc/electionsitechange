A civil war descendant with deep ancestral roots within the state of Maryland, Mr.
Blackwell has dedicated his career to bridging the gap between historical legacy and modern-day community empowerment.
As a moderate voice in the political landscape, he is a steadfast advocate for inclusive policies designed to benefit all Marylanders, regardless of their background or walk of life.
A Legacy of Resilience & Valor The foundation of Mr.
Blackwell’s identity is built upon a significant and poignant family history.
The Blackwell lineage traces back to a dark chapter in global history, beginning with ancestors who were forcibly transported from Christiansborg Castle (Osu Castle) in Accra, Ghana.
Sold to England’s Lord Joseph Blackwell and relocated to the Anchorage Plantation - known as Walnut Lodge - in Northumberland County, Virginia, the family began a multi-generational struggle for autonomy and recognition.
A central figure in this history is Mr.
Blackwell’s great-great-grandfather, Royston Austin "Nii Armah" Blackwell.
Born into slavery in 1833, Royston’s life was forever changed on May 15, 1864, during a daring Union Army raid led by Republican Brigadier General H.
Judson Kilpatrick.
This liberation not only granted Royston his freedom but also established a historic connection between the GaDangme American community and the Republican Party, highlighting the intersection of political affiliation and the fight for human rights.
Following his liberation, Royston strategically relocated to Baltimore, Maryland, adopting the name Royston "Austin" to protect himself from recapture.
He served with distinction in the Revenue Cutter Service during the Civil War, positioning himself as both a participant and a witness to the nation’s transformation.
This legacy of service was shared by his first cousin, Peter Adam’s Blackwell, a freedman who served in the United States Colored Troops.
These ancestors laid the groundwork for the Blackwell family’s enduring spirit and commitment to the American story.
Entrepreneurial Leadership & Community Impact Today, Yahu Blackwell honors this rich heritage through a multifaceted professional career.
As a professional boxing champion, he embodies the discipline and strength of his forebears, competing at an elite level.
Beyond the ring, he is a prominent figure in Baltimore’s business community.
Mr.
Blackwell is the owner of several highly successful franchises, including Rita’s Italian Ice and Auntie Anne’s Pretzels, where he utilizes his platforms to provide economic opportunities and uplift the local workforce.
His innovative spirit is further evidenced by the launch of “HEWALE”, his signature energy drink brand.
The name, which signifies strength and health, encapsulates his mission to inspire innovation and local pride.
Through his business ventures, Mr.
Blackwell demonstrates that entrepreneurship is not merely about commerce, but about creating a sustainable and thriving environment for the community he serves.
Advocacy & Personal Dedication Mr.
Blackwell’s commitment to service is deeply personal, shaped by both his triumphs and his challenges.
The tragic loss of his mother in 2015 due to medical malpractice served as a pivotal moment in his life, solidifying his resolve to honor her memory through tireless advocacy and personal integrity.
This dedication to family and justice fuels his work as a community activist, where he focuses on systemic improvements and the well-being of his fellow citizens.
As civil war descendant and a modern community leader, Yahu Blackwell continues to navigate a path that honors the unbreakable spirit of those who came before him.
Whether through his moderate political advocacy, his various business enterprises, or his work in the community, he remains focused on a singular goal: ensuring that the legacy of resilience, strength, and service remains a guiding light for future generations in Baltimore County and beyond.
