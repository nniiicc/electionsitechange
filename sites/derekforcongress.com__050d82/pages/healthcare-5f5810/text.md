We’ve been talking about it for decades, yet we’re still stuck with a system where middle-class families are squeezed while insurance companies and Big Pharma maximize profits for their shareholders. ​The GOP says we need free-market competition, but we’ve seen that often leads to collusion and "middlemen" pocketing the difference, not savings for the American people.
The Democrats calls for a Universal Single-Payer system, but they haven’t explained how to pay for it beyond "tax the rich".
There are around 110 million Americans who when polled say they don't want a federalized mandate for healthcare. ​The truth is, our country is far too diverse for a "one-size-fits-all" solution.
We often talk about diversity in terms of race and ethnicity, and while we are the most diverse nation on Earth in those ways, it goes much deeper.
We are a "Diversity Stack", we are the most politically, economically, geographically, and religiously diverse country on the planet as well.
That diversity is a great strength, but we have to be honest: it creates massive complications for creating and agreeing on national policies.
A system that works in a small, uniform, homogeneous country simply cannot be copy-pasted onto a nation as beautifully complex as ours. ​That’s why I’m proposing a new path forward: The Multi-State Healthcare Coalition (MSHCC). ​Instead of forcing a controversial system on the entire nation, I want to pave the legal pathway for states to band together and prove that a single-payer system can work.
If the "Blue States" truly believe in Universal Healthcare, I say: Lead the way. ​If the states that currently favor this system joined a coalition, they would have a buying pool of roughly 153 million lives.
That is a pool larger than most developed nations.
It’s a group with enough leverage to negotiate lower pharmaceutical prices and remove the layers of "middlemen" who siphon off billions in profits. ​By moving this to the state level and empowering local decisions, we respect our regional differences and avoid the federal sabotage from the GOP that has killed previous reforms.
We let the states that believe in this vision be the "guinea pigs." The GOP always likes to scream about "states' rights" so let's have them help us provide the states with a clear path to use those rights in relation to healthcare.
If these states can maintain high-quality care, keep wait times low, and lower costs without going broke, the rest of the country will see the proof and be more willing follow along. ​It’s time to stop using healthcare as a campaign talking point and start treating it as a challenge for leadership.
Let’s pave the legal way for states to innovate, compete and show us what’s actually possible with a single payer system in America.
