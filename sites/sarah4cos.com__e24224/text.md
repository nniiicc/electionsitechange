SARAH EMERY FOR COLORADO HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES - DISTRICT 14
My Political Why
I think of politics like a bell curve.
The loudest voices live on the far left and far right, but most people are somewhere in the middle trying to raise kids, pay bills, and live decent lives.
Unfortunately, the media and too many politicians spend their time pandering to those outliers, leaving a lot of us feeling politically homeless, even though we’re actually the majority, especially in House District 14.
This is why I was an unaffiliated voter until December 2025.
I didn’t feel like anyone was listening.
I didn’t feel represented.
I was tired of the constant bickering and disrespectful discourse, and it felt like too many elected leaders had forgotten who they were supposed to be working for.
For a while, that left me feeling hopeless.
But that’s not who I am.
When I see a problem, I don’t stand by and complain.
I try to fix it.
I realized that if I wasn’t willing to step up and take action myself, I couldn’t expect anyone else to do it for me.
I determined the best way to get on the ballot was to join a party, use their platform, and start shining light on the issues I’m passionate about.
Despite the historic voting odds being against me, I’m showing up anyway.
All people of House District 14 deserve a real voice, no matter their party affiliation.
I plan on speaking honestly and openly about the issues, listening even when it’s hard, and pushing for practical things like consistent school funding (so my kids’ school doesn’t need to put out buckets when it rains) and real solutions to the cost of living so our families can afford to stay in this community.
I’m not about left or right.
I'm about neighbors and community.
When we slow down and genuinely listen to each other, it’s amazing how much common ground we can find.
Most people want the same basic things: roads that don’t have their car needing an alignment, a safe, affordable place to live, and a better future for their kids and grandkids.
And the best solutions don’t come from division or extremes, they come from everyday people working together.
