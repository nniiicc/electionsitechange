Biography
The son of a farmer and a public school teacher, Tom grew up in a modest, working-class family on a farm in Richmond County.
His parents instilled in him the value of education, personal responsibility, and perseverance.
Tom McInnis never had the luxury of a large inheritance or handouts; instead, he worked his way through school and built small businesses that have created jobs in our community for years.
Now serving his sixth term in the State Senate, Tom has worked consistently to reduce unnecessary government intrusion and eliminate overbearing regulations that stifle the entrepreneurial spirit of job creators—all while safeguarding the health, safety, and welfare of all North Carolinians.
He has championed efforts to expand vocational and technical education, helping students gain the skills needed for sustainable, well-paying careers.
He has also worked to raise teacher pay, expand pathways to teacher certification, and support school choice programs that provide families with broader educational opportunities.
Perhaps most importantly, Tom has fought tirelessly against the illegal drug epidemic.
Having witnessed firsthand the devastation that opioid addiction brings to families and communities, he remains deeply committed to combating addiction in all its forms.
Senator McInnis currently serves as Senior Chair of the Senate Finance Committee, Chair of the Regulatory Reform Committee, and Co-Chair of the Revenue Laws Study Committee—three committees that play critical roles for Moore and Cumberland Counties.
He also serves on several other key Senate committees.
He is seeking reelection to the State Senate because he believes the constituents of the 21st District deserve a proven leader who is bold, decisive, and committed to working tirelessly to meet constituent needs, strengthen educational opportunities, and expand job growth across Moore and Cumberland Counties.
Senator McInnis’ open-door policy reflects his dedication to serving the people he represents.
Tom’s wife, Janice, is a retired Richmond County Schools teacher.
They have 4 children and 11 grandchildren, and make their home in Pinehurst, NC.
