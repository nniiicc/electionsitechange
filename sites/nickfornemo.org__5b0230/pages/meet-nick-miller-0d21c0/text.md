Home Meet Nick Platform Contact DONATE Home Meet Nick Platform Contact DONATE Who Nick Is Nick Miller is a working-class Missourian, Army veteran, and candidate for State Senate in Missouri’s 18th District.
He is not a career politician, and he is not interested in becoming one.
After leaving the Army, Nick built a life rooted in rural Missouri, where he could work hard, speak plainly, and live the kind of liberty he believes too many politicians only talk about.
He knows what it means to punch a clock, come home dirty from work, and still care enough to show up and fight for his community.
He has worked blue-collar jobs by choice, and he has walked away from comfortable management roles when those positions required him to defend corporate decisions that hurt working people.
What He’s Done Over the last 15 years, Nick has worked across supply chains in both the private and public sectors, from shipping and receiving to management, compliance, and multi-site logistics.
His experience includes regulated industries and operational environments where mistakes carry real consequences.
From ammunition and healthcare to cannabis, he has seen firsthand how policy affects real people, and how often the gap between government decisions and everyday life falls hardest on working families.
Why He’s Running Nick is running because he believes Missouri’s government has stopped serving ordinary people.
While working families struggle with hospital closures, rising costs, weak labor protections, and a political system dominated by money, the people in power keep distracting voters with culture-war nonsense and serving donors instead of communities.
He believes liberty means more than slogans.
It means freedom from oppression, freedom from corruption, and freedom from a government that answers to corporations and billionaires before it answers to its own people.
Nick’s campaign is built on a simple idea: government should belong to the people again.
He supports getting money out of politics, fully funding public education, expanding healthcare access, defending public goods, and rebuilding a culture where neighbors speak to neighbors and communities organize for themselves instead of waiting to be rescued by political professionals.
