North Fulton NAACP Sets October 15 Debate in Georgia’s 7th
Congressional District Race
Kozycki will complete a six-county public debate tour across GA-07 as McCormick remains absent from
the debate stage, raising questions about whether voters will have any opportunity to question their
congressman publicly before Election Day.
Alpharetta, GA - The North Fulton County Branch of the NAACP (Branch 53AA), a nonpartisan
organization, will host the final debate in the race for Georgia’s 7th Congressional District between
Democratic nominee Tony Kozycki and incumbent Republican Rep.
Rich McCormick on Thursday,
October 15, at 6:30 p.m. at St.
James Church in Alpharetta.
The North Fulton debate is the sixth and final stop in the challenge Kozycki issued this summer on June
30th: one debate in each of the six counties of the district, from Dawson, Lumpkin, Cherokee and Hall to
Forsyth and Fulton.
Prior to the Fulton County debate, the series will head to Forsyth County for its fifth stop on Monday,
October 5, at 6:30 p.m.
The Forsyth County debate will be held in the Board of Commissioners Meeting
Room, 2435 Freedom Parkway in Cumming, and moderated by Maya T.
Prabhu, Editorial Director at
PeachPol.com and former Georgia politics reporter for The Atlanta Journal-Constitution.
The event is open
to the public.
McCormick has refused the first four debates.
When asked about the series by the AJC, McCormick
claimed he wasn’t aware of the debates and suggested a debate would only give Kozycki publicity he
“badly” needs.
The record says otherwise.
Kozycki hand-delivered a written debate invitation to
McCormick’s Alpharetta office on July 7, McCormick’s office responded to the debate challenge with a
statement to the Cherokee Tribune and AccessNorthGA that same month, and outlets across the district
have covered the series since it was announced, with moderators contacting McCormick’s campaign ahead
of individual debates.
Kozycki argues that debates aren’t about publicity for candidates.
They are one of the few tools voters still
have to evaluate the people asking for their vote, and taking part is a basic duty of anyone who wants to
represent the district.
“Rich has it backwards.
A debate isn’t a favor one candidate does for another, and it isn’t a publicity stunt,”
said Tony Kozycki.
“Campaigns control the ads, the mailers, the photos and the talking points.
A debate is
the one place where voters take that control back.
Anyone who wants to represent this district has a duty to
stand in front of the people he works for and answer their questions.
Rich doesn’t owe me a debate.
He
owes it to the voters of the 7th District.”
The invitation for McCormick to participate remains open for both, and Kozycki is encouraging him to join
the nonpartisan, NAACP-hosted final debate.
“The North Fulton NAACP is offering a nonpartisan stage, a neutral moderator and a room full of voters.
There’s no better opportunity for Rich to explain his record,” Kozycki said.
“His podium will be waiting in
Cumming on October 5 and in Alpharetta on October 15.
I hope he finally shows up to fill it.”
“This event is about the voters,” said Erika Barnes Ford, PhD, MPA, President of the North Fulton
NAACP.
“Our goal is to create a fair and respectful environment where constituents can hear directly from
both candidates, better understand their positions and priorities, and have the information they need to make
their own decisions at the ballot box.”
