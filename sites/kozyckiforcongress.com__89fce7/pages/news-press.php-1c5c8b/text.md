Alpharetta, GA - The North Fulton County Branch of the NAACP (Branch 53AA), a nonpartisan organization, will host the final debate in the race for Georgia’s 7th Congressional District between Democratic nominee Tony Kozycki and incumbent Republican Rep.
Rich McCormick on Thursday, October 15, at 6:30 p.m. at St.
James Church in Alpharetta.
The North Fulton debate is the sixth and final stop in the challenge Kozycki issued this summer on June 30th: one debate in each of the six counties of the district, from Dawson, Lumpkin, Cherokee and Hall to Forsyth and Fulton.
Prior to the Fulton County debate, the series will head to Forsyth County for its fifth stop on Monday, October 5, at 6:30 p.m.
The Forsyth County debate will be held in the Board of Commissioners Meeting Room, 2435 Freedom Parkway in Cumming, and moderated by Maya T.
Prabhu, Editorial Director at PeachPol.com and former Georgia politics reporter for The Atlanta Journal-Constitution.
The event is open to the public.
McCormick has refused the first four debates.
When asked about the series by the AJC, McCormick claimed he wasn’t aware of the debates and suggested a debate would only give Kozycki publicity he “badly” needs.
The record says otherwise.
Kozycki hand-delivered a written debate invitation to McCormick’s Alpharetta office on July 7, McCormick’s office responded to the debate challenge with a statement to the Cherokee Tribune and AccessNorthGA that same month, and outlets across the district have covered the series since it was announced, with moderators contacting McCormick’s campaign ahead of individual debates.
Kozycki argues that debates aren’t about publicity for candidates.
They are one of the few tools voters still have to evaluate the people asking for their vote, and taking part is a basic duty of anyone who wants to represent the district.
“Rich has it backwards.
A debate isn’t a favor one candidate does for another, and it isn’t a publicity stunt,” said Tony Kozycki.
“Campaigns control the ads, the mailers, the photos and the talking points.
A debate is the one place where voters take that control back.
Anyone who wants to represent this district has a duty to stand in front of the people he works for and answer their questions.
Rich doesn’t owe me a debate.
He owes it to the voters of the 7th District.” The invitation for McCormick to participate remains open for both, and Kozycki is encouraging him to join the nonpartisan, NAACP-hosted final debate.
“The North Fulton NAACP is offering a nonpartisan stage, a neutral moderator and a room full of voters.
There’s no better opportunity for Rich to explain his record,” Kozycki said.
“His podium will be waiting in Cumming on October 5 and in Alpharetta on October 15.
I hope he finally shows up to fill it.” “This event is about the voters,” said Erika Barnes Ford, PhD, MPA, President of the North Fulton NAACP.
“Our goal is to create a fair and respectful environment where constituents can hear directly from both candidates, better understand their positions and priorities, and have the information they need to make their own decisions at the ballot box.” Debate Details: Debate 5, Forsyth County: Monday, October 5, 6:30 p.m.

Senate and Congressional Races First public poll shows Ossoff and Collins tied among GA-07 voters and Democrat Tony Kozycki just three points behind Republican incumbent Rich McCormick in a district long considered safely Republican.
Alpharetta, GA - A new poll released this week by 20/20 Insight, LLC shows a surprisingly competitive political landscape inside Georgia's Republican-held 7th Congressional District.
At the top of the ticket, likely GA-07 voters split evenly between Senator Jon Ossoff (D) and Mike Collins (R), 46% to 46%, with 8% undecided.
In the congressional race, the news is just as striking.
Democrat Tony Kozycki trails incumbent Rep.
Rich McCormick by just three points, 43% to 46%, with 11% still undecided.
The three-point gap falls within the survey's ±4.7% margin of error, putting a race national handicappers haven't had on their radar squarely in play.
“I’m glad to see the poll, but I didn’t need one to tell me this race is competitive,” said Tony Kozycki.
“I’ve spent months knocking doors and talking to people from Johns Creek to Dahlonega and everywhere in between.
They’re tired of watching everything get more expensive while Washington gets nothing done, tired of a congressman who dodges debates, and tired of a politician who’s forgotten who he works for.
This poll just puts numbers behind what we’ve been seeing on the ground.
We’re going to keep showing up, keep listening, and earn every vote it takes to win this race.” The survey also tested how voters respond to Kozycki’s biography: raised in Georgia, a Naval Academy graduate who flew in the F/A-18 through three combat deployments and earned three Air Medals before earning his law degree summa cum laude and building a career representing Georgia families.
When surveyed voters heard his background, 56% said they would consider voting for Kozycki, including 35% who said they would greatly consider it.
“For a Democrat introducing himself in a district this red, that’s an extraordinary result,” said GA-07 voter Alyce Goldberg.
“It tells you voters across the political spectrum are responding to Tony’s record of service and his willingness to put country over party and that they’re ready for a representative who will actually show up for them, unlike Rich McCormick.” The poll comes as voters weigh McCormick’s record on healthcare and veterans, his vote to block release of the Epstein files, and his refusal to debate Kozycki.
Methodology: 20/20 Insight, LLC surveyed 445 likely voters in Georgia's 7th Congressional District from August 20–26, 2026.
The margin of error for the full sample is ±4.7% at the 95% confidence level.
