Kozycki concludes districtwide debate series at NAACP-hosted event in Alpharetta as Republican voters question incumbent's accessibility and priorities Alpharetta, GA – An empty podium and a cardboard cut out stood beside Democratic congressional nominee Tony Kozycki Thursday night, a familiar sight throughout his districtwide debate series.
Congressman Rich McCormick had once again declined an opportunity to face the voters he represents.
The final debate, held at St.
James Community Church in Alpharetta and hosted by the North Fulton Branch of the NAACP, brought together voters from across the political spectrum to discuss the cost of living, health care, tariffs, military conflicts, and the future of Georgia's 7th Congressional District.
McCormick was invited to debates in all six counties of the district.
He attended none.
"Rich McCormick doesn't owe me a debate.
He owes the people of Georgia's 7th District an explanation for his record," said Kozycki.
"The voters showed up.
For the sixth time, their congressman didn't." Political attacks instead of answers Earlier this week, McCormick's campaign released a statement that Kozycki was running on "failed socialist policies that never work." Kozycki rejected the characterization.
"Rich McCormick calls me a socialist because he can't defend his own record.
I'm a smallbusiness owner.
I believe in capitalism, competition, and the free market.
What I don't believe in is crony capitalism, where giant corporations buy out their competition, drive up prices, and then use their money to buy influence in Washington." 1 "Look at health insurance.
Families are paying more and fighting harder to get care while enormous insurance companies dominate the market.
Rich McCormick is a doctor who promised to fix healthcare.
Where's his plan to restore competition and bring down costs?" Kozycki also challenged McCormick's claim that lowering costs is his priority, pointing to his support for tariffs and his votes against congressional efforts to end unauthorized hostilities with Iran.
"He says he's laser focused on lowering costs while supporting tariffs that raise prices for American families.
He talks about keeping people safe while voting against efforts to stop another war.
He ought to have the courage to stand before the families paying more at the grocery store, at the gas pump, and for their healthcare, look them in the eye, and defend the votes that are making their lives more expensive.” Kozycki, a Navy veteran who completed three combat deployments overseas, further responded: “He can try to scare voters by calling a guy who fought three combat deployments for this country a communist, but he ought to have the backbone to stand on a stage, look me in the eye, and say it to my face.” Republican voters are asking questions, too Among those attending Thursday's debate were longtime Republican voters who say they no longer feel represented by their congressman.
Jim Needle, a Republican who has lived in and around the district for 34 years, met Kozycki on the campaign trail and is supporting him in November.
“Former congressmen like Newt Gingrich, Tom Price, Johnny Isakson, and Karen Handel were always very accessible.
If I ever wanted to talk to them, I could, very quickly, and they would give me the time of day,” said Needle.
“That’s not the case with Rich McCormick.
He’s not around for debates.
He’s not around for town halls.
And I feel like he should get another profession if he does not want to face his constituents.” Needle said traditional Republican values, including America’s post-World War II alliances in Europe and Australia, have been squeezed out of this year’s race.
“I feel like if Reagan were alive today, he’d be supporting the Democrats,” Needle said.
Of Kozycki, he added simply: “He’s a great guy.” McCormick distracted looking toward his 2028 Senate campaign?
Kozycki first challenged McCormick to a debate in each of the district's six counties in June.
Debates were organized in Dawson, Lumpkin, Cherokee, Hall, Forsyth, and Fulton counties.
McCormick attended none.
For Thursday's final debate, McCormick personally contacted North Fulton NAACP President Dr.
Erika Barnes-Ford to decline the invitation, claiming to be prescheduled for an event in Tifton, Georgia, according to event organizers.
2 Meanwhile, McCormick traveled to Americus in South Georgia earlier this week, where he addressed a local Rotary Club.
Kozycki questioned whether the congressman's interest in a possible future Senate campaign is already taking attention away from the district he represents.
"We're in the middle of an election for Georgia's 7th District, and Rich McCormick is nowhere to be seen.
Rich has time to travel across the state but not to attend one debate here at home.
If he's this unresponsive when he needs your vote, imagine what the next two years will look like if he spends them preparing for a Senate campaign." Kozycki said McCormick's absence reflects a broader problem with the political system.
"This is what's wrong with Washington.
Gerrymandering makes politicians think they own their seats.
Corporate money gives powerful interests more influence than ordinary voters.
And career politicians start looking for the next rung on the ladder before they've finished the job they were elected to do." Georgia’s 7th Congressional District has been at the center of partisan redistricting battles for years.
McCormick lost his first congressional race to Carolyn Bourdeaux in 2020.
He then won a different congressional district in 2022 after redistricting reshaped the map in Republicans' favor.
"Rich McCormick is the epitome of that problem.
He's happy to attack me from a distance, but he's unwilling to stand in front of the people he represents and answer their questions." One final challenge With early voting underway, Kozycki renewed his invitation for McCormick to debate before Election Day.
"Rich, day or night.
Name the time.
Name the place.
I'll debate you anywhere in this district.
Call me a socialist.
Call me a communist.
But you'll have to defend those accusations and defend your own record in front of the people who pay your salary." "You've had six opportunities.
You've taken none." "Congressional seats don't belong to political parties, corporations, or career politicians.
They belong to the American people.
And the people of Georgia's 7th District deserve a congressman who has the backbone to show up." Early voting began October 13.
Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

About Tony Kozycki: Tony Kozycki is a dad, Naval Academy graduate, and attorney running to represent Georgia’s 7th Congressional District.
A former naval flight officer who completed three combat deployments in the F/A-18 fighter jet and earned three Air Medals, Tony went on to earn his law degree summa cum laude and build a career fighting for Georgia families.
He is the Democratic nominee for GA07.
