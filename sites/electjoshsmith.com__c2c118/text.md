Issues Education From our pioneer beginnings to today, Utahns have always prioritized education.
Thanks to the tireless investments of teachers and our communities, Utah is one of the most educated states in the country.
Adequate Funding School funding comes primarily from property taxes and the state income tax.
That partnership only works when the state carries its fair share of the Minimum School Program and Weighted Pupil Unit—including the enrollment and inflation adjustments already required by law.
I will focus on making sure the state fully funds classrooms so school districts aren’t forced to keep raising property taxes on homeowners just to cover the gap.
Challenging Curriculum Every student should be stretched—whether they’re catching up or ready for more.
I support strong core academics, expansion of career and technical pathways, and advanced options so kids aren’t stuck in a one-size-fits-all track.
Support For Educators Educators are professionals.
I will listen to teachers and parents before writing classroom rules from the Capitol, keep political fights out of lesson plans, and focus on pay, retention, and time to teach—not micromanagement from politicians who haven’t been in a classroom.
Voucher Accountability Anyone taking public education money for personal education should be required to take the same tests that are required for public schools.
Funds should be used for education purposes and not for expensive equipment or hobbies.
Re-enrolling in a public school part way through the school year should return a prorated return of the funds.
Utah Values Faith With our strong religious heritage, We understand the importance of freedom to worship according to the dictates of our consciences.
Community With strong desire to help our neighbors and volunteer in our communities .
We want to make sure our communities are safe places for individuals to interact and share experiences.
Self-Determination Adults should have room to build a life: own a home, start a business, raise a family, and make decisions without unnecessary gatekeepers.
Government’s job is to set fair rules and get out of the way—not manage people’s lives for them.
Housing With the cost of housing doubling in the last decade many Utahn find home ownership out of reach.
We need to ensure that Utah is and remains an affordable place for our famlies.
Increase Housing Supply Because of the 2008 housing crisis, Utah has not been building enough homes to keep up the increased demand.
Promote the building of affordable homes along Wasatch Front.
Accessory Dwelling Units Working with cities we can make it easier to have more accessory dwelling and enable sub-leasing which will make renting cheaper and alleviate some of the housing demand.
Diverse Housing Options When building communities it’s important to have a range of housing options so that family can get started with the first homes and move into larger homes as their families expand.
It also balances our neighbors with more established household with new ones.
Prosperity Utah’s economy continues to grow with our unique culture of hard work, dedication to individual and collective success and family values.
Quality Jobs A growing Utah economy only works if wages keep up with housing and grocery costs.
I will push for workforce training tied to real employer demand, portable skills (not just one company), and policies that don’t force families to choose between a paycheck and raising kids here.
Entrepreneurship Utah brings life to the American Dream when it supports small business and entrepreneurs.
We need policies that streamline business creation and support local business.
These business enrich our state and make it more enjoyable to live here.
Government by the People Our leaders are elected to represent all of their constituents.
They should care more about good policies than scoring political points or consolidating more power for themselves.
Eliminate Spoiler Effect We can make our races more competitive and viable independent or third party candidates by using Ranked and Approval Voting methods.
Voters can elect the best candidate regardless of party without worrying the about throwing away their vote.
Final Four Voting We can copy Alaska’s popular Top Four Voting which puts everyone that qualifies for the primary on the same ballot.
The top four candidates with the most votes move on to the General Election.
Votes rank the remaining four, using instant run off voting to eliminate candidates until someone gets above the 50% threshold.
Independent Redistricting People have the right to choose their representatives rather than representatives choosing their voters.
The Independent Commissioning Board present a selection of maps that are fairly drawn to ensure proper competition and representation across the state.
The State Legislatures job is to pick ONE of the maps provided by the commission.
The Utah Supreme Court has upheld that Utahns have the right to “alter or reform their government” and that the legislature is still able to do their constitutional duty.
Privacy & Surveillance You should control your own data, devices, and movements.
The Fourth Amendment protects against unreasonable searches, these same protections apply to data collected from cameras and the web.
Flock Cameras & LPRs Automated license plate readers and camera networks like Flock can map where we drive, when we travel, and who we associate with.
We should not trade Fourth Amendment privacy for the illusion of security.
Government access to this data should require probable cause and a judge-issued warrant.
Retention should be short and automatic; I support deleting collected location data on a fixed timeline (such as six months) unless a warrant or active case requires otherwise.
Vendors should not be shielded from accountability when these systems are misused.
Consent & Ownership Personal data about ourselves, our images, and the things we create should be respected and protected from use by governments, artificial intelligence, and corporations without our consent or a court order.
We should strengthen Utah’s privacy laws so people can know what was collected and demand it be deleted, closer to the spirit of strong consent-and-deletion rules used under frameworks like Europe’s GDPR.

Artificial Intelligence Your likeness, voice, and creative works should not be copied or used by AI without your consent.
Data Centers Data centers are part of the modern internet works.
Utah should be able to host them, but only if they are built at a responsible scale, in the right places, with honest accounting for water, power, heat, and taxes.
Mega-campuses sized in the tens of thousands of acres put communities and the Great Salt Lake watershed under strain that we cannot ignore.
Scale Limits Cap new data-campus footprints so projects stay manageable for local infrastructure, not proposals on the order of tens of thousands of acres.
A working limit I support is no larger than about 50 acres per facility.
Water & Cooling Require modern non-evaporative cooling, public metering of water use, and proportional penalties for overuse.
New high-demand users should prove they will use less water than the land they replace.
Water rules for these facilities must serve Great Salt Lake restoration, not cancel it out.
No Special Tax Deals Unelected authorities should not divert local tax growth to subsidize private mega-projects.
I oppose sweetheart energy and property-tax arrangements that shift costs onto families and schools while developers capture decades of relief.
Communities and the state should benefit from the growth they host, not underwrite it.
Local Control & Transparency Bodies like MIDA should stay focused on the military mission, not approve sprawling data centers, resorts, and unrelated development.
When local tax dollars are at stake, elected county officials should have to approve the deal, and meetings, budgets, and records should be as open as city and county government.
Great Salt Lake With much of the lakebed exposed and toxic dust blowing across the Wasatch Front, restoring the Great Salt Lake is not optional.
A healthy lake protects our air, snowpack, wildlife, economy, and quality of life.
Leaders have promised to save the lake in time for the 2034 Winter Olympics, including through the Governor’s Great Salt Lake 2034 Charter.
Promises without policy are not enough.
Like The People’s Great Salt Lake 2034 Charter , I believe Utahns deserve a concrete roadmap that puts more water in the lake and keeps it there, with real accountability for results..
Restore to 4200 feet I support defining a healthy lake level of 4,200 feet and treating anything below that as an emergency that requires action.
Progress should be measured in water in the lake, not press conferences.
All major water use and restoration progress must be publicly tracked and reported so Utahns can see whether we are actually raising the lake.
If we are serious about 2034, the Legislature should fund the tools that work, remove barriers that slow water transfers and leasing to the lake, and report regularly on elevation, inflows, and whether new growth is canceling out the gains we pay for.
Ensure saving go to the lake Everyone needs to conserve, but the largest water users should lead, including farms, industry, and high-demand projects such as data centers.
The state should keep investing in water-saving technology for agriculture and business, and guarantee that at least half of every gallon saved goes back to the lake, not into new growth that wipes out the benefit.
When the state buys, leases, or retires water rights for restoration, those rights must stay locked for the lake, not quietly redirected to new construction.
New development should prove water is available for the long term (on the order of the next hundred years) and use less water than the land currently does.
That is how we honor farmers and communities who conserve while still delivering a wetter lake for our kids.
Technology “We have Paleolithic emotions, medieval institutions and godlike technology” — Edward Wilson.
It is our responsibility to ensure technology is used for the benefit of Humanity Privacy Personal data about ourselves, images, and things we create should be respected and protected from use by governments, artificial intelligence and corporations without our consent or a court order.
Right to Repair It is unsustainable to replace our appliances and electronic device every few years because of the cost of difficulty in repairing these devices.
We should own the products we buy and be able to fix and maintain without them being vendor locked.
Online Safety Social media and other media online can be used for great good in our society, but has also helped to divide our communities.
Social media companies need to be transparent and accountable for their content algorithms.
We need to protect ourselves from foreign actors influencing us and protect and safeguard harmful content from our youth.
