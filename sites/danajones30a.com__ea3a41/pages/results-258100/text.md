This is a vital piece of consumer protection legislation I have been proud to sponsor for two years in a row.
Known as "Zombie Mortgages," these loans were purchased for pennies on the dollar by private equity firms, targeting homeowners who hadn't received statements in years and believed their debt was forgiven after the Great Financial Crisis of 2008.
Impacted Marylanders are now losing $193,000 in home equity on average, if not losing their home entirely.
Homeowners have been affected in every corner of Maryland, from Garrett to Wicomico County.
However, these mortgages have disproportionately impacted communities of color and working families.
Zip codes with the highest number of zombie mortgages are demographically predominantly Black and or Hispanic.
In Prince George's County, four zip codes with over 80% Black or African American populations are among the hardest hit.
The data makes clear that Zombie Mortgages are stripping wealth disproportionately from low-to-moderate-income communities and communities of color.
This problem has been so pervasive that Bloomberg News has published extensive coverage of the issue, including an interview with me about this legislation.
HB 523 would have instituted a statute of limitations on mortgage foreclosures, safeguarding home equity for Maryland’s working families.
Maryland and Alabama are the only two states that do not have this vital consumer protection.
It is very unfortunate that this crucial bill was held on the Senate floor by a fellow Democrat, but I am determined to keep reintroducing this legislation until it passes and Marylanders are protected.
