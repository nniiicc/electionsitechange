Andrew sponsored HB 13 to give municipalities optional property tax tools they can use to encourage long-term rentals, support mobile home park improvements, help low-income renters, assist owner-occupied housing, and make it easier for first-time homebuyers to put down roots.
HB 13 does not force a one-size-fits-all policy on local governments.
Instead, it gives municipalities the choice to adopt targeted property tax exemptions that can encourage short-term rentals to become long-term homes, support construction and renovation in mobile home parks, help low-income renters, assist owner-occupied housing, and make it easier for first-time homebuyers to put down roots.
This kind of flexibility matters because Alaska’s housing challenges look different from community to community.
In Anchorage, the Assembly pointed to a significant housing affordability crisis, high rental costs, concerns about the effect of short-term rentals on the long-term rental market, and the need to preserve mobile home parks as an essential source of affordable housing.
The Assembly also cited a statewide need for approximately 13,500 homes for low- and extremely low-income households.
Which is why the Anchorage Assembly passed a resolution strongly supporting HB 13.
The Greater Fairbanks Chamber of Commerce also supported HB 13, calling housing one of Interior Alaska’s most pressing challenges.
The Chamber warned that constrained housing supply limits workforce recruitment and retention, raises costs for residents, and restricts business growth.
It backed HB 13 because the bill would help municipalities encourage long-term housing, support low-income residents, strengthen mobile home parks, and help first-time homebuyers.
Andrew’s bill gives local governments more choices, not more mandates.
A community that wants to encourage long-term rentals can do that.
A community that wants to protect mobile home parks can do that.
A community that wants to help low-income renters, owner-occupants, or first-time homebuyers can do that.
And a community that does not want to use a particular exemption does not have to.
Andrew Gray is fighting for more housing, more local control, and lower costs for Alaska families.
HB 13 is exactly the kind of practical, community-driven solution Alaska needs: flexible tools, local choice, and a clear focus on keeping Alaskans housed.
