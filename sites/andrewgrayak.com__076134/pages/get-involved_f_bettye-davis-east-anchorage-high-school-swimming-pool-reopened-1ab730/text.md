June 19, 2026 Some wins in public service are easy to measure: a bill passed, a budget item secured, a vote counted.
Others are measured in something more personal: students walking back into their own pool, families gathering in a community space, and kids getting the chance to learn how to swim.
After being closed for more than five years, the Bettye Davis East Anchorage High School swimming pool reopened in 2024.
For East Anchorage, that reopening matters.
The East High pool is more than a school facility.
It is home to the BDEAHS swim and dive team.
It is a place where students practice, compete, and build confidence.
It is also an important community center where families gather, neighbors recreate, and children learn a skill that can save lives.
When Andrew Gray heard from students, families, coaches, and community members about how much the pool mattered, he got to work.
He helped bring people together, pushed for practical solutions, and facilitated the reopening of a facility that had been closed for far too long.
That is the kind of leadership Andrew believes in: listening to the community, working through the details, and staying focused until something gets done.
The reopening of East High Pool is a win for students, families, and the whole neighborhood.
It means the Thunderbird swim and dive team has a home again.
It means more opportunities for healthy recreation.
It means a safe place for kids to learn to swim.
And it means East Anchorage got back a community resource it never should have had to go without.
This is the kind of result Andrew fights for: solving real problems, improving daily life, and making sure East Anchorage gets the attention it deserves.
This is what leadership looks like: real results, close to home.
