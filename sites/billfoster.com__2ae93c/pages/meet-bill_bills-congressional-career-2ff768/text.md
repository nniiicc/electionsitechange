Bill is running for reelection in Illinois’ 11th Congressional District in the far suburbs of Chicago.
The newly configured district 11th District spans 8 counties and includes parts of two of Illinois’ largest cities, Aurora and Naperville.
From March 2008 until January 2011, Bill Foster represented Illinois’ 14th Congressional District.
Although the district had been heavily gerrymandered by its previous occupant, Republican Speaker J.
Dennis Hastert, Bill was not afraid to take tough votes: the Affordable Care Act, the Automobile Rescue, and Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform.
After taking these tough votes, Bill lost his race in the 2010 Tea Party Wave election, then fought his way back to Congress in 2012 by defeating an entrenched Republican and has represented the IL-11 district since then.

THE 2008 SPECIAL ELECTION
- Bill began his political career in 2007 by challenging former Speaker of the House J.
Dennis Hastert in one of the most famously Republican districts in the nation — a district that Hastert had gerrymandered to provide himself a safe seat in Congress.
- Hastert’s resignation halfway though his term triggered a special election that drew national interest when it became clear that scientist, businessman, and Democrat Bill Foster had a realistic shot at winning this seat.
The race became a proxy for the upcoming 2008 presidential election, as Illinois Sen.
Barack Obama endorsed Foster in a campaign video advertisement and presumptive Republican nominee John McCain came in to campaign for the Republican candidate Jim Oberweis.
In a groundbreaking Saturday special election, volunteers poured in from all over northern Illinois.
- In what was described as “A Political Earthquake” Foster won the election by six points, 53%-47%, becoming the first Democrat to represent the district in over 60 years.
