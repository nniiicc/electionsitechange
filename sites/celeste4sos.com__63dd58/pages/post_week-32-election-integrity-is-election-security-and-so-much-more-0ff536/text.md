top of page Home Meet Priorities News Newsletters Events Help Donate Week 32: Election Integrity Is Election Security AND So Much More Celeste Landry Sep 22 2 min read Dear Colorado Voter, Election integrity – one of my priority issues – may seem synonymous with election security.
Yet, election integrity is so much more.
The goal of election integrity is free and fair elections with trusted results.
The entire electoral process – from ballot access (how candidates get on the ballot) to voter registration to campaign finance to media access to ballot delivery to the voting method (choose-one, ranking, rating) to our semi-open primaries – affects the integrity of elections.
Election security is just one part of election integrity.
A secure election provides chain of custody, pre-election equipment testing, post-election tabulation audits, and other safeguards that protect ballots from being altered and ensure every valid vote is counted.
County clerks and I have discussed closing two security loopholes: one that currently affects a small number of mail ballots and another with risk-limiting audits (RLAs).
In July in Weld County, I participated in a pilot program that independently verified RLA results.
Let’s expand from a pilot to a standard procedure.
Competitive elections are an important part of free and fair elections, but when a competitive single-winner contest has 3 or more candidates, and voters are limited to choosing one candidate, there is no guarantee that the electorate’s preferred choice wins.
In the primary, we had 3 gubernatorial candidates on the Republican side and 4 attorney general candidates on the Democratic side.
Since neither winner got a majority of the vote, we are left wondering if a more expressive ranking or rating voting method would have resulted in different winners.
Because the vote on a ranked ballot isn’t necessarily stable – during tabulation a vote may transfer from a first choice to a second choice – ranked voting methods are harder to audit than our traditional plurality voting.
Two voting methods that are easy to audit are Approval Voting (which Littleton has inquired about) for a single-winner contest and a Party List system for a multi-winner contest.
Party List systems elect the top vote-getters from each party in proportion to the electorate’s party preferences AND solve our gerrymandering wars!
Nonpartisan municipal elections can’t use Party Lists; after the SoS writes the long-delayed rules called for in 1-7-1004 C.R.S., municipalities can use a proportional ranked voting method (which Longmont voters will see on their November ballot).
