top of page Home Meet Priorities News Newsletters Events Help Donate Week 33: The Politics of Endorsements and Voter Guides Celeste Landry Sep 29 # min read Updated: #ago Dear Colorado Voter, By now, Colorado’s big Blue Book on ballot measures has arrived in voters’ mailboxes.
Various other voter guides cover candidates and/or may include endorsements.
I didn’t realize how much I would appreciate the voter guides that use the candidates’ exact words.
A candidate like me with a platform that goes beyond a soundbite doesn’t always get portrayed accurately when the voter guide editors do the writing.
As a minor/third-party candidate, I also appreciate the voter guides that treat all candidates equally.
You can share my Week 22 email to give voters an overview of my purpose in running. https://www.celeste4sos.com/post/why-is-celeste-running Ballots go out on Friday.
Third-party candidates aren’t given much of a chance to win and therefore struggle for endorsements.
In Colorado statewide races these days, Republicans aren’t given much of a chance either, but because they have an R by their name, they are automatically “serious” candidates.
The Forward Party is trying a new paradigm: run qualified, serious, well-vetted candidates in races that would otherwise be uncompetitive in the general election.
We give voters real choices and permission NOT to vote a straight-party ticket, especially for an office that should be nonpartisan, like SoS.
Imagine the message Colorado would send the nation by electing me, a nonpartisan-type candidate, as SoS.
And in Colorado’s sesquicentennial year, no less!
Some media only report on the major-party candidates.
This is a disservice to voters.
The media’s response is typically we are “capped by resources.” The end result is that the media are effectively putting their thumb on the scale.
Current (major-party) candidates and elected officials feel bound by party discipline to endorse their party’s nominees.
I have received and reported donations from a major-party candidate, activists, and former elected officials, but they aren’t willing to publicly endorse a Forward Party candidate.
(Maybe my fight against two party-protection bills passed in 2025 doesn’t help, but I’ll continue to put people and principles above endorsement politics.) Activist groups understandably want to back a winner who will have power when serving in office.
The Democrat is the safest choice for SoS.
With President Trump’s approval ratings at an all-time low, Democrats are running against Trump, not necessarily running for the people.
Even with me in the race, the Republican doesn’t have a chance, but I’m being called a spoiler nevertheless.
I’ve got an uphill climb, but I’m not giving up.
With enthusiasm and purpose, Celeste Celeste Landry for Secretary of State P.O.
