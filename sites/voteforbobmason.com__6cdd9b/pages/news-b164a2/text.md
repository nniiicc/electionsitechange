The Amazingly Expanding New Mexico State Budget
New Mexico has a strange business plan.
The state takes more and more money from its citizens while not improving services.
In the last eight years the states budget has increased by about 80%.
Do you feel like you are getting 80% better service from the state?
As a comparison, even with the high inflation of the 2021 – 2024 the total inflation during those same eight years was “only” 27.4%.
The state’s budget grew by almost 3 times that.
By the way, in the last year the state took in a $3.5 billion in surplus revenue.
So where does all that money come from?
About 33% of the state’s revenue comes from the oil and gas industry.
The rest comes from various other sources.
Of course, in reality all the states revenue comes from individuals, but two of those sources come directly from you, the taxpayer.
I’ll cover that in a minute.
But first a quick discussion about where that money goes.
The actual number of line items in the states budget are too numerous to cover here, but in general a good bit of that money goes to schools, from elementary to university.
Some goes to state programs.
Some flows to local and county governments.
As of this year some even goes to provide child care for everyone in the state, regardless of need.
Importantly, some of that revenue goes to the state’s various permanent funds.
That basically means a large part of our state’s money is locked up in long-term investment accounts.
At this point, those accounts are worth more than $70 billion dollars, and because those funds are invested, those accounts are growing in value.
At a conservative 6% rate of return that $70 billion fund will grow to $7.4 billion in the next year.
And yet, our state government is uncomfortable using that money to improve the state.
As an example, the 2027 budget includes money for much needed road repairs and improvements.
However, rather than using this huge source of funds, they decided to raise additional money through bond issues, which are paid for through property taxes.
Let’s return to the sources of state revenue that come directly from you.
There are two that just don’t make sense.
The first is income tax.
We have the highest personal income tax among all our southwestern neighbors.
That’s your money!
There are several states in the country that have no income tax and they are doing quite well.
Although it may seem counterintuitive, it has been proven many times that lower taxes result in higher revenue while higher taxes tend to suppress revenue.
Having one of the higher state income taxes discourages business from moving here and helping us diversify our economy.
Remember that surplus revenue I mentioned?
That is more than enough to compensate for the personal income tax.
The second is the gross receipts tax.
You may think of this as a sales tax, but it’s really much more.
Let me explain.
The gross receipts tax, or GRT, is a tax at all stages of production.
If your business sells a product or service, you pay a GRT on every one you sell.
But you also pay a GRT on each component you buy to make your product.
The business that sells you those components also pays a GRT on the pieces they need to make that component.
GRT is often called a pyramid tax and that’s exactly what it is.
So, if you are paying a 7% GRT, you are probably really paying 14%, or 21%, or even more.
A straight sales tax makes more sense.
The sales tax is only paid on the final product which reduces the cost of that product.
There is another form of tax that you pay indirectly.
It’s is normally referred to as a corporate tax.
Last year the federal government passed a significant reduction in the taxes that corporations pay.
Most states follow federal guidelines for these taxes, but this year, our state government passed a law to specifically exclude New Mexico from that tax reduction.
Remember, we may call it a corporate tax, but you still pay it in the cost of goods you buy.
What can be done?
First, we must significantly reduce, or preferably eliminate the personal income tax.
Doing so will be a stimulus to the state’s economy.
Next, we must eliminate the gross receipts tax in favor of a straight sales tax.
Watch the costs of goods and services decline.
Finally, we need to reevaluate how the state manages our permanent funds.
Although it is smart management to maintain a “rainy day” fund for future contingencies, it doesn’t make sense to lock these funds up so that additional revenue (taxes) is the only way to fund projects that benefit the state, like better roads.
We often hear it said that we’re a poor state.
We are not.
But, we are a poorly managed state.

How Should We Approach Crime in New Mexico?
Crime.
When I speak with people in my district here in Albuquerque they tell me it is their number one concern.
It’s true that this city has an incredible and unacceptable level of crime, especially violent crime.
Many just don’t feel safe.
But, this isn’t just an Albuquerque problem.
Crime is a New Mexico problem.
As the Legislative Finance Committee reported, this state has levels of crime that far exceed the national average.
Why?
There is no simple answer to this question.
The problem is the result of several closely related issues.
First is the state’s approach to crime; with soft-on-crime policies.
There are many suggestions for interventions and attempts to prevent incarceration, and that’s good, but at the most basic level, our laws must ensure that if someone commits a crime, they are going to be held and prosecuted.
Our legislature needs to take positive action to hold both the criminals and the criminal justice system accountable.
This applies to juvenile crime as well.
Currently, if a juvenile commits a crime the law makes prosecution very difficult.
Older criminals know this and use young people, making those juveniles a sort of junior criminal-in-training.
Necessary Actions
- Repeal the New Mexico constitutional amendment (cashless bail) that hampers a judge’s ability to keep criminals locked up.
- Properly staff and empower law enforcement agencies.
That also means partnering with the federal government to arrest and deport criminal aliens.
- Strengthen laws relating to juvenile crime.
- Continue work on diversion programs, but not at the expense of these other priorities.
These approaches are important, but are just one part of the equation.
One of the contributors to crime is unemployment.
Studies have shown that there is a definite correlation between unemployment and crime, and that brings us to our state’s finance and budget policies.
I’ll dive deeper into this topic in another post.
For now, the important point is that our tax and spend policies discourage good, job creating companies from locating in our state.
More specifically, the Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) is a burden that discourages business in New Mexico.
Often referred to as a pyramid tax, it levies a fee on each part of the manufacturing or service supply chain.
Those costs are passed on to you, the consumer.
Essentially you pay tax multiple times for a single product or service.
The legislature should abolish the GRT in favor of a straight sales tax.
A significant reduction or better yet elimination of the income tax would also provide an incentive for companies to move to New Mexico.
These steps would make New Mexico more business friendly resulting in more, better paying jobs.
Necessary Actions
- Eliminate the Gross Receipts Tax in favor of a straight sales take on the final product or service.
- Eliminate the state income tax.
Education also plays a role in the crime equation.
New Mexico has consistently been rated as the worst in the nation for educational accomplishment.
The result is often young adults who are not ready to contribute to the working world.
Instead they are stuck in low-paying jobs or are unemployable.
Crime becomes the alternative.
Studies clearly show there is a positive correlation between educational success and reduced crime.
The state continually throws money at our schools, apparently with no expectation of positive results.
We must start holding our schools and administrators responsible for the dismal return on that investment.
A good place to start is with reading.
New Mexico has the lowest literacy rate in the nation.
It’s hard to find a good job if you can’t read.
Necessary Actions
- Direct schools to concentrate on basic skills.
- Allow parents to decide what school is the best choice for their children.
- Give authority to the local school districts who know what’s best for their communities.
- Opt in to the new federal program to provide tax credits to many families allowing them to select the best education source for their children.
- Strengthen commitment to vocation training to provide marketable skills.
There isn’t a single answer to reducing crime in our state.
We must approach this issue from several angles and make crime a much more difficult and less desirable choice.
