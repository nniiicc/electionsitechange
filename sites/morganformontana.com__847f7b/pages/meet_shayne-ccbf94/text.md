Meet Shayne Issues Volunteer Contribute Home Yard Signs Endorsements Meet Shayne I’m Shayne Morgan, and I’m running as an independent candidate for House District 76 because Montana deserves leadership that answers to people not parties.
I was born and raised in Butte and graduated from Butte High School in 1990.
I went on to attend Montana Tech and Carroll College, but my real education came from working in Montana communities and building a life here.
I was also a Golden Gloves boxer, where I learned discipline, resilience, and the value of hard work lessons that have stayed with me throughout my life.
I started my career in radio while still in high school and spent years working at stations across the region.
Later, I moved into newspaper and television, eventually becoming a publisher and launching my own paper, The Valley Explorer .
I’ve seen firsthand how corporate consolidation has changed local media—and how important it is to have independent voices that stand up for local communities.
When I was ready for a new challenge, I turned to wildland firefighting.
I spent eight seasons as a contractor in the Pacific Northwest, running Type 4 and Type 6 engines.
That work taught me discipline, teamwork, and what it means to show up when it matters most.
I later transitioned into cybersecurity, studying at Chemeketa Community College and building a career in coding and cyber security.
I’ve also helped teach others these skills, because I believe in preparing people for the future not just talking about it.
Today, I work for Montana Fish, Wildlife & Parks at the Warm Springs unit, continuing my commitment to public service and to protecting the resources that define our state.
My roots in Montana run deep.
My grandfather worked as a night foreman at the Berkeley Pit.
My grandfather on my mother's side served as vice principal at Butte High School for decades.
My family helped build small businesses like Norman’s Ranch Wear and continues to ranch in Lewistown.
I’m not a career politician.
I’ve worked in the private sector, in public service, and on the front lines.
I’ve built things, led teams, and adapted to change.
I’m running because I understand what makes Montana work and I know we can do better by putting people first, protecting our way of life, and making decisions based on common sense, not party politics.
