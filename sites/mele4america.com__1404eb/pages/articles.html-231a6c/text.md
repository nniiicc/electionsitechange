Public safety Radical agenda Voting Affordability Accountability Public safety What Voters Should Know About Adam Hamawy’s Radical Ties Candidates for federal office should answer plainly for their associations, endorsements, and public record—especially when public safety and extremism are involved.
The campaign believes voters deserve a direct accounting of the people and organizations surrounding every candidate.
Scrutiny is not guilt by association; it is the ordinary transparency owed by anyone asking to exercise federal power.
Gregg’s standard is straightforward: condemn terrorism and antisemitism without qualification, support law enforcement, and tell voters where you stand before Election Day—not after.
Gregg’s line: Public office requires clarity, judgment, and accountability.
Campaign contrast Stop the Radical Agenda: Vote Gregg Mele for Congress New Jersey families should not be forced to choose between affordability, safety, and basic individual liberty.
Washington’s ideological extremes create expensive mandates, weaken public confidence, and distract from the practical work families need: lower costs, safer communities, better schools, and accountable government.
Gregg offers a different approach—country before party, results before ideology, and a willingness to challenge any policy that fails the people of the district.
The choice: Practical representation rooted in the district, not a national ideological script.
Civic participation If You Don’t Vote, Don’t Complain—Take the Mele 10 Challenge Elections are decided by people who show up, help others participate, and follow through.
The Mele 10 Challenge asks each supporter to help ten people confirm their registration and make a voting plan.
The goal is not another social-media argument.
It is ten respectful, person-to-person conversations that end with ten prepared voters.
Check your status, learn the deadlines, decide how you will vote, and make sure the people around you can do the same.
Take the challenge Official NJ voter information → Affordability Washington Can’t Spend Its Way to Affordability Another federal promise is not relief if it arrives with more debt, higher prices, and no measurable result.
New Jersey families feel federal policy in grocery aisles, utility bills, rent, mortgage payments, and taxes.
Real relief starts with spending discipline, reliable American energy, fewer barriers to building and business, and competitive markets that give families choices.
Gregg will judge legislation by its full cost and its real-world effect—not by the size of the press release attached to it.
Bottom line: Lower the cost of living by addressing the sources of cost, not by sending taxpayers another bill.
Accountability Don’t Give Washington a Blank Check Every proposal should disclose its cost, its funding, its owner, and the result taxpayers should expect.
Congress too often measures commitment by dollars announced instead of outcomes delivered.
Programs expand, deadlines move, and responsibility disappears.
Gregg believes legislation should be written so voters can understand what was promised and later determine whether government kept that promise.
That means plain-language cost estimates, measurable goals, regular audits, and the courage to repair or end what does not work.
Gregg’s test: What will it cost?
How will it be paid for?
Will it actually work?
