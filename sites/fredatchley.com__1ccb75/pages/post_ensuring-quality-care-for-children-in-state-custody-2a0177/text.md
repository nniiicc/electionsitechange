Ensuring Quality Care for Children in State Custody
Every child in Tennessee deserves safety, stability, and the chance to thrive.
For children in state custody, that responsibility falls to the Department of Children's Services.
It is one of the most important and difficult jobs in state government.
This session, the General Assembly passed several measures to strengthen oversight of DCS and ensure that children in state care receive consistent, high-quality services.
One key piece of legislation establishes a statewide quality assurance program within the department.
This is not about adding bureaucracy.
It is about creating real accountability.
The program will include a state-level quality assurance committee and regional coordinators to monitor and evaluate service delivery across all regions of Tennessee.
The focus will be on outcomes that matter: child safety, permanency, and overall well-being.
The program will require regular review of compliance with federal, state, and departmental policies, identify barriers to effective service delivery, and incorporate feedback from local advisory boards and citizen review panels.
Another piece of legislation creates a task force to examine cases involving children in DCS custody for serious offenses, including murder or attempted murder, as well as children found incompetent to be adjudicated or who have exhibited violent behaviors.
These are some of the most challenging cases in the system.
They require specialized attention and resources.
The legislation also sets clear limits on how long children may remain in state custody for treatment.
In most cases, custody is capped at six months, with the possibility of extension if the child still needs in-custody treatment.
This ensures that children are not languishing in the system indefinitely while also providing flexibility for cases that genuinely require more time.
I believe in the mission of DCS.
The men and women who work there are dedicated public servants doing incredibly difficult work.
But like any government agency, they need oversight, accountability, and the resources to do their jobs well.
These measures help provide all three.
Our children are counting on us to get this right.
