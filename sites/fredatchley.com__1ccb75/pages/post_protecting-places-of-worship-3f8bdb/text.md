Protecting Places of Worship
Every Tennessean has the right to worship in peace – you should be able to do so without fear of intimidation, disruption, or violence.
Unfortunately, we live in a time when these sacred spaces are increasingly under threat.
Across the country, we have seen vandalism, harassment, and even deadly attacks targeting people at prayer.
It is a disturbing trend, and Tennessee will not tolerate it.
This session, the General Assembly passed legislation to increase protections for houses of worship and those who gather there.
The new law creates a Class A misdemeanor for intentionally interfering with or intimidating individuals during a religious service.
If you show up to disrupt people's worship, you will face criminal charges.
The legislation also strengthens penalties for property crimes against houses of worship.
Intentionally damaging or destroying the property of a religious institution is now a Class E felony for a first offense and a Class C felony for subsequent offenses.
In addition to the criminal sentence, a judge must impose a fine of at least twice the total amount of any property damage or restoration costs.
Beyond criminal penalties, the law allows houses of worship and individuals to file civil lawsuits for damages and injunctive relief.
The attorney general is also authorized to seek civil penalties for violations.
Our churches, synagogues, mosques, and other places of worship are sacred spaces.
They are pillars of our communities.
They provide comfort in times of grief, guidance in times of uncertainty, and fellowship in times of need.
Tennessee will protect them.
This legislation makes that commitment clear.
