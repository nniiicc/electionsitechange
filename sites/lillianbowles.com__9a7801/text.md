For too long, politics has felt disconnected from the real challenges families face.
Lillian Bowles is running for Utah House District 49 because she’s lived the policies politicians debate — as a mother, a caregiver, and a grandmother.
District 49 deserves leadership rooted in real life, not partisan noise.
I've Lived What Families are Facing.
Public policy isn’t abstract to me—it shapes real lives, including my own.
I raised a child with disabilities as a single mother.
Today, I care for my elderly mother while helping raise the next generation as a proud grandmother of five (soon to be six).
For more than 20 years, I worked in financial services helping families plan for stability and navigate tough decisions.
As an AmeriCorps VISTA with Utah Refugee Services and a longtime community advocate, I’ve spent decades showing up for women, children, and working families.
I’m also a first-generation, non-traditional graduate of the University of Utah, where I earned degrees in Political Science and Sociology, graduating Cum Laude.
My Priorities
I’m running for the Utah Legislature because I’ve lived the experiences our lawmakers write laws about—experiences that shaped my values and why I care so deeply about dignity, opportunity, and service.
This election is about real priorities: making sure people have a fair shot at opportunity, that families can access the resources they need to succeed, and that our communities are strong, inclusive, and built to last.
That means expanding access to education, supporting economic stability, and making sure everyday voices are part of the decisions that shape our future.
