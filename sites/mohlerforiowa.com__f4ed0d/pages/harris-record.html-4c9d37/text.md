The Area Education Agencies are how a school the size of Moravia or Moulton affords a speech pathologist at all.
HF 2612 stripped their media and education-services money and pushed them toward fee-for-service.
Iowa's nine AEAs lost 429 staff the very next school year.
Fewer AEA staff means fewer services for special-needs kids in small districts.
He voted for it.
Source: HF 2612, legis.iowa.gov.
Caps non-economic damages when a clinic or hospital's negligence wrecks a life: $1 million for clinics, $2 million for hospitals.
The rural catch: the uncapped part of an award leans on lost wages, and rural wages run lower.
For a retiree, a kid, or anyone in a low-wage job, the capped pain-and-suffering money is often the biggest share of what a jury can give.
He voted to let a statute, not twelve Iowans, decide what your pain is worth.
As of the 2026 session, he chairs the House Health and Human Services Committee: healthcare in this state runs through his room.
Source: HF 161, legis.iowa.gov · roll call mirrored at LegiScan.
That room changed hands in the fall of 2025, when leadership named him Health and Human Services chair for the 2026 session.
Before that fall, no health-industry PAC had ever written him a check bigger than $2,500.

Centene and Elevance, whose checks arrived weeks apart, are parent companies of two of the corporations that run Iowa's privatized Medicaid.

On the 208 contested bills in our database, the ones that split the chamber where a vote is a real choice, he voted with his party 200 times.
Give him the 8 breaks, on the record: no on the state ban on local guaranteed-income pilots, leaving that spending call with cities and counties instead of Des Moines; no on a charter-school funding bill that sent per-pupil money out with departing students and could force districts to sell school buildings to charter or private bidders; no on opening public-school sports and activities to private-school students; no, on both roll calls, on letting dental assistants work without registering with the dental board; one of five Republicans to vote no on the 2025 law rewriting how Iowa statutes define sex and gender; and three more in the 2026 session, on side matters: the rules for school-board and city elections, hunter education requirements, and a school-security grant funded by a fee on wire transfers.
