Nothing new from the general fund.
State law already puts $5 million a year from the general fund into the renewable fuel infrastructure fund, which pays for grants to put ethanol and biodiesel tanks and pumps in at gas stations and fuel terminals (sections 159A.16 and 159A.17).
This bill ends that deposit after the budget year that closes June 30, 2028.
Starting July 1, 2028, it splits the same $5 million two ways: $4.5 million a year into the new Iowa Ag Diversification Fund, and $500,000 a year straight to the Department of Agriculture for testing motor fuel at the pump.
Same source, same total.
The fuel testing piece is there because the pump fund pays for it now, out of its balance.
Lawmakers paid $500,000 for motor fuel inspection out of the pump fund for the budget year that started July 2025, in Senate File 646, and again for this one, in House File 2771, even though the general fund put nothing into the pump fund either year.
State law already says fuel testing gets paid from state money not set aside for anything else (section 214A.14), which, in effect, means the general fund.
So if we moved the whole deposit, then once the pump fund's balance ran out, fuel testing would land on the general fund at about $500,000 a year.
That would be a new general fund cost.
Paying it straight from the deposit keeps it out in the open and keeps the total where it is.
That's why the bill stays off the Pay As You Go Act's scorecard: it adds no new general fund cost, because it moves an existing appropriation.
The scorecard counts what a bill adds to general fund spending or takes away in tax credits, and it can only trim what a bill creates.
This bill creates new deposits and ends an old one of the same total, so there's nothing on net for the scorecard to count or trim.
When the money comes, and why Here are the dates, plainly.
The credits end January 1, 2028, under current law.
This bill takes effect July 1, 2027, so the department can write rules and hire Iowa State University before the credits end: the contract for the ten-year plan has to be signed by October 1, 2027.
The fund's first money comes on July 1, 2028, six months after the credits end.
The plan is due December 31, 2028.
Once money comes in, the bill pays for the plan, including what the university does under the contract before then.
If lawmakers skip that first deposit, the payment waits.
That six-month gap is on purpose.
Our Pay As You Go Act skips the fuel-pump deposit for the budget year that starts July 1, 2027, the same way lawmakers skipped it this year in Senate File 2484.
This bill starts the year after, so the two bills never count the same dollars.
After that skipped year the deposit comes back, split between the new fund and fuel testing, not pump grants.
So when the credits end, there's a plan being written and money set in law for the July after.
There isn't a replacement already running on January 1, 2028, and we don't say there is.
Each year, the new fund pays for: Running it: up to five percent of the year's deposit.
Iowa State University's ten-year plan: up to $250,000 in total, paid once there's money in the fund.
Third-crop markets: at least forty percent of what's left.
A grant pays up to fifty percent of a project's cost, and up to $250,000 for one project.
The money is for cleaning, drying, storage, dehulling, milling and other processing of oats, rye, barley, wheat and food crops other than corn and soybeans, and for a first buyer setting up to buy those crops from Iowa farmers.
Pasture: at least forty percent of what's left, for fence and livestock water.
The rest goes where the demand is.
In the first year, third-crop markets get at least $1.61 million and pasture gets at least $1.61 million, even if running the fund and the plan both take their whole share.
In a year with no plan to pay for, each gets at least $1.71 million.
The pasture money goes to the Department of Agriculture's soil conservation division.
If the FAIR Act's grazing infrastructure cost-share fund exists, the money goes into it and follows the FAIR Act's rules: the state pays up to half the cost, there's a cap per person, and the producer signs a ten-year agreement to graze under a written plan.
FUEL's money pays for fence and water only.
The FAIR Act lets its fund spend a share on running the program, and this bill says none of FUEL's money goes to that, since FUEL already took its own share for running its fund.
If the FAIR Act hasn't passed, the division pays it through the state's existing cost-share for permanent conservation practices (section 161A.73), which pays up to half the cost.
That program doesn't list grazing fence or livestock water today, so this bill makes them eligible for this money, with the same ten-year agreement and grazing plan the FAIR Act asks for.
Either way it's one program, not two.
The new fund sits in the part of state law that holds the Department of Agriculture's marketing programs (chapter 187).
That chapter has its own fund for the Choose Iowa program, and the bill says that money can't be used here and this fund's money can't be used anywhere else.
Who pays: state taxpayers, the same as now.
Who gives something up: stations and fuel terminals that would have applied for pump grants out of the deposit after June 2028.
The fuel fund itself stays in law with whatever balance it has, and lawmakers can still put money into it from the state's infrastructure fund, the way they have in most recent years.
What Iowa spends on ethanol and biodiesel now, and what's already ending Three state tax credits pay gas stations for each gallon they sell of gasoline with fifteen percent ethanol or more, short of flex fuel (section 422.11Y), of flex fuel (section 422.11O), and of diesel with at least eleven percent biodiesel (section 422.11P).
A credit bigger than the station's tax bill is paid out as a refund.
In the budget year that ended June 30, 2025, stations claimed $27.1 million for biodiesel, $17.6 million for the higher-ethanol blends and $5.5 million for flex fuel, $50.2 million together, according to the Department of Revenue .
The cost swings from year to year: the same report puts the three at $34.6 million in the budget year that ended June 2023, and $17.9 million the year after.
The department's forecast runs from $50.6 million to $57.9 million a year through the budget year that ends June 2029, including about $56 million in the one that ends June 30, 2027.
All three are repealed January 1, 2028, under current law.
That date isn't new.
Lawmakers moved the higher-ethanol credit's end date from January 1, 2026, to January 1, 2028, in Senate File 657 last year.
The cost won't stop on that day, because a station claims a year's credit on the next year's tax return.
The department expects $50.6 million in claims in the budget year that starts July 2028, all of it after the repeal and the same year this fund gets its first $4.5 million, and about $4.3 million the year after.
Biodiesel plants get a separate refund of the sales tax they pay, at four cents for every gallon they make in Iowa, up to a yearly cap for each plant (section 423.4).
It also ends January 1, 2028.
The Legislative Services Agency counts eight producers that get it.
We didn't find a published yearly total for it.
None of those end dates is this bill's doing, and this bill doesn't move them.
A statute can't stop a future Legislature from extending a credit, so the bill doesn't pretend to.
What it can do is start the plan before the credits end, set the money in law for the July after, and hand lawmakers a plan to follow by the end of that year.
The pump grants: in the eighteen budget years that ended in June 2024, the state awarded $53.5 million for 1,314 pump and tank projects, according to a Department of Revenue study built on Department of Agriculture records.

BE IT ENACTED BY THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY OF THE STATE OF IOWA: Section 1.
NEW SECTION.
Iowa ag diversification fund —— third-crop markets and pasture.
This section joins subchapter III of chapter 187.
1.
For purposes of this section, and notwithstanding section 187.102, unless the context otherwise requires: a.
“Agricultural producer” means a person who produces crops or livestock in this state. b.
“Division” means the division of soil conservation and water quality of the department. c.
“Financial assistance” means a grant made under subsection 5. d.
“First purchaser” means a person in this state that buys a third crop from the agricultural producer who grew it. e.
“Fund” means the Iowa ag diversification fund created in subsection 2. f.
“Grazing infrastructure” means any of the following, installed to manage livestock grazing on pasture in this state: (1) Permanent perimeter or interior fence, including fence that keeps livestock out of streams, ponds, or timber.
(2) Livestock watering facilities, including tanks, pipelines, wells, spring developments, and pumps that supply water to livestock on pasture. g.
“Grazing plan” means a written plan for grazing the pasture served by the grazing infrastructure that meets the prescribed grazing conservation practice standard of the natural resources conservation service of the United States department of agriculture in effect for this state. h.
“Small grain” means oats, rye, barley, or wheat. i.
“Specialty crop” means a crop, other than corn or soybeans, that is grown for human food. j.
“Third crop” means a small grain or specialty crop grown in this state.
2.
An Iowa ag diversification fund is created in the state treasury under the control of the department.
The fund shall consist of any moneys appropriated by the general assembly for purposes of this section and any other moneys that are lawfully available to the department.
Moneys in the fund are appropriated to the department to administer this section, and shall not be used for any other purpose.
Moneys in the choose Iowa fund created in section 187.201 shall not be used for the purposes of this section.
Notwithstanding section 12C.7, interest or earnings on moneys in the fund shall be credited to the fund.
Notwithstanding section 8.33, moneys in the fund that remain unencumbered or unobligated at the close of each fiscal year shall not revert but shall remain available for expenditure.
3.
There is appropriated from the general fund of the state to the Iowa ag diversification fund for the fiscal year beginning July 1, 2028, and for each fiscal year thereafter, the sum of four million five hundred thousand dollars.
4.
Moneys in the fund shall be used as follows: a.
The department may use not more than five percent of the moneys appropriated to the fund for a fiscal year for its administrative costs. b.
From any moneys in the fund, and subject to the availability of moneys in the fund, the department shall pay the cost of the plan required by subsection 7, which shall not exceed $250,000 in total.
The payment may include the cost of work performed under the contract required by subsection 7 on or after the effective date of this Act. c.
Of the moneys appropriated to the fund for a fiscal year that remain after paragraphs “a” and “b”, the department shall use not less than forty percent for the third-crop markets program under subsection 5, and shall transfer not less than forty percent to the division for grazing infrastructure under subsection 6.
The department shall allocate the rest between subsections 5 and 6 according to the demand for each. d.
Other moneys in the fund, including interest, earnings, and moneys that remain available from a previous fiscal year, shall be used for the purposes of paragraph “b” and subsections 5 and 6.
5. a.
The department shall establish a third-crop markets program to provide financial assistance for any of the following: (1) Buying, building, or installing equipment or facilities in this state to clean, dry, sort, store, dehull, mill, or otherwise process a third crop.
(2) Developing a market for a third crop as a first purchaser, including testing, receiving, contracting with agricultural producers, and marketing a product made from the third crop. b.
Any of the following is eligible for financial assistance under this subsection for a project in this state: (1) A cooperative.
(2) A first purchaser.
(3) A group of agricultural producers.
(4) Any other person. c.
Financial assistance under this subsection shall not exceed fifty percent of the cost of a project, or $250,000 for a project, whichever is less. d.
In awarding financial assistance under this subsection, the department shall give priority to both of the following: (1) A cooperative a majority of whose members are agricultural producers.
(2) A project that buys a third crop from agricultural producers who grow it in rotation with corn or soybeans, so that the third crop adds a crop to that rotation. e.
A person receiving financial assistance under this subsection shall sign an agreement with the department to report to the department, each year for three years after the award, each third crop it bought from agricultural producers in this state, the quantity of each, the number of agricultural producers it bought from, and the acres those producers report planting to supply it.
6. a.
Moneys transferred to the division under subsection 4 shall be used only as cost-share for establishing grazing infrastructure. b.
If a grazing infrastructure cost-share fund has been created in chapter 161A, the division shall deposit the moneys in that fund, and the moneys shall be used and administered in the same manner as other moneys in that fund, except that moneys deposited under this paragraph shall be used only for cost-share payments and shall not be used for, or counted in determining the amount available for, the costs of administering that fund. c.
If no fund described in paragraph “b” has been created, the division shall allocate the moneys as financial incentives under section 161A.73, subsection 1, paragraph “a”.
For purposes of moneys allocated under this paragraph, all of the following apply: (1) Grazing infrastructure is a permanent soil and water conservation practice.
Replacement of an existing fence is eligible only if the complete fence is replaced.
(2) A person receiving moneys shall sign an agreement with the division to maintain the grazing infrastructure and to graze the pasture it serves under a grazing plan for at least ten years.
A person who violates the agreement shall repair or replace the grazing infrastructure or repay the moneys received.
7. a.
By October 1, 2027, the department shall contract with Iowa state university of science and technology to prepare a ten-year Iowa agricultural diversification plan.
The plan shall do all of the following: (1) Identify the markets, storage, and processing needed for agricultural producers to grow third crops profitably in each region of this state, including southern Iowa.
(2) Identify ways to expand livestock grazing on pasture, including on land converted from row crops.
(3) Assess how the repeal of the tax credits provided in sections 422.11O, 422.11P, and 422.11Y and the corresponding credits provided in section 422.33, and of the refund provided in section 423.4, subsection 9, affects agricultural producers, retail dealers, and biofuel producers in this state.
(4) Recommend state policies and funding, with an estimated cost for each, and measurable goals for each of the ten years of the plan. b.
In preparing the plan, the university shall consult with agricultural producers, cooperatives, first purchasers, retail dealers, biofuel producers, the division, and soil and water conservation districts, and shall take public comment. c.
The university shall submit the plan to the governor and the general assembly by December 31, 2028.
The department shall publish the plan on the department’s internet site.
8.
By January 15 of each year, beginning January 15, 2030, the department shall submit a report to the general assembly, and publish it on the department’s internet site, stating all of the following for the previous fiscal year: a.
Each award of financial assistance under subsection 5, by recipient, county, third crop, and amount. b.
The information reported to the department under subsection 5, paragraph “e”. c.
The moneys transferred under subsection 6, and, as reported by the division for those moneys, by county, the number of cost-share agreements, the amount of cost-share moneys paid, the feet of fence and number of watering facilities established, and the acres of pasture served. d.
The moneys used under subsection 4, paragraphs “a” and “b”. e.
The balance of the fund at the close of the fiscal year.
9.
The department shall adopt rules pursuant to chapter 17A to implement and administer this section.

EXPLANATION The inclusion of this explanation does not constitute agreement with the explanation’s substance by the members of the general assembly.
This bill relates to agricultural diversification.
IOWA AG DIVERSIFICATION FUND.
The bill creates an Iowa ag diversification fund under the control of the department of agriculture and land stewardship (department), in the programs subchapter of Code chapter 187 (Choose Iowa Act), and appropriates four million five hundred thousand dollars to it from the general fund each fiscal year, beginning with the fiscal year that begins July 1, 2028.
The fund’s money may be used only for this program, and the choose Iowa fund under Code section 187.201 (choose Iowa fund) may not pay for it.
The department may use up to five percent of each year’s appropriation to run the fund, and pays for the plan described below from any money in the fund, when money is available, up to $250,000 in all, including costs under the plan’s contract after the bill takes effect.
Of the rest of each year’s appropriation, at least forty percent goes to third-crop markets and at least forty percent goes to cost-share for grazing infrastructure on pasture.
The department divides the remainder according to demand.
THIRD-CROP MARKETS.
The bill creates a third-crop markets program.
The department may award financial assistance to a cooperative, a first purchaser, a group of farmers, or any other person, for a project in this state, for equipment and facilities to clean, dry, sort, store, dehull, mill, or process oats, rye, barley, wheat, and crops other than corn and soybeans grown for human food, and for developing a market as the first buyer of those crops from the farmers who grow them.
An award may pay up to fifty percent of a project’s cost, up to $250,000 per project.
Priority goes to cooperatives whose members are mostly farmers and to projects that add a crop to a corn or soybean rotation.
A recipient reports for three years what it bought from Iowa farmers and from how many.
PASTURE.
The department transfers the pasture share to the division of soil conservation and water quality, to be used only as cost-share for permanent fence and livestock watering facilities on pasture.
If a grazing infrastructure cost-share fund has been created in Code chapter 161A, the money goes into that fund and follows its rules, except that none of it pays for running that fund.
If not, the division pays it as cost-share for permanent practices under Code section 161A.73 (voluntary establishment of soil and water conservation practices), treating grazing fence and livestock water as eligible practices for this money, and requires a ten-year agreement to maintain them and to graze the pasture under a written plan meeting the federal prescribed grazing standard.
TEN-YEAR PLAN.
By October 1, 2027, the department contracts with Iowa state university to prepare a ten-year Iowa agricultural diversification plan, covering the markets, storage, and processing third crops need in each region of the state, ways to expand grazing, how the scheduled repeal of the ethanol and biodiesel retail tax credits and the biodiesel production sales tax refund affects farmers, retailers, and biofuel producers, and recommended policies, costs, and goals.
The plan is due December 31, 2028.
ANNUAL REPORT.
Each January, the department reports every award, what recipients bought from Iowa farmers, the fence and watering facilities paid for by county, its administrative costs, and the fund’s balance.
RENEWABLE FUEL INFRASTRUCTURE FUND.
The bill strikes Code section 159A.17 (appropriation) and inserts a version that ends the standing general fund appropriation of five million dollars a year to the renewable fuel infrastructure fund after the fiscal year ending June 30, 2028.
Beginning with the fiscal year that begins July 1, 2028, it instead appropriates five hundred thousand dollars a year from the general fund to the department for motor fuel inspection, which recent appropriations have paid from the renewable fuel infrastructure fund.
With the four million five hundred thousand dollars a year to the Iowa ag diversification fund, the general fund appropriation stays five million dollars a year.
The renewable fuel infrastructure fund and its programs remain.
The bill does not change the ethanol and biodiesel retail tax credits or the biodiesel production refund, which current law repeals on January 1, 2028, or the requirement that retail dealers sell higher-ethanol gasoline.
The bill takes effect July 1, 2027.
