NEW SECTION.
Nutrient management acreage tax credit.
This section joins chapter 466B.
1.
For purposes of this section, unless the context otherwise requires: a.
“Certified nutrient management plan” means a written plan for the rate, source, timing, and placement of commercial fertilizer, manure, and other nutrients applied to agricultural land, that meets the nutrient management conservation practice standard of the natural resources conservation service of the United States department of agriculture in effect for this state, that is prepared or approved by a person qualified under rules adopted by the division, and that is reviewed and updated at least once each year.
For pastureland, “certified nutrient management plan” includes a written grazing plan that meets the prescribed grazing conservation practice standard of the natural resources conservation service in effect for this state. b.
“Division” means the division of soil conservation and water quality of the department of agriculture and land stewardship. c.
“Eligible person” means the person who carries out the certified nutrient management plan on an enrolled acre, whether that person is the owner or the operator of the acre. d.
“Enrolled acre” means an acre of cropland or pastureland in this state that is managed under a certified nutrient management plan for all of a tax year, and that the soil and water conservation district in which the acre is located has verified as provided in subsection 4.
2.
The taxes imposed under chapter 422, subchapters II and III, shall be reduced by a nutrient management acreage tax credit equal to $5 for each enrolled acre.
Only one eligible person may claim the credit for the same acre for the same tax year.
3.
An individual may claim a credit under this section of a partnership, limited liability company, S corporation, estate, or trust electing to have the income taxed directly to the individual.
The amount claimed by the individual shall be based upon the pro rata share of the individual’s earnings from the partnership, limited liability company, S corporation, estate, or trust.
Any credit in excess of the taxpayer’s tax liability is refundable.
In lieu of claiming a refund, the taxpayer may elect to have the overpayment credited to the tax liability for the following tax year.
4.
An eligible person shall submit the certified nutrient management plan, and a record of the nutrients applied under it during the tax year, to the soil and water conservation district in which the acres are located.
The district shall verify that the acres were managed under the plan and shall issue the eligible person a certificate stating the number of enrolled acres.
The eligible person shall attach the certificate to the tax return on which the credit is claimed.
The division may review any plan, record, or certificate.
5.
The division shall not require an eligible person, as a condition of the credit, to plant any particular crop, to follow any particular rotation or tillage system, or to establish any practice other than managing nutrients according to the eligible person’s own certified nutrient management plan.
6.
Receiving the credit does not make an acre ineligible for any other state or federal conservation payment, cost-share, or premium discount.
7.
If the division determines that an eligible person claimed a credit for an acre that was not managed under a certified nutrient management plan, the division may rescind the certificate.
The amount of any credit claimed under a rescinded certificate shall be a tax due and payable to the department of revenue.
8.
The division shall publish on its internet site, not later than January 15 of each year, the number of enrolled acres in each county and the total amount of credits certified for the previous tax year.
9.
The division and the department of revenue shall adopt rules pursuant to chapter 17A to administer this section.
10.
This section is repealed July 1, 2038.

EXPLANATION The inclusion of this explanation does not constitute agreement with the explanation’s substance by the members of the general assembly.
This bill relates to water quality.
NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT ACREAGE TAX CREDIT.
The bill creates a refundable income tax credit of $5 an acre for cropland or pasture managed for the whole year under a written nutrient management plan that meets the federal conservation standard, or, for pasture, a written grazing plan.
The person who carries out the plan, owner or renter, claims it, once per acre.
The local soil and water conservation district verifies the acres.
The state may not require any particular crop, rotation, tillage system or practice as a condition of the credit beyond following the farmer’s own plan.
The state publishes enrolled acres by county each year.
The credit applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2028, and ends July 1, 2038.
CONSERVATION EQUIPMENT LOANS.
The bill creates a revolving loan fund that makes zero-interest loans to farmers for equipment that reduces soil erosion or nutrient loss, including no-till and strip-till planters, cover crop seeders, variable-rate fertilizer and manure equipment, and guidance systems.
A borrower may have up to $150,000 outstanding, repaid over up to seven years.
The bill appropriates $20 million to start the fund.
Repayments go back into it.
CONSERVATION CONDITIONS.
A person who takes state conservation money on or after July 1, 2028, meaning the credit, an equipment loan, or state cost-share or other incentive money under the soil and water conservation chapter or the water quality initiative, must manage that land under a nutrient management plan: for the tax year of a credit, for the term of a loan, and for cost-share, for as long as the person agreed to maintain the practice, up to twenty years.
Money to build or restore a wetland, from the bill's wetlands fund or any other state source, does not carry the plan requirement.
The condition applies only to state money.
It does not touch federal farm payments, and the condition does not apply to a person who takes no state conservation money.
WETLANDS.
The bill creates a water quality wetlands fund, with $10 million a year, to help landowners build or restore wetlands that catch tile water and runoff.
Wetlands above lakes that supply drinking water come first.
MONITORING.
The bill appropriates $600,000 a year to the University of Iowa to run continuous in-stream water quality sensors and publish the readings as they come in.
CONFINEMENT FEEDING OPERATIONS.
The bill lets a county board of supervisors adopt a siting ordinance for confinement feeding operation structures that need a state construction permit, which generally means operations of 1,000 animal units or more, or an operation of any size building an earthen manure basin or lagoon.
Confinement means totally roofed, so cattle on pasture and open feedlots are not covered.
In a county that does, the board must approve the location as well, may attach conditions and set larger separation distances, and must decide within ninety days after a public hearing.
Until a county has either adopted an ordinance or voted not to, the department of natural resources may not approve new construction permits there, except for work that does not add capacity and applications already complete when the bill takes effect.
That pause ends statewide July 1, 2029.
The bill is subject to the pay-as-you-go scorecard, which a separate bill would create.
In any fiscal year, the bill’s cost to the general fund is limited to the savings the scorecard counts against it.
The bill takes effect upon enactment.
