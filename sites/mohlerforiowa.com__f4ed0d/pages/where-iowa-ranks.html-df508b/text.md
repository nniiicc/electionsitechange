Act Four Who Benefited This section holds itself to one standard: documents.
Every entry below is a position an organization registered itself, in the Iowa Legislature's own published lobbyist declarations, on a specific bill, on a specific date.
Nothing here says any organization caused any outcome.
That claim is not made, and the underlying database is built so it cannot be made: an entry can only exist if it is a registered lobbyist declaration, a documented state contract or award, a recorded campaign contribution, a market share figure from a government source, or a figure from an SEC filing.
"This industry did well while that number got worse" is not evidence, so it is stored and never shown.
Which is why the profit figures below are not the story you might expect.
Where a company files with the SEC, its reported net income is shown, taken from its own Form 10-K.
Two of the three earned less in 2025 than in 2021.
Corteva went from $1.76 billion to $1.09 billion.
Elevance went from $6.16 billion to $5.66 billion.
If the argument here were that these companies got rich while Iowa got worse, their own filings would refute it.
The argument is narrower than that, and it holds: these are the organizations that showed up, in writing, on the bills.

The measurement: Of the 65 Iowa counties that had hospital maternity care in 2010, 20 do not now, the worst rate of any state : 30.8 percent of our counties against 16.8 percent nationally.
Iowa has the second fewest OB-GYNs per woman of reproductive age and fewer doctors per person than all but eight states .
Rural Iowa is in an access crisis, and counting buildings will hide it from you.
Iowa has lost two rural hospitals since 2005, out of 197 closures and conversions nationally, and on rural health clinics Iowa is doing better than the country: 15.4 percent of the 2015 clinics have closed here against 25.7 percent nationally.
Anyone claiming the buildings are vanishing can be proved wrong with one search, and that is exactly how this gets waved off.
The buildings stayed.
The care left.
Count the services instead of the buildings and the picture flips.
Iowa hospitals closed 41 birthing units between January 2000 and December 2021 .
Of the 76 counties that ever had one, 23 were left with nowhere in the county to have a baby, and of the 65 counties that still had hospital maternity care in 2010, 20 have lost it since, the worst rate of any state .
Today 34 of Iowa's 94 rural hospitals still deliver babies.
Sixty do not , and six of those units have closed since 2020.
That is how a state with its hospitals still standing ends up last in the country on counties losing hospital-based obstetric care, second to last of 51 on OB-GYNs , and dead last of 51 on state psychiatric beds .
In this district and right next door, four clinics have gone since January.
An open front door you cannot get an appointment behind is not access.
Two things I will not overstate here.
The state's own report says the 41 include a few closures of an entire hospital, not only of the unit inside it, so that number is not purely service lines.
And you may have seen a figure going around that 842 Iowa health care entities closed against 468 opening.
That one comes from the state licensing database, it counts license holders rather than services, and about a quarter of the closures are a single day in May 2021 when a batch of staffing-agency records was cleared out.
It is not the number that proves this.
The birthing units are.
Iowa Healthy Water Act Pay farmers to lead it.
A tax credit for every acre under a real nutrient management plan, zero percent loans for precision equipment that already reduces runoff, and batch-and-build wetlands and saturated buffers.
The measurement: 72.6 percent of Iowa's assessed river miles are impaired , against 60.4 percent nationally.
Central Iowa Water Works banned lawn watering in 2025 and again in 2026 because of nitrate.
I am not going to tell you nitrate is causing Iowa's cancer rate.
I do not know that, and neither does anyone else yet.
Iowa is not an outlier on the cancers usually linked to nitrate: 8th on colorectal, 7th on bladder, and 28th on ovarian, which is below the national rate.
A large Danish study found elevated colorectal risk below the current standard.
A systematic review found no association.
That is a contested question, not a settled one.
The Iowa Cancer Registry also reports that Iowa farmers, the people most exposed to farm chemicals, had 13 percent fewer cancers than Iowans generally.
So the ask is measurement, not blame.
The state put $1 million into asking why Iowa's cancer rate is rising.
Phase one found no single cause, and it did not examine environmental or genetic factors at all.
That is the gap.
Fund phase two, and keep the instruments that measure our water running, because the Legislature just stopped guaranteeing them.
You cannot manage what you refuse to measure.
