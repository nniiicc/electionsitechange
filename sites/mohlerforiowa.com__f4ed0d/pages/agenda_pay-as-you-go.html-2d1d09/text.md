NEW SUBSECTION. 111. a.
For purposes of this subsection, unless the context otherwise requires:
(1) “Affiliate” and “control” mean the same as defined in subsection 92, paragraph “e”.
(2) “Business” means a web search portal business that claims an exemption under subsection 92 or 93, or a data center business that claims an exemption under subsection 95.
A purchase for a location by an affiliate that claims an exemption under subsection 92, paragraph “c”, or subsection 93, paragraph “c”, is a purchase by the business for that location.
(3) “Certificate period” means the period from February 1 of a calendar year through January 31 of the following calendar year.
(4) “Exemptions” means the exemptions provided in paragraph “a” of subsections 92, 93, and 95, for computers and equipment, for backup power generation fuel, and for electricity.
(5) “Location” means a physical location in this state for which a business purchases property or electricity for which the exemptions are claimed.
Buildings on contiguous land owned or leased by a business or its affiliates are one location.
A location begins operating in the first calendar year for which its megawatt-hours are more than zero.
(6) “Megawatt-hours” means, for a location and a calendar year, the kilowatt-hours of electricity purchased in that calendar year for use at the location in the operation of the business’s web search portal or data center, divided by one thousand.
Megawatt-hours include electricity purchased by the business, by its affiliate, or by a lessor that supplies the electricity to the business.
Electricity is measured by the electric utility’s meter.
Electricity a lessor supplies is measured by the lessor’s meter for the space the business leases or, if that space is not separately metered, is the electricity measured by the electric utility’s meter for the lessor’s building times the business’s share of the information technology power capacity that all tenants of the building have contracted for, as certified by the lessor.
Megawatt-hours do not include electricity the business supplies to a lessee that files its own annual report under subsection 92, 93, or 95 for the space it leases.
For purposes of this subsection, electricity is purchased on the last day of the billing period for which it is billed.
(7) “Payment rate” means $10 for each megawatt-hour for calendar year 2028.
For each later calendar year, the payment rate is the payment rate for the preceding calendar year increased by the percentage, if any, by which the annual average of the consumer price index for all urban consumers, United States city average, all items, published by the bureau of labor statistics of the United States department of labor, for the preceding calendar year exceeds that annual average for the calendar year before that, with the resulting payment rate rounded to the nearest cent.
If the bureau stops publishing that index, the payment rate for each later calendar year is the payment rate for the last calendar year for which the index was published.
The payment rate for a calendar year shall not be less than the payment rate for the preceding calendar year.
The department shall publish the payment rate for each calendar year on its internet site by March 1 of that calendar year.
b.
A business may claim the exemptions for purchases for a location made on or after January 1, 2028, only under an exemption certificate for that location issued under paragraph “d” that is valid on the date of the purchase, and only on the condition that the business makes the Iowa future fund payment for that location for each calendar year in which it claims the exemptions for purchases for that location.
c.
The Iowa future fund payment for a location for a calendar year in which a business claims the exemptions for purchases for that location equals the payment rate for that calendar year times the location’s megawatt-hours for that calendar year.
The payment is due on or before January 31 of the following calendar year, with the annual report for that location.
d.
(1) The department shall issue each exemption certificate for a location for a certificate period.
A certificate is valid for purchases made from the first day of its certificate period, or from the date it is issued if that is later, through the last day of its certificate period.
If a certificate is issued after the first day of its certificate period, the exemption for electricity under it applies only to electricity billed for a billing period that begins on or after the date it is issued.
(2) The department shall issue the certificate for a location for the certificate period that begins February 1 of a calendar year after the business files the annual report for that location due January 31 of that calendar year and pays the Iowa future fund payment for that location for the preceding calendar year, if one is due, or, if it did not pay it, the tax due under paragraph “e” for that location.
(3) For a location that has not been the subject of an annual report, the department shall issue the certificate when the business registers the location with the department.
That certificate is valid from the date it is issued through the last day of the certificate period that includes that date.
(4) An exemption certificate issued to a business under subsection 92, 93, or 95, paragraph “b”, subparagraph (6), before January 1, 2028, is a certificate for each location of that business issued under this paragraph and is valid for purchases made through January 31, 2028.
e.
If a business does not pay the Iowa future fund payment for a location for a calendar year by its due date, the department shall send the business notice.
If the business does not pay it within sixty days after the date of the notice, all of the following apply:
(1) The exemptions do not apply to purchases for that location made during that calendar year.
(2) If that calendar year is the calendar year in which the location began operating or the calendar year after it, the exemptions also do not apply to purchases for that location made during any calendar year before the calendar year in which the location began operating, beginning on or after January 1, 2028.
(3) The business shall pay the sales and use tax that would have been due on the purchases described in subparagraphs (1) and (2) had the exemptions not been claimed, with interest under section 421.7 from the date the tax would have been due.
The tax is due sixty days after the date of the department’s notice of the amount due, and if it is not paid by then, it is collected in the same manner as other delinquent sales and use taxes under this chapter and chapter 421.
(4) Notwithstanding section 423.37, the department may assess and determine the tax due under this paragraph within three years after the due date of the Iowa future fund payment that was not paid or, for a shortfall under paragraph “f”, within one year after the date of the notice of the shortfall, whichever is later.
f.
The department may examine the records of a business and its affiliates to verify the megawatt-hours reported for a location.
A business shall obtain and keep its lessor’s meter records, and any certification by its lessor under paragraph “a”, subparagraph (6), for electricity a lessor supplies to it.
If the department determines, within three years after the due date of the Iowa future fund payment for a location for a calendar year, that the business paid less than the payment due, it shall send the business notice of the shortfall.
If the business pays the shortfall, with interest under section 421.7 from the payment’s due date, within sixty days after the date of the notice, the payment is treated as paid when due.
If it does not, paragraph “e”, subparagraphs (1) through (4), apply as if the payment had not been made, and the amount the business paid for that location for that calendar year is credited against the tax due.
g.
This subsection does not impose a tax.
A business is not required to make an Iowa future fund payment, and no penalty or interest applies to a payment that is not made.
A business that does not make the payment for a location for a calendar year may not claim the exemptions for purchases for that location during that calendar year, and owes sales and use tax on those purchases as provided in paragraph “e”.
h.
The annual report for a location, and information about a business’s Iowa future fund payments, are confidential in the same manner as returns and return information filed under this chapter.
i.
The department shall deposit each Iowa future fund payment, and each shortfall and its interest paid under paragraph “f”, as follows: the general fund share for the calendar year for which the payment is made in the general fund of the state, and the remainder in the Iowa future fund.
The general fund share is:
(1) For calendar year 2028, 75 percent.
(2) For each calendar year after 2028 and before 2033, the general fund share for the preceding calendar year reduced by five percentage points.
(3) For calendar year 2033 and each later calendar year, half.
j.
Sales and use tax revenues collected because a business did not make a payment under this subsection, including tax due under paragraph “e”, shall be deposited in the general fund of the state and are not revenues attributable to the expiration of an exemption period for purposes of section 423.2A, subsection 2, paragraph “h”, or section 423.43, subsection 3.
However, revenues that the business would have owed had it made the payment remain subject to those provisions.
k.
By March 31 of each year, the department shall publish on its internet site, for the preceding calendar year, the number of locations for which payments were made, the total megawatt-hours on which they were made, the total of the payments, and the number of locations for which no payment was made.
The department shall publish these figures only in the aggregate, and shall not publish a figure that would disclose information about an individual business.
l.
The department shall adopt rules pursuant to chapter 17A to administer this subsection.
