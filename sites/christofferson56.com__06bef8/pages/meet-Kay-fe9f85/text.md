Experience That Gets Things Done.
Kay Christofferson has spent his career solving real problems, building strong communities and working to keep Utah a place where families can thrive.
A Builder Before a Politician
A civil engineer by training, Kay built his career in the heavy civil construction industry, helping lead companies responsible for the roads, infrastructure and projects communities depend on every day.
That experience taught him to approach problems practically: understand what needs to be done, plan carefully, use resources wisely and deliver results.
Kay brings that same mindset to public service.
Whether the issue is transportation, taxes, water or government spending, he believes solutions should be practical, responsible and built to last.
Solve Problems.
Don't Create New Ones.
Kay believes good government should focus on practical solutions, respect taxpayers and avoid unnecessary complexity.
Listen
Start by understanding the problem and listening to the people who are actually affected by it.
Plan
Look beyond the immediate issue and consider long-term costs, consequences and practical options.
Solve
Find the approach that works, uses resources responsibly and delivers a real result for Utahns.
Utah Is Home
For Kay, the decisions made today are about the Utah his children and grandchildren will inherit.
Kay and his wife, Suzanne, have built their life and raised their family here in Utah.
They are the parents of seven children and grandparents of sixteen.
Family is at the center of why Kay cares so deeply about keeping Utah affordable, protecting its quality of life and preserving the opportunities that have made this state such a special place to live.
His goal is simple: leave Utah stronger for the next generation.
Listen.
Work.
Solve.
Kay believes public service should be about results, not titles.
That means listening to constituents, working through difficult issues and finding solutions that make a real difference.
He focuses on the issues Utah families feel every day: keeping taxes low, improving transportation, protecting water, strengthening families and keeping government limited and accountable.
