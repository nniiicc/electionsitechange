Housing Property Tax SB24-233 The property tax bill adopted by the legislature exempts TABOR-limited counties such as Arapahoe County from the growth cap and requires our assessor to implement the assessment rate reductions and exemptions for residential and commercial properties.
It is anticipated that this will result in slower growth and/or decreases in property valuations.
In 2026 and beyond, the bill would reduce property taxes by seven to 10 percent compared to current law for homes valued at $700,000 or less.
Annual savings would equal $300-$400 for a home valued at $700,000.
Savings would depend on local mills and any changes in assessed home values.
SB24-233 would also reduce the commercial property assessment rate to 25 percent by 2027, down from 29 percent.
So the state of Colorado has answered the call to reduce property taxes!
However, this means that Arapahoe County's budget will be impacted to the point that our voters will have to make some determinations in our upcoming elections about how to proceed.
Arapahoe County is already facing an immediate budget shortfall.
Without new funding by 2025, it will not be possible to maintain essential services at the same level our residents deserve and have come to expect.
Services across all County departments will be impacted; County Commissioners are seeking your input on various alternatives and would like to hear your opinion before making a decision.
We would like to encourage you to look at the budget of Arapahoe County here, www.acbudget.com .
Saving Seniors Money on Housing New legislation will expand access to the senior homestead property tax exemption to reduce property taxes for older Coloradans, by allowing them to still claim the exemption if they move or downsize their homes, which was previously not allowed ( SB24-111 ).
Colorado Democrats also created a new tax credit for older Coloradans who rent their homes that will save seniors money on housing ( HB24-1052 ).
Education Eliminating the Budget Stabilization Factor We eliminated the BS Factor!
The funding for our public schools has been stuck at an extremely low level, this year in fact we raised the funding for schools and it brought it to 1989 levels, this is something that I will work on moving forward, bringing it to where it should be at (a 2024 level.) The 2024 state budget ( HB24-1430 ) and School Finance Act ( SB24-188 ) provide historic funding for K-12 public schools by eliminating the Budget Stabilization Factor, which will increase total funding for schools by over $560 million and average per-pupil funding by $780 next year.
These new funds can be used by districts to reduce class sizes, increase teacher pay and help ensure students have the resources they need to thrive.
Fully Funding Special Education For the first time, Colorado will fully fund special education to better support students with disabilities, increasing this funding by over 10 percent, or $34.7 million, and bringing the state’s current investment in special education funding to $375.5 million ( HB24-1430 ).
Starting in 2025, a modernized, student-centered school funding formula ( HB24-1448 ) will further increase funding for special education by an additional $375 million and will drive those new dollars to the districts that serve more special education students.
Environment Improving Air Quality and Reducing Pollution To help reduce ozone air pollution in the Denver metro area and northern Front Range, Colorado Democrats are tightening pollution standards in permitting for oil and gas operations, especially in disproportionately impacted communities, and ramping up penalties for repeat violators ( SB24-229 ).
Other laws will reduce aviation-caused air pollution ( HB24-1235 ), and develop neighborhood-scale alternative energy projects to reduce the cost of using natural gas ( HB24-1370 ).
Protecting Colorado Water Water is Colorado’s most precious resource, which is why lawmakers are restoring critical protections for Colorado’s streams, rivers, and wetlands ( HB24-1379 ) and studying new and modernized ways to achieve compliance with water quality standards, specifically green infrastructure ( SB24-037 ).
Additionally, local governments will soon authorize the use of greywater in new construction to promote water reuse ( HB24-1362 ).
Gun Violence Protection Enforcing Federal and State Gun Laws Colorado Democrats passed legislation to give the Colorado Bureau of Investigation (CBI) tools to investigate illegal activity involving firearms ( SB24-003 ).
Those who hold a federal firearms dealer license will also be required to acquire a new state permit, providing the state a new regulatory framework that will crack down on illegal firearm sales and reduce gun violence ( HB24-1353 ).
Promoting Responsible Firearm Ownership Democrats passed legislation to require people seeking a concealed carry permit to attend an in-person handgun training class on safe handling and storage of firearms and ammunition.
The bill also requires people renewing a concealed carry permit to take a refresher class ( HB24-1174 ).
Colorado Democrats also passed legislation that requires firearms in unattended vehicles to be stored in a locked container in a locked vehicle, or in a locked trunk of the vehicle. ( HB24-1348 ).
Healthcare Bolstering Colorado’s Health Care Workforce With bipartisan support, lawmakers funded four projects to support health care and veterinary care at institutions across the state, including establishing a new medical college at the University of Northern Colorado (UNC) ( HB24-1231 ).
Marking the state’s largest higher education investment to bolster the health care workforce, the new law funds: Construction of a new College of Osteopathic Medicine at UNC; Construction of the Health Institute Tower at Metropolitan State University of Denver; Construction of the Veterinary Health Education Complex at Colorado State University; and Renovation of the Valley Campus Main Building at Trinidad State College.
Increasing Prescription Drug Affordability & Improving Accessibility Colorado Democrats built on previous legislation to make updates to insulin and epi-pen affordability programs to better enforce compliance with existing affordability mechanisms and disseminate information at pharmacies ( HB24-1438 ).
New legislation also protects access to provider-administered prescription drugs by breaking down unnecessary barriers, including added fees, that make it difficult to access life-saving medication ( HB24-1010 ).
Lawmakers made prescription drug labels more accessible by requiring pharmacies to provide blind, visually impaired and print-impaired Coloradans with access to prescription drug label information ( HB24-1115 ).
Lawmakers also improved prior authorization requirements to ensure Coloradans have better access to prescription drugs ( HB24-1149 , SB24-110 ).
Supporting Workers Protecting Coloradans in the Workplace With work in legislation like my work on my TRAP bill ( HB24-1324 ), we have moved forward in Colorado to pass legislation to protect workers in Colorado.
We have also increased transparency for transportation network drivers like those for Uber and Lyft and delivery drivers for companies like Grubhub and Doordash ( HB24-1129 ; SB24-075 ), cracking down on child labor violations ( HB24-1095 ) and ensuring better safety protocols for freight rail ( HB24-1030 ).
The legislature also passed bills to combat wage theft in the construction industry ( HB24-1008 ), protect workers from attending captive audience meetings ( HB24-1260 ), increase worker compensation benefits and eligibility ( HB24-1220 ) and promote educator safety ( HB24-1320 ).
Workforce & Higher Ed Supporting the Educator Workforce This year, the legislature created a task force to examine educator safety and address critical issues impacting educator retention and recruitment ( HB24-1320 ).
The legislature also created a new program to help school districts recruit and retain new teachers with an educator stipend ( HB24-1290 ).
Helping Coloradans Fill Critical Jobs in Growing Industries The legislature passed new laws to make it easier for Coloradans to find and enter degree programs in construction, infrastructure, and building trades to fill jobs in fields with critical shortages ( HB24-1365 ).
We also passed laws to study new pathways to degree programs in high-demand industries ( HB24-1364 ).
A new tax credit will reimburse employers for the costs of bringing on apprentices, expanding opportunities for student-workers ( HB24-1439 ).

Bills That I've Had Nonstop Attention On TRAPs ( HB24-1324 ) - TRAP agreements are typically used for higher-skilled professions; medical careers, lawyers, engineers, pilots, etc., and typically involve a payment plan or period of time to work with an employer to satisfy repayment for training that is paid for by an employer (basically a student loan provided by an employer).
However, it is becoming increasingly common for employers in low wage industries to use this type of agreement to “TRAP” their employees in unfavorable employment positions, requiring large payments to the employer if the employee leaves for another job.
This bill standardizes the process for recovering these expenses, treating them similarly to other forms of consumer and student debt.
Colorado Disability Opportunity Office (CDOO) ( HB24-1360 ) - Rep.
David Ortiz, and Sen.
Robert Rodriguez and I have created CDOO, People with disabilities have statistically higher rates of poverty and employment, which is why this legislation is crucial in helping Coloradans with disability to build a successful life for themselves and their families.
CDOO is a an office that will create a statewide strategy to facilitate economic stability for people with disabilities and promote successful economic, social, and community integration.
Repeal CBI Criminal Justice Record Sealing Fee - ( HB24-1432 ) I have passed this bill with Rep.
Soper and Sen.
Michaelson Jenet, to create an easier path for sealing records, the majority of people who have requested to seal certain criminal history items from their records often are unaware of the required $20 processing fee, causing more time for staff to attempt to collect these funds than it would take to process the request.
HB24-1432 removes this fee, making it easier for people with a past criminal record to move forward.
Airports ( HB24-1235 ) - Working with Rep.
Brown on this bill, it addresses the need for change with the smaller airports that are growing along side Colorado’s population growth, airports like ours in Centennial are seeing more traffic than ever before, this leads to increased noise and air pollution in our neighborhoods.
This bill ensures communities are shielded from aviation noise and lead pollution by easing and accelerating the transition to unleaded fuels for aircraft owners and small regional airports.
State Employee Deductions Bill ( HB24-1293 ) - This bill will give state employees the ability to negotiate voluntary and flexible benefits.
