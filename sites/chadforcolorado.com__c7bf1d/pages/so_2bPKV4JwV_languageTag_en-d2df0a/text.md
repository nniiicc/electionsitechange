HB25-1017 , Community Integration Plan Individuals with Disabilities if passed will ensure Colorado provides access for Coloradans with disabilities to live and receive treatment in integrated community settings if it is appropriate for their needs, versus institutionalization.
The disability community came to me with a fear that if the US Supreme Court were to overturn their Olmstead v LC decision, longstanding protections could be at risk and cause issues for people with disabilities.
This bill codifies these protections and allows for the new Colorado Disability Opportunity Office to create plans that will ensure that we provide the best chance possible for people to live their lives side by side with differently abled people.
I am always reminded that I'm only potentially one accident away from being disabled and that if any of us live long enough we will likely have some form of disability.
HB25-1031 , Law Enforcement Whistleblower Protection will give peace officers protections from retaliation by their employing agency for reporting a violation of law or policy.
Last Session, a group of peace officers who faced retaliation came to the Legislature asking for help.
Since then, we have had many officers come forward to speak up about their own experiences with adverse actions taken against them based on them reporting violations of the law inside their departments.
Under the current bill, a peace officer can seek damages for emotional and financial harm, attorney fees, reinstatement, and potentially back pay.
Likewise, it provides legitimate protections to the employers so they can defend themselves if their actions towards the peace officer were not retaliatory.
The statute of limitations to bring the action is 2 years.
The overarching goal of the goals coming out of the Law Enforcement Working Group is to better protect the people who protect us by providing transparency in protection in the workplace.
HB25-1136 , Peace Officer Conduct Database is another bill stemming from the Law Enforcement Working Group.
It will enable the Peace Officer Standards and Training (P.O.S.T.) Board to get the necessary information to fully review the investigation files that could result in an officer being placed in the Peace Officer Misconduct database that was created in 2020.
This bill builds on the policies implemented in SB20-217 and HB21-1250 by further clarifying and improving the P.O.S.T.
Board’s ability to collect data and work with peace officers to correct any items that get reported inaccurately.
HB25-1095 , Petitioner Requirements in Certain Property Tax Appeals mandates that property tax appeal valuations for non-agricultural and nonresidential properties follow uniform appraisal standards at the BAA Appeals level.
This bill aims to clarify documentation requirements for petitioners looking to appeal commercial property valuations.
HB25-1181 , Colorado Rangers Law Enforcement Shared Reserve gives the Board of the Colorado Rangers the authority to adopt payment language to police officers utilized by their agency in emergencies such as wildfires or whenever they support relief efforts out of state as part of Colorado's Multistate Compact Agreements.
The bill also authorizes the Board to accept equipment and grant funds from partner agencies and cleans up statutes from "Colorado Mounted Rangers" to "Colorado Rangers" (which haven't been updated in C.R.S. since their change from a volunteer organization to a government entity in 2018).
HB25-1214 , Appropriate Use of Prison Beds expands and improves the process of parole for inmates who are considered low, or very low risk.
This bill would expand the treatment and recovery options for parolees, including certified recovery residences.
It would also remove a rule that requires people to pay for the treatment programs mandated as conditions of their parole.
Ultimately, t he primary aim of this bill is to provide low-risk inmates with improved treatment and community resources, helping them reintegrate more successfully into society upon release SB25-035 , Limitation of Actions Against Appraisers sets a five-year statute of limitations for filing claims against real estate appraisers, with the exception of a consumer who was the original party to the transaction or a mortgage originator where the origination process forms the basis of the action.
Nowadays, it is almost always a bank who orders an appraisal -- and most appraisers are local, small businesses.
While it isn't happening now, it is predictable that in the next economic downturn banks may try to shift responsibility onto the individual appraisers.
The five-year limit begins from the date the appraisal report is completed and is sent to the client.
However, there are exceptions; cases involving fraud, misrepresentation, and discrimination housing practices will not be subject to the five-year limit.
This is a common sense way of protecting our individual appraisers from future liability over forces largely outside of their control.
SB25-064 , Safe2Tell Handle with Care Notice requires the Safe2Tell program to notify schools when a student has experienced a traumatic event.
If a peace officer responds to the event they are required to report the child’s name and age to Safe2Tell who will then send a notice to the school.
The notice will only include the child’s name and the phrase “handle with care.” Schools can then share it with relevant staff but should not take any further action unless necessary.
Education and the safety of our children are paramount, and this bill helps strengthen the relationship with schools and local law enforcement.
SB25-155 , Legislation Inside Advisory Council creates an advisory council to help identify, examine, and discuss issues, interests, and needs affecting people who are incarcerated.
I have been invited to Legislation Inside multiple times and I am consistently impressed by their organization and advocacy.
The new council will be required to contract with a nonprofit organization to help assist in its work.
Starting 2027, the council will be required to report to the Judiciary Committees in both chambers.

Immigration / ICE / The Federal Government I have been alarmed by some actions taken by the Presidential Administration, and primarily that hateful rhetoric targeting immigrants makes our communities less safe.
Immigration is an issue primarily determined and administered at the federal level, and Colorado has been careful to make it clear where these jurisdictional lines exist to protect the rights of our citizens and residents.
Our office has been working in close partnership with our federal delegation (Congressman Jason Crow and Senators Michael Bennet and John Hickenlooper).
I am standing in solidarity with community organizations and individuals who have been impacted, and working closely with members of the Colorado Democratic Latino Caucus to advocate on behalf of Latino communities across Colorado.
We always have concerns about family separation as well as the safety and well-being of all residents.
Previous Legislation that provides clarity and protections for both our residents as well as to those who enforce laws in Colorado: HB19-1124: The act prohibits a law enforcement officer from arresting or detaining an individual solely based on a civil immigration detainer.
A probation officer or probation department employee is prohibited from providing an individual's personal information to federal immigration authorities.
SB20-083: The act protects an individual from civil arrest while the person is present at a courthouse or in its environs, or while going to, attending, or coming from a court proceeding.
SB21-131: The act specifies measures in several categories to protect personal identifying information (PII) kept by state agencies.

Bills My Intern Thinks Are Interesting HB25-1041 Student Athlete Name Image or Likeness (NIL) This bill allows Colorado schools to stay competitive with other states that hold similar NIL policies, by allowing student athletes, including those who might play college sports in the future, to get compensated for the use of their name, image, or likeness.
It will grant colleges and athletic organizations the ability to directly pay student athletes for NIL deals.
Additionally, it would repeal the current restriction that prevents student athletes from entering into contracts that conflict with their team contracts.
Lastly, NIL related contracts and discussions will be kept private, ensuring that they won't be shared under the Colorado Open Records Act.
This bill will allow Colorado student athletes more financial opportunities and control over their NIL, which is a privilege that other states have already granted their athletes.
HB25-1250 Gun Violence Prevention & Parents of Students Under this bill, the Office of Gun Violence Prevention within the Department of Public Health and Environment must provide gun prevention materials on it’s website.
These resources will be available to all, but the bill aims to place these resources in the hands of local education providers, parents, and guardians of elementary and secondary school students.
Schools will be responsible for sharing the information with families in a written or electronic form at the beginning of each school year.
In Colorado, we want to ensure that we encourage safe gun ownership, especially in homes with children.
