Highlights of My Week at the Capitol We have had a great week at the Capitol as we wrap up the last 9 days of this legislative session.
Here is a recap of some of the bills that we engaged last week....
HB24-1449 - This bill is all about cleaning up waste management in Colorado.
It gets rid of old committees and programs and replaces them with a new one called the Colorado Circular Communities Enterprise, which helps local governments, businesses, schools, and colleges to reduce waste and recycle more.
Trash dump operators now have to pay fees to this new program instead of the old ones, with different rates depending on where they are and what they're dumping.
Overall, it's a push to make Colorado cleaner and greener.
HB24-1052 - This bill focuses on tax policy changes and reinstates a refundable income tax credit for qualifying seniors for the 2024 tax year.
To qualify, individuals must be 65 or older, meet certain income criteria, and not have claimed a senior property tax exemption for 2024.
The credit amount varies based on filing status and income, with reductions for higher incomes.
Additionally, seniors who qualify for certain assistance grants can receive the full credit amount.
The bill also mandates reports from counties regarding taxpayers eligible for and claiming the homestead property tax exemption.
HB24-1157 - The bill establishes the Employee Ownership Office as a permanent part of the Office of Economic Development and International Trade (OEDIT).
It would also introduce a refundable income tax credit for the years 2025 to 2029, covering up to 50% of specified costs incurred by new employee-owned businesses, with a maximum limit of $50,000.
The businesses that qualify would be those owned by employees for seven years or less.
The Employee Ownership Office will oversee the administration of these tax credits.
To kickstart operations, the Governor's Office for the Employee Ownership Office appropriated some funds to start the next fiscal year.
Let's Talk about Housing: This week's housing conversation will be about housing density: Why we need it and why we need local governments to manage where it goes and how it's used.
Did you know that Greenwood Village has more dense housing than any city in the state EXCEPT for Glendale (which will always keep the #1 spot since their city has all apartment buildings and 1 house)?
If you engage the City Councils or Mayors of Centennial or Greenwood Village, they will tell you that housing density is needed - but where it's needed is the key to effectively managing housing.
The idea that we can put heavy density along transit areas is not a new idea - this has worked effectively in many communities across the United States and there are some areas of our state where building this organically may work.
It has all the fun talking points "environmentally friendly", "encourages use of public transit", "increases supply which reduces housing costs".
These talking points are interesting, but in HD37, they just aren't true.
Our transit isn't currently capable of handling the influx of riders, our roads certainly can't effectively manage traffic if we put this many additional cars concentrated in these areas, and we have more need for housing than we can build - so these measures won't reduce housing prices.
I have had at least 30 meetings regarding "Transit Oriented Communities", including meetings where I facilitated our Mayors and City Councils of both Centennial and Greenwood Village to meet with the Governor's team about these proposals.
We looked at land use issues in at least 6 areas that would be designated as "TOC's" and we couldn't make the plan fit on any of them.
It was one of the best exercises to demonstrate that local governments must have a say in these decisions, because in these meetings the local representatives knew the land and knew the issues that would get in the way of building.
At no point in time did I experience unwillingness about dense housing in these conversations - only WHERE this housing would go.
We do need dense housing - preferably ownable housing like condominiums or townhomes.
We need this type of housing to adjust our housing inventory so that we have a "middle" option of housing that is more affordable for new home buyers as well as homes to downsize into.
If you raised a family here, it's not predictable that most children will be able to stay in our community when they set out on their adult pathways - likewise, when you get older and the 5 bedroom house is now too much, you'll have a hard time finding something affordable to downsize into.
We do need to address the middle, but we still need to follow efficient planning for where this housing goes.
In HD37, we're working with mostly land that has been developed for decades.
This is not a matter of "lets build something new", it's more a matter of "lets build something new that can fit our existing infrastructure".
Local governments are sensitive to many of the issues that overarching "fits all" approaches can't.
Can we handle additional traffic at Belleview and I25?
Do we have sufficient water and sewage resources at County Line?
There are thousands of details that need to be addressed, and sometimes by multiple local governments - fire protection districts, water districts, school districts, etc.
So while I appreciate the interest in Transit Oriented Communities - in order for this to work, we need to tend to transit and transportation infrastructure first and focus on putting housing where it makes sense to do so.
In fact, we could even get transit directed to areas that we build more dense housing - but for HD37, the current proposal just doesn't work for so so many reasons.
(Plus it takes almost 50% of the Affordable Housing Grant Funds that are already being utilized in our community and moves them to a program that we likely can't participate).
Bills That I Have Nonstop Attention On TRAPs ( HB24-1324 ) - This bill has passed the House & Senate and is being sent to the Governor.
This bill gives the Attorney General the ability to go after employers that use training repayment agreements as traps to force low wage workers to stay in unfavorable employment positions by having to repay the employer for training that doesn't fit under these types of agreements.
There are many employers who utilize these arrangements effectively, so we had to very carefully protect the appropriate use while carving out the bad actors.
Construction Defects Liability Reform ( SB24-106 ) - This bill has passed the Senate and is now in the House!
As most of you have probably read in previous newsletters this is huge.
If passed through the House it will give homeowners the option of requiring the contractor that caused a construction defect a way to remedy the defect without first going to litigation.
I will be continuing this bill's work throughout the House as we try to limit lawsuits from construction defects, as they can sometimes overcomplicate and bring up some hidden surprises, and focus on “if it’s broken, we should make the builder fix it.” Colorado Disability Opportunity Office (CDOO) - ( HB24-1360 ) CDOO has passed the HOUSE!
This bill would create an office to support the integration of Coloradans with disabilities into their communities, and it is moving forward at a good pace.
I’ve heard from many of you who are excited by this bill in particular - please continue to provide feedback and your thoughts!
Repeal CBI Criminal Justice Record Sealing Fee - ( HB24-1432 ) This bill, which has passed the House and is waiting for a hearing in the Senate.
This bill help sthe majority of people who have gone through the process in court to seal certain criminal history items from their record, as they often are unaware of an existing required (additional) $20 processing fee, causing more time for staff to attempt to collect these funds than it would take to process the request.
We are simply removing the fee charged by CBI.
Airports ( HB24-1235 ) - This bill has passed Seconds in the Senate - one big step closer to being enacted.
The bill aims to reduce the use of leaded fuels in aviation and support airports in developing programs to reduce noise over populated areas.
State Employee Deductions Bill ( HB24-1293 ) - The bill has passed completely through the House and the Senate!
This bill allows State employees to negotiate for non-healthcare-related benefits.
