Highlights of My Week at the Capitol Early days - late nights... we are in the home stretch of the legislative session and we're burning the midnight oil.
SB24-073 - This bill that I Co-Sponsored brought to the house by Rep.
Velasco and Titone, aims to adjust the maximum number of employees that a business can have while still remaining as a small business that qualifies for small employer healthcare insurance coverage.
It is important to allow for growth and to support our local small businesses and this is an effective way of not punishing small businesses for that growth and an increase of employment in our state.
HB24-1175 - I want to highlight this bill that passed seconds through the House because it would give local governments options to preserve long-term affordable housing opportunities by purchasing the housing themselves.
“Our bill is one of many steps that we are taking to combat housing instability and displacement,” said Rep.
Emily Sirota, Denver.
“We need a multi-faceted approach to address our affordable housing shortage, which is why we’re carrying this legislation to create new tools for local governments that keep existing affordable housing properties in the rental market.” HB24-1399 - The bill repeals the Colorado indigent care program and adjusts criteria for identifying uninsured or medically indigent patients.
It establishes an annual reporting requirement on the status of the primary care fund and creates a Hospital Discounted Care Advisory Committee to advise on healthcare billing policies.
Additionally, it mandates the promulgation of rules by July 1, 2025, regarding qualification for disproportionate share hospital payments.

Let's Talk about Housing Guns: RMGO This week's housing section got upended by what I spent an odd amount of time dealing with this week.
While "business as usual" had to continue with long committee days and lots of work on the floor of the House of Representatives - I had to deal with a surprising political event this week that you shoul know about.
On Wednesday a group called "Rocky Mountain Gun Owners" spent the day parading through the capitol with "petitions" before coming over to our offices in the State Services Building across from the Capitol.
First - you should know the thousands of documents that were distributed were not at all legitimate petitions.
In our office, we didn't receive a SINGLE document that even had a name of an HD37 resident... and the forms delivered to all House members didn't have even any basic contact information - just photocopied documents personalized for each Representative that had (sometimes very fake and even some vulgar) names, cities and zipcodes.
Wednesday was a very busy day in our office.
When this group placed the documents on one of my aide's desk, he quickly checked them and then took them across the hall to clear his desk.
Shortly after, the group's leader and a helper came and faced the aide with a camera to confront him for "being disrespectful to our constituents by throwing away their petitions".
The aide responded with a long sigh and a question "What do you expect people to do, keep these on the desk all day..." the men from RMGO then tried to taunt him further when I heard the commotion and called them into my office to engage with me instead.
I was mostly unaware that anything happening and was unsure if the aide had done any of the things that he was being accused, so I only asked them for a form from a constituent (which there were none) when they began to taunt me as well.
I listened to what they had to say and then acknowledged proudly that I'd meet my voters on the issue.
I figured that was the end of it, and I even rode down the elevator and walked across the street with the men with all friendly dialog from there.
Later, that video was posted multiple times on multiple platforms with headlines like "Chad Clifford tears up petitions from constituents", etc.
The real kicker was the organization also posted my personal cell phone with these videos that were sent to other organizations and took on a national, viral scale overnight.
So, you'll probably be hearing more about this saga in other news sources including how the college student aide has been continually harassed, and how we've had to deal with security concerns as well as hundreds and hundreds and hundreds of calls, texts, and emails from people who who were upset by this - both pro-gun and pro-gun-control.
You should know that we are fine (and taking necessary security precautions).
Also, I personally answered every call and text to my cell phone and every email.
These calls have been non-stop since Wednesday and I've enjoyed meeting so many new people from around the country.
I'll share more about the surprising result of me becoming the face of gun legislation in Colorado - but for now, I just wanted you to know there is more to the story.
I fully stand behind the actions of the aide - he never "ripped anything up" or threw anything away and we still have these "petitions".
I trust that our voters will be well informed of the situation as it unfolds - with so many new words around our office like "investigation" and "fraud" and "lawsuits" - this is sure to get more press coverage.
If you poke around X (Twitter), TikTok, or Youtube you can probably find the video - or wait a few days and you'll probably see all of this on the news.
I will say to all of you from me personally : Please don't give those RMGO people money.
If you ever came to watch them work in the Capitol, you'd see that these people don't have any real interest in advocating - and I have the opinion that literally every action that they take is intended to raise more money (millions).
There are other legitimate organizations advocating for these causes - but in my opinion RMGO is NOT one of them (and this was my opinion before this event as well).
And just as one final statement of fact: As far as I know, since I've been in office in January, my office (and usually me personally) has returned or responded to every call, text, email, or visit from EVERY constituent that has engaged my office on any issue, no matter how big or small.
We're very reliable for being accessible - so if you want me to know where you stand on any issue - you can reach me very easily.
My email is chad.clifford.house@coleg.gov and my cell # is 720-949-4593.

Colorado Disability Opportunity Office (CDOO) - ( HB 24-1360 ) - Rep.
David Ortiz and I are Co-Prime sponsoring legislation to create a new government office within the Department of Labor and Employment to serve as a resource for State Agencies, Private/ Non-Profit Organizations, and the public concerning disability issues in Colorado.
The funding comes entirely from the revenue generated by these license plates - not tax dollars.
Construction Defects Liability Reform ( SB24-106 ) - Due to the Senate inheriting the Budget Bill from us in the House this week SB24-106 has been rolled over to next week before it can run its process in the House.
This bill gives homeowners the option of requiring the contractor that caused a construction defect a way to remedy the defect without first going to litigation.
We are aiming with this legislation to limit lawsuits as they can sometimes overcomplicate and bring up some hidden surprises, and really focus on “if it’s broken, we should make the builder fix it.” Airports ( HB24-1235 ) - This bill is in Appropriations.

The bill aims to reduce the use of leaded fuels in aviation and support airports in developing programs to reduce noise over populated areas.

TRAPs- ( HB24-1324 ) - It is now onto the Senate to get this passed through to law, it has been assigned to the Business, Labor, & Technology committee in the Senate.
State Employee Deductions Bill (HB24-1293) - The House passed this bill and it is now being taken up in the Senate!

JYACAP - We got the bill draft for JYACAP this week.
This bill hopes to reduce a very serious bottleneck for youth who are convicted as adults in our system by removing the need for the governor's signature from parole board decisions.
This measure was originally put in place when there were doubts about how Juvenile Parole would function and is no longer necessary - the change streamlines the process.
Even the Governor thinks that Juvenile Parole can be managed without his oversight.
