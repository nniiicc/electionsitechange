Below are the legislative priorities I am entering this Session with: Housing : Another year another newsletter, you would likely guess from past newsletters that Housing is what I’m starting off with...
One piece of legislation that I am working hard to pass to address our housing problem is something I spoke about at length last session -- construction defects.
This must get this passed; giving alternate pathways to resolve construction defect claims besides only litigation is crucial for us to be able to build condominiums and ownable dense housing.
The average home price in HD37 is about $700,000.
For anyone looking to enter the housing market, or re-enter the housing market to downsize, there is virtually no housing at an "affordable" (pretend affordable is a $350k) level.
One of many steps needed to combat this is to allow for alternative resolutions to resolving construction defects.
It really has to be the foundation to start building middle-income housing, and if not addressed House District 37 is going to lose young people, families, and likely impact our workforce.
Public Safety : As the only sworn Peace Officer in the General Assembly, my voice in the law enforcement space is often useful.
I've spent every Thursday for the last eight months Co Chairing a Legislative Law Enforcement Working Group, and out of it have come four Topic Groups.
Of these groups, so far two bills have been introduced, HB25-1031 Law Enforcement Whistleblower Protection, and HB25-1136 Peace Officer Conduct Database.
Both of these bills protect the Peace Officers who protect us.
There will be a lot more conversation about these bills to come as they move through the process.
Another important aspect of public safety I'm engaged in is the implementation of Proposition 130, which even though it was on your ballot in November and passed with 52.79% of the vote, it still requires statute and some structures created to be implemented.
Prop 130 allocates $350 Million in state general funds to county and municipal law enforcement for training, recruitment, and retention as well as a $1 Million in death benefits to certain first responders who die in the line of duty.
There are some sticking points that myself and many members of the legislature want to make sure are properly addressed, and I anticipate that the final implementation language may take us all session to work out - and we all expect this to be implemented and funding started this year.
Accessibility : One of my bills every year is dedicated to the disability community's wants and needs.
This year, they approached me with the idea to codify the 1999 Olmstead v.
L.C. decision.
HB25-1017 , Community Integration Plan Individuals with Disabilities.
This states that the government cannot institutionalize people with disabilities without justification, and furthermore must do its best to integrate those with disabilities into society.
It emphasizes community-based living, supports employment, education, and healthcare, and fosters collaboration among state agencies, service providers, and local communities to enhance the quality of life for individuals with disabilities.
Airport : While I have no knowledge of legislation coming through addressing airports, I do see it as my responsibility to bridge the concerns of the community and of the airport industry.
This should be an ongoing dialogue, and I will create and support spaces with the airport and community groups that support communication.
Healthcare : I have heard from a number of constituents who have had problems with the state's Medicaid program.
29% of HD37 residents are on Health First Colorado, and I am looking for ways to make the process easier.
There is often overlap with federal regulation that makes some areas more difficult.
Another issue that has been brought to my attention is the reality that there exist harmful elder care facilities and problems in the state with oversight over these facilities.
I am working to address this problem with our state agencies.

Other Bills to Highlight SB25-003 Semiautomatic Firearms & Rapid-Fire Devices The Semiautomatic Firearms & Rapid-Fire Devices bill regulates rifles and “pistols” that are gas fired, aiming to reduce gun violence and enhance public safety.
It proposes restrictions on the sale, possession, and use of specific firearm types and accessories, while implementing stricter background checks and enforcement measures to prevent misuse.
The main reason I am on this bill is because I can go to a local gun club, today, and purchase equipment that is currently illegal.
Colorado needs better enforcement mechanisms to make sure that our communities are safe, and law abiding gun owners are, in fact, law-abiding.
This bill is currently in the Senate, but I will engage with it more if it gets to the House.
SB25-005 Worker Protection Collective Bargaining This bill aims to enhance labor rights by allowing workers, particularly in essential sectors, to engage in collective bargaining for better wages, benefits, and working conditions.
The bill eliminates the requirement for a second election to negotiate a union security agreement clause in the collective bargaining process.
HB25-1010 Prohibiting Price Gouging in Sales of Necessities The bill Prohibiting Price Gouging in Sales of Necessities aims to prevent excessive price increases during emergencies, such as natural disasters or public health crises.
The legislation ensures that essential goods, like food, water, and medical supplies, remain affordable, protecting consumers from exploitative pricing during times of need.
HB25-1005 Tax Incentives for Film Festivals This offers financial incentives to attract Sundance Film Festival to come to Colorado.
Besides the excitement that a film festival can bring it would also this would boost tourism, create jobs, and promote the economy by encouraging filmmakers and audiences to participate in events that showcase their cultural contributions through cinema.
HB25-1001 Enforcement Wage Hour Laws The Enforcement of Wage and Hour Laws ensures compliance with state labor regulations, including minimum wage, overtime, and proper payment practices.
The initiative empowers the Colorado Department of Labor and Employment to investigate violations, protect workers' rights, and hold employers accountable to ensure fair compensation and working conditions for employees.
HB25-1036 Missing Murdered Indigenous Relative License Plate Missing and Murdered Indigenous Relatives (MMIR) License Plate initiative aims to raise awareness of the crisis of missing and murdered Indigenous people.
The program allows individuals to purchase a specialized license plate featuring a design symbolizing the issue.
Proceeds from the sales support efforts to address and combat violence against Indigenous communities in Colorado.
HB25-1042 Air Quality Control Regulation Workforce Impact This focuses on addressing the economic effects of air quality regulations on workers and industries.
It seeks to balance environmental protections with job preservation by evaluating the impact of air quality standards on employment, business operations, and economic growth.
The initiative aims to ensure sustainable, fair transitions for affected workers and industries.
SB25-001 Colorado Voting Rights Act This ensures that all eligible voters, particularly communities of color and underserved groups, have equal access to the voting process.
The law includes provisions for language assistance, protections against discrimination, and measures to improve election fairness and accessibility.
SB25-014 Protecting the Freedom to Marry The "Protecting the Freedom to Marry" bill in Colorado aims to safeguard the right of individuals to marry regardless of gender.
It codifies the legal recognition of same-sex marriage into state law, ensuring marriage equality and protecting it from future legal challenges, reinforcing the state's commitment to equal rights and protections for all couples.
HB25-1001 , Concerning the enforcement of wage and hour laws Nothing should prevent workers from receiving the wages they earned.
New legislation will seek to improve wage theft protections for workers while boosting transparency and accountability for employers.
