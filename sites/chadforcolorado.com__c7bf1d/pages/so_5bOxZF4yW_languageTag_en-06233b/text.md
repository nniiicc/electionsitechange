Thank you SHRM for the invitation to speak.
While typically I refrain from delving into the politics of it all in the recaps, this week I'll provide insight into my voting decisions and the thought process behind them.
HB24- 1292 - Prohibit Certain Weapons Used in Mass Shootings Last week, I wrote about an organization that caused a lot of noise in my office, this is the bill that was the catalyst.
If passed, this bill will ban the manufacturing and sale of assault rifles in Colorado.
It was extremely difficult to decide my vote on this bill because I honestly believe that this bill has issues that will cause an immediate and massive influx of assault weapon purchases between now and July 2025 when it goes into effect.
I’m also a police officer, an armorer for these types of weapons, as well as a former law enforcement firearms instructor - so I have many biases that affect how I think of this legislation.
However, as my title and job is "Representative", I will always represent the people in my district.
I’ve heard from many many many of you directly who have wanted this legislation, so I voted Yes.
I was honestly thankful that I wasn't a swing vote, and even more thankful for the people that have contacted me all day today to share stories and those who appreciated where I stand.
Now it is in the Senate to start the process over.
I still have reservations - and I took all the actions that I know to take to resolve those concerns, but now I have a bit more gray hair and my thoughts are with the Senate as they confront this legislation.
SB24-068- Medical Aid-in-Dying This bill from the Senate has passed third reading and is now passed in both the House and Senate.
It aims to expand end-of-life options for individuals with terminal illnesses by allowing advanced practice registered nurses to prescribe medication for hastening death, and has shortened the waiting period for medication access from 15 to 7 days.
It also includes safeguards against insurance discrimination or coercion.
This is a sensitive topic, but I think it is only right that people in end of life circumstances have access to options that allow them to choose their own dignified path.
SB24-066- Firearms Merchant Category Code This bill having now passed both the Senate and House copies other states in requiring credit card processing companies to capture payment transaction network codes that identify firearm and ammunition sales.
It separates firearm purchases from other purchases with unique codes like groceries, gas, restaurants, etc.
This information will not be available to the public and would only be seen by credit card companies, however if a credit card company identifies unusual activity they will be able to report the activity to law enforcement.
For instance, in a recent event a person opened a new credit card account and charged $30k in firearms and ammunition in the same day - this would have been useful information to law enforcement the day before a significant incident occurs.
HB24-1313- Transit Oriented Communities Against my efforts, 1313 has narrowly passed the House and is now starting it's process in the Senate.

Let's Talk about Housing: When to pick your battles: This week was a tough week for me on Housing Bills - I have made no qualms about my views that most housing issues need serious engagement from local governments and I reject the premise based on significant research that our local communities in Arapahoe County aren't actively at work on bringing more affordable housing to HD37... or at least they are authentically trying.
This year in the General Assembly we have had a barrage of bills that deal with Housing - some that will have little impact on us locally, and some that could "rob" our communities of significant money that we desperately need in service of programs that we know that due to current infrastructure and land use not addressed by these programs just simply don't work or won't result in additional housing.
I have made it clear that I need all of my energy for the "big" fights - and as expected they all seemed to come at once this week.
And, I'm already at work on the next big fights that will start in 2025 and likely go on for years to come.
We must build ownable, dense housing to address a "middle" housing market (starter homes / downsizing homes).
When we talk about affordability, even these will likely be priced at sums most brains don't call "affordable" ($300k on average) but will still address a growing need to supply housing that allows home ownership to tons of people where this remains out of reach, while those same people spend significantly more in monthly rent than they would pay for their mortgage.
There is no "one size fits all" for a community.
The "bones" of most of our infrastructure in this district has existed since the 1970's and that creates some realities that communities to the north and south of us don't encounter - it cost more to build here.
Due to these costs, developers don't eye some of our projects when they can go 10 miles away, build easier, faster, and earn greater profits.
The equations to solve our housing issues are local... we can hopefully start looking at state-government ideas to spearhead or mitigate issues, but I haven't found an answer with many of the statewide initiatives before us currently.
With less than 1 month left in the current legislative session, we'll be confronting the new realities of housing policy - and I will be actively at work picking battles and working with our local governments to come up with plans and proposals that will work for our district.
