Judicial Corruption Lawfare Connect HOME Subscribe Log in No MOE Corruption 6.28.2026 11:32 PM Uncategorized DeSantis says Alligator Alcatraz is closed, TPS Status for Haitians REVOKED, as Haitian-American candidate Moe Dimanche Qualifies for Florida Governor race MIAMI — The landscape of South Florida politics shifted irrevocably this week.
In a rapid succession of announcements that have left our neighborhoods reeling, Governor Ron DeSantis declared the closure of the “Alligator Alcatraz” tent facility, the Supreme Court greenlit the revocation of Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for hundreds of thousands of Haitian families, and Moe Dimanche, the first Haitian-American candidate for governor in Florida’s history, officially qualified to be the next Governor of Florida.
For the residents of Miami-Dade County, these events are not isolated headlines, they are direct impacts on our doorsteps.
The closure of the Everglades facility is a tactical retreat by an administration facing intense scrutiny, but the anxiety in Little Haiti, Doral, and Homestead has never been higher.
With the federal administration leveraging 287(g) agreements to turn local police into an extension of ICE, and the TPS ruling threatening to tear apart families who have spent decades building Miami’s infrastructure, our community is facing an existential crisis of trust, safety, and economic survival.
Yet, amidst the fear, long before Tallahassee acknowledged the human rights nightmare building in the Everglades, it was Moe Dimanche who stood at the front lines, demanding the closure of Alligator Alcatraz, and the recognition of the dignity of every human being.
He recognized early what career politicians ignored: that running secret detention operations outside public view, against the backdrop of no-bid contracts seeking outrageous fees with our tax dollars, destroys the very fabric of local community safety and public trust.
A BLUEPRINT BEYOND SLOGANS Moe Dimanche advocating for law-abiding migrants while calling for a common sense approach to illegal immigration and drug trafficking.
Florida does not need more empty promises or predictable political rhetoric.
We live in the logistics and trade capital of the Americas.
Targeting laborers instead of drug traffickers drives up the price of your groceries.
Targeting tax-paying home-builders drives up to cost of buying homes.
Housing developments are decaying without being completed and crops are wasting away because the folks who help build this nation are afraid to continue contributing to our success as a nation.
While our neighborhoods face federal immigration overreach, our local economy is simultaneously being choked by the recent federal rollbacks on trade exemptions.
The sudden elimination of the de minimis rule has created a regulatory bottleneck that threatens the livelihoods of working families just as severely as shifting immigration policies.
Dimanche’s qualification changes the math of this gubernatorial race because he is the only candidate treating these issues not as talking points, but as connected realities of the working class.
As a candidate who understands the meticulous mechanics of local governance and constitutional advocacy, Dimanche has built a platform centered on immediate structural reform.
His vision rejects the passive compliance of municipal leaders who allow local law enforcement to spend city resources chasing federal immigration targets, advocating instead for absolute transparency and the protection of municipal autonomy.
“When local police are forced to act as federal agents, community trust vanishes.
Families hide in the shadows, crimes go unreported, and local commerce stalls.
We need an executive in Tallahassee who understands that protecting Florida means protecting the people who build it.” THE CHOICE FOR SOUTH FLORIDA The revocation of TPS status is an attempt to signal that certain communities do not belong in the future of this state, and Haitians are only the first target.
But South Florida’s identity is inseparable from its diaspora.
The economic vitality of South Florida relies on the stability of its workforce, from the logistics hubs to the neighborhoods where families deserve to live without the perpetual fear of sudden disruption.
As the gubernatorial race intensifies, Miami voters deserve a leader who matches the urgency of the moment.
DeSantis may declare missions accomplished, and federal courts may strip away legal protections, but the entry of Moe Dimanche into the official race provides a clear alternative.
For a region caught between federal overreach and economic bottlenecks, a candidate who was the first to sound the alarm on the abuses in our backyard isn’t just a choice, he is the necessary leader to steer Florida toward real justice and economic stability for all.
