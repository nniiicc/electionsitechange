1 PREFACE This Code constitutes a codi cation of the general and permanent ordinances of the City of West Orlando, Florida.
The chapters of the Code of General Ordinances, Part II, have been conveniently arranged in alphabetical order, and the various sections within each chapter have been catchlined to facilitate usage.
Notes which tie related sections of the Code together and which refer to relevant state law have been included.
A table listing the state law citations and setting forth their location within the Code is included at the back of this Code.
Chapter and Section Numbering System The chapter and section numbering system used in this Code of General Ordinances, Part II, is the same system used in many state and local government codes.
Each section number consists of two parts separated by a dash.
The gure before the dash refers to the chapter number, and the gure after the dash refers to the position of the section within the chapter.
Thus, the second section of chapter 1 is numbered 1-2, and the rst section of chapter 6 is 6-1.
Under this system, each section is identi ed with its chapter, and at the same time new sections can be inserted in their proper place by using the decimal system for amendments.
For example, if new material consisting of one section that would logically come between sections 6-1 and 6-2 is desired to be added, such new section would be numbered 6-1.5.
New articles and new divisions may be included in the same way or, in the case of articles, may be placed at the end of the chapter embracing the subject, and, in the case of divisions, may be placed at the end of the article embracing the subject.
The next successive number shall be assigned to the new article or division.
New chapters may be included by using one of the reserved chapter numbers.
Care should be taken that the alphabetical arrangement of chapters is maintained when including new chapters.
Page Numbering System The page numbering system used in this Code is a pre x system.
The letters to the left of the colon are an abbreviation which represents a certain portion of the volume.
The number to the right of the colon represents the number of the page in that portion.
In the case of a chapter of the Code, the number to the left of the colon indicates the number of the chapter.
In the case of an appendix to the Code, the letter immediately to the left of the colon indicates the letter of the appendix.

2-830. - Purpose.
This investment policy sets forth the investment objectives and parameters for the management of public funds of the city.
This policy is designed to ensure the prudent management of public funds, the availability of operating funds when needed, and a competitive investment return.
Sec.
2-831. - Scope. (a) In accordance with F.S. § 218.415, this investment policy applies to all nancial assets of the city in excess of those required to meet current expenses.
This policy shall not apply to pension funds or funds related to the issuance of debt where there are other existing policies or indentures in effect for such funds. (b) The city will consolidate various funds into a single portfolio for investing purposes.
Consolidation maximizes investment earnings and increases ef ciencies relative to investment pricing, safekeeping, and administration.
Investment income will be allocated to the various funds based on their respective participation.
Sec.
2-832. - Investment objectives. (a) Safety of capital.
The primary objective of the investment program covered by this policy is the preservation of capital of those funds in the portfolio.
The portfolio shall be managed to minimize capital losses by adherence to the restrictions outlined in this policy.
While the city’s investment portfolio will consist of relatively low-risk securities, the occasional recognition of a paper loss on a security will be acceptable.
The risk of capital losses from interest rate uctuations in the markets will be minimized by the practice of holding investments through to maturity. (b) Maintenance of liquidity.
The portfolio shall be managed in such a manner that funds are available to meet reasonably anticipated cash ow requirements in an orderly manner.
Periodic cash ow analyses will be completed to ensure that the portfolio is positioned to provide adequate liquidity. (c) Return on investment.
The investment portfolio shall be designed with the objective of attaining a market rate of return throughout budgetary and economic cycles, fi fi fl fl fl 124 while considering the investment risk constraints and liquidity needs.
Return on investment is of least importance compared to the safety and liquidity objectives described in this section.

The "Prudent Person Rule" states the following: "Investments should be made with judgment and care, under circumstances then prevailing, which persons of prudence, discretion, and intelligence exercise in the management of their own affairs, not for speculation, but for investment, considering the probable safety of their capital as well as the probable income to be derived from the investment." (b) Nonconforming signs existing lawfully at adoption may continue as legal nonconformities until abandoned, destroyed, or altered beyond 50% of their value, as determined by the Orange County Property Appraiser, after which they must comply or be removed within 60 days. (c) Illegal nonconforming signs must obtain permits within 30 days of notice and comply within six months, or be removed.
Sec.
11-3. - Administration. (a) The city manager or designee shall oversee sign regulation, issuing permits and enforcing compliance through the building department. (b) Permits are required for all signs unless exempted, with violations subject to doubled fees if installed prematurely.
Sec.
11-4. - Measurement and Design Standards. (a) Copy Area.
Sign surface area shall be calculated using the smallest geometric shape enclosing all text, symbols, and integral design elements, excluding incidental supports.
Double-faced signs count as one face if within two feet and angled no more than 45 degrees. (b) Illumination.
Signs may be externally illuminated with shielded, non- ashing light sources not exceeding 11 watts for exposed bulbs, avoiding glare onto streets or adjacent properties.
Internal illumination is prohibited for cabinet signs unless lettering alone is lit. fl 186 (c) Design.
Signs must integrate architecturally with buildings and sites, using compatible materials and colors, and avoid obstructing key features or natural elements.
Sec.
11-5. - Maintenance and Removal. (a) All signs must be maintained in good condition per Chapter 13 standards. (b) Unsafe, unlawful, or abandoned signs must be removed by the owner within 10 days of notice, or the city may remove them at the owner’s expense, with immediate action for imminent hazards.
ARTICLE II. - PERMANENT SIGNS Sec.
11-6. - Residential District Signs. (a) Identi cation Signs.
One ground or wall sign per development entry, up to 24 square feet and 4 feet high (6 feet if on a wall), set back 5 feet from rights-of-way, externally illuminated, with landscaping required at the base. (b) Instructional Signs.
Signs up to 3 square feet and 3 feet high, externally illuminated, as needed for safety or direction, per city approval.
Sec.
11-7. - Non-Residential District Signs. (a) Ground Signs.
(1) One sign per street frontage, up to 9-10 feet high and 36-150 square feet based on street type and building size, set back 3 feet from property lines (25 feet near residential zones), with landscaping and earth-toned bases.
(2) Way nding and directional signs permitted onsite, up to 6 square feet and 4 feet high, for internal navigation. fi fi 187 (b) Building Signs.
(1) Wall signs: 1-1.5 square feet per linear foot of frontage, based on setback, not obstructing architectural features.
(2) Projecting/Under-Canopy Signs: 4 square feet, with 8-foot clearance, deducted from wall sign allocation.
(3) Awning Signs: Limited to wall sign allocation, no backlighting.
(4) Directory Signs: 4 square feet for multi-tenant centers. (c) Flags.
Up to 3 ground poles (30 feet high) or 4 building-mounted poles (5 feet above roo ine), with ags up to 40 square feet in non-residential zones.

One 100-square-foot wall sign (“EMERGENCY”) and way nding signs up to 36 square feet per access point. (b) Gas Stations.
Canopy logos up to 4 square feet per side; pump signs up to 1.5 square feet each. (c) Government Buildings.

11-9. - Off-Site Signs. (a) Billboards.
Permitted in designated commercial zones with conditional use approval, up to 400 square feet and 30 feet high, with setbacks (e.g., 35 feet from rights-of-way, 300 feet from residential). (b) Directional Signs.
Up to 16 square feet for businesses off arterial streets, with city approval.
ARTICLE III. - TEMPORARY SIGNS Sec.
11-10. - Authorized Temporary Signs. (a) Banners.
One per establishment, up to 21 square feet (non-residential) or 6 square feet (residential), for 30-90 days annually, no illumination, securely mounted. (b) Real Estate Signs.
One per street frontage, up to 32 square feet (nonresidential) or 6 square feet (residential), no illumination. (c) Construction Signs.
One per lot, up to 32 square feet (non-residential) or 6 square feet (residential), during construction, up to 18 months.

The city manager may grant variances for dimensional standards upon written request, proving unique hardship, with ARC review and appeal to the board of adjustment.
