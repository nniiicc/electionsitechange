Wade: A terrifying new reality for parents who are expecting | Opinion - July 1, 2022 by Amanda Marie Green, Your Turn Why I am endorsing Amanda Green for Florida’s CD 2 | Opinion - April 23, 2026 by Leon Sherriff Walt McNeil Statements First Anniversary of Shooting at Florida State University I am proud to be a Florida State Nole.
In November 2014, I was asleep at home on West Georgia Street when a gunman entered Strozier Library and terrorized students, many of whom were close friends of mine.
It was a night defined by fear and confusion, and by the painful recognition that the person responsible was someone in clear need of mental health support who never received it.
I remember the heavy silence that settled over campus in the days, weeks, and months that followed.
Less than a decade later, a year ago today, my alma mater endured another act of violence where two lives were lost, six were injured, and a whole community was left brokenhearted.
Students were forced to hide while hearing their classmates cry out for help.
No one should ever have to experience that—once, let alone twice.
I cannot make sense of what drives someone to commit such violence.
What I do know is that we will never fully understand or effectively prevent it if we continue to avoid the hard work.
That means investing in serious, sustained research into gun violence, funding accessible mental health treatment, advancing safe storage laws that responsible gun owners already practice, and holding systems accountable when they fail to protect lives .
There is no room for excuses.
Our elected officials have a responsibility to pursue thoughtful, evidence-based solutions to address the leading cause of death for children and teens in this country.
I am proud to be a Nole.
And I am proud of the community that showed up for one another in the aftermath, both then and now.
But pride alone is not enough.
It must be matched with the courage to act.
