It took me a full year, I started last October, but I've knocked on all 10k doors in the district and talked over 4,000 people!
And behind those doors, I’ve met people from every walk of life and every political leaning.
I met a woman who told me she barely leaves her home anymore because of how toxic our environment has become.
I met another woman whose adult son was recently shot and killed by a random thief.
A few days after our emotional conversation on her doorstep, she sent me a kind and deeply moving email — thanking me for listening.
I’ve talked with people who are incredibly supportive of my effort to bring respectful dialogue and civil discourse back into our public life.
I’ve also met people who are very skeptical — one person even told me I didn’t have a snowball’s chance in hell of making any difference.
But far more often, I meet people who are simply grateful that someone is willing to show up and listen.
I’ve had people offer, right there on the doorstep, to help with the campaign — some of whom are now part of my core team.
I’ve had people come back out of their homes after our first conversation just to ask more questions.
One young man tracked me down to ask, very sincerely, what we can do about all the disinformation that’s floating around.
In another case, a passionate conservative came out into the street about thirty minutes after we first spoke and asked me rapid-fire, hard-hitting questions about my beliefs — vaccines, immigration, and other issues we tend to fight about along party lines.
We agreed on some things.
We disagreed on a lot.
But we talked — respectfully — and we both tried to understand each other.
And that’s the key.
Over and over again, people tell me the same thing: They’re tired of fighting.
They want to come together.
That’s a basic human desire — to unify, to connect, to build community again.
I truly believe that the intentional effort to reconnect and restore productive conversation is the key issue of our time.
And I’ll go a step further — if we don’t restore basic civil discourse, I fear we risk failing as a nation.
We can disagree without attacking each other.
We can listen honestly.
We can compromise when it’s necessary.
Karen and I have been married for 35 years, and we’re both pretty opinionated.
In fact, her intelligence and willingness to speak her mind were part of what attracted me to her in the first place.
We still disagree — regularly — but we respect each other, we work through the hard conversations, and we’re stronger because of it.
Right now — in Utah and across the country — we’re navigating incredibly complex issues: immigration, gerrymandering, the rule of law, the balance between voter initiatives and legislative power, health insurance, and more.
These problems won’t be solved by shouting past one another.
They can be solved — but only through respectful dialogue, honest listening, and shared problem-solving.
That is my core campaign message.
That’s why I’m knocking on 10,000 doors.
That’s why I’m holding cottage meetings.
Let’s talk.
Let’s listen.
And let’s figure this out — together — before it’s too late.
