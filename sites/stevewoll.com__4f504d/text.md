Hampton Roads:
Where America Became America.
For 400 years, this corner of Virginia has been where the American Experiment was tested - in war and peace, in failure and progress, in division and unity.
From the first English settlers to the first African slaves, from the climax of the Revolution to the horror of the Civil War, from the most powerful naval fleet in history to the ongoing fight for civil rights, Hampton Roads has never looked away from the hard work of America.
In 2026, that work continues.
The country is more divided than at any point in our lifetimes.
Washington has stopped governing and started posturing.
But Hampton Roads has always stepped up and found a way through - not by picking sides, but by getting down to work and showing the way.
250 years ago, the founders realized that we needed to declare independence and cut ties with a petty, self-serving, and ineffective political class that no longer served or respected our interests.
Eleven years after that, they gathered again to strengthen the bonds of the new nation.
They did not want political parties and made no place for them in the new Constitution - and now we know why.
In 2026, America is terribly stressed, and once again we need to declare independence - not only from a President who acts like a king, but from the two political parties that have gotten us here.
We need to have the courage of the founders - we need to show the political establishment that we have had enough, and the nation needs the citizens of Hampton Roads in the vanguard.
We are uniquely positioned to send the message, and that's what we're doing here - taking back our government, our economy, our society and putting them all to work FOR us, not against us.
