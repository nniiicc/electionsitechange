ICYMI: Hinojosa’s “Populist Message – Everyday Texans v.
Billionaires” Gives “Democrats Their Highest Hopes in 20 Years”
CNN, Houston Chronicle, and CNBC outline how Hinojosa’s focus on bringing back Latino voters, ending the data center madness, and putting money in Texans’ pockets have made her “the most serious electoral challenger that Greg Abbott has ever faced”
Austin, TX – Democratic nominee for Texas Governor Gina Hinojosa is at the center of a changing political landscape in Texas, according to new reporting, as economic pressures and growing frustration with Greg Abbott’s failed leadership create “the likeliest chance for Democrats to break one-party control” of the state in nearly three decades.
According to the Houston Chronicle, “Hinojosa has honed [a] populist message – everyday Texans v. billionaires – over the last year as she has gone from a virtual unknown Austin state representative to perhaps the most serious electoral challenger that Greg Abbott, the state’s three-term Republican governor, has ever faced.”
To do this, Hinojosa has worked to connect with Texans across the political spectrum.
As a result, she’s found herself “in a series of unusual rooms,” wrote CNBC.
After sit-downs with MAGA voters in deep-red rural Red Oak and fraternity brothers in Austin, she told CNBC, “It was people who typically don’t give someone like me a chance.
And they were very curious about my campaign.”
Throughout her campaign, Hinojosa has also led the effort to win back Latino voters from border communities – including her hometown of Brownsville – who voted for Trump in 2024 by focusing on the economy and dissatisfaction with the political system.
“People voted for Trump because he said the system’s rigged.
Republicans have been in power, and so they are primarily to blame here, but there is blame to go around.
To be able to acknowledge that, to see it and say it — that’s what we’re about,” she told CNN.

Houston Chronicle: Gina Hinojosa is betting that voters are tired of culture wars — and Greg Abbott
- Gina Hinojosa had a plea for the crowd that came to hear her stump in McAllen last week during a swing through the Rio Grande Valley: set aside the culture wars.
- People are struggling to make ends meet, the Democratic gubernatorial candidate said.
Some teachers have told her they started donating blood to earn extra money to pay their bills.
One left the profession to drive for Uber because they said the pay and insurance were better.
- “Nobody has the luxury of the culture wars anymore,” Hinojosa told the couple hundred spectators, adding: “The only winner in those wars is like 15 billionaire tech dudes who have amassed money, who have amassed power, and who have amassed, it turns out, all of our data in the last 10 years, and they are about to blow up the world if we don’t come together fast to save ourselves.”
- Hinojosa has honed that populist message – everyday Texans v. billionaires – over the last year as she has gone from a virtual unknown Austin state representative to perhaps the most serious electoral challenger that Greg Abbott, the state’s three-term Republican governor, has ever faced.
- She has been buoyed by political winds that are shaping the midterms into a potentially disastrous election cycle for Republicans in Texas as voters fume over rising prices and seemingly unchecked growth of big tech.
Hinojosa has pinned the fault squarely on Abbott, who has held the highest office in the state for 12 years.
- From opposition to private school vouchers to data centers and Flock cameras, Hinojosa’s central message is that Abbott has “sold out” Texas to his donors.
- “That’s what this election is about,” she told an energetic crowd of hundreds in her hometown of Brownsville.
“Who does Texas belong to?”
- Abbott has sailed to double-digit victories over every one of his past Democratic challengers.
But several recent polls have shown a closer race and at least one tracker, Real Clear Politics, has deemed it a “toss up.”
- A September survey, by the Emerson College Polling Center, showed Hinojosa trailing by 3 points among likely voters but narrowing the governor’s lead from August.
It also showed her up with key voting blocs, including independents and Latinos, that Abbott was winning at the same point in his 2022 bid against Beto O’Rourke.
- The fact that the governor’s campaign is even mentioning Hinojosa by name may be notable.
For most of this year, Abbott has instead been laser-focused on James Talarico, the Democratic U.S.
Senate candidate at the top of the ticket, who had much higher name recognition coming out of a competitive primary.
- But in recent weeks, Abbott has gone after Hinojosa directly, labeling her as a radical leftist and rolling out nicknames like “Gender Bender Gina” and “High Tax Hinojosa.”
- The campaign decided to split the state into regions and run mini campaigns in each to get a better feel for where voters were on the ground, helping craft localized messages around school takeovers, data centers and Flock cameras.
- The data center debate that erupted this summer, both in Texas and nationally, played right into her campaign themes.
In July, she called on Abbott to halt construction and bring lawmakers back to Austin for a special session to set rules on where they can be built.
- Her first broadcast TV ad focused on the issue and ran exclusively in rural markets, part of an effort to win over Republicans and independents — or at least discourage them from voting for Abbott.
- To win in November, Hinojosa will likely need to drive up turnout among Latino voters, especially in her home region of South Texas.
