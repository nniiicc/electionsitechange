Healthcare Mandates
America's Medical System is Broken
With healthcare costs out of control, insurance companies restricting access, and rural healthcare disappearing in Mississippi, we need changes to our nation's health policies.
And taking a fresh look at some of our nation's outdated thought process might be a great start to having a dialogue about how we want our healthcare policies to perform in America.
As a healthcare professional for decades, I know that adopting a one size fits all healthcare system will never work.
People are different.
People respond to different kinds of medications differently.
We have to be cognizant that people need individualized healthcare solutions to the individual problems that affect their families.
The MAHA ("Make America Healthy Again") movement, largely driven by Robert F.
Kennedy Jr. as Secretary of Health and Human Services (HHS), focuses on addressing chronic disease through nutrition reform, scrutinizing environmental toxins, and overhauling public health institutions.
The most widely known parts of the movement include:
* Food and Nutrition Reform: This is a central and broadly popular aspect of MAHA.
Initiatives include phasing out petroleum-based artificial food dyes and other chemical additives (like Red dye No. 3) from the U.S. food supply, overhauling the FDA's "Generally Recognized as Safe" (GRAS) loophole, and promoting whole, unprocessed foods.
There is also a push to restrict ultra-processed foods, candy, and soda in federal programs like SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) and school meal plans.
* Environmental Chemicals: The movement places a strong emphasis on the role of environmental toxins in chronic disease.
It frequently criticizes specific pesticides like glyphosate and atrazine, and seeks to reduce exposure to chemicals like PFAS and phthalates.
* Vaccine Policy and "Medical Freedom": A highly visible, though more controversial, part involves scrutinizing vaccine safety and the childhood vaccine schedule, and promoting "medical freedom" and informed consent.
This includes calls for more transparency and the investigation of potential links between certain medical products (like acetaminophen and some psychiatric medications) and childhood conditions, despite a lack of consensus from mainstream scientific bodies.
* Transparency and Research: A key policy goal is bringing "radical transparency" to government health data and ensuring that federally funded health research is free from conflicts of interest.
* Lifestyle and Preventive Care: The movement advocates for preventive healthcare through lifestyle changes, such as physical activity initiatives (like the re-establishment of the Presidential Fitness Test) and addressing the harms of excessive screen time.
I approve of bringing new ideas to healthcare problems, and look forward to seeing how this new phase of medical innovation leads to results that make Americans healthier again.
