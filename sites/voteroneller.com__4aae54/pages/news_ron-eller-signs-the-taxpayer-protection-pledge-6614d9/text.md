Conservative Republican Ron Eller, candidate for the Second Congressional District of Mississippi, pledged to voters his opposition to any tax increase by signing the Americans for Tax Reform “Taxpayer Protection Pledge.” Citing the fact that all elected officials should control spending and not raises taxes, Eller committed himself on behalf of every voter in Mississippi to oppose any tax increases.
“I am against raising anybody’s taxes.
Government needs to get on a fiscal diet and stop the runaway spending that has created government swamps all across America,” said Eller.
“If elected I pledge to not give lip service to fiscal restraint, but never vote to raise taxes and instead free the private sector to promote job growth.
I'm committed to that with this pledge.”
Candidates and elected officials began taking the pledge in 1986.
The Americans for Tax Reform and a broad-based coalition of groups including the National Federation of Independent Businesses and hundreds of taxpayer groups throughout the nation began to ask all candidates for public office to "take the pledge."
“Some Republicans act just like Democrats.
They continue to vote to raise your taxes and vote in favor of spending on their special projects,” said Eller.
“By going along with bigger government Establishment Republicans are just spending America into bankruptcy and aiding and abetting the decline of America.”
Distinguishing him from others in the race, Eller said “If I am elected as Congressman I will actually work to stop the runaway spending and increased taxes that some politicians proposed and passed this past year.
I remain committed to providing Mississippi County with a truly small-government, constitutionally conservative choice in 2026.”
