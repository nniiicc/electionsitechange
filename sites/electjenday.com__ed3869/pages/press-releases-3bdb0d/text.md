The first step to lowering costs and pushing back on corruption.” The ad releases come as recent polling shows a close race for Kansas Secretary of State, with Jennifer Day and Pat Proctor just two points apart.
The polling shows Day taking a sizable lead once voters hear her message, highlighting the importance of introducing Kansans to her vision for protecting voting access and restoring Kansas values to the Secretary of State’s office.

### July 31, 2026 Jennifer Day Candidate for Kansas Secretary of State Statement on Wyandotte County Mail In Ballots Issue “Every Kansan who takes the time to vote deserves confidence that their ballot will make it where it needs to go.
Reports that completed mail ballots are being returned to voters in Wyandotte County because of confusion over election mailing addresses are deeply concerning, especially with the primary just days away.
Voters should not have to navigate confusion or uncertainty after they have already done their part to participate in our democracy.
The Secretary of State’s office needs to work with Wyandotte County to make sure affected voters know exactly what they should do next, that county election officials have the support they need, and that no eligible voter is left without a clear path to have their vote counted.
If you have your completed mail ballot, I would encourage you to take it directly to your county’s official ballot drop box rather than relying on the mail with the election so close.
If your ballot was returned to you or you are unsure about its status, contact your county election office immediately and make a plan to vote.” ### July 16, 2026 Jennifer Day Applauds Court Decision Protecting Kansas Voters While Legal Challenge Continues Overland Park, Kan. - Today, Democratic candidate for Kansas Secretary of State Jennifer Day issued the following statement regarding a Douglas County District Court judge's decision to temporarily block enforcement of a law eliminating Kansas' three day ballot receipt grace period for mail ballots while the case moves forward.
"Today's ruling is an important victory for Kansas voters.
Every eligible vote, cast according to the law, deserves to be counted.
“The court recognized what election officials and voters have known for years: the three day grace period exists to protect voters from postal delays that are completely outside their control.
As the judge noted, the grace period 'did what it was designed to do,' preventing thousands of legally cast ballots from being rejected simply because they arrived after Election Day through no fault of the voter.
“It's also worth remembering that the three-day grace period was created with bipartisan support because lawmakers understood that voters should not lose their voice due to circumstances beyond their control.
“Kansans deserve elections that are both secure and fair.
Those values go hand in hand.
This ruling preserves a commonsense safeguard that has helped ensure voters who follow the law are not disenfranchised while this case is decided.” ### July 10, 2026 Jennifer Day Statement on the Removal of Bipartisan Election Assistance Commission Members Overland Park, Kansas. – Jennifer Day, Democratic candidate for Kansas Secretary of State, released the following statement regarding the removal of members of the bipartisan U.S.

Supreme Court decision allowing states to continue using mail ballot grace periods when authorized under state law, Rep.
Jennifer Day, Democratic candidate for Kansas Secretary of State, welcomed the ruling and called on Kansas lawmakers to reconsider eliminating the state’s three-day grace period for mail ballots.
Last year, the Kansas legislature eliminated the state’s three-day grace period.
“For Kansans who mail their ballot back on or by Election Day, they shouldn't have their right to vote revoked because it takes a few days for their ballot to make its way to their Board of Election.
It's just common sense.
I'm glad the Supreme Court has ruled in favor of common sense -- and I hope the Kansas Legislature follows." says Jennifer Day.
As Secretary of State, Jennifer will fight for every vote because what Kansans care about goes through the ballot.
Her focus will always be on ensuring every eligible Kansan can participate in elections that are secure, accurate, and worthy of the public's trust.
Election administration should be guided by the law, facts, and the goal of serving voters, not by partisan politics.
