Lately it has been you.
Raleigh has cut its own taxes for a decade and left the counties to make up the difference, which is where your property tax bill comes from.
Your power bill climbs on a separate track, and you did not get a vote on either one.
First bills Expand the property tax circuit breaker to owner-occupied homes at any age below the area median income, and drop the lien that keeps people from using it.
Vote against rate increases that charge you for plants that are not built yet, and against deals that move a large user's costs onto residential bills.
Stop the corporate rate slide to zero in 2030, and put the next cut where the work is instead.
Long-term goal No state income tax on the first $100,000 you earn.
Childcare deductible and the state child tax credit back.
Sales tax that lands on professional services instead of groceries and repairs.
Full plan and sources Guides: where your property tax goes · the tax calculator · how your power bill works Public Schools Courts told the state to fund schools.
It still has not.
Fund the schools almost every kid in this district actually attends, before expanding a voucher program nobody is allowed to audit.
First bills Refile the Voucher School Accountability Act: test scores, graduation rates, and an annual audit from every private school taking Opportunity Scholarship money.
No reporting, no check.
Fund the Leandro plan, which the state paid to have written and then ignored.
Restore the advanced degree pay supplement North Carolina eliminated in 2013.
Long-term goal Teacher pay in the top 15 states.
North Carolina is 43rd, and every state on the border pays more.
Per-student funding that does not depend on which county you happen to live in.
Who checked Endorsed by the NCAE Great Public Schools Fund , the political fund of the North Carolina Association of Educators, and by the Forsyth County Association of Educators , its local affiliate.
School funding is what they ask candidates about.

Healthcare Getting sick should not be the thing that ruins you.
Pay for the coverage the state already promised, keep a doctor within reach of the people who live out here, and make a medical bill something a person can actually read.
First bills Require the legislature to fund Medicaid at the level its own agency says is needed to keep current services.
No new spending.
Pay for what was already promised.
Fund rural loan repayment for doctors who stay five years or more, and expand residency slots at community hospitals.
Put the billing rights that already exist in front of patients: posted prices, itemized bills without asking, and charity care offered instead of buried.
Long-term goal The # people who gained coverage keep it, rural hospitals stay open, and 92 of North Carolina's 100 counties stop being provider shortage areas.
Full plan and sources Guides: what to do about a medical bill Data Centers If it needs your power and your water, you get a say.
A project the size of a small town does not arrive by accident.
If one is coming here, it should pay for the power and water it uses, and the county should know what it is voting on before it votes.
First bills Create a statewide zoning class for data centers, with binding operating limits and independent monitoring, so counties stop shoehorning them into heavy manufacturing rules written for something else.
Create a separate electric rate class for facilities that size, so they pay for the grid they need instead of putting it on residential bills.
End code-name votes.
State the power draw, the water demand, and the tenant in the application before anybody rezones anything, and write the developer's own jobs and tax numbers into the contract with a clawback attached.
Long-term goal If a company profits here, the community profits too.
Tax benefits tied to permanent local jobs per megawatt, a share of the tax break in a fund the community controls, and clawbacks enforced the way Forsyth County enforced them on Dell.

This district was drawn so the result was settled before anybody voted, which means whoever holds it answers to the map instead of to you.
I support taking the pen away from whichever party is winning.
First bills Pass the Redistricting Transparency Act: every proposed map and the data behind it published # days before a committee vote, with # days of public comment.
Support an independent redistricting commission, and putting it on the ballot so voters decide it rather than legislators.
Oppose new voting restrictions, and any change to the rules inside an election year.
Long-term goal Politicians do not pick their voters, and voting is boring, predictable, and easy enough that nobody gives up before they get to the ballot.
Full plan and sources Guides: how to vote in NC · how the maps got drawn Abortion Abortion is health care.
Let doctors be doctors.
The decision belongs to the person having it and their doctor, along with anyone else they choose to bring into it.
Not the General Assembly.
There are a few doctors in the General Assembly.
There are not 170 of them, and none of them is in the room with you.
The last few years have shown what happens when lawmakers write medicine into statute.
Real cases carry more nuance than a bill can hold, doctors who know exactly what to do find out they are not allowed to do it, and women have died waiting, including a 34-year-old police officer an hour up the road in Greensboro (ProPublica told Ciji Graham's story in full).
Whatever you believe about abortion, none of that was a doctor's call.
What I'm for Repealing the parts of the 2023 law that are paperwork rather than medicine, starting with the extra in-person visit seventy-two hours ahead that turned one appointment into two or three.
It makes nobody safer, and it lands hardest on people who work shifts and drive an hour each way.
Putting the decision itself back with the patient and the doctor, instead of a date on a calendar chosen by people who have never met her.
Protecting a doctor who follows the standard of care from losing a license or facing a charge for it.
Leaving contraception and IVF where they are, which should not need saying and lately does.
Who checked Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Votes South Atlantic and Pro-Choice North Carolina PAC .

I want to look out for everyone in this district.
LGBTQ+ North Carolinians are part of everyone, and lately the legislature has been treating them like an exception.
If protecting children were the point of these bills, the same legislature would not have spent a decade underfunding those kids' schools, and House Bill 713 would not be stuck in a committee instead of feeding every student in the state.
It is a small population to pick on, and that is what makes it useful.
While everybody argues about a few hundred children, nobody answers for the other five things on this page.
What I'm for Co-sponsoring the five bills Equality NC has put in front of the legislature every session since 2016: statewide nondiscrimination protections in housing, employment, and public places, a ban on conversion therapy, an end to the gay and trans panic defense, repeal of what is left of HB 142, and domestic violence protection orders that cover LGBTQ+ people too.
Co-sponsoring and voting for the resolution to ratify the Equal Rights Amendment.
That is what the ERA-NC Alliance certification commits a candidate to, and I signed it.
Keeping treatment decisions for a kid with that kid's family and their doctor.
The American Academy of Pediatrics says that care saves lives, and North Carolina took it away in 2023.
