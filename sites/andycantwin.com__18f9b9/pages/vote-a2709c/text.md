The fine print Enough fine print In November 2025, NCSBE voted 3-2 to sign a memo with USCIS to run NC voter records through the federal SAVE database, the same citizenship-verification system the SAVE Act tried to make mandatory nationwide.
That federal bill cleared the House but has been voted down in the Senate, repeatedly.
It couldn't get even a simple majority, let alone the 60 votes it needs, with four Republicans crossing the aisle to help kill it.
Trump keeps trying to force it back through.
None of that changes anything here: the state signed up on its own, and it's already running.
NCSBE submitted 7.4 million voter records for review on April 17, 2026.
This is happening now, not later.
Other states have seen high false-positive rates.
The fine print Enough fine print Missouri ran a similar program, and local election officials found it flagged plenty of citizens as non-citizens.
In one county there, more than half the voters it flagged turned out to be naturalized citizens whose records hadn't caught up to their status, and dozens of county clerks from both parties signed a letter warning the lists were "deeply flawed." Even one of North Carolina's own board members called the SAVE database "not a very reliable tool," right before the board voted 3-2 to use it anyway.
If you're naturalized, registered at a naturalization ceremony, or have a name on file that doesn't match your birth certificate, you're more likely to land on the list.

Filed · NCGA Bills to shrink early voting.
SB 1084 would cut early voting from 17 days to 10 (opening weekend gone), and as written it applies to every election, including this November.
A stalled House bill, HB 66 , would cut it to six, killing Sunday voting entirely.
Senate leader Phil Berger has told reporters the version that passes might only touch primaries, but the bill on paper still hits the general, and a comment to the press isn't an amendment.
Until the text actually changes, treat November as a target.

Filed · NCGA A bigger rewrite is moving fast.
House Bill 958 is a sprawling, 30-page election bill, and it stopped being a someday problem.
In late June it cleared the House Rules committee and got slotted for a floor vote.
It's still a long way from law... it would have to pass the full House, then the Senate, then survive a likely Stein veto, and even some Republicans have balked.
