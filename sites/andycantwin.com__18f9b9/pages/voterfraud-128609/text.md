The pitch They call it election security Every session in Raleigh brings another bill to secure the elections.
Shorter early voting, more paperwork, new ways to challenge a ballot after it has already been cast.
Elections should be secure, no argument there.
The lucky part is that North Carolina audits itself and publishes what it finds, so nobody has to guess about any of this.
The 30-second version Fraud here is real and it is tiny.
After the 2016 election the state checked every ballot cast, nearly 4.8 million of them, and found 508 people who should not have voted.
That is one hundredth of one percent, and almost all of them were on probation and thought their rights were back.
The worst case anybody can name, Bladen County in 2018, was run by a paid operative for the Republican campaign, caught under laws already on the books, and thrown out 5 to 0 by a bipartisan board.
The State Board's own words for it: ineligible voting in North Carolina is "neither rampant nor non-existent." That is the whole answer.
