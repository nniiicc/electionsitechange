I own MrSippy BBQ in Rochester.
The doors are open five days a week, though there’s no day off from running it, and I manage about fifteen folks on my crew.
Every day I hear the same stories from the folks I serve: food, insurance, electricity, childcare, property taxes… all these costs are climbing at once and working families are falling behind, even though they’re doing everything right.
I know the struggle, because I’m living it too.
You deserve a State Senator who’ll work to improve our schools, bring down our property tax bills, protect our rivers and forests, and stand up for our personal freedoms, not one who lets large corporations write the rules while the rest of us pick up the tab.
I never planned on running for anything besides my own kitchen.
But I was asked to step up, and honestly… somebody who’s living what the rest of us are living ought to.
Cecil Where Cecil Stands Five things I hear about at every door.
This isn’t a full list of everything I’ve got a position on, just the five topics that come up most when I’m out talking with folks in the district.
Cost of Living & Property Taxes Property taxes just keep climbing.
Rochester’s rate went from $14.85 to $15.30 this year.
Strafford’s went from $19.76 to $20.96.
That’s not a one-year blip, it’s the trend, and it’s squeezing families and seniors on fixed incomes right out of the towns they’ve lived in for decades.
My first-term priority is real property tax relief, so folks can stay put.
Education Funding Every dollar the state doesn’t put toward public schools, or spends without real accountability for results, is a dollar your town has to make up on its own.
That gap shows up on your property tax bill, not a line item in Concord.
I want the state funding public education honestly, before it asks local budgets to cover the difference.
Healthcare & Rural Hospital Access Rural hospitals and the people who work in them are under real strain right now, staffing, costs, consolidation.
District 6 isn’t insulated from any of it.
My starting point: decisions made in Concord shouldn’t leave rural families further from the care they need.
Energy & Utility Costs New demand on the grid shouldn’t get paid for with higher bills for everyday ratepayers.
I want growth that pays its own way, not costs quietly passed down to families and small businesses through their electric bill.
Small Business & Local Economy MrSippy has never taken a special tax deal, and I don’t think it should have to compete against businesses that did.
I want a level playing field, accountable, capped incentives instead of open-ended giveaways, so small businesses aren’t squeezed out by whoever can afford the tax lawyers.
Meet Cecil Not a politician, a problem-solver.
Cecil Abels III runs MrSippy BBQ in Rochester, five days a week, standing over a smoker before sunrise, meeting a payroll, watching every invoice for propane and paper goods creep up a little more each month.
He knows what it feels like when costs keep climbing and nobody explains why, because he lives it too.
He studied to teach at Coe-Brown Northwood Academy before he ever ran a kitchen, and he still carries that with him, the patience to explain something twice, and the habit of meeting people where they actually are.
He grew up outdoors right here in District 6, hiking and biking Blue Job, swimming Bow Lake, fishing the Isinglass, and wants to keep it that way for the next generation.
He’s not a career politician.
He was recruited by the NH Senate Democratic Caucus to run, because what this seat needs isn’t another insider, it’s somebody who actually runs something and knows what it costs.
Cecil said yes.
Ask him a policy question he doesn’t know the answer to yet, and he’ll tell you the truth: “I don’t know yet, but give me a week and I’ll find out.” That’s not a weakness.
It’s the same skill that took MrSippy from one smoker to a fifteen-person crew: figure out what you don’t know, go find the people who do, and get it done.
