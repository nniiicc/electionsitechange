Being surrounded by the many citizen representatives who have served this state over the years was a pretty humbling reminder of what an honor — and responsibility — it is to be part of that history.
During the program, it was noted that few of us come to Pierre as polished politicians.
We come as ranchers and teachers, small business owners and retired grandpas, stay-at-home parents and pastors.
We come because, at its best, South Dakota’s citizen Legislature reflects the people who live here.
That’s part of why I’ve often described my first session as the best roller coaster I’ve ever been on.
And like any good roller coaster, there were highs, lows, and moments I wasn’t totally sure I’d keep my lunch.
High points: Standing up for public schools Some of the brightest moments came in our fights — and wins — for public education.
We passed SB 70, increasing funding for small, rural schools without raising local property taxes.
We passed HB 1092, giving stipends to student teachers.
And I was proud to support SB 6, which gave the Douglas School District a 0% interest loan for a new school to meet its growing needs.
We also defeated HB 1009 and HB 1020, two voucher bills that would have sent public tax dollars to private schools.
South Dakota’s public schools serve the vast majority of our kids — they deserve our investment, not competition for limited dollars.
The lows: Government overreach and undercutting local control Of course, not every turn on this ride was one to celebrate.
There were moments that felt like steep drops — especially when it came to the growing number of bills that expanded state control and limited local decision-making.
We saw more votes this session in favor of government overreach — from HB 1259 (the bathroom bill) to HB 1239 (putting books on trial), to a series of bills aimed at limiting South Dakotans’ long-standing right to citizen-led ballot initiatives.
At the same time, there was a steady push to override local school boards — telling districts across the state how to govern their classrooms from Pierre.
Meanwhile, legislation that would have supported long-term economic growth — like funding for regional airport infrastructure — couldn’t make it through.
Different issues, same theme: a growing tendency to centralize power in the Capitol while missing opportunities to invest in communities and trust local voices.
Still climbing: Issues that heed work There’s still so much work to do.
Criminal justice reform remains critical.
We passed SB 83, which moves toward treatment instead of jail for drug ingestion — but our prison infrastructure is crumbling, and we have to think bigger about rehabilitation and reducing recidivism.
Property taxes were a big topic this year (26 bills), with plenty of ideas but plenty of disagreements on solutions.
The summer study will give us a chance to dig in more deeply, and I look forward to the continued conversations around the Governor’s proposed half-cent optional sales tax for counties as one possible tool in the toolbox.
And we cannot build a strong economy without investing in childcare and education.
It’s all connected: workers need childcare, schools need teachers, and our future workforce starts in our classrooms.
Lessons from the underdog seat As a freshman legislator, I learned a lot — including when to speak up, when to hold back, and when to offer an amendment to make a bad bill slightly better versus when a bad bill just needs to die.
But more than anything, I learned that relationships matter.
Some of the people I disagreed with most fiercely on one bill were the very same people I worked alongside on another.
That’s the reality of a citizen legislature — and honestly, it’s what gives me hope.
Conclusion: The ride isn’t over This first session was a wild ride — and while the session may be over, the work certainly isn’t.
Looking ahead, I’m especially interested in continuing conversations around updating our education funding formula and how we can help schools innovate.
I’m also keeping a close eye on legislation around water, data centers, and tax exemptions, and how we ensure those policies truly benefit South Dakotans long-term.
As always, I know the best ideas often come from outside the Capitol.
So please continue to share your ideas, concerns, and events with me — I learn so much from those conversations.
