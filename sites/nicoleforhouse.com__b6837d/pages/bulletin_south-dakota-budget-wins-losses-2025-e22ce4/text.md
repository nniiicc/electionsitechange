Nicole Uhre-Balk Mar 18 Written By Nicole Uhre-Balk Read the full article at the Rapid City Journal → | Read the full article as an Image → Tuesday, March 18th, 2025 With the session basically over, except for veto day, our last vote was on the “G Bill.” Appropriators worked tirelessly to finalize the state budget, making tough choices about where to invest and where to cut.
Their work is essential in balancing immediate needs, long-term priorities, and fiscal responsibility.
While some critical funding was restored, we also saw cuts that could have lasting impacts on South Dakota communities.
Managing the budget is always about priorities, and while I’m grateful for the victories, I remain concerned about how some cuts will affect families, schools, and local services.
Rapid Recap: Wins, losses, and challenges ahead Restored funding wins South Dakota Public Broadcasting fully funded — a major win for public access to information.
State Library funding protected (though with staffing reductions).
Graduate medical education funding preserved, ensuring continued support for rural health care.
County and Tribal Veteran Services funding reinstated, keeping critical services for those who served.
Investing in immediate needs $7.5 million for emergency, disaster, and fire suppression funds. $2.1 million for rural healthcare reimbursements. $53.7 million in bonds paid off to reduce long-term state liabilities.
Budget cuts that hurt Reduced state support for dual credit programs, increasing costs for students.
Elimination of the TeachInSD website, a tool for recruiting teachers. $5.3 million cut in TANF funding, impacting families in need. $250,000 cut from meth prevention marketing, despite ongoing addiction challenges. $16.8 million reduction in maintenance and repair funds, potentially leading to bigger infrastructure costs down the road. $4.5 million decrease in Medicaid enrollment/utilization funding, raising concerns about access to care.
Looking ahead Riding this legislative roller coaster has been a huge honor, filled with challenges, learning, and incredible moments of collaboration.
I’ve had the privilege of working alongside dedicated colleagues and meeting amazing organizations that are making a real difference in South Dakota.
The passion and commitment I’ve witnessed give me hope for our state’s future, and I’m grateful to be part of this work.
The “off season” will continue to stay busy, as I want to stay connected and continue learning about the issues that matter most to our communities.
If you or your organization have an event, grand opening, or informational meeting, I’d love the opportunity to attend, listen, and learn.
Engaging directly with South Dakotans helps me be a better-informed policy advocate and voter.
Rep.
Nicole Uhre-Balk is a Democrat who represents District 32, which includes downtown and north Rapid City.
Reach her at nicole.uhre-balk@sdlegislature.gov .
