Monday, March 3rd, 2025
Tuesday, Feb. 25 was Crossover Day, the longest day of the session, where House bills must be heard in the House, and Senate bills in the Senate before moving forward.
Tradition calls for everyone to wear black, making it the only day this session I wasn’t in a yellow blazer.
With just two weeks to go, the sun is out longer, the weather is warming up, and the pace is still cooking as we wrap up House business and shift to voting on Senate bills.
Rapid Recap
- Prison Reset – After the failed vote to reconsider the prison funding transfer, Gov.
Larry Rhoden has called for a new task force to explore options before a special session in July.
This will give legislators time to evaluate solutions.
- SB 83 – A Criminal Justice Win – It was exciting to see SB 83 pass the House, shifting the penalty for ingesting certain controlled substances toward treatment instead of incarceration.
The margin of victory reflected the Democratic swing power.
Education Rollercoaster
High: HB 1092 passed, providing a stipend for student teachers who fill a teaching role — a small but important step in addressing our teacher shortage and keeping more young educators in South Dakota.
Lows: Education funding took several hits, including cuts to National Board Certification funding (HB 1039), increased costs for dual-credit courses (HB 1040), and no expansion of reduced lunch reimbursements (HB 1089).
These missed opportunities hurt both students and teachers.
The Mystery “Vehicle” Bills – Several vehicle bills passed, meaning major policy changes could be inserted at any time.
