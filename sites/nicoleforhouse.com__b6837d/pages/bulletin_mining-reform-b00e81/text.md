Nicole Uhre-Balk Sunday February 8th, 2026 A major bill I am bringing and encouraging constituents to watch is House Bill 1273 , which is expected to be scheduled for hearing Thursday, February 12th, in the House Agriculture and Natural Resources Committee.
This bill updates South Dakota’s mining statutes, which have not seen comprehensive revision since the 1980s, to reflect modern mining practices, close regulatory gaps, and strengthen protections for landowners and water resources.
Importantly, this legislation is not about saying no to mining.
Mining remains a necessary part of our state’s economy and national supply chains.
HB 1273 is about ensuring that mining is done responsibly, with appropriate guardrails in place to protect property owners, groundwater, surface water, and surrounding communities.
The bill is long, but the idea is simple: update mining statutes and a one-pager with more information can be found here .
Get the Facts on HB 1273 → Why This Matters for the Black Hills Mining policy in the Black Hills is not abstract.
It directly affects private property rights, water resources, and the long-term health of an area that supports tourism, which remains the second-largest driver of revenue in our state.
We must act soon, as more and more mining exploration is being done in the Black Hills, with 17% of the hills having mining claims on the land.
Protecting the Black Hills is both an environmental responsibility and an economic one.
I also want to acknowledge the work of Clear Water Alliance in helping shape and support this legislation, bringing practical expertise and community perspectives into the policy conversation.
How Constituents Can Engage Public participation will matter greatly for HB 1273 .
If this issue is important to you, I encourage you to contact your representative and members of the House Agriculture and Natural Resources Committee.
Testimony can be given in person or remotely, and written public comment is equally important.
When comments are submitted and shared on the official record, they help demonstrate public interest and ensure concerns are documented for current and future consideration.
Thoughtful engagement does make a difference.
