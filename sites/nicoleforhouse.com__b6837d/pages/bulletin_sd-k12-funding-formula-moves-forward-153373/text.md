Nicole Uhre-Balk

What moved forward
House Bill 1205 received a full hearing in the Education Committee and was referred to House Appropriations with a motion of no recommendation, meaning the conversation continues.
This bill makes a narrow but important adjustment to South Dakota’s K–12 funding formula.
Currently, state aid grows by 3% or inflation, whichever is less.
When inflation exceeds 3%, schools lose purchasing power even if funding technically increases.
HB 1205 changes one word so that growth would be 3% or inflation, whichever is greater.
The goal is straightforward: prevent inflation from quietly cutting into classrooms, align the formula with our recent commitment to teacher pay, and reduce long-term pressure on local property taxes by ensuring the state meets its responsibility.
The discussion now moves to Appropriations, where the fiscal details will receive closer examination.
House Bill 1273, my proposal to modernize and align mining statutes under one regulatory framework, did not advance out of committee.
While that outcome is disappointing, the hearing provided valuable insight.
We heard constructive feedback and identified areas where the legislation could be strengthened.
Responsible development and responsible oversight must go hand in hand, and that conversation will continue.
What raised concerns
House Bill 1209, an e-verify mandate, passed the House.
While employment verification may appear straightforward in principle, the enforcement structure raises concern.
The bill allows the attorney general to review complaints against businesses based solely on suspicion.
In practice, that creates risk for small businesses and opens the door to complaints driven by appearance or language rather than evidence.
House Bill 1153, which would have allowed medical providers and institutions to refuse services based on moral or religious objections, failed on the House floor.
Freedom of religion is a core constitutional principle.
At the same time, patient care must be guided by established medical standards.
I was encouraged to see the House reject a proposal that could have allowed denial of care.
What’s coming next
Budget conversations are accelerating.
It was encouraging to hear that the state may be able to support at least a 1% increase for education, Medicaid providers, and state employees, with the possibility of slightly more.
These investments directly affect classrooms, healthcare access, and the stability of our state workforce.
This week, our Democratic caucus will present HB 1281, the Feed Families, Fund Schools Act.
This comprehensive proposal would raise the state’s sales tax to 5% (still the lowest in the region), bring the state sales tax on groceries to 0%, establish South Dakota’s first School Construction Fund through 0% revolving loans, providing money for classrooms by lowering property-tax-increasing opt-outs.
The aim is to address affordability while strengthening core public services.
I will also be bringing HB 1300, which would exempt victims of human trafficking or domestic abuse from certain clemency notice requirements, and HB 1299, which permits the use of biological filtration systems in certain lodging-based recreational water facilities.
