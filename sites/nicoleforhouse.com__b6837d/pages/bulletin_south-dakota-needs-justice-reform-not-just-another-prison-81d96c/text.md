South Dakota Needs Justice Reform, Not Just Another Prison
Rep.
Nicole Uhre-Balk
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
Monday, February 24th, 2025
Today, the motion to reconsider an additional $148 million in funding for a new men’s prison failed, meaning no new funds have been allocated for the project at this time.
A yes vote was to allow another vote.
I was open to reconsidering my vote and was hoping to get confirmation of a plan to both address the failing infrastructure and pass meaningful incarceration reform.
Democrats have been trying to get this addressed for decades, and I needed confirmation that a no vote would actually accomplish that.
Let me be clear: I never wanted this prison.
But I also could not ignore the fact that our current facility, built in 1881, is now 144 years old, operating at 182% of capacity, has only one classroom for 12 people out of 900 inmates to participate in rehabilitation, and is generally considered an inhumane facility.
My vote to reconsider was not a vote for mass incarceration—it was an acknowledgment that doing nothing also has consequences.
Choosing to vote yes or no does NOT reflect any person's value of the current men's prison plan.
Personally, as I've shared, I have significant concerns about the current prison plan that need to be addressed before I would vote yes to construct a prison outside of HB 1025A.
This legislature has spent years talking about the need for a new prison, but it has failed to have the harder conversation about why our prison population keeps growing in the first place.
The reality is:
- Native Americans are disproportionately incarcerated in South Dakota.
- Our laws treat addiction as a crime instead of a health issue.
- Recidivism remains high because we prioritize punishment over rehabilitation.
- Severe overcrowding—at nearly double capacity—limits access to education, rehabilitation, and treatment programs, keeping people trapped in a cycle of incarceration.
Now that the funding has failed, the question remains: What happens next?
If we’re not building a prison, are we going to fix the sentencing laws that put so many people there?
How can we both make the facility more humane for current prisoners—by providing more room for rehabilitation—and invest in preventative measures?
A place to start is supporting practical sentencing reforms that reduce incarceration for nonviolent drug offenses while ensuring access to treatment.
That’s why I will be supporting SB 83, which makes long-overdue changes to how South Dakota handles simple drug ingestion cases.
The bill shifts the focus toward supervised probation and treatment rather than immediate incarceration—an approach that keeps people out of prison and connected to the help they need.
South Dakota cannot wait until our prison system collapses under its own weight to take action.
The failure of this funding does not mean the problem goes away—we need to do more to fix the system itself.
Rep.
Nicole Uhre-Balk
South Dakota House of Representatives, District 32
Follow up: A constituent questioned the vote in favor of HB 1025, arguing that expanding the prison system contradicts the need for reforming harsh drug laws.
In response, Rep.
Erin Healy explained that the current prison conditions are inhumane and unsafe for both inmates and staff.
Overcrowding severely limits rehabilitation efforts, with men stacked on top of each other—sometimes three to a room—despite current standards being set much lower.
A new facility is necessary to improve safety and expand rehabilitation services.
The constituent then asked whether alternatives, such as housing and rehabilitation programs outside of prison, could be funded instead.
Rep.
Healy reinforced that the current prison conditions are unsafe and overcrowded, limiting rehabilitation opportunities.
She referenced a note from Secretary Wasco, explaining that while work-release space is sufficient, there is a severe shortage of over 1,300 medium-security beds, making the new prison necessary.
The constituent acknowledged the clarification, concluding the discussion.
A sincere thank you to Rep.
Healy for her thoughtful engagement and for helping provide key information on this issue!
