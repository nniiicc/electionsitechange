Meet Nicole
Rooted in the Black Hills
Nicole has called the Black Hills home for most of her life.
Some of her earliest memories are of playing among the hay bales at her family’s Rocking Chair Ranch.
Watching her father run a ranch and small business while serving in the military taught her the value of hard work, responsibility, and getting your hands dirty.
After moving into Rapid City and attending Pinedale Elementary, Nicole saw her mother model a lifelong commitment to learning and community involvement.
Her family’s “Sunday Fun Days” also sparked a lasting love of rock climbing, mountain biking, backpacking, and exploring the outdoors.
These early experiences led Nicole toward a life of education, service, and adventure.
She led an afterschool program and student garden at the Oyate Center in Lakota Homes, participated in service and leadership programs in Nepal and Europe, contributed to prairie restoration work in Wisconsin, and climbed Mount Rainier and Mount Fuji through the Teacher-Ranger-Teacher program.
Educator and Lifelong Learner
Nicole earned degrees in Biology and Environmental Studies from Bethel University.
After working for the U.S.
Forest Service, she returned to school and earned a Master of Science in Curriculum and Instruction from Black Hills State University.
Over more than a decade in education, Nicole has taught in communities along Interstate 90, from the Muckleshoot Tribal School near Mount Rainier to Central High School in Rapid City and a STEM lab in Boston.
Her work has focused on middle and high school biology, environmental science, and computer science.
Today, Nicole works at Compass, formerly Technology & Innovation in Education, where she supports educators across South Dakota.
Her work includes curriculum mapping, computational thinking, artificial intelligence, computer science education, relational trust, and the Oceti Sakowin Essential Understandings.
Whether teaching students or supporting fellow educators, Nicole believes strong schools begin with listening to educators, respecting local communities, and helping students understand and shape a changing world.
From Community to Capitol
Nicole’s commitment to service extends beyond the classroom.
She has served on the Rapid City Sustainability Committee and the board of Friends of Rapid City Parks, contributing her experience to work that strengthens neighborhoods, protects shared spaces, and improves the quality of life in Rapid City.
She brought that same community-focused approach to the South Dakota Legislature.
Nicole believes effective public service requires preparation, curiosity, empathy, and a willingness to work across differences.
At the Capitol, she focuses on listening to the people of District 32, understanding the details behind each issue, and pursuing practical solutions that strengthen South Dakota’s communities.
Beyond the Work
Outside of work and public service, Nicole is happiest exploring the Black Hills and spending time with the people and animals she loves.
She enjoys hiking, rock climbing, mountain biking, and other outdoor adventures.
Nicole also cherishes time with her four nieces and nephews, her spouse of more than ten years, Forest Balk, and their two pugs, Pixel and Qubit.
