Investing in Rapid City’s Future: Why SB 127 Deserves Our Support
Rep.
Nicole Uhre-Balk
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
Sunday, March 9th, 2025
Rapid City is growing fast.
Our economy, our tourism industry, and our local businesses all depend on a modern, well-equipped airport to keep up with that growth.
But right now, our infrastructure isn’t keeping pace, and if we don’t act soon, we risk falling behind.
That’s why I support SB 127, a critical investment in our state’s airports—particularly in Rapid City and Sioux Falls, which handle 95% of South Dakota’s air travel.
Originally introduced as SB 132, this bill was designed to invest $30 million into airport expansion, ensuring that our terminals can handle increasing passenger numbers, attract new airline routes, and keep air travel affordable and accessible for South Dakotans.
Unfortunately, due to a procedural mishap, SB 132 was killed on the Senate floor, but its spirit lives on in SB 127, which still has a chance to make a difference.
Rapid City’s Airport Needs Upgrades for Growth and Safety
While touring Rapid City Regional Airport, I saw firsthand the urgent need for expansion—not just to support economic growth, but to improve safety conditions for workers and travelers alike.
- Baggage Handling Area Concerns: The space constraints in the baggage area create safety hazards for workers.
Employees are forced to operate in tight, outdated conditions, increasing the risk of injury.
- Terminal Congestion: Passenger traffic is at record levels, and our current infrastructure is struggling to keep up.
- Ongoing Construction Projects: Rapid City is already investing in upgrades, but without state funding, these projects could be delayed or incomplete.
- Tourism and Business Travel Demands: As a gateway to Mount Rushmore, the Black Hills, and major events like the Sturgis Motorcycle Rally, Rapid City needs an airport that can handle increased demand.
Rapid City’s airport is currently in a multi-phase expansion project totaling over $200 million.
This isn’t just about convenience—it’s about keeping up with demand, ensuring worker safety, and securing South Dakota’s place as a competitive travel hub.
How SB 127 Unlocks Federal Dollars for South Dakota
A major reason to pass SB 127 is to leverage federal funds that multiply our investment.
The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) Airport Improvement Program (AIP) provides grants for airport infrastructure, but state and local contributions are required to unlock those dollars.
Here’s how federal matching works under the AIP program:
- Large and Medium Primary Hub Airports (like Sioux Falls and Rapid City) are typically eligible for FAA grants covering 75% to 80% of project costs.
- Small Primary, Reliever, and General Aviation Airports (like Watertown, Pierre, and Spearfish) are eligible for 90% to 95% federal funding, meaning local and state funds cover just 5-10% of costs.
This means that every dollar South Dakota invests in airport improvements could be multiplied by federal funding.
What This Means in Real Numbers
- If South Dakota allocates $30 million, and the FAA matches at 90%, that turns into a $300 million total investment in our airports.
- If the match is lower, say 75% for a large airport project, then $30 million could bring in an additional $90 million—a $120 million impact.
- Without a state commitment, we risk losing out on these federal dollars, which could instead go to other states that prioritize airport funding.
Why This Matters for Rapid City
For Rapid City, airport expansion isn’t just about adding a few gates—it’s about securing our economic future.
Air travel plays a direct role in tourism, business recruitment, and job creation, making it one of the smartest investments we can make.
More Flights, Lower Fares
- Right now, South Dakotans pay more to fly due to limited airline competition.
- Expanding Rapid City Regional Airport means more gates, more airlines, and more competitive fares—saving travelers money and making our region more accessible.
- The FAA prioritizes funding for airports that increase passenger capacity, meaning a state match would directly help attract new airlines and routes.
Strengthening Tourism
- The Black Hills region is a world-class destination, drawing millions of visitors each year.
- More flights and better airport facilities increase accessibility for tourists, boosting our hotels, restaurants, and attractions.
- A well-connected airport ensures we remain a top choice for travelers, rather than losing them to larger regional hubs like Omaha or Denver.
Fueling Business Growth
- Businesses looking to relocate consider airport accessibility a key factor.
- Without expansion, Rapid City risks losing out on new companies, job opportunities, and economic investment to cities with better infrastructure.
- North Dakota has aggressively invested in its airports, and it’s working—South Dakota must do the same to stay competitive.
Enhancing Medical and Emergency Services
- Airports are vital for medical evacuations and emergency transport.
- Sioux Falls and Rapid City serve as regional hubs for life-saving flights, including organ transplants, trauma care, and neonatal transport.
- If we don’t expand and modernize, we risk straining these essential services.
Opposition and The Reality of State Investment
Critics argue that state funding hasn’t traditionally gone toward airport terminals and that the FAA should cover these costs.
But federal funding rules have changed—South Dakota must take the lead in investing in our own infrastructure.
The Bureau of Finance and Management (BFM) opposes SB 127, saying that South Dakota has historically relied on federal AIP grants without using state general funds.
However, they fail to acknowledge:
- The FAA prioritizes funding for airports that provide local or state matches.
- North Dakota has already stepped up, aggressively funding its airports.
- Without investment, we risk losing airline competition, economic growth, and tourism dollars to other states.
The Time to Act Is Now
South Dakota took an important step last year by allocating $10 million for airport improvements, but it wasn’t nearly enough to meet the growing demands of our largest airports.
- Rapid City Regional Airport is in the middle of a multi-phase expansion project totaling over $200 million.
- Sioux Falls Airport has a $110 million project underway but still faces a $20 million shortfall.
- If we fail to invest now, these projects could be delayed or even canceled, causing South Dakota to lose out on federal funds and economic opportunities.
SB 127 gives us another chance to do what’s right for Rapid City, for South Dakota, and for our economic future.
It’s time to invest in the infrastructure that connects us to the world, creates jobs, and ensures our region continues to thrive.
I urge our state legislators to support SB 127 and make sure Rapid City gets the resources it needs to remain a strong, competitive hub for travel, business, and tourism.
Let’s invest in our future—let’s get this done.
