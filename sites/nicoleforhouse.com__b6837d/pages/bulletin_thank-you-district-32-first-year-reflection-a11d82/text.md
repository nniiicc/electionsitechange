Nicole Uhre-Balk Monday, November 10th, 2025 It’s hard to believe it’s already been a year since my team helped elect me to the South Dakota House of Representatives.
I say it often, but being in the Legislature really is like the best roller coaster I’ve ever been on.
There were incredible highs — it was the 100th Legislative Session and the most women ever to serve in the South Dakota Legislature.
But there were sharp turns, too.
On my very first night in Pierre, before I’d even unpacked, I found myself at dinner surrounded by pipeline lobbyists while pipeline protesters filled the rest of Mad Mary’s Steakhouse.
That moment set the tone for the year ahead.
In South Dakota politics, you often find yourself right in the middle — literally and politically.
I came, I saw, and I did my best to represent the most constituents in District 32 to the best of my ability.
Education and community wins As a lifelong educator, I was especially proud to vote “yes” on several bills supporting students, teachers, and families: SB 70: Improved funding for rural schools, helping smaller districts keep teachers and programs in place.
HB 1092: Created stipends for student teachers, reducing financial barriers for future educators.
Defeated two voucher proposals: On the House Education Committee, I opposed efforts to divert public money to private schools, protecting taxpayer dollars for public education.
I also supported key investments that strengthen South Dakota families and communities: Graduate Medical Education (GME): Restored funding to train doctors in-state, helping rural and underserved areas attract physicians.
South Dakota Public Broadcasting (SDPB): Fully funded our state’s public broadcaster to preserve local journalism and educational programming.
It was devastating to see our efforts undercut by recent federal cuts. $180,000 for sexual assault kits: Ensures evidence is processed quickly so justice isn’t delayed. $7.5 million for disaster and fire suppression: Strengthens our ability to respond to natural disasters statewide.
Each of these investments adds up to something meaningful: stronger schools, healthier families, safer communities, and a more informed democracy.
Harder lessons Not every bill reflected our best selves.
We saw proposals to police bathrooms, put books and ideas on trial, and weaken the citizen ballot initiative process.
These efforts expanded state control over local communities and personal freedoms.
Those moments reminded me why South Dakotans value local voices — and why we have to keep protecting them.
We also have work to do on the budget.
Cuts to maintenance, Medicaid, and Temporary Assistance for Needy Families weaken the supports that hold our communities together.
The Joint Democratic Caucus urged Governor Rhoden to call a special session to use reserves for SNAP benefits, because no one should go hungry while our budget sits in surplus.
And with just a 1.25 percent increase for schools, teachers are again asked to do more with less.
The steady erosion of essential services is hard to describe — it takes a toll that ripples through classrooms, clinics, and kitchen tables.
Next session, I’ll continue pushing for sustainable, locally centered solutions, including exploring zero-percent loans for schools through the SD Housing Infrastructure Financing Program so districts can invest in safe, modern learning spaces without draining classroom resources or increasing property taxes.
Building bridges and carrying the work forward Not everything makes the headlines, but a lot of good work happens quietly in the middle.
Nearly every conversation I had — no matter the party — was a good one.
When I didn’t know the answer, I asked.
When I disagreed, I listened.
That approach paid off.
I tied with Representatives Mike Derby and Curt Massie, both Republicans, in Elevate Rapid City’s legislative scorecard and placed in the top five overall.
I also earned an A+ rating from the Voter Defense Association for supporting transparency and open government.
This summer, I served on the Community Support Providers (CSP) Summer Study Committee, which brought together legislators, families, and providers to examine how South Dakota supports individuals with disabilities.
The committee voted to create an ongoing oversight group — a small step toward continuing the conversation to improve care and accountability.
I was also selected for two national leadership programs — the Bowhay Institute for Legislative Leadership Development (BILLD) and the State Legislative Leaders Foundation (SLLF) — to strengthen my skills and bring new ideas home to South Dakota.
In October, we returned for a special session on the state prison project.
My “yes” vote was one of the hardest I’ve made, but leaving the current penitentiary as it is simply wasn’t an option.
The conditions are unsafe for employees and inhumane for inmates.
Knowing that our state is beginning to focus more on rehabilitation and prevention helped me move forward with that decision.
You can find more information and details at nicoleforhouse.com/bulliten .
And this issue brings up why investing early matters.
Research shows that access to quality early education reduces incarceration rates and improves long-term success.
This year, I was invited to attend The Hunt Institute’s National Conference on Early Childhood Engagement and to serve as a panelist at South Dakota’s Early Learner Summit.
When we invest in kids early, we build safer, stronger communities for everyone.
Closing reflection Serving in the Legislature has been an incredible honor and a crash course in humility, patience, and persistence.
I’m learning to stand alone when necessary, to balance urgency with understanding, and to stay grounded in empathy.
Like many of you, I’m also trying to make sense of the terrifying uncertainty at the national level, but I stay focused on representing South Dakotans with fairness, transparency and hope.
Of course, none of this would be possible without the support and technical assistance from my spouse, Forest Balk, and the “A Team”: Paul Sauser, Annie Dunkel, Cody French, Curt Prochardt and Mary Kelley.
I could list so many more who helped at events, door-knocking, and making phone calls, but I hope the rest of the Democratic cousins, uncles, and aunties know who you are.
I’m deeply grateful for my colleagues, constituents, and community members who supported me this year.
Thank you, District 32 — and so many supporters across the state — for trusting me with this first ride.
I’m grateful for your partnership, your feedback, and everything you’ve taught me this year.
Democracy is a team sport, and I am so excited to be on your team!
We all do better when we all do better.
