What happened, in four lines
- 2025-04-17.
Sen.
Dan Roberts (R-Mountain Brook) introduced SB330.
Sen.
Jabo Waggoner (R-Vestavia Hills) was a named co-sponsor — not merely a supporter — alongside Sen.
Shay Shelnutt (R-Trussville).[1]
- 2025-05-07.
Gov.
Ivey signed SB330 into law as Act 2025-297, shrinking the Birmingham Water Works Board from 9 seats to 7 and cutting the City of Birmingham's share from 6 seats to 2.
The utility was rebranded "Central Alabama Water."[5][1]
- 2026-02-20.
Two former board members filed a federal lawsuit in the Northern District of Alabama seeking $1.6 billion in damages plus injunctive relief.
Waggoner is a named defendant, alongside Gov.
Ivey, Lt.
Gov.
Ainsworth, former Gov.
Bentley, Sen.
Roberts, and county commissioners.[6]
- Waggoner's response.
Per WBRC: "Senators Waggoner and Roberts both said they will have no comment on the lawsuit."[6] The senator who co-sponsored the statute won't defend it to a Birmingham newsroom.
The people of SD-16 deserve better than "no comment" on a case that names their senator.
Everything in this section is from primary state records (Alison, LegiScan), the plaintiffs' own filings, or on-the-record reporting from WBRC, WBHM, the Alabama Reflector, the Alabama Political Reporter, the Alabama Daily News, and 1819 News.

Waggoner is not hiding from why he wrote the bill.
He explained it in a committee hearing.
He explained it to a Birmingham newspaper.
He co-signed a defense of it in four Alabama newsrooms the week before the Senate vote.
His rationale is on the record, in his own name, in his own words.
Read them together.
Then read the sentence he gave a Birmingham TV station the morning the federal complaint was filed.
1.
His stated reason for being back for a second bite, ten years after the first
Two things live inside that quote.
The first is Waggoner's stated motive: the same complaints from the same constituents that pushed him to pass the 2015 reform pushed him to pass the 2025 one.
The second is Waggoner's own concession — the 2015 statute he sponsored, in his words, "was very successful" at the time, "but here we are again." His argument for the 2025 takeover is that his 2015 takeover did not hold.
Sixty years in Montgomery, two rewrites of the same board, and by his own account we are back where we started.
2.
His framing that the push was consensus, not a partisan grab
This is the "bipartisan cover" theory of the case: 21 Democratic and Republican legislators signed the January 2024 audit letter, so — Waggoner argued — the reform push had support across the aisle.
Worth holding against the roll call fifteen months later.
When SB330 actually passed the House on 2025-05-01, it broke 66–27 straight down party lines.[9] The bipartisan letter was 2024.
The party-line vote was 2025.
Whatever consensus existed at the audit stage did not survive to the takeover stage.
3.
His published defense of the bill — in his own byline
Two days before the Senate vote, Waggoner co-signed a defense of SB330 with Sen.
Dan Roberts, Sen.
Shay Shelnutt, and Rep.
Jim Carns.
It ran on 2025-04-22 across the Alabama Political Reporter, 1819 News, the Alabama Daily News, and Yellowhammer News.[3][4] Waggoner's name is on the byline.
These are his stated reasons, in print, in his voice:
"While the City of Birmingham is home to only 25 percent of the Water Board's customer base, it disproportionally controls the Board through its power to appoint two-thirds of the directors...
This is taxation without representation, the same complaint that led to the Boston tea party."
"BWWB bills and collects for only about half (50%) of the water it treats."
"BWWB is on a 300-year replacement cycle for its pipe infrastructure" — against an industry standard of roughly 100 years.
"The rates charged by the BWWB are among the highest in the Southeast" — an "unacceptable barrier to economic growth."
"Reform isn't just overdue, it is essential to avoid the type of crisis which recently occurred in Jackson, MS."
"We urge our colleagues in the Legislature to support SB330, not because it is easy, but because it is right."
This is not opposition research.
This is the senator's own byline.
He argued the case for the bill publicly, in four Alabama newsrooms, under his own name, and he closed with "not because it is easy, but because it is right." He knew the record he was building.
4.
And then the complaint hit federal court
Ten months after the op-ed, plaintiffs William Muhammad and Brenda Lewis filed a $1.6 billion federal complaint in the Northern District of Alabama, with Waggoner named first among the defendants.
WBRC — the same Birmingham newsroom whose viewers include a fair share of the customers the op-ed claimed to be helping — called him for comment.
Two words back.
Five months on, the case is very much alive.
On 2026-06-08 the Central Alabama Water board — one of the defendants alongside Waggoner — formally retained a second law firm, Montgomery's Capell & Howard, P.C., specifically to defend the Muhammad case, following an executive session on pending litigation.[22] Defendants staffing up is not the posture of a case on its way out.
The check is still due, and the senator whose name leads the caption is still silent.
Here is the tension, and it is his own.
He wrote the bill.
He passed the bill.
He defended the bill in print — in his own byline, in four Alabama newsrooms, closing with "not because it is easy, but because it is right." He will not defend the bill under oath.
Voters in SD-16 are entitled to hear him do the fourth.

Rep.

Former BWWB Chairwoman Tereshia Huffman testified in a House committee hearing that the bill "made the board more political by allowing elected officials to appoint themselves or other politicians to the board."[18]
Whether Act 2025-297 survives constitutional review is a question for Judge Manasco and the parties to litigate.
Spencer's question is simpler and it's the one every Alabama voter can answer: why did the senator whose name is on the bill refuse to defend it when the federal complaint was filed?
The constitutional questions here — Contract Clause, Takings, Equal Protection — are for the federal court to decide, and Judge Manasco has already declined to grant emergency relief.
That's the process working.
Spencer's issue is upstream of the courtroom.

If it wasn't, own that.
"No comment" isn't representation.
It's an escape hatch.
