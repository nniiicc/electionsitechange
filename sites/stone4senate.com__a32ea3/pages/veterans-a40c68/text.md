What I can commit to today Six questions I can answer right now — because they're about the kind of representative I intend to be.
Will you commit to meeting regularly with local veterans and Veterans Service Organizations?
Yes Representation requires being accessible to the people you represent.
I will meet with veterans, Veterans Service Organizations, military families, caregivers, and survivors — including people who disagree with me.
I won't expect veterans to come to Montgomery to be heard.
Will you commit to holding regular town halls, listening sessions, or public forums focused on issues affecting Alabama veterans?
Yes — but I don't think the answer always needs to be asking veterans to attend another meeting.
If veterans and Veterans Service Organizations are already gathering, I'll come to you.
I'll make myself available to attend your meetings, listening sessions, events, and other gatherings when invited.
My job as your senator shouldn't be to create another obligation on your calendar just so I can say I held a town hall.
It should be to make myself accessible in the places where veterans are already doing the work and having these conversations.
And when a dedicated public forum would be useful, I'll be available to hold one.
How will you keep veterans informed about your legislative work, votes, proposed legislation, and progress on veteran-related issues?
I've already made this commitment to everyone in District 16: if elected, I will publish a monthly report explaining what happened in Montgomery, how I voted, and why.
Veterans shouldn't have to search legislative databases or depend on campaign mail to learn what their senator is doing.
If I vote on legislation affecting Alabama veterans, I'll tell you how I voted and explain why.

What do you believe is the most significant issue facing Alabama veterans today, and why?
From what I've read, and from conversations with veterans in my own family, one concern keeps bothering me: we make promises to people when they serve, and too often accessing the benefits and services they've been promised can be difficult, confusing, or painfully slow.
But I also recognize the limits of my own experience.
Talking to people in my own bubble isn't enough.
So if you're asking me to name the single most significant issue facing Alabama veterans today, my honest answer is: I don't know yet.
