Waggoner's campaign committee reported a $60,000 expenditure to Gene Hallman , its stated purpose — in the filing's own words — “ for the publication of the book ‘Jabo Waggoner: An Alabama Political Legend.’ ” [1] Who Hallman is.
He chairs the J.T.
Waggoner Book Foundation , a 501(c)(3) whose stated purpose is “to write a book on J.T.
Waggoner,” and it operates out of the office of his company, the sports/event-marketing firm Eventive Sports (600 Luckie Drive, Birmingham). [2] The board — and the lobbyist.
Its directors include Curt Lee , a registered Alabama lobbyist, who filed the state paperwork for a Feb.
12, 2025 book-signing at RSA Plaza where, in the filing's own words, “legislators, lobbyists and others” bought the senator's book, “funded through outside contributions from businesses … and associations.” [3] The pledge.
The board signed organizing documents promising “no part of the net earnings … shall inure to … any private person” — and named no charity or cause to receive the book's proceeds. [4] Then the accounting stopped.
The IRS revoked the foundation's tax-exempt status in 2026 for failing to file its returns three years running, and its Alabama charitable registration expired .
There is no public financial accounting of the money. [5] [6] ▶ Video: The Book Every figure on screen is on this page, sourced.
The payment The Alabama Fair Campaign Practices Act (FCPA) database records every campaign expenditure over $50, filed under oath by the committee.
Waggoner's committee reported a $60,000 payment to Gene Hallman on August 5, 2024 , and the filing's purpose field states, in full: “ FOR THE PUBLICATION OF THE BOOK ‘JABO WAGGONER: AN ALABAMA POLITICAL LEGEND.’ ” [1] The receipt for that line is hosted below .
Campaign expenditure — 2024-08-05 Committee of James T.
Waggoner Jr. → Gene Hallman — $60,000 Gene Hallman is the co-founder of Eventive Sports (formerly Bruno Event Team) and the Chairman of the J.T.
Waggoner Book Foundation. [2] He is not a book publisher; the biography was written by columnist Steve Flowers and published by New South, Inc. [7] The charity, and who runs it The J.T.
Waggoner Book Foundation (EIN 93-2739387) is a real 501(c)(3), formed in 2023.
Its state charitable-registration form lists its purpose plainly: “Contributions will be solicited and used to write a book on J.T.
Waggoner.” The signer of that registration is Gene Hallman, Chairman .
The listed address — 600 Luckie Drive, Suite 100, Birmingham — and contact email ( [email protected] ) are those of Hallman's company, Eventive Sports. [2] In other words: a senator's campaign paid the chairman of a charity, run from that chairman's own company office, whose single purpose is a flattering biography of the senator.
The board — and the lobbyist The foundation's bylaws are signed by four directors: Gene Hallman, Curt Lee , Nick Sellers, and Christy Swaid. [4] A registered Alabama lobbyist by the same name and mailing address — D.
Curt Lee , P.O.
Box 1605, Jasper — appears on the state's lobbyist rolls, and it was he who filed the state disclosure for the foundation's book-signing. [3] The signing On November 9, 2024, lobbyist Curt Lee filed Alabama Ethics Commission precertification #2265 on the foundation's behalf, approved November 12.
It describes the event in the foundation's own words: “On February 12, 2025, the J.T.
Waggoner Book Foundation would like to host the Alabama Legislature to a ‘book signing’ at the RSA Plaza.
Senator Waggoner will attend and sign books that are purchased from the foundation by legislators, lobbyists and others.
Additionally, the J.T.
Waggoner Book Foundation will be funding this event through outside contributions from businesses and or individuals and or associations.” [3] That is the state's public record, verbatim: legislators and lobbyists buying a sitting senator's book, at an event funded by businesses and associations.
The pledge — and the silence The foundation's Certificate of Formation and Bylaws both bar private benefit: “No part of the net earnings … shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its directors … officers or other private person” — while permitting “reasonable compensation for services rendered.” [4] Nowhere in the records does the foundation name a charity, scholarship, or cause that receives the book's proceeds.
Then the paper trail runs out.
We asked the State of Alabama for the foundation's financial accounting under the Open Records Act.
The Attorney General produced the formation paperwork — and no annual financial report, no Form 990, and no renewal of the registration. [6] The registration itself is expired (Nov.
29, 2024). [6] And the IRS has placed the foundation on its Auto-Revocation List : its 501(c)(3) status was automatically revoked in 2026 for “not filing a Form 990-series return or notice for three consecutive years,” and it has not been reinstated. [5] A charity created to publish a book about the senator, run out of his event-marketer's office, with a lobbyist on its board — that took $60,000 of campaign money and then filed nothing the public can inspect, until the IRS revoked it for not filing at all.

Where Spencer stands This isn't about whether a legislator can be honored.
It's about accountability.
Campaign donations are given to win elections and serve constituents — not to route $60,000 into a private foundation, controlled by a political fundraiser, to publish a flattering biography of the man himself.
And when the public asks a charity to account for that money, there should be an answer on file.
Here, there isn't one.
