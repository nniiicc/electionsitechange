Fixing and Fully-Funding Public Education As a public school teacherOhio's public schools are the foundation of our communities and the engine of our economy.
But decades of underfunding, political meddling, and a growing private voucher program are leaving our kids, and our teachers, behind.
Rick is committed to fixing Ohio's broken school funding system and making sure every child in Wood County gets a world-class education.
The Problem Ohio has long struggled to equitably fund its public schools, relying heavily on local property taxes: a system that creates dramatic inequality between wealthy and low-income districts.
The Fair School Funding Plan, developed by Ohio educators using district spending data and best practices, has provided a path forward: The FSFP raised the state's base cost per pupil from $# in FY 2021 to $# in FY 2025, and has provided approximately $# billion in increased state funding since FY 2022: but it must be made permanent.
Despite this progress, the plan has not been fully implemented, and Ohio's voucher program has diverted hundreds of millions of public dollars away from public schools, and overwhelmingly not to low-income families who need them most.
Data shows that nearly 90% of Ohio voucher program participants are not low-income, making it a taxpayer-funded subsidy for private schools for the wealthy.
Meanwhile, Ohio teachers are underpaid, under-resourced, and being forced to spend their own money on classroom supplies.
Teacher retirements are under threat, substitute teacher quality has declined, and school mental health resources are woefully inadequate.
My Commitments Support full, permanent implementation of the Fair School Funding Plan.
End Ohio's private school voucher program, which overwhelmingly subsidizes families who were already attending private school.
Secure teacher retirement and mandate Cost-of-Living Adjustments (COLAs) so educators don't outlive their savings.
Dramatically increase funding for school mental health resources.
Expand loan availability for trade schools and apprenticeship programs as a complement to four-year degrees.
Support universal pre-K so every Ohio child starts school ready to succeed.
Environment & Ohio's Agricultural Future Wood County is farm country.
Our agricultural heritage is part of who we are, and the men and women who farm our land deserve a state government that has their back: on trade, on technology, and on protecting the soil, water, and air they depend on.
I'm also committed to responsible environmental stewardship and a transition to clean energy that protects Ohio families and natural resources.
The Problem Ohio's farmers face a triple threat: tight margins, rapid technological change, and trade policy instability.
Soybean farmers have been particularly hard hit by tariffs and trade wars, with market disruptions threatening the viability of family farms.
Meanwhile, farmland loss continues as development pressure mounts.
Locally, the rapid expansion of data centers and industrial development is creating new environmental risks for Wood County communities and farmland.
My Commitments Encourage and subsidize green energy projects, including nuclear, to give Ohio farmers and homeowners access to cleaner, more affordable energy.
Oppose reckless trade policies that harm Ohio's soybean, corn, and grain farmers.
Support responsible wildlife reintroduction programs in Ohio's state forests and parks to restore ecological balance and support hunting and tourism.
Require extensive environmental impact studies before approving new data centers, with mandatory plans for energy usage offsets and end-of-lifecycle facility management.
Protect Ohio's water, soil, and air quality for future generations of farmers and families.
Utility Accountability & Ending Corruption In 2020, Ohio became the center of the largest energy bribery scandal in American history.
The HB 6 scandal - a $1.3 billion ratepayer-funded bailout of two failing nuclear and coal plants - exposed how deep the rot of special interest corruption runs in Columbus.
Our families are still paying the price.
I will fight to restore integrity and accountability to Ohio's energy policy.
The Problem Ohio House Bill 6 was sold to the public as clean energy legislation.
In reality, it was a $1.3 billion bailout for two uncompetitive power plants, baked into ratepayer utility bills, secured through over $60 million in bribes to state officials.
Former House Speaker Larry Householder and associates were convicted.
The scandal's roots run deep, implicating lobbyists, dark money, and utility companies that placed their profits above Ohio families.
In August 2025, Ohio finally ended subsidies for two scandal-linked coal plants: a step forward, but one that came years late and only after sustained public pressure.
The lesson is clear: without vigilant oversight and campaign finance reform, Ohio utility policy will continue to be written by the highest bidder.
Meanwhile, the rapid expansion of data centers across Ohio raises new utility concerns.
These facilities consume enormous amounts of energy and water, and currently face insufficient environmental review.
Massive corporations should not be able to dictate terms to Ohio communities.
My Commitments Demand full accountability for the HB 6 scandal and support a Department of Justice investigation into all connected parties.
Repeal any remaining ratepayer subsidies that flow from the HB 6 era.
Require extensive environmental and community impact studies before new data centers are approved.
Mandate long-term planning for data center energy usage, water consumption, and end-of-lifecycle facility management.
Encourage and subsidize green energy projects, including nuclear power, to diversify Ohio's energy mix and reduce reliance on fossil fuels.
Push for campaign finance reform to get utility money out of Ohio politics.
