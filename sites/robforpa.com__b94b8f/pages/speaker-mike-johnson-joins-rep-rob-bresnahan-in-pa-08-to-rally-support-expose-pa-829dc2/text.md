Pittston, PA – Speaker of the House Mike Johnson joined Congressman Rob Bresnahan in Pennsylvania’s 8th Congressional District today, highlighting Bresnahan’s work on behalf of Northeastern Pennsylvania and rallying supporters ahead of November’s election.
Speaker Johnson and Congressman Bresnahan were joined by Congressman Dan Meuser (PA-09), Wyoming Area Regional Police Chief and registered Democrat Mike Turner, Kingston Borough Police Chief Rich Kotchik, Pike County Sheriff Brian Vennie, Wyoming Valley FOP Lodge 36 President Mike Huntzinger, Frank Albert Memorial FOP Lodge 43 President Steven Scoble, Robert Yonick of Wyoming Valley FOP Lodge 36, and Idaho Congressman Russ Fulcher.
Speaker Johnson praised Bresnahan as a strong advocate for the district who has remained focused on delivering results for the working families he represents, and exposed Scranton Mayor Paige Cognetti as an extreme liberal who’s embraced radical policies and would hurt Northeastern Pennsylvania in Congress.
“Rob Bresnahan knows exactly who he works for: the people of Northeastern Pennsylvania,” said Speaker Johnson.
“In Congress, Rob has been a strong partner in securing our border, cutting taxes for working families, and eliminating waste and fraud in Washington.
Meanwhile, for two decades Paige Cognetti has embraced the out-of-touch policies championed by the Democrat Party that have left hardworking men and women, like the ones who make up the backbone of this district, behind.
We need Rob in Washington to keep fighting for workers and families.”
Bresnahan emphasized that his first priority in Congress remains Northeastern Pennsylvania and ensuring the district has a representative willing to fight for its interests in Washington.
“I’m grateful to Speaker Johnson for coming to Northeastern Pennsylvania and standing with us in this fight,” said Congressman Bresnahan.
“The people of this district sent me to Washington to go to bat for them, and that’s what I’ve done every single day.
We’re fighting to lower costs, protect taxpayers, secure our border, and make sure working families in Northeastern Pennsylvania have a voice in Congress.”
The visit underscored the stakes of the PA-08 race and Republicans’ commitment to defending a key battleground district in November.
Responding to Expected Cognetti Spin
The following stats are pulled from the Pennsylvania Uniform Crime Reporting System without spin.
Violent Crime, Overall
- She’ll cite a WVIA article stating that crime is down .
That figure only works because 2021 was cherry-picked as the starting point, the year after she took office, and it erases everything before it.
Her own police chief’s numbers still show violent crime rising 15.5% in 2022 and 1.5% in 2024 inside that same window.
Compare her tenure to what came before it, 2019-2024 versus 2013-2018, and homicides, rapes, and arson all rose.
Homicides
- She’ll point to a better 2025 and note two of the seven 2024 homicides were vehicular.
Homicides still rose from 11 (2013-2018) to 14 (2019-2024), a 27% increase confirmed by WVIA against federal and state data.
From 2021-2025 alone, the city recorded 15 homicides, and 2024 was the deadliest year in Scranton in over a decade, the same year a Scranton detective was shot in the line of duty.
One better year doesn’t erase that record, and recasting two homicides as “just vehicular” doesn’t make them less deadly.
Rapes
- She’ll blame a 2021 reporting methodology change that reclassified assaults as rapes.
If that were the real driver, aggravated assault numbers should have dropped sharply once the change took effect and stayed down.
They didn’t: 169 (2021), 165 (2022), 142 (2023), 114 (2024), 139 (2025), essentially flat.
Reported rapes still rose 62% between 2019 and 2024, confirmed by WVIA.
A flat assault line and a soaring rape line together mean this isn’t just paperwork.
Arson
- Arson cases rose 116% from 2019 to 2024, confirmed by WVIA using the city’s own reported figures.
Guns and Ammunition
- She will try to say that she didn’t disarm the police.
That doesn’t erase what her administration actually did: cut the guns and ammunition budget from $43,500 to $20,000 in 2021, a cut of more than 50%, after engaging with anti-police activist groups.
City Council, not Cognetti, added a separate $23,629 “special operations ammunition” line item that year to patch the hole.
She did not sign the 2021 budget.
She let it become law without her signature specifically because she objected to Council restoring that ammunition funding.
She can say now she doesn’t want to disarm police, but her 2020 comments and her refusal to sign the budget that funded them tell a different story about where she actually stood when it counted.
